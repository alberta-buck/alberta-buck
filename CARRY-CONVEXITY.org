* Purpose, goals, and how to read this document

** The question

The Alberta Buck stabilizes BUCK against a defined basket of commodity
tokens.  Two kinds of flow act on that equilibrium:

- *Organic flow*: BUCK buyers and sellers arriving at varying rates and
  intensities for their own reasons -- savers accumulating and spending,
  debtors converting mortgages and retiring, depositors entering and
  leaving the basket, and their waves and runs.  Organic behaviour is
  to be SUPPORTED (deep liquidity at par, cheap execution, no gates) and
  REWARDED (the basket's yield against demurrage).
- *Inorganic flow*: agents attempting to disrupt or corner the BUCK/TOKEN
  markets for profit or malice.  Inorganic behaviour is to be DETECTED
  and HARVESTED: the attacker's funds transferred to the BuckBasket
  investors' ROI.

The basket's issuance capacity and its reserves from prior profits are
to stand ready to amplify that harvest for all position holders --
while never holding positions that work AGAINST the restoration of
equilibrium (a wall of basket positions that K must push the credit
issuers through, or inventory that stalls the recovery it was bought
to hasten).  Ideally the cost of the basket's positions is borne by the
malicious actor, who must eat through them to recover their funds after
disrupting the equilibrium.

** The answer, in one paragraph

The system already holds equilibrium against organic flow and, when a
private defender cast exists, against inorganic flow.  But the harvest
goal is not met, and until 2026-09-01 was unmeasured: at x4 an
undefended attacker breaks the peg at zero cost to itself while
depositors realize -$2.0M to -$2.7M; with the full cast, depositors are
spared, the harvest (+$1.8M realized) goes to private defenders, and the
attacker's -$10.6M lands on third-party pool LPs and arbs.  Harvest
accrues to whoever is the COUNTERPARTY at the venue where the attack
lands, and the basket is not that counterparty today.  The remedy is a
laddered target zone: private liquidity harvests day-to-day traffic near
par; citizens' latent credit -- unleveraged insured assets behind
BuckCredit limits, paying no demurrage while poised -- issues into
premiums and retires into discounts through an automated facility; and
the basket stands behind both as a currency board in bundle units, with
an announced UNLIMITED strong-side undertaking (sell BUCK for the bundle
at par + eps) and an NAV-bounded weak-side undertaking (buy BUCK for the
bundle at par - eps).  K sees the SHADOW deviation -- bvib plus the
inventory the desk and the facility have absorbed or issued -- so the
slow lever mid-ranges every fast actuator it can see and the wall never
blinds the controller.  Restoration dominates harvest; the harvest is a
small, certain margin on both edges plus a stress fee on duress exits,
shared pro rata by a single receipt class; and the real return to
investors is the excursion that no longer happens.  A bvib-settled
perpetual with a funding rate keyed to the shadow deviation charges the
DURATION of positions held against the band -- the missing long-side
toll -- and its telemetry sees the accumulation phase.  Every element is
specified as a catalogue cell with a pass/fail gate before any contract
work.

** The common-mode loop: par, K, and the funding factor

- *Par is definitional.*  In the direct embodiment BUCK measures itself
  against its basket: =basketValueInBuck= (bvib) = sum_i basketAmount_i
  x price_i-in-BUCK, read as a 600 s TWAP over the basket's own
  TOKEN/BUCK pools with spot fallback on a cold pool.  Setpoint 1.0;
  error = 1 - bvib (=alberta-buck-ethereum-direct.org=; master doc,
  "Direct Variant").
- *Sign convention (decision of record).*  bvib > 1: the basket costs
  more BUCK than it should, BUCK is CHEAP (a DISCOUNT excursion), error
  < 0, K FALLS, credit contracts, supply shrinks, BUCK recovers.  bvib
  < 1: BUCK is DEAR (a PREMIUM excursion), K RISES, credit expands.
  Canary tests guard the direction.
- *K is the LTV cap*: creditLimit = activatedValue x K.  K0 = 0.75,
  rails (0, 0.95) so a railed K is still solvent; integral-dominant, a
  sustained 10% deviation spends rail-to-rail authority over tau_I = 90
  days with a 2% proportional kick; conditional-integration
  anti-windup at the rails; bumpless retune and re-prime; dT 1800 s
  (=EQUILIBRIUM.md=; =BuckKControllerBase.sol=).  "Deliberately slow: K
  is a structural lever, not a market maker" (=deploy.py=).
- *The funding factor* is the on-chain counter-cyclical toll on insured
  issuance: ff = max(0, 1 + 10 (b - p)/b), 2x at a 10% discount, ZERO
  once BUCK is 10% dear -- issuance is cheapest exactly in a premium.
  It scales the pre-mint reserve on the insurance principal, not the
  gross mint; zero-premium credit is exempt.  The sim's issue-rate arm
  (ff_amp x rate^ff_pow) has no on-chain counterpart.
- *The theta law* (sim-only, the debtors' decision rule): deploy credit
  while the all-in execution discount <= theta x apr; under an
  oversupply flood the marginal issuer stops at theta x apr, so the
  standing discount is set by the most aggressive unconverted debtor
  (~8% median, ~19.5% tail) -- the flood is untested
  (=REBALANCING-EQ.org= recommendation 4).
- *Latent credit* costs nothing until drawn: activation happens only
  inside =Buck.mint=, premium is charged on present insured value at
  activation (=CREDIT-IMPROVEMENT.org= D6), demurrage accrues only on
  HELD BUCK, and a drawn position "becomes cheaper to close the longer
  it is carried" through Jubilee relief.  D8 -- unvetted insurers can
  write themselves zero-premium credit and mint for free -- is the
  unclosed mainnet blocker.

** The basket: custody, issuance, redemption, treasury

- The BuckBasket registers one TOKEN/BUCK Uniswap V3 pool per
  constituent and custodies a SINGLE FULL-RANGE Buck-owned LP position
  in each; it is the sole LP in every basket pool (=deploy.py=
  pool_meta).  Full-range means the CPMM identity NAV = 2 x buckReserve
  and a 50/50 value split at any price (=BASKET-REDESIGN.md= 5.1).
- *DirectMint*: a depositor's TOKEN is paired with basket-minted BUCK at
  spot -- 100% LTV, K-immune (=Buck.mintFromBasket= bypasses credit and
  funding factor; master doc #10) -- and LP'd full-range; the depositor
  receives an ERC-721 receipt.
- *Redemption is sell-high*: the value claim theta x NAV is drawn from
  the overweight pools by a closed form over the BUCK reserves, valued
  against a price-scaled fixed-quantity target; value conservation
  keeps the remaining holders' coverage ratio unchanged.  The depositor
  is paid in TOKEN ONLY; BUCK profit (Bw - R) accrues to the treasury;
  a BUCK shortfall (Bw < R, the deflation branch) is covered from the
  depositor's TOKEN within =maxConversionLossBp= (1%), and NAV <
  principal reverts (=BASKET-REDESIGN.md= 5, 6).
- The regime table (=BASKET-REDESIGN.md= 6) predicts treasury revenue
  "largest exactly under inflation, when the peg most needs defending"
  and a costly exit under deflation "so holders keep P outstanding";
  the measured version (=plot_basket_split.py=) is that a single agent
  pushing BUCK 19% dear made every redemption take the deflation branch
  and cost depositors ~$1.54M "rather than the agent causing it".
- The directors (=alberta-buck-rebalance.org=): =pairs= (deployed) runs
  a seven-scale tick-EMA ladder per leg, pairwise quorum 4/7 on the
  confirmed turn, leash 30/25%, matched-pair trades; =vrate= is the
  single-window engine; =factor= (trend regime) is Python-only.  The
  COMMON MODE is the mean of the same ladders and was being discarded.
- The ops desk (=alberta-buck-operations.org=; =BuckBasketOps.sol=):
  the common mode drives four quadrants -- Q1 absorb / Q3 supply as
  self-reversing range orders (repo), Q2 retire / Q4 issue outright
  under a 30-day-past-the-leash persistence test.  Three bounds, each
  found by a runaway: per-leg 0.4% of the pool's BUCK reserve, position
  10% of NAV with inventory-based escalation, cumulative 10% of NAV.
  The desk is capitalized by =capitalizeMonetary= ("a currency board
  can only defend with reserves it built up earlier") and may not fund
  itself from depositor TOKEN.  =monetaryDeviationOffset()= exposes what
  the desk's inventory has taken out of K's error, deliberately not
  acted on.  Default =enabled: false=; it has never run inside a matrix
  or catalogue arm.
- The fence (=BuckBasketFence.sol=): caps basket issuance at K x TOKEN
  value at TWAP in a +/-15% band -- "a continuum of resting limit
  orders" -- so that K falling forces withdraw-and-burn and K rising
  lets it deploy.  Its motivating measurement: K at its floor 21% of a
  730-day run with the K-immune basket tranche at 47-52% of supply.
  Limitation 1: the budget is denominated in the unit that moves.

* The critique, where it lands, and the basket's own side of the catalogue

** The critique

Panoptic's "Yin (Carry) & Yang (Convexity) Require Each Other to Survive
& Thrive" (X, 2026-08-27; [[https://x.com/Panoptic_xyz/status/2093081191427805570][post]])
is the onchain transplant of the Artemis/Cole framing.  In our terms:

  A passive AMM LP is a carry trader -- a seller of perpetual optionality
  who collects a fixed fee (the implied volatility) while eating adverse
  selection (the realized volatility).  Onchain, the structural
  oversupply of LP positions suppresses IV below RV, and crypto is the
  most momentum-prone, fat-tailed asset class -- so the profitable side
  is BUYING convexity from LPs, not being one.  Carry books show negative
  skew ("sawtooth": steady fees, rare wipeouts); convexity books show
  positive skew ("stairstep").  A market is complete and stable only
  when both sides exist and both are PRICED.

L) as counterparty.  Static fee tiers (TOKEN/BUCK 30 bp, BUCK/USDC 5
bp): one implied vol for every regime and counterparty.  The depositor

treasury holds a one-sided call.

Evidence already in the repo, not previously named: the $1.54M

** Carry and convexity: what the basket sells and what it buys

Kyle (1985) and Glosten-Milgrom (1985): a standing quote is a free
option to informed traders and a paid service to uninformed ones; the
quoter's profit is the uninformed flow's spread minus the informed
flow's markout.  Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden and Zhang (2022) put
the AMM case in closed form: loss-versus-rebalancing sigma^2/8 per unit
time against a martingale price.  Panoptic's contribution is the
empirical inversion onchain (RV > IV) and the framing that carry and
convexity books need each other to be PRICED.

*Clarifies* =alberta-buck-ethereum-basket.org=: "the world's
arbitrageurs, working for free" is true of the BENIGN flow -- the
uninformed cycles that repin the pools and pay the fee -- and false of
the informed flow, which the same pools sell the basket's optionality
to.  The basket doc's yield thesis (Shannon's demon, +322 to +496 bp/yr
measured in =BASKET-REBALANCE.md=) is a DIFFERENTIAL harvest that
requires reversion; the COMMON-MODE convexity the same full-range
positions sell is a different exposure with a different owner of the
reversion (K), and the two must be separated in the position structure.
*Contrasts* the routing doc's sim, which measured that BUCK-unaware arbs
genuinely route through the basket pools (~1100 routed trades vs ~120
whale snaps) -- that flow is the workforce the treasury seeder of D5
exists to lubricate, and it must stay cheap.

** Target zones: a credible band stabilizes inside itself

Krugman (1991): with a credible commitment to intervene at the edges of
a band, the exchange rate inside the band is LESS volatile than the
fundamentals -- the honeymoon S-curve -- because speculators front-run
the edge.  Bertola and Caballero (1992): with IMPERFECT credibility the
effect reverses; expected realignment makes the rate more volatile near
the edges.  The asymmetry is empirical: Hong Kong's strong-side
Convertibility Undertaking (unlimited HKD sales at 7.75, 2005) has
never been abandoned and never been beaten; the SNB's EUR/CHF floor
(2011-2015), mechanically identical, was abandoned because unlimited
issuance accumulated a balance sheet approaching GDP in a foreign asset
against a FUNDAMENTAL pressure it could not make revert.

*Clarifies* =BuckBasketFence.sol=: "a continuum of resting limit orders"
is a band without announced edges and with a budget "denominated in the
unit that moves" (limitation 1); D3 gives the band announced,
bundle-denominated edges and keeps the fence for what it is good at --
K-scaling the PERSISTENT depositor stock.  *Contrasts* the ops doc's
reserve doctrine ("any bound it publishes will be probed -- the 1992
ERM crisis is the standing lesson"): that is the WEAK side's truth,
where the defender spends finite reserves; on the STRONG side the
defender issues its own liability, and the correct doctrine is the
opposite -- announce it unlimited.  *Explains* the catalogue: the x4
failures are all on the premium side because the weak side of the
current design (private absorbers, the desk's TOKEN book) is reserve-
bounded AND the strong side (issuance) is K-scaled and slow.

** The backstop at a penalty rate

Bagehot (1873): in a panic, lend freely, at a penalty rate, on good
collateral.  The penalty rate is what keeps the backstop from being used
in normal times and pays it when it is.

*Clarifies* the design owner's ruling that "the BuckBasket isn't a
market-making liquidity provider, but a stabilizing backstop": the
basket's depth sits OUTSIDE the private band, at a small fixed premium
eps, and earns that premium for depth-on-demand.  *Contrasts* the ops
doc's measured trade-off ("stabilization and extraction trade off":
10-day signal peak 1004 bp / desk +$623k; 80-day 1344 bp / +$746k;
outright inverts past 80 days): the penalty rate is a FIXED small
premium, never a stress-scaled extraction, and the recovery is never
held hostage (D1).

** Mid-ranging: two actuators, one process variable

Shinskey's valve-position (mid-ranging) control: when a fast small
actuator and a slow large one act on the same variable, the fast one
controls the process variable and the slow controller does NOT look at
the process variable -- it looks at the fast actuator's POSITION and
drives it back to mid-range.  Two controllers on one variable without
this separation oscillate or wind up.  The Federal Reserve's pre-2008
open-market desk is the monetary instance: the FOMC sets the target; the
desk holds the funds rate there with daily temporary repos; ever-larger
temporary operations in one direction are the signal for an outright
purchase.  The FX literature (Jurgensen 1983; Dominguez-Frankel 1993)
is the negative result: sterilized intervention -- the desk acting while
the policy lever is unaware -- is weak and transient.

** Corners and the ability to deliver

A corner breaks when the cornered asset can be delivered in any size at
a known price.  The Hunt silver corner (1980) was broken by changing the
rules mid-game -- effective, and corrosive to trust in the venue.  Hong
Kong's strong side breaks any HKD corner by standing rule.  The SNB
shows the one way unlimited delivery fails: when what it accumulates is
a liability you cannot make revert.

*Clarifies* the ops doc's "what only the contract can do -- Q4 is not
capped by K" and the fence's "the most K-immune half of the float": the
K-immune =mintFromBasket= is the system's ONLY anti-corner power, and it
must not be blunted by K-scaling; D3 resolves the conflict by duration
(persistent stock K-scaled; standing facility K-immune and
self-reversing).  *Explains* why the basket's unlimited undertaking is
SAFER than the SNB's: it sells BUCK dear for TOKEN that is cheap in BUCK,
on an axis where par is definitional and K forces the reversion, and Q2
closes the round trip.  The only path to the SNB's fate is governance
abandoning par.

** The elastic currency and the real-bills doctrine

The Federal Reserve Act (1913) created an elastic currency: money supply
expands when banks discount real bills at the window and contracts as
the bills self-liquidate.  Its historical flaw is pro-cyclicality
(credit expands as collateral values rise), which a counter-cyclical
lever exists to correct.

*Clarifies* the design owner's D6 ruling: the poised capital is NOT BUCK
but LATENT CREDIT -- unleveraged insured assets behind BuckCredit limits
-- issued into premiums and retired into discounts.  That is a privately
discounted elastic currency with K as the haircut and the funding factor
as the discount rate, and the on-chain funding factor already makes
issuance cheapest in a premium.  *Contrasts* =alberta-buck-demurrage.org=
(demurrage punishes idle BUCK) with the demand for standing defense
capital: no conflict, because the standing capital is not BUCK.
*Sharpens* =EQUILIBRIUM.md= approach forward (C), "a direct TOKEN/BUCK
stabilization channel for savers" -- the auto-pay-down facility of D6 is
that channel, on the credit side.

** Swing pricing: the exiting investor pays for the liquidity they consume

The Reserve Primary Fund broke the buck in September 2008 and those who

Liquity's Stability Pool (opt-in, paid) versus passive LPs in the
UST/3CRV pool of May 2022 (conscripted, expropriated) is the same
lesson in DeFi.

*Clarifies* =BASKET-REDESIGN.md='s regime table -- its deflation branch
is the pre-2014 money-market fund, and the measured expropriation

not fund from depositor TOKEN" (it refuses to conscript explicitly while

** Reserve adequacy and the two generations of crisis model

Krugman (1979) / Flood-Garber (1984): a peg defended with finite reserves
against a persistent fundamental misalignment fails at a computable
date.  Obstfeld (1986, 1994): with costly defense, attacks can be
self-fulfilling.  Both say the same thing about the weak side of a band:
its credibility is its reserve relative to the plausible outflow over
the horizon on which the slow lever can correct.

*Clarifies* the ops doc's "reserves are finite and the bound is public"
and the catalogue's x4 grind-down with the full cast (parity held, rail
margin spent: K 0.936, 17% railed) -- that cell is the empirical
adequacy frontier.  *Sets* the sizing rule of 7.5.

The first-generation model has a constructive corollary: the attack is
timed to the DISCONTINUITY -- the moment a flat edge backed by a finite
reserve stops being honored.  Remove the discontinuity and the timing
argument has nothing to bite on.  A defender whose bid price declines
smoothly with its remaining reserve is never "out"; it is only ever
"dearer", and the attacker's marginal cost rises with every unit sold
into it.  This is the shape of every constant-product bid (the
catalogue's superlinear squeeze cost is this property of the BUCK/USDC
pool), of Curve's stableswap between its flat centre and its convex
tails (the amplification parameter A is the dial), and of a central
bank that sells reserves by declining-price auction rather than at a
fixed rate.  *Clarifies* the weak-side design: the design owner's
tranche ladder (D3 refinement) is the discrete form, and the flat edge
of a classical target zone is its delta -> 0 limit -- the one limit
with a cliff.

** D1. Restoration dominates; the backstop earns a fixed premium

/Question./  When harvest and restoration conflict, which dominates?

/Historical./  Hong Kong, August 1998: the "double play" (short HKD to
force rates up under the currency board; short Hang Seng futures to
profit from the crash).  The HKMA spent HK$118bn over ten trading days
buying 33 constituents -- a bid wall in the venue where the attackers'
PROFIT was booked -- lifting the index from ~6,700 to ~7,800 into the
August expiry; the shorts were forced to cover into the wall (harvest at
ENTRY).  The unwind was restoration-first: held ~15 months, then
disposed through the Tracker Fund (Nov 1999; HK$33.3bn first tranche to
180,000 retail investors; >HK$100bn recouped over three years; ~HK$100bn
profit) -- from the RECOVERY, into organic demand, never sold back to the
attackers at a premium.  Then the commitment was hardened (the September
1998 technical measures); no comparable attack since.  Sterling 1992 is
the contrast: reserves spent against a misaligned fundamental.  The
difference is whether the defender can MAKE the reversion; par is
definitional and K manufactures it.

/Synthetic./  The ops doc's measurement-scale ladder (above): extraction
bought +$123k of desk profit for +340 bp of peak.  A ~20% deviation held
a year swung depositors' realized P&L by ~$2.7M: the organic cost of
prolonging an excursion is of the order of the ENTIRE harvest.

/Decision./  Restoration dominates.  The conflict exists on ONE of four
legs:

#+LATEX: {\scriptsize
| side                | entry leg                                              | exit leg                                                              |
|---------------------+--------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| squeeze (BUCK dear) | Q4 sell issuance at a premium: harvest AND restoration | attacker dumps back -> Q1/Q2 buy cheap, burn: harvest AND restoration |
| dump (BUCK cheap)   | Q1 buy the dump at a discount: harvest AND restoration | selling inventory back at a premium: harvest AGAINST restoration      |
#+LATEX: }

Harvest is taken on the three coinciding legs.  The fourth is unwound
TraHK-style -- rate-limited into organic flow at par over K's correction
horizon -- or burned (Q2) when persistence says the move was real.
Extraction is not an objective function.

/Refinement./  The basket is a stabilizing backstop, not a market maker,
and earns a SMALL FIXED premium eps for depth-on-demand (Bagehot).
Private LPs inside +/-x% harvest the traffic; the basket's deep
liquidity sits outside.  A recovering dump reaches the basket's ask side
only if it OVERSHOOTS into a premium, so inventory bought on the bid
side is sold at a premium only when the market pays it; the remainder is
disposed at par.  Spot tails (V3 now, V4 directional-fee tails later)
are the facility; a perpetual is not (a trader needing depth wants
conversion, not a derivative -- D5).

** D3. Two issuance classes; the Convertibility Undertakings

/Question./  The x4 failures are corners the K-scaled issuers cannot
answer; the only actor that can deliver BUCK on demand is the basket via
K-immune =mintFromBasket=; the fence wants basket issuance K-scaled.

/Historical./  Hong Kong's strong side (unlimited, standing, never
beaten); the SNB floor (unlimited, abandoned -- the balance sheet it
accumulated); the Hunt corner (rules changed mid-game).  Standing rules
beat emergency ones.

/Synthetic./  Squeeze x4 undefended: whale -$9.13M, bvib 0.69, K railed
all year.  In the isolation runs Q4 fired 2 and 10 times and Q4+Q2 was
what the external agent could not do.  The fence's coupling has the
SAME sign as the ops quadrants; its concerns -- a persistent K-immune
STOCK and a budget in the unit that moves -- do not apply to
self-reversing issuance retired by Q2.

/Decision./  Two issuance classes, distinguished by DURATION:

1. *Depositor issuance* (persistent stock: DirectMint partner BUCK living
   as long as the deposit) -- K-scaled via the fence once fence-in-the-
   loop passes its cell, so K governs the stock the economy carries.
2. *The Convertibility Undertakings* (standing, announced, UNLIMITED in
   aggregate on the strong side):
   - *Strong side.*  Anyone may buy BUCK from the basket at par + eps
     paying the basket BUNDLE in =basketAmount= proportions; or at
     par + p*eps paying ANY token with a conversion path known to the
     basket at deposit time (USDC, BUCK, WBTC, PAXG, ...), the basket
     spot-converting the incoming asset atomically into its underweight
     constituents by its rebalancing metrics.  p is a convenience factor
     (~2x the route conversion fees; may scale with size for impact).
     "Unlimited" is the bundle path; the convenience path is bounded by
     conversion capacity (=maxLegBp= on the route, so the redemption
     guard never bricks).
   - *Weak side.*  Anyone may sell BUCK to the basket for the bundle at
     par - eps, or for a single token at par - p*eps, the mix chosen by
     the basket's under-/over-weight metrics (sell-high) and converted
     to one TOKEN atomically in the redemption transaction.
     Reserve-bounded by NAV; the stress fee of D2 composes under duress.
   - *The weak side is a LADDER, not an edge* (refinement, 2026-09-02).
     Because the weak side's reserve is finite and "must outlast K's
     correction", its bid price scales DOWN with the reserve capacity
     remaining: a set of positions sized and priced so that liquidity
     is always available, but successive tranches are available at
     successively poorer rates.  Tranche k is struck at a discount
     eps + (k-1)*delta and sized at a fraction phi of the reserve
     remaining when it is struck, so the reserve depletes geometrically
     and never reaches zero at a finite price.  There is no cliff for an
     attacker to time (4.8), the attacker's marginal cost rises with
     cumulative size by construction, and the deviation itself becomes
     an honest gauge of reserve stress -- which is the coupling to K the
     shadow term wants (7.4).  delta -> 0 is the flat edge; a fixed
     delta/phi ratio is the constant-product bid (the shape the basket's
     full-range position already has); Curve's stableswap amplification
     is the interpolation.  The same rule applies to the STRONG side's
     capacity-bounded convenience path (the premium p*eps rising as the
     epoch's conversion capacity is consumed); only the unlimited bundle
     path is flat.
   - K-immune by design (fires only at a premium/discount, exactly when
     the funding factor already says issuance/retirement is cheap);
     accounted to the monetary book; retired/unwound by Q2/Q1 under the
     persistence test; bundle-priced so it carries no differential
     exposure.
   - *Announced unlimited.*  A published cumulative cap would be probed;
     what unlimited issuance accumulates here is reversion inventory on
     a controlled axis, and Q2 guarantees the balance sheet shrinks back.

/Consequence./  A currency board in bundle units: while both sides are
honored, bvib is pinned inside [1/(1+eps), 1/(1-eps)] and Krugman's
honeymoon applies inside.  The x4 squeeze re-run: the pool price cannot
exceed par + p*eps; the whale's $40M buys BUCK from the basket; the
basket books ~2 p eps over the round trip; the excursion never exceeds
the band; K barely moves; depositors never enter the deflation branch.
Deterrence replaces harvest -- and for basket investors (from -$2.7M to
~+$1M) deterrence wins.

** D4. K sees the shadow bvib (mid-ranging)

/Question./  The desk absorbs a dump, bvib improves, K sees less error
and stops tightening, the desk is stranded; under D3 the desk's book is
unbounded on the strong side.  Sterilized or unsterilized?

/Historical./  The FX-intervention literature; the Fed desk's temporary-
to-outright rule; mid-ranging control (4.4).

/Synthetic./  Runaway one (49.9% of pool depth, profitable in 7/9 seeds,
never escalated); the 90-day run (1.25M BUCK held while K still fell
0.745 -> 0.584 -- "not suppressed AT THAT SIZE").

/Decision./  MID-RANGING.  K's process variable becomes the shadow
deviation

  bvib_shadow = bvib + lambda * (net monetary inventory / reference depth)

with BUCK ABSORBED under the weak side counting positive and BUCK ISSUED
under the strong side counting negative -- bvib as it would have been
without the desk.  Equivalently, K is tasked with driving the desk's
inventory to zero.  Three things follow: the desk's position is never a
bet on "K might keep forcing"; Q2 burns need no special treatment
(burned inventory leaves the book and the shadow term drops to zero --
the temporary/outright and sterilized/unsterilized distinctions are the
same distinction); and it IS the achieved-action anti-windup the ops doc
asked for.  The design owner's statement of intent: "even if we manage
to raise bvib back to parity, K must reflect the error we've damped by
our action."

3. *The perp is a bvib FUNDING-RATE LEVER, not a trading venue.*

** D6. Demurrage stays; poised capital is latent credit; the auto-pay-down facility

/Question./  Should capital verifiably parked in defense posture be

/Historical./  Woergl (demurrage's target is the idle hoard); interest on
reserves vs negative deposit rates; catastrophe bonds (coupon for

/Synthetic./  Which poised capital EARNED in the catalogue: the USDC

BUCK-holding mix bled; the credit base was scale-stable, +$0.63M marked,
and paid nothing while poised because it holds no BUCK.

/Decision (design owner)./  Demurrage on poised BUCK is not something
that should happen -- because the poised capital is NOT BUCK.  It is
LATENT CREDIT: a hopefully revenue-generating insured asset (farms,
homes, businesses -- the plentiful pent-up un-leveraged asset base)
behind a BuckCredit contract with an unused limit, ready for deployment,
with no BUCK sitting in an account accruing demurrage.  When BUCK is at
a premium, holders issue against their limit and buy assets with the
temporarily too-valuable BUCK; at a discount they buy BUCK cheap and pay
their credit down.  Four rules:

1. *No readiness rebate.*  Demurrage stays; BUCK parked in the basket

   and never paid demurrage; the strong side is issuance, not inventory;
   the monetary book's own BUCK pays demurrage as an internal transfer
   to the Jubilee.
2. *eps is LARGER than a backstop alone would choose*, leaving an inner
   zone where ordinary citizens with available credit "get in on the
   deflation action" and "buy back their credit limit at a temporary
   inflation moment".  The private latent-credit cast is the middle of
   the ladder; the basket is the wall behind it.
3. *The auto-pay-down facility.*  Automated, user-parameterized
   machinery attached to a BuckCredit position: issue against the limit
   when bvib < 1 - x (premium) and place the proceeds (default: a basket
   deposit under the single receipt class); when bvib > 1 + y

   the BUY; the burn closes the line).  Parameters: x, y, maximum

   repays when it is cheap.
4. *Facility utilization enters K's shadow term* (7.3): deployment in a

   ever wanted, takes the cat-bond form (capped vault, only-toward-par,

** D7. K mid-ranges the level's POSITIONS; the shadow bvib and the position vector are the two alternatives to test

(Design owner + Claude, 2026-09-03.)

/Question./  As built, K is one PID on one process variable.  Several
fast facilities now act on that variable at higher frequency -- the
desk's Q1/Q3, the undertakings and their ladder, the facility
population, the seeder -- and D4 feeds their books back to K through a
reconstituted price.  Are the facilities themselves P, I or D
accumulators of K?  Is the whole a higher-level controller, and is it
drifting toward an estimator (Kalman) or an optimal controller (LQR /
MPC) that should be built explicitly?  And is the shadow bvib the
right coupling, or should each facility report how over- or under-sold
it is, with the metadata a higher controller needs to sum it?

/What is built, in control terms./  None of the facilities writes K;
the agents read =buckK= only for their own decisions.  Each is a
BANDED PROPORTIONAL ACTUATOR on bvib -- a price band triggers a trade
-- whose STATE is an inventory q_i = integral of (absorbed - issued),
bounded by a cap.  In flow terms that state is an integrator; in price
terms, while the facility is unsaturated and the book is linear, q_i /
D is the price error the facility removed, so it reads as a
proportional reconstruction of the masked error.  Saturation is a
constraint flag, not a PID quantity.  The one coupling to K is the
observer's sum, bvib + sum_i lambda_i q_i / D, read by the same PID as
the price, plus gamma boosting the integral increment when any
facility is at a bound.

Expanding that through the PID shows what the shadow term is:

  e_s = e + sum_i lambda_i q_i / D
  K   = K0 + Kp e + Ki Int(e) + Kd de/dt                                (the price loop, unchanged)
          + sum_i (lambda_i / D) [ Kp q_i + Ki Int(q_i) + Kd dq_i/dt ]   (an inventory loop per facility)

So D4 already contains P on position, I on position, and D on
position -- which is P on FLOW, the disturbance itself measured
directly, i.e. feedforward.  The terms are right.  Three things about
the packaging are not:

1. *One scalar lambda_i locks the inventory loop's P:I:D ratio to the
   price loop's.*  The two loops have different plants.  From K to
   bvib the process is self-regulating (the cast reverts price); from
   K to inventory it is INTEGRATING -- level control -- which wants a
   slow, P-heavy PI (Skogestad's SIMC rules for an integrating
   process: Kc = 1 / (k' tau_c), tau_I = 4 tau_c).  One lambda cannot
   tune both.  This is the missing lever.
2. *The units drag the reference depth D into the on-chain law.*  D is
   the pools' BUCK reserve: time-varying, and the very quantity a
   concentrated position (the seeder's, a private LP's) distorts
   (WAVE3.org decision 8).  Written as position over cap, the
   controller never needs D.
3. *gamma and "pinned vs idle" are symptoms.*  With the position loop
   explicit, a saturated facility's position pins at its cap and the
   price error carries the margin automatically; no switching, no
   schedule.  An unreadable NAV is a sensor fault, not saturation
   (WAVE3.org decision 9, closed 2026-09-03).

/Historical./  Mid-ranging / valve-position control (Shinskey; Allison
and Isaksson 1998; Allison and Ogawa 2003; Haugwitz, Karlsson, Velut
and Hagander 2005 on anti-windup in mid-ranging), already in 4.4.
HABITUATING CONTROL (Henson, Ogunnaike and Schwaber 1995): two inputs,
one output; the fast expensive input takes the transients, the slow
cheap one takes the steady-state load, and the cost asymmetry is
explicit in the objective -- that asymmetry IS the carry critique
(Friedman 1951's carry-cost objection to the commodity-reserve
currency; the habituating answer is to keep the stock near zero by
letting the cheap lever do the persistent work).  AVERAGING LEVEL
CONTROL (Cheung and Luyben 1979; McDonald, McAvoy and Tits 1986):
inventory is a surge tank, K the outflow valve, net demand the inflow;
the objective is the smoothest outflow that never overflows -- "K
glides" plus the capacity constraint -- and its optimal policy is a
small MPC.  The INTERNAL MODEL PRINCIPLE (Francis and Wonham 1976): a
persistent demand shift is a step into the inventory integrator, so
zero steady-state inventory REQUIRES an integrator on inventory; the I
term on position is not optional.  Skogestad (2000, 2004) on plantwide
control-structure design: the levels table of 6.4 is the standard
regulatory / supervisory / optimization layering, and "aggregate
position = 0" is a self-optimizing controlled variable (hold it, and
carry is minimized without knowing the disturbance).  In market
microstructure the shadow bvib formula is the Avellaneda and Stoikov
(2008) reservation price, mid + inventory x risk aversion -- a market
maker's OWN quoting variable, a level-1 object.  The monetary
instances: HKMA's Convertibility Undertakings are level 1 with no
level 3, and the aggregate balance swings accordingly; the Fed's
temporary-to-outright rule is level 2; sterilized intervention
(Jurgensen 1983) is the negative result.

/Optimal control and estimation./  The reduced plant (7.2) is linear
with a handful of states: price error, aggregate position, their
integrals, K.  With costs on price error squared, position squared
weighted by carry, and the change in K squared, LQR returns a linear
feedback on exactly the vector [e, Int(e), s, ds/dt] -- "each
facility's signal, with metadata, summed by a higher controller" --
with the weights DERIVED from stated costs instead of swept, and it
derives the lambda knee D4 asserts.  LQR ignores constraints; MPC
handles them, but a QP per =compute()= is not an on-chain law.
Explicit MPC for a box-constrained system this small is
piecewise-affine (Bemporad et al. 2002): a linear law inside the
operating region plus rail logic -- which is what a mid-ranging
cascade with anti-windup already is.  So: optimal control EXPLICITLY
IN THE DESIGN, offline in the sim (WP-16), and a fixed linear state
feedback with rails on-chain.  Take gain RATIOS from LQR and absolute
gains from stability margins, because the elasticity from K to flow
is the least-known parameter.  A Kalman filter is not the tool:
inventory is measured exactly and the observer is algebraic.  The one
hidden variable is the PERSISTENCE of the demand flow, which decides
temporary versus outright (level 2); that is a disturbance observer
(Chen et al. 2016) -- a low-pass on the position rate with a threshold
and a dwell -- and it is the ops doc's persistence test made explicit.
The price gauge is the one noisy sensor, and its fix is decision 8,
deterministic.  The K loop's bandwidth is bounded by its dead time
through credit issuance; the facilities exist to cover that window,
and their capacity must cover dead time x worst-case flow (7.4's
reserve-adequacy sum).

/Decision (design owner)./  BOTH designs are kept as alternatives, to
be implemented and tested on the same grid (WAVE3.org, "The
controller-alternatives test plan"); the missing levers are corrected
in both.

*The corrected controller (one implementation, two aggregation modes).*
K runs TWO loops on one lever, one integrator each:

  K = K0 + [ Kp e + Ki Int(e) + Kd de/dt ]         the price loop (Direct)
         + [ Kq s + Kqi Int(s) + Kqd ds/dt ]       the position loop

with rails and per-integrator anti-windup, s the observer's aggregate
position, and (Kq, Kqi, Kqd) INDEPENDENT of (Kp, Ki, Kd).  The
observer offers two aggregations of the same =IStabilizer= books:

- *S (shadow, D4 in its own units)*: s_S = sum_i lambda_i q_i / D,
  dimensionless, in price units.  With (Kq, Kqi, Kqd) = (Kp, Ki, Kd)
  the corrected controller reproduces D4 as built, term for term in
  the interior (at a rail the anti-windup is per integrator, so the
  identity is interior-exact and rail-approximate); with independent
  gains it is D4 with the missing lever.
- *V (position vector)*: s_V = sum_i w_i q_i / cap_i, with cap_i the
  facility's own inventory bound in BUCK and w_i a governance COST
  WEIGHT (sum w_i = 1), so s_V is the level's cost-weighted fill in
  [-1, 1], the same units as saturation, and D never enters the law.

Facilities do NOT declare a P, D or I type: every facility reports the
same physical state, and the controller derives position, its
integral and its rate, as it does for price.  The metadata a facility
owns is its cap, its saturation, and its cost weight.  =IStabilizer=
gains =position()= (q_i / cap_i, signed 1e18, absorbed positive),
=positionCap()= (cap_i in BUCK) and =stale()=; =netInventory=,
=capacity= and =saturation= stay.  gamma is retained as an optional
schedule with default 0: with the position loop explicit its job is
done by structure, and it is measured, not assumed.  With Kq = Kqi =
Kqd = 0 (and gamma = 0) the controller IS =BuckKControllerDirect=;
that identity replaces the lambda = gamma = 0 identity as the
regression test.  The price loop and =fundingFactor()= read the RAW
bvib (WAVE3.org decision 10, 2026-09-04): the position enters K only
through the position loop, and participants learn of the interventions
through K's motion, not through the mint gate.

*The lever count.*  Integral action can zero at most as many
controlled variables as there are independent manipulated variables.
K is one lever; it can zero ONE position -- the aggregate s -- so the
controller carries one position integrator, never one per facility
(two integrators on one lever with different residuals wind against
each other).  The DISTRIBUTION of inventory among the facilities (the
differential modes) therefore needs its own levers, and today it has
none: no facility skews its own band by its own inventory.  Each
level-1 stabilizer gets a LOCAL SKEW,

  band_i(t) = band_i0 + kappa_i q_i / cap_i

the Avellaneda-Stoikov reservation-price adjustment applied to the
facility's own edge (the desk's Q1/Q3 thresholds, the undertakings'
eps on each side, the facility's x / y, the seeder's range centre): a
facility long inventory bids lower and offers sooner, so its own flow
reverts its own book.  This is where the shadow-price idea belongs --
one level down.  kappa_i is a per-facility P (or slow PI) on its own
position; K's aggregate integrator is the common mode.  Common mode
to K, differential modes to the local skews: n positions, n levers
(WP-14).

*Sensor faults.*  A facility whose cap cannot be read reports its LAST
GOOD cap with a staleness flag; position is always readable
(=netInventory= depends on nothing external); saturation follows from
the held cap; a disabled stabilizer is excluded from the aggregate.
Never "pinned", never "idle" (WAVE3.org decision 9, where the
mechanics of an unreadable NAV are recorded).

*What is NOT done.*  No on-chain MPC or QP; no Kalman filter on
inventory; no per-facility integrators in K; no reconstituted price as
K's input under V (S keeps it, as the alternative under test); no
fast-controller filtered signals into K (6.4 stands).

*Optimal control, as derived (WP-16, 2026-09-12).*  The LQR of record
is LOOP-AT-A-TIME.  With one lever and both integrals costed, the
reduced plant has a conserved direction -- k_s (a Int(e) + e) + k_e s
is unchanged by any input, so the price integral and the position carry
the same information (the impact-scaled cumulative net flow) and cannot
both be zeroed by K -- and the joint Riccati iteration diverges.  Each
loop's LQR on its own plant reproduces the sim's price gains exactly
(the anchor) and gives the position loop Kq 2.42 per unit s_S, Kqi
8.1e-8/s, tau_s 127 d at the carry weight of record; the joint closed
loop then carries a structural eigenvalue at 1 (the two integrators'
contents can shift between them without moving K), bounded in the sim
only by the band and the rails.  A joint law would need the band -- a
hybrid, which the cascade with anti-windup already is.  Two facts of
the sim shape what those gains can do: K is a weak lever (~50k
BUCK/day of flow per unit), so the derived position loop spends its
whole rail at a 21% fill of the undertakings' reserve and the
S0-equivalent lambda is ~24 against the 0.5-1 the D4 cells ran; and
the undertakings' book on a calm arm is transient, unwound at par
within days.  WAVE3.org decisions 27-31 carry the choices this
leaves: the persistence horizon, the position loop's authority, the
depth axis, P9's window, the two-integrator drift.  What follows
from these findings for the structure itself is D9 (2026-09-13).

*What decides between S and V.*  The grid: the same disruptions, the
same facility mixes and capacities, both aggregations, gains derived
the same way (WP-16), judged by G1-G8 plus the habituation and
book-loading gates (predictions 9 and 10).  V is expected to win on
units (no D), on the guard-trip days, and on tuning transparency; S
is the incumbent and may win on simplicity if the position loop's
gains turn out to want the price loop's ratios anyway.  R5 is

/The finding in control terms./  The design owner's reading is right,
and the conserved direction states it exactly.  The price integral and
the facilities' inventory are two GAUGES on one quantity: the
cumulative net demand for BUCK at the peg.  K moves supply, and the
band routes that supply into one gauge or the other.  While a facility
holds price the flow lands in inventory and the price gauge reads
nothing; when the facility is drained or absent the same flow lands in
price and the inventory gauge is pinned at its cap.  K never has two
independent errors to serve.  It has one error read by two
instruments, and which instrument is live depends on the regime.  4.4
already said so: "the slow controller does NOT look at the process
variable -- it looks at the fast actuator's POSITION ... Two
controllers on one variable without this separation oscillate or wind
up."  D7's corrected controller put BOTH loops on K, and WP-16
measured the consequence.

The fight has a specific shape.  A facility absorbs a lump and unwinds
it at par within days (finding 2).  The position integrator keeps the
residue of that excursion; the residue holds K slightly low; the
standing price error that leaves is what the price integrator then
winds against, on its 90-day constant.  The two integrators end up
holding offsetting contents whose sum is the K the plant wants, and
the split between them drifts freely -- the eigenvalue at exactly 1
(finding 4).  In the contract only the rails bound it, and the worst
case is both integrators pinned at opposite rails with K interior, the
per-integrator anti-windup fighting a saturation that is not real.

/Why faster K gains are not the answer (way (a))./  Three measured
facts make K a slow lever by physics, not by tuning:

1. The daily price error is noise on the day scale (std 0.70%, lag-1
   -0.53): the cast restores the price inside the day and overshoots.
   A larger Kp moves K by that noise; a Kd amplifies it.  Finding 9 is
   the derivative form: the implied flow gain would jerk K by tenths
   from one lumpy fill.
2. K acts through the borrowers' arrival hazard and their K-scaled
   limits (=equilibrium_agents.py=, the k / k_ref hazard).  A step in K
   yields a supply RAMP that compounds late (+8k BUCK at day 240,
   +2.15M at day 364 for +0.20).  A K move lasting a day moves supply
   by a few thousand BUCK.  No gain makes a one-day K move matter.
3. The lever is asymmetric.  A cut to 0.55 delevered 6.3M BUCK at once
   -- a margin call; a rise is an offer nobody has to take (cuts 55k
   vs rises 44k BUCK per K-day at depth 10; 44k vs 75k at depth 40).
   A linear controller of any speed does not know this.

Short-horizon events belong to the level-1 facilities by physics: they
hold inventory, K does not.  What CAN be faster is not K's response
but the DECISION about which part of the flow is K's business -- the
persistent part.  Way (b) delivers exactly that, and it is a different
mechanism from higher gains.  Way (a)'s single-integrator half is
right and is kept (stage 1); its higher-gain half is not.  This also
settles WAVE3.org decision 7 in the direction WP-16's anchor found: the
2% P term is what a quadratic cost on the instantaneous error wants
when the cast itself reverts within the day, and no larger P is
justified by any measurement in hand.

/What way (b) is, in this system's terms./  D7's paragraph "Optimal
control and estimation" named the one hidden variable, the PERSISTENCE
of the demand flow, and its tool, a disturbance observer.  WP-16 then
derived the law that wants it: the LQR's flow-state gain L_d = 210
says the optimal K is MOSTLY FEEDFORWARD from the disturbance flow,
and finding 9 says it was unrealisable only because the flow was taken
by differencing one lumpy gauge.  The missing component is an
ESTIMATOR that fuses every channel through which excess demand is met
-- the facilities' fills, the desk's net inventory, direct-mint net
flow, debtor draws and repayments, the pools' reserves -- all on chain
already, so the sensor is free.  With the disturbance modelled as a
persistent component (decay T_p) plus a transient one (decay tau_t,
days), the steady-state Kalman filter is an alpha-beta tracker on the
fused flow: two exponential averages in the contract (Kalman 1960;
Kalata 1984).  The persistent estimate lags by ~tau_t, not by the
90-day price integral.  The law:

  e_s   = e + lambda s                  the shadow error: ONE signal (S units; in V units s_V / lambda_V)
  F_hat = the fused net flow (BUCK/day, sold positive); F_p its persistent part (a slow average); F_t = F_hat - F_p
  K     = K0 + clip(-F_p / g, rails)    feedforward: the K whose supply ramp cancels the persistent flow
             + Kp e_s + Ki Int(e_s)     ONE PI -- the price loop's -- on the shadow error (S0k as built)

with g the supply ramp per unit K (WP-16's F_K, identified, not
assumed) and the one integrator covering only the part of g we got
wrong -- the internal model principle (4.6) doing the one job it is
needed for.  The landing: K leaves the rail when the supply still in
the pipe -- g times the uptake lag tau_K times the K excursion --
equals the book remaining; that is the powered-descent timing of the
design owner's analogy (Acikmese and Ploen 2007), and P9's
one-sign-change test measures it.  The price loop on K is not a second
loop: the price term of the shadow error covers the regime in which no
facility is engaged, the position term the regime in which one is.
The second lever is the FACILITY (its inventory and its local skew,
D7); the third, where it exists, the desk's outright operations.

Two limits on the analogy, of record.  The rocket knows its physics to
a percent and the wind is a perturbation; our g differs 2x between
cuts and rises and depends on the cast, and a dump is 5-8x K's
authority.  So during a disruption the feedforward sits at the rail,
the facilities absorb, and the estimator earns its keep on the
RELEASE.  And every number above is from the thin cast: the ratio of
K's authority to the disturbance scale is an assumption about how much
of the money supply is credit-created, and belongs on the organic
ladder (=ORGANIC-SCALE.org=) as a headline number per rung.

/The structural conclusion./  K takes ONE integrated signal.  The
second lever is the facility.  The sensor is an estimator of the flow
and its persistence -- level 2 of 6.4 made explicit: the ops doc's
persistence test as a filter instead of a dwell.  D7's S and V stay
under test as the two-integrator alternative, and cycle 1 (in flight)
is read as the single-versus-double integrator test it already
contains.  Proposed for the design owner (WAVE3.org decision 38): the
two-loop form of D7 becomes the alternative and one PI on the shadow
error the incumbent once stage 1 reads that way; the estimator follows
stages 2-3.

** The plant, reduced

The ops doc's =monetary_ops.py= abstraction is adequate here: one
common-mode price with constant-product impact against depth and an OU
reversion whose rate is what the private cast and K supply.  A flow F
(BUCK sold, positive) moves bvib by ~F/D at impact; the cast reverts it
on its threshold-gated timescales; K moves supply through creditLimit
on tau_I.  Nothing below depends on the reduced form except the
qualitative claims; the catalogue runs the real contracts.

** The controller and the shadow term

  bvib_shadow = bvib + lambda_d * I_d / D + lambda_c * I_c / D
  e           = 1 - bvib_shadow
  dK/dt       = Kp * de/dt + Ki * e         (conditional integration at the rails)

Signs: BUCK absorbed under the weak side (or retired by the facility)
makes I positive, raises bvib_shadow, makes e more negative, and K
falls harder -- the contraction the absorption was betting on.  BUCK
issued under the strong side (or by the facility) makes I negative,
lowers bvib_shadow, makes e more positive, and K rises -- restoring the
headroom the fast actuators spent.  A Q2 burn removes the inventory
from I_d; the shadow term drops; K sees the real bvib; no double count.
lambda -> 0 is today's controller; lambda large makes K chase every
temporary operation.  The mid-ranging claim: with lambda at its knee,
the desk's inventory returns to zero within ~tau_I of any excursion
that K can correct, and grows without bound only when the deviation is
structural -- which is the signal the persistence test uses to
escalate to Q2/Q4.

** The position loop (D7)

The two-loop form, with s in either the S or the V units of D7:

  s_S = sum_i lambda_i I_i / D            s_V = sum_i w_i I_i / cap_i
  e   = 1 - bvib
  dK/dt = Kp de/dt + Ki e + Kq ds/dt + Kqi s + Kqd d2s/dt2     (conditional integration at the rails)

Signs are D4's: absorbed inventory (I > 0) drives K DOWN through Kq
and Kqi (the contraction the absorption was betting on); issued
inventory drives K up.  With (Kq, Kqi, Kqd) = (Kp, Ki, Kd) and s = s_S
this is 7.3's law exactly.  The plant from K to s is INTEGRATING: a
change in K moves the net flow F through creditLimit on tau_I, and
the active facility absorbs F while it holds price, so ds/dt ~ F /
cap while it is unsaturated.  The position loop is therefore LEVEL
CONTROL of a surge tank fed by the demand flow and drained by K, and
its tuning rules are the SIMC integrating-process rules: choose the
loop's closed-loop time constant tau_s (slower than tau_I, faster
than the persistence horizon), then Kq ~ 1 / (k_s tau_s) and Kqi =
Kq / (4 tau_s), with k_s the flow per unit K per unit cap -- the
least-known number, to be identified in the sim (WP-16) and confirmed
on the grid.  The ds/dt term is inflow feedforward; it may be zero.

The mid-ranging claim restated for D7: with the position loop tuned,
the aggregate position returns to within 10% of zero inside 1.5 tau_s
after any excursion K can correct, with at most one sign change (no
limit cycle from the two integrators), and grows without bound only
when the deviation is structural -- the level-2 signal.  The local
skews (kappa_i) redistribute inventory among the facilities without
moving K: under a differential disturbance (one facility loaded, the
level's aggregate near zero) K holds and the loaded facility's own
skew unwinds it.

** The undertakings

Strong side: the basket sells 1 BUCK for (1 + eps) bundles' worth of
TOKEN (bundle path) or (1 + p*eps) bundles' worth of any convertible
token, converting atomically.  Whenever the pools price BUCK above
(1 + p*eps) bundles -- bvib < 1/(1 + p*eps) -- an arbitrage exists: buy
BUCK from the basket, sell it in the pools.  So

  bvib >= 1 / (1 + p*eps)     always, up to conversion capacity,

and >= 1/(1+eps) with no capacity bound at all on the bundle path.  Weak
side: the basket buys 1 BUCK for (1 - eps) bundles; whenever the pools
price BUCK below (1 - p*eps) bundles the arbitrage runs the other way:

  bvib <= 1 / (1 - p*eps)     while R_w > 0, up to conversion capacity.

The band is asymmetric in what it is backed by: issuance (unbounded) on
the strong side; NAV on the weak side.  The monetary book records
issued and absorbed BUCK; the persistence test escalates a standing book
to outright (Q2 burn of absorbed BUCK; Q4 acknowledgment of issued BUCK
as permanent supply, K then carrying it) exactly as the desk does today.

*The weak-side ladder.*  Let R_0 be the weak-side reserve in bundle
units and rho = R / R_0 the fraction remaining.  Tranche k (k = 1, 2,
...) bids for BUCK at (1 - eps - (k-1) delta) bundles per BUCK and is
sized at phi R_{k-1}, where R_{k-1} is the reserve remaining when it is
struck.  After n filled tranches

  R_n     = R_0 (1 - phi)^n                 (never zero at finite n)
  b_n     = 1 - eps - n delta               (the next bid, bundles per BUCK)
  bvib    <= 1 / b_n                        (the deviation the ladder permits)
  S_n     = R_0 (1 - (1 - phi)^n) / b_avg   (cumulative BUCK absorbed)

so the deviation an attacker can force grows only as the LOGARITHM of
the reserve fraction spent, while its average sale price worsens
linearly in n.  Limits: delta = 0 is the flat edge (finite reserve at
one price -- the cliff); delta/phi held fixed as both -> 0 is the
constant-product bid, whose price-versus-size curve is the basket's
existing full-range position; the stableswap family interpolates.  Two
couplings to K follow.  As tranches fill, bvib rises past 1/(1 - eps)
into the sloped region -- the raw error grows -- AND the absorbed
inventory I_d grows, raising bvib_shadow; both push K to contract, so
reserve depletion is what ACCELERATES the structural correction rather
than what precedes its failure.  lambda_d is therefore tuned with the
ladder present (3c before 3d).  The strong side's convenience path uses
the same rule against conversion capacity: p_k rises as the epoch's
C_leg is consumed; the bundle path is flat because it is unbounded.

The per-leg bound C_leg is a liveness constraint (the redemption guard
must never brick), not a policy bound; it is the reason a one-day
squeeze larger than the day's conversion capacity still leaves a
transient trough, and the reason the directional dynamic fee (3b) and
the perp's funding (3f) exist.

** The attacker's cost, before and after

Today (squeeze of size S in the BUCK/USDC pool): cost = slippage against
the pool's depth on the way in and out + fees, paid to the pool's LPs
and the arbs; damage unbounded until the cast and K respond; at x4
undefended, damage lasts a year and the whale ends flat.

Under the ladder: the pool price is capped at par + p*eps by the
undertaking, so beyond the band the whale buys from the basket; cost >=
S * (p*eps entry + p*eps exit) + fees + f * duration (if the position
is expressed or hedged in the perp).  Damage: |bvib - 1| <= p*eps outside
the conversion-capacity window; inside it (S > C_leg per epoch), the
transient is bounded by what the cast and the directional fee price at
tick speed.  The harvest per attack is small and certain -- ~2 p eps S
-- and accrues to the single receipt class; the attacker's expected
value is negative at every size, so the rational attacker does not
attack.  This is the target-zone result: the band converts the harvest
into deterrence.

** Reserve adequacy on the weak side

The weak side is the reserve-bounded flank.  With a flat edge its
credibility condition would be

  R_w  >=  max over plausible scenarios of  integral over tau_I of (net BUCK sold to the basket at par - eps)

-- the reserve must outlast K's correction of the deviation producing
the outflow, and the attack is timed to the day it does not.  With the
ladder the condition changes character: the reserve is never exhausted
at a finite price, so adequacy becomes a statement about the DEVIATION
the system tolerates per fraction of reserve spent -- the choice of
(delta, phi) -- and about whether K's correction arrives before the
deviation the ladder permits at the plausible cumulative outflow exceeds
the tail gate.  Formally: for the worst plausible outflow S over tau_I,
the ladder's n(S) satisfies 1 / (1 - eps - n(S) delta) within the
deviation the gates allow, with the shadow-coupled K restoring rho
toward 1 inside tau_I.  The catalogue's x4 grind-down with the full cast
(parity held, K 0.936, 17% railed) is the empirical frontier at today's
NAV with an implicit CPMM ladder; wave 3c re-measures it across (delta,
phi) and wave 3h with the seeder.  Sources of R_w beyond NAV: the desk's
capitalization grant and treasury accrual (existing), and the cat-bond
vault (D6, optional).  Nothing else.

** The facility

Participant i has limit L_i, drawn d_i, thresholds (x_i, y_i), and a
deployment cap m_i.  Hazard of issuing rises with the premium beyond
x_i and with headroom (L_i - d_i); hazard of retiring rises with the
discount beyond y_i and with d_i.  Aggregate utilization u = sum d_i /
sum L_i is a fast actuator's position and enters I_c.  Pro-cyclicality
(4.6) is bounded by K: a premium regime raises K and headroom, so the
facility can issue more into the premium -- which is the correct
direction -- and a discount regime lowers K, which forces retirement on
holders near their limit, again the correct direction.  The structural
risk is D8: a participant whose "insured asset" is fictitious issues
free BUCK into every premium with no retirement obligation that bites
(9.1).

** The perp funding lever

A perpetual settled on a medianized bvib TWAP, with funding f = g(e)
paid continuously by the side positioned AGAINST par (longs-bvib pay
when bvib > 1; shorts-bvib pay when bvib < 1) to the house, which is the
monetary book.  Because the band bounds bvib, the perp's payoff is
bounded, and its equilibrium funding is the market's price for holding a
band-break bet -- the currency board's automatic interest-rate
mechanism applied to speculators only.  Solvency condition for listing:
for the defended excursion envelope measured in wave 2 (peak x
duration at x1..x4), the funding + eps + fee surge an attacker pays to
express the raid through the perp must exceed the payout the bounded
excursion can return; OI is capped against the defense budget.  If it
does not price safely, it is not listed.

** Implementation notes for the sim

- *UndertakingAgent* is the fastest honest stand-in for D3.  SimLP can
  mint against a zero-premium credit with an arbitrarily large face and
  hold both sides of a V3 position; the basket-rotation execution path
  already trades TOKEN <-> BUCK in every constituent pool in
  =basketAmount= proportions.  Standing orders at par +/- eps in bundle
  units are then eight range orders (one per pool) re-struck daily, or,
  more simply, a daily check that fires when bvib crosses the edge and
  trades up to C_leg per pool.  The agent must book its issued and
  absorbed BUCK so 3d can read I_d.
- *The shadow controller* needs Solidity: =BuckKControllerDirect= reads

  whatever the repo compiles.  Until the undertakings are contract-
  level, the agent's booked inventory must be pushed into the offset
  (a governance setter on the ops shell for the sim, removed later).
- *The facility* is the credit base with two changes: the issue leg
  deposits into the basket instead of selling into BUCK/USDC, and the
  retire leg redeems the receipt and repays.  Utilization telemetry is

- *The perp* is a Python-side ledger (positions, mark = medianized bvib,

* Risks, open questions, and what would change the decisions

** D8 and the facility

The facility lets latent credit issue into premiums.  A participant
whose insured asset is fictitious (D8: =createCredit= is permissionless
and zero-premium credit is exempt from the funding gate) issues free
BUCK into every premium with a retirement obligation that never bites
(no real asset, no reason to close).  This is the oversupply flood of
=REBALANCING-EQ.org= recommendation 4, automated.  The facility must be
restricted to vetted insurers' credit (the on-chain solvency-plus-rating
scheme the master doc names as the mainnet blocker) or, until then, to a
whitelisted insurer set in sim and pilot.  3h measures the flood with
and without the facility to size the exposure.

** Band-break credibility on the weak side

The weak side is NAV-bounded.  Bertola-Caballero says an imperfectly
credible edge destabilizes near it, and Krugman-Flood-Garber say the
attack is timed to the day a flat edge stops being honored.  The ladder
(D3 refinement, 7.4) is the structural mitigation: there is no day on
which the weak side stops being honored, only successively poorer
prices, so there is no discontinuity to time and no size to probe --
the RULE (the tranche schedule against the on-chain reserve) is what is
announced.  The residual risk moves to the ladder's parameters: a
delta too small reproduces the cliff, a delta too large lets a modest
outflow force a deep deviation.  Wave 3c's (delta, phi) grid, the perp
pricing band-break bets, and a reserve visibly large relative to tau_I
outflow (7.5) are the answers.

** Sensor damping by the invisible cast

Private defenders damp bvib and K cannot see their books.  Their capital
is finite and their positions temporary by design, so the lag is bounded
by their size; but a very large private cast makes K late.  The shadow
term could include an estimate of private inventory from flow
attribution; it is deliberately left out until 3d shows the desk-only
term is insufficient.

** Conversion capacity and the one-day trough

The bundle path is unlimited but the convenience path and every
constituent-pool leg are bounded by C_leg for liveness.  A one-day
squeeze larger than a day's capacity still troughs.  The directional
fee is the tick-speed answer; if 3b shows it insufficient, the remaining
lever is a larger per-leg bound with a redemption guard that tolerates
it (a TWAP-window-aware guard rather than a flat =maxLegBp=).

** Governance abandonment

The SNB failure mode is available to any governance that can move par.
Par is definitional here and the undertakings are rules, not
operations; the residual is a governance that changes eps, p, lambda or
the basket under pressure.  Time-locks on those parameters, and the
announcement discipline of Hong Kong's technical measures, are the
answer; they are policy, not code.

* Glossary

- *bvib*: basketValueInBuck, the BUCK cost of one basket bundle; the
  controller's process variable; > 1 = BUCK cheap (discount), < 1 = BUCK
  dear (premium).
- *common mode / differential mode*: BUCK against the bundle (K's job)
  vs constituent against constituent (the director's job); the mean and
  the differences of the same tick-EMA ladders.
- *the desk*: =BuckBasketOps=; Q1 absorb, Q2 retire, Q3 supply, Q4 issue.
- *the undertakings*: the strong-side (sell BUCK, unlimited) and
  weak-side (buy BUCK, NAV-bounded) standing offers at par +/- eps in
  bundle units; the currency board.
- *the weak-side ladder*: the weak side struck as tranches at
  successively deeper discounts (eps + (k-1) delta), each sized at a
  fraction phi of the reserve remaining; no cliff, superlinear attacker
  cost, and a deviation that gauges reserve stress.
- *the facility*: the credit auto-pay-down facility; latent credit
  issuing into premiums and retiring into discounts on user-set
  thresholds.
- *the shadow bvib*: bvib plus the inventory the visible fast actuators
  have absorbed minus issued, scaled; what K sees.
- *the seeder*: the treasury's temporary BUCK/USDC position from the
  current price toward the ideal.
- *the house*: the monetary book as counterparty and funding recipient
  of the bvib perp.
- *the cast*: the private excursion agents of the catalogue (usdc, buck,
  credit, basket, crb).
- *manufactured reversion*: the property that par is definitional and K
  plus the cast force bvib back to it -- what makes selling common-mode
  convexity sound.

- *the position loop*: K's second loop, on the level's aggregate
  position s (in S or V units), with its own gains (Kq, Kqi, Kqd) and
  one integrator (D7).
- *S / V*: the two aggregations of the stabilizers' books under
  judgment -- the shadow bvib in price units (D4; sum lambda_i q_i / D)
  and the cost-weighted fill in [-1, 1] (sum w_i q_i / cap_i).
- *the local skew*: kappa_i q_i / cap_i added to a facility's own band,
  so its own flow reverts its own book; the differential-mode lever
  (D7).
- *habituation*: the return of the fast actuators' inventory to zero as
  the slow lever takes over the steady-state load (Henson et al.
  1995); measured as the time for |s| to fall within 10% of zero.
- *the shadow error*: e_s = e + lambda s, the one signal K integrates
  under D9 -- the price term live while no facility is engaged, the
  position term live while one is; S0k's input as built.
- *the flow estimator*: the alpha-beta (steady-state Kalman) filter on
  the fused net BUCK flow through every peg-supporting channel, split
  into a persistent and a transient part; the sensor of D9's
  feedforward; level 2 of 6.4 made explicit.
- *the perfect-measurement K*: D9's ceiling -- the feedforward law run
  with the sim's true flows and a known g; the bound no estimator can
  beat, and the measure of whether K is the lever at all.
- *the uptake lag (tau_K)*: the time the supply takes to answer a K
  move through the borrowers' hazard; with g it sets the release
  timing (the supply still in the pipe).
- *the drift*: the total variation of pid_i - pid_qi, the two
  integrators' contents shifting between them without moving K
  (WP-16 finding 4; WAVE3.org decision 31).
