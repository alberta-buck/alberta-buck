#+begin_abstract
The Alberta Buck identity system ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-identity][Identity]], [[http://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-identity-example][Examples]]) rests on
two cryptographic transformations: the /Identity Fountain/ blinds a
Pointcheval-Sanders credential into a hiding presentation \( (A, B) \)
that no holder of the identity, the issuer included, can test,
and the =approve= handshake re-encrypts the credential's ElGamal
ciphertext under the counterparty's public key with a Chaum-Pedersen
proof of equality.  Everything downstream -- bilateral transfers, the
BUCK Notes spend circuits ([[http://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-notes][Notes]]), Identity Guardian authorizations,
regulatory recovery -- inherits its correctness from these two
primitives.

This document states and proves the correctness properties that the
rest of the series relies on:
- *Theorem 1*: rerandomization preserves PS signature validity
  (completeness; a wallet-internal step).
- *Lemma 2*: a uniform random rerandomization scalar yields a
  signature distributed as a fresh one on the same message.  It
  does not hide the message: a rerandomized pair is a candidate
  test for anyone holding \( m \), so the wallet never publishes one.
- *Theorem 1'*: the published presentation
  \( (A, B) = (a \sigma_1,\ a \sigma_2 + b Y_1) \) satisfies the masked
  verifier equation (completeness).
- *Theorem 2'*: for fresh uniform \( (a, b) \) the triple
  \( (A, B, P) \) that the registration transcript reveals is
  independent of \( m \) up to \( 1/q \) (perfect hiding).
- *Theorems R-HVZK, R-sound, R-comp*: the registration NIZK is
  honest-verifier zero-knowledge given \( P \), specially sound
  (extracting a plain PS signature), and composes the credential,
  ciphertext and key relations on one \( m \).
- *Theorem R-unforge*: registration forgery -- by a party with no
  credential, or with every registration of the target and its
  identity scalar in hand -- reduces to PS unforgeability in the
  committed-message key form, in the random-oracle model.
- *Theorem R-blind*: the registration transcript gives even the
  issuer, holding its own key and every certified identity, no
  advantage beyond DDH in telling which identity registered.
- *Theorem 3*: an honest Chaum-Pedersen prover always verifies
  (completeness).
- *Theorem 4*: from two accepting transcripts with distinct
  challenges an efficient extractor recovers the witness
  (special soundness, 2-extractability).
- *Theorem 5*: the sigma-protocol transcript is perfectly
  simulatable given only public inputs and the challenge (HVZK).
- *Theorem 6*: combined, an accepted on-chain Chaum-Pedersen proof
  implies that the re-encrypted ciphertext carries the same
  issuer-certified identity point \( M = m \cdot G \) as the
  registered credential (end-to-end re-encryption correctness).
- *Theorem 7*: at mint the issuer addresses the recipient's /receiving
  key/ \( pk_{\text{recv}} = kG \), independent of its Identity; the
  ciphertexts decrypt under \( k \) to the intended points, a mint binding
  forces them over the issuer's /own/ registered Identity (anti-framing),
  and the issuer learns neither \( k \) nor \( m_{\text{rec}} \).
- *Theorem 8* (the folded deposit gate, A1/A2): one proof over one witness
  shows the spender reads the note, that its account decrypts to the
  Identity \( m_{\text{rec}} \), and that a certified accumulator leaf
  pairs \( m_{\text{rec}} \) with the reading key -- so a thief holding a
  stolen payload cannot spend, and (A2) the decrypted issuer is registered.
- *Theorem 9* (depositor-binding soundness, B1): an accepted bearer spend
  proves the depositor's Identity \( M_{\text{dep}} \) is registered AND
  encrypted for the public issuer, who alone names it; a bogus
  \( M_{\text{dep}} \) has no certified leaf and reverts.
- *Theorem 10*: the flavor-agnostic nullifier
  \( nf = \mathrm{Poseidon}_3(\rho, idHash, \mathrm{tag}) \) deterministically
  prevents double-spend and, as a PRF over the secret opening, hides the
  \( cm \leftrightarrow nf \) link from any party without the opening
  (transaction-graph privacy against bulk harvest).
- *Theorem 11*: every accepted spend is mutually decryptable -- both
  counterparties name the other as a /registered/ Identity, /enforced/ by
  the membership half (an un-nameable counterparty is un-spendable) -- while
  no third party names either or links mint to spend.
- *Theorem 12* (the A2 key tie): the issuer Identity an accepted A2
  deposit certifies is the minter's own, not a registered sock puppet the
  same ciphertext also opens to under the recipient's key -- because the
  note commits the mint binding's blinded \( T \) and the fold opens it,
  so re-aiming the key needs a discrete logarithm to \( H_{\text{PED}} \).

Every theorem is accompanied by an executable =py_ecc= sanity check on
BN254.  The proofs assume PS unforgeability in the committed-message
key form (Pointcheval--Sanders 2016, Section 6.1), the discrete
logarithm and decisional Diffie--Hellman assumptions on
\( \text{alt\_bn128}\ G_1 \), the random oracle model (for the
Fiat-Shamir transform and Poseidon hashing), and knowledge soundness
of the underlying SNARK proof system (for Theorems 8-9 and 12), with
\( H_{\text{PED}} \) hashed to the curve so that no one knows its
logarithm.  The
presentation results (Theorems 1', 2', R-HVZK, R-sound, R-comp)
describe the A' registration implemented on branch =feature/a-prime=;
they are stated with proofs here but await independent review, and
that branch is not a production release. ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/images/alberta-buck-proofs.pdf][PDF]], [[https://perry.kundert.ca/images/alberta-buck-proofs.txt][Text]])

** Hardness Assumptions

   Five assumptions underpin the theorems in this document.  Four are
   already required by Ethereum's own security model or by any
   pairing-based / zk-SNARK construction built on the =ecPairing=
   precompile; the PS assumption is specific to the credential
   scheme and, since the issuer key now includes \( y G \), it is
   the committed-message form of that assumption.

   1. *Discrete Logarithm (DLog) on \( G_1 \)*: given \( P, Q \in G_1 \),
      finding \( \alpha \in \mathbb{Z}_q \) with \( Q = \alpha P \) is
      infeasible.  Protects ElGamal IND-CPA security, Schnorr
      unforgeability, and Chaum-Pedersen soundness, and keeps
      \( \log_G H_{\text{PED}} \) unknown, which Part IV's commitments
      rest on.

   2. *Decisional Diffie-Hellman (DDH) on \( G_1 \)*: distinguishing
      \( (G, \alpha G, \beta G, \alpha\beta G) \) from a random tuple is
      infeasible.  Protects ElGamal plaintext hiding, which is the
      only computational lock left on a registration after Theorem
      2' (the issuer-blindness theorem of the main paper and the
      long-horizon section below).

   3. *PS unforgeability, committed-message key form (PS-CM)*: the
      Pointcheval-Sanders single-message scheme is EUF-CMA when the
      public key also exposes \( Y_1 = y G \) in \( G_1 \).  Section
      6.1 of their 2016 paper[fn:ps] publishes exactly
      \( (G, Y_1;\ g_2, X, Y) \) and lets the signer sign commitments
      \( t G + m Y_1 \), which the user unblinds to
      \( (h, (x + m y) h) \); a plain-message signing oracle is the
      special case \( t = 0 \), so an adversary against our scheme is
      weaker than the Section 6.1 adversary, whose scheme is proved
      EUF-CMA under that paper's Assumption 2, an interactive
      assumption in type-3 groups.  Theorem R-unforge reduces
      registration forgery to it directly.  The issuer keeps the
      signature base \( h \) with unknown discrete logarithm, which
      =ps_sign= does by sampling it.  Nothing in this document depends
      on q-SDH.

   4. *Random Oracle Model (ROM)*: the Fiat-Shamir transform is applied
      to sigma protocols whose security is proved in the ROM, with the
      hash function \( H \) modelled as a random oracle bound to all
      public inputs and context (caller address, chain id, etc.).
      Poseidon (commitment hash, accumulator leaves and nullifier PRF)
      and the hash-to-curve that derives \( H_{\text{PED}} \) are also
      modelled as random oracles for the BUCK Notes proofs in Part IV.
      Each transcript's domain word keeps the oracles of different
      protocols apart.

   5. *Knowledge soundness of the underlying zk-SNARK proof system*
      (Part IV only): the SNARK system used for BUCK Notes spend
      circuits (Groth16, PLONK, or any knowledge-sound construction
      over BN254) admits a polynomial-time witness extractor on
      every accepting proof.  This is a system-specific assumption
      depending on the chosen scheme's trusted setup or universal
      setup.

   In Part I, Theorems 1 and 1' follow from bilinearity alone, Lemma 2
   and Theorem 2' are information-theoretic, and Theorem R-HVZK is a
   perfect simulation (the NIZK invokes the ROM); Theorem R-sound's
   extractor is unconditional; Theorem R-unforge is computational
   (PS-CM, ROM) and Theorem R-blind is computational (DDH, ROM).  No
   assumption is made about the issuer beyond honest issuance: it
   holds \( (x, y) \) and can sign at will, so unforgeability is
   stated against every party except the issuer, and issuer-blindness
   against the issuer.  The Chaum-Pedersen
   proofs (Part II) are interactive-sigma-protocol facts; the NIZK
   corollary (Part II, end) invokes the ROM.  Part IV (Notes spend
   predicates) additionally invokes assumption (5) --
   knowledge soundness of the underlying SNARK system -- to lift the
   sigma-protocol guarantees of Theorems 4-5 into the SNARK setting.

* Part I: Pointcheval-Sanders Credentials and the Hiding Presentation

** Status and Scope of This Part

   The registration presentation proved here (the "A'" design) is
   implemented on branch =feature/a-prime= across the Python executable
   specification (=alberta_buck/wallet/ps.py=, =nizk.py=), the Solidity
   verifier (=src/IdentityRegistry.sol=: =trustIssuer=,
   =_verifyCredential=, =_fsRegister=, =_checkPresentation=), the Rust
   kernel (=core/rust/buck-identity/src/{ps,nizk}.rs=) with its PyO3 and
   wasm bindings, and the JavaScript wrapper
   (=core/js/src/identity-core.js=), all checked against one set of
   vectors.  This part is the security argument for it, written in full
   so that an independent reviewer can confirm or refute each step; the
   review itself has not happened, and until it has every result below is
   a claim of that branch, not a property of the system.  Section
   [[#review-status]] lists what the reviewer is asked to check and what
   would strengthen the argument further.

   What is claimed.  A holder of a PS credential on an identity scalar
   \( m \) registers an account by publishing a blinded pair \( (A, B) \)
   and a Schnorr-family proof.  The pair and the proof together reveal
   nothing about \( m \) beyond what the account's ElGamal ciphertext
   reveals: unconditionally on the credential side (Theorems 2', R-HVZK)
   and under DDH on the ciphertext side (Theorem R-blind).  An accepting
   registration implies possession of a credential on the encrypted
   \( m \), and neither a party who has seen any number of the holder's
   registrations nor one who has learned \( m \) can produce one:
   registration forgery reduces to PS unforgeability in the
   committed-message key form, in the random-oracle model, with the usual
   forking loss (Theorems R-sound, R-unforge).  Fresh randomness per
   registration is security critical (Proposition R-fresh).

   What is not covered here: the identity-derived A1/A2 Note receiving
   keys and the issuance, policy and accumulator metadata of the review's
   findings R5 and R6; duplicate issuance by a corrupt issuer and the
   optional holder secret; wallet-side leakage (RNG quality, side
   channels, backup formats that might export \( \sigma \), any of which
   reintroduces credential harvesting); and post-quantum security, for
   which see the long-horizon section.

** The Construction

   A Pointcheval-Sanders signature on \( m \in \mathbb{Z}_q \) is a pair
   \( \sigma = (\sigma_1, \sigma_2) \in G_1^2 \) with \( \sigma_1 \neq O \)
   satisfying the pairing equation

   \[
   e(\sigma_1,\; X + m \cdot Y) \;=\; e(\sigma_2,\; g_2).
   \]

   *Keys and issuance.*  The issuer holds \( (x, y) \) and publishes

   \[
   X = x \cdot g_2, \qquad Y = y \cdot g_2, \qquad Y_1 = y \cdot G .
   \]

   The registry accepts a key only if \( Y_1 \neq O \) and
   \( e(Y_1, g_2) = e(G, Y) \) (=trustIssuer=).  Issuance is the plain
   scheme's: on \( m \) the issuer samples \( h = t \cdot G \) for uniform
   nonzero \( t \) it does not disclose and returns
   \( \sigma = (h,\ (x + m y) h) \).  The credential never leaves the
   wallet.  /Rerandomization/,
   \( \sigma' = (t \cdot \sigma_1,\; t \cdot \sigma_2) \) for
   \( t \in \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \), is a wallet-internal step.

   *Presentation.*  For each account the wallet samples independent
   uniform nonzero \( a, b, sk, r \) and publishes

   \[
   A = a \cdot \sigma_1, \qquad
   B = a \cdot \sigma_2 + b \cdot Y_1 = (x + m y) \cdot A + b \cdot Y_1, \qquad
   pk = sk \cdot G, \qquad
   E = (R, C) = (r G,\ m G + r\,pk),
   \]

   and discards \( a, b \) after proving.  Write \( P = m A + b G \); then
   \( B = x A + y P \).  \( P \) is the one credential-side value a
   verifier can derive from the transcript, and the results below are
   stated in terms of it.

   *Registration proof.*  Witness \( (m, b, r, sk) \); nonces
   \( (m_t, b_t, r_t, sk_t) \) uniform in \( \mathbb{Z}_q \); context
   \( \mathrm{ctx} = (\text{registrant}, \text{chainid}, \text{registry}, \mathrm{DOMAIN}) \)
   with \( \mathrm{DOMAIN} = \mathrm{keccak}(\texttt{AlbertaBuck/FiatShamir/IdentityRegistry/Register/v2}) \):

   \begin{align*}
   C_1 &= m_t A + b_t G,  & T_C &= m_t G + r_t\,pk, & T_R &= r_t G, & T_{key} &= sk_t\,G, \\
   e &= H(A, B, R, C, pk, C_1, T_C, T_R, T_{key}, \mathrm{ctx}) \bmod q, \\
   s_m &= m_t + e m, & s_b &= b_t + e b, & s_r &= r_t + e r, & s_{sk} &= sk_t + e\,sk.
   \end{align*}

   Published: \( (A, B),\ pk,\ E,\ (e, s_m, s_b, s_r, s_{sk}, C_1, T_C, T_R, T_{key}) \),
   plus the issuer's address, which selects the key.  Credential-based
   contract binding uses the identical proof with the target contract as
   registrant.

   *Verifier.*

   \begin{align*}
   \text{(g)}\quad  & A, B, pk, R \neq O;\ \text{all points on the curve};\
                      e, s_m, s_b, s_r, s_{sk} < q;\ \text{registrant unregistered};\ \text{issuer trusted} \\
   \text{(fs)}\quad & e = H(A, B, R, C, pk, C_1, T_C, T_R, T_{key}, \mathrm{ctx}) \bmod q \\
   \text{(b)}\quad  & s_m G + s_r\,pk = e C + T_C \\
   \text{(c)}\quad  & s_r G = e R + T_R \\
   \text{(k)}\quad  & s_{sk} G = T_{key} + e\,pk \\
   \text{(a')}\quad & e(s_m A + s_b G - C_1,\; Y)\cdot e(e A,\; X)\cdot e(-e B,\; g_2) = 1
   \end{align*}

   One commitment \( C_1 \) carries both credential exponents; the nonce
   \( m_t \) is shared between \( C_1 \) and \( T_C \), and \( r_t \)
   between \( T_C \) and \( T_R \).

** Security Definitions

   Three games.  Advantage is \( |\Pr[\text{guess} = \beta] - 1/2| \)
   for the distinguishing games and the success probability for the
   forgery game.

   *Presentation hiding (HIDE).*  The adversary chooses the issuer key
   \( (x, y) \) -- it may /be/ the issuer -- two scalars \( m_0, m_1 \)
   and valid credentials \( \sigma^0, \sigma^1 \) on them.  The challenger
   samples uniform nonzero \( a, b \) and a bit \( \beta \) and returns
   \( (A, B) = (a \sigma^\beta_1,\ a \sigma^\beta_2 + b Y_1) \) together
   with \( P = m_\beta A + b G \).  The adversary guesses \( \beta \).
   Returning \( P \) makes the game at least as strong as anything the
   real transcript reveals (Theorem R-HVZK's remark).

   *Registration unforgeability (RU).*  The challenger samples
   \( (x, y) \) and publishes \( (X, Y, Y_1) \).  The adversary has a
   random oracle and two oracles: \( \mathsf{Sign}(m) \) returns a
   credential on \( m \) of its choice, and the set of queried messages
   is \( Q \); \( \mathsf{Present}(m, \mathrm{ctx}) \), for /any/
   \( m \) including ones not in \( Q \), returns a complete honest
   registration transcript \( (pk, E, A, B, \pi) \) of \( m \) with
   fresh \( (a, b, sk, r) \) and the given context, and the adversary is
   also given \( m \) itself.  This models a party who has seen the
   target's registrations and has learned the target's identity scalar,
   as every counterparty does.  The adversary wins if it outputs an
   accepting transcript whose extracted witness (Theorem R-sound) has
   \( m^\ast \notin Q \); \( m^\ast \) may well be a message it obtained
   presentations of, since only credentials count as queries.  One game
   therefore covers both "no credential on the encrypted identity"
   (soundness) and the review's R3 (harvesting).

   *Issuer-blindness (IB).*  The adversary is the issuer: it holds
   \( (x, y) \) and chooses two certified identities \( m_0, m_1 \) with
   their credentials.  The challenger registers one at random with fresh
   \( (a, b, sk, r) \) and honest randomness and returns the full public
   transcript \( (pk, E, A, B, \pi) \) with a context of the adversary's
   choice.  Many registrations are handled by a hybrid over them.

** Theorem 1 (Completeness of Rerandomization)

   *Statement.*  For every valid PS signature \( \sigma \) on \( m \)
   under issuer key \( (X, Y) \), and for every \( t \in \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \),
   the pair \( \sigma' = (t \sigma_1, t \sigma_2) \) is also a valid PS
   signature on \( m \) under the same issuer key.

   *Proof.*  By bilinearity of \( e \):

   \begin{align*}
   e(\sigma'_1,\; X + m Y)
     &= e(t \sigma_1,\; X + m Y)                   & \text{(definition)} \\
     &= e(\sigma_1,\; X + m Y)^t                   & \text{(bilinearity)} \\
     &= e(\sigma_2,\; g_2)^t                       & \text{(original validity)} \\
     &= e(t \sigma_2,\; g_2)                       & \text{(bilinearity)} \\
     &= e(\sigma'_2,\; g_2).
   \end{align*}

   Non-degeneracy: \( G_1 \) has prime order \( q \), so \( t \sigma_1 = O \)
   iff \( t \equiv 0 \pmod q \) or \( \sigma_1 = O \).  Both are excluded
   by hypothesis.  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Scope.*  The wallet uses this step to re-issue the credential to
   itself; nothing produced by it is published.  The published object
   is the presentation, whose completeness is Theorem 1'.

** Theorem 1' (Completeness of the Masked Verifier)

   *Statement.*  For every valid credential, every
   \( a, b \in \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \) and every \( sk, r \), the honest
   transcript is accepted; in particular the presentation satisfies

   \[
   e(B,\; g_2) \;=\; e(A,\; X) \cdot e(m A + b G,\; Y),
   \]

   equivalently \( B = x A + y P \).

   *Proof.*  Split \( B \):

   \begin{align*}
   e(a \sigma_2,\; g_2)
     &= e(\sigma_2,\; g_2)^a
      = e(\sigma_1,\; X + m Y)^a
      = e(A,\; X + m Y)
      = e(A,\; X) \cdot e(m A,\; Y),                  \\
   e(b Y_1,\; g_2)
     &= e(b y G,\; g_2)
      = e(b G,\; g_2)^{y}
      = e(b G,\; Y).
   \end{align*}

   Multiplying, \( e(B, g_2) = e(A, X) \cdot e(m A + b G, Y) \).  Then
   \( s_m A + s_b G - C_1 = e (m A + b G) = e P \), so (a') is
   \( [e(P, Y)\, e(A, X) / e(B, g_2)]^{e} = 1 \); (b), (c), (k) are the
   Schnorr identities; and \( A \neq O \) since \( \sigma_1 \neq O \) and
   \( a \neq 0 \).  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Implementation guard.*  With \( A = O \) the masked equation reads
   \( e(B, g_2) = e(b G, Y) \), which \( B = b Y_1 \) satisfies /for
   every/ \( m \): the credential term vanishes and the relation says
   nothing about the identity.  The registration contract MUST reject
   \( A = O \) (and \( B = O \)).  This is the same degenerate case that
   the earlier published form met as \( \sigma'_1 = O \) (from
   \( t = 0 \)); under the presentation it is \( a = 0 \).  Theorem 1'
   depends on \( a \neq 0 \); the contract's guard is the runtime witness
   of that hypothesis, and it is security critical rather than hygienic.

** Lemma 2 (Fresh-Signature Distribution for a Fixed Message)

   *Statement.*  Let \( \sigma \) be any fixed valid PS signature on \( m \).
   If \( t \) is sampled uniformly from \( \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \), then the
   distribution of \( \sigma' = (t \sigma_1, t \sigma_2) \) is identical to
   the distribution of \( \text{PS.Sign}(sk_{\text{issuer}}, m) \) for
   \( h \) sampled uniformly from \( G_1 \setminus \{O\} \), and is
   /independent/ of \( \sigma \).

   *Proof.*  Since \( G_1 \) is cyclic of prime order \( q \) and
   \( \sigma_1 \neq O \), the map
   \( \varphi_\sigma : \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \to G_1 \setminus \{O\},\ t \mapsto t \sigma_1 \)
   is a bijection.  Hence \( \sigma'_1 \) is uniform on
   \( G_1 \setminus \{O\} \) regardless of \( \sigma_1 \).  Given
   \( \sigma'_1 = h' \), the second component is forced:
   \( \sigma'_2 = (x + m y) h' \), exactly what a fresh
   \( \text{PS.Sign} \) with random \( h' \) would output.
   \( \blacksquare \)

   *What this lemma does not say.*  The rerandomized pair is a valid
   signature on \( m \): any party holding a candidate \( m_i \) decides
   whether \( e(\sigma'_1, X + m_i Y) = e(\sigma'_2, g_2) \).  The issuer
   holds every \( m \) it has certified, and every counterparty learns
   \( m \) by design, so a /published/ rerandomized pair is a candidate
   test on the identity, not an unlinkable credential (review finding
   R1 in =doc/review/identity-findings-2.org=).  The lemma is a
   statement about the wallet's internal step; the privacy property of
   the published object is Theorem 2'.  Earlier versions of this
   document called this result "statistical unlinkability" and drew the
   issuer-blindness of the identity layer from it; that reading is
   withdrawn.

** Theorem 2' (Perfect Hiding of the Presentation)

   *Statement.*  Every adversary in HIDE, computationally unbounded and
   holding the issuer key, has advantage at most \( 1/q \).  More
   precisely: for a fixed credential on \( m \) and independent uniform
   \( a, b \in \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \), the distribution of \( (A, B, P) \) is
   within statistical distance \( 1/q \) of
   \( (U_1,\ x U_1 + y U_2,\ U_2) \), where \( U_1 \) is uniform on
   \( G_1 \setminus \{O\} \), \( U_2 \) is uniform on \( G_1 \), and the
   two are independent; this reference distribution does not depend on
   \( m \).

   *Proof.*  Fix \( \beta \).  \( A = a \sigma^\beta_1 \) is uniform on
   \( G_1 \setminus \{O\} \) by the bijection of Lemma 2.  Conditioned
   on \( A \), the map \( b \mapsto m_\beta A + b G \) is a bijection
   \( \mathbb{Z}_q \to G_1 \), so for \( b \) uniform on
   \( \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \) the point \( P \) is uniform on
   \( G_1 \setminus \{m_\beta A\} \), independent of \( A \).  By
   Theorem 1', \( B = x A + y P \) is a function of \( (A, P) \).  The
   distributions for \( \beta = 0 \) and \( \beta = 1 \) therefore differ
   only in which single point \( P \) avoids, at statistical distance at
   most \( 1/q \), and nothing else the challenger outputs depends on
   \( \beta \).  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Remarks.*  (i) The adversary knows \( x, y \): the result is
   unconditional and holds against the issuer.  (ii) Allowing
   \( b = 0 \) would make the distribution exactly independent of
   \( m \), but \( b = 0 \) publishes a rerandomized signature, which
   Lemma 2's remark shows is testable; excluding it costs \( 1/q \).
   (iii) For every candidate \( m_i \) the point \( P - m_i A \) is a
   well-formed \( b_i G \), so no candidate is distinguished by any
   relation among public values.  (iv) Whatever the registration
   transcript reveals about \( m \) beyond \( (A, B, P) \) is what the
   ElGamal ciphertext \( (pk, R, C) \) reveals (Theorem R-HVZK), which is
   the plaintext-checking problem under DDH (Theorem R-blind).

** Theorem R-HVZK (Zero-Knowledge of the Registration Transcript)

   *Statement.*  Given \( (A, B, P, pk, R, C, \mathrm{ctx}) \) and a
   challenge \( e \), the simulator draws \( s_m, s_b, s_r, s_{sk} \)
   uniform on \( \mathbb{Z}_q \) and sets

   \[
   C_1 = s_m A + s_b G - e P, \quad
   T_C = s_m G + s_r\,pk - e C, \quad
   T_R = s_r G - e R, \quad
   T_{key} = s_{sk} G - e\,pk.
   \]

   The simulated transcript is distributed identically to an honest
   transcript with the same challenge.  In the random-oracle model the
   NIZK is zero-knowledge: the simulator programs \( H \) at the
   transcript to \( e \), aborting if that input was queried before,
   which happens with probability at most \( q_H / q \) over the
   adversary's \( q_H \) oracle queries because \( C_1 \) is uniform and
   fresh.

   *Proof.*  In an honest transcript with fixed witness and challenge,
   each response is a uniform nonce plus a constant, so the four
   responses are uniform and independent, and the four commitments are
   the unique points satisfying the verification equations for those
   responses.  The simulator samples responses from the same
   distribution and solves the same equations.  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Remark (what the simulator needs, and what the old proof leaked).*
   The simulator needs \( P \), which is public:
   \( P = e^{-1}(s_m A + s_b G - C_1) \).  The transcript is therefore a
   deterministic function of \( (A, B, P, pk, E, \mathrm{ctx}) \) and
   simulator randomness, and Theorem 2' says \( (A, B, P) \) is
   independent of \( m \).  The pre-A' proof published a separate
   commitment \( A_{ps} = m_t A \) with the response \( s_m \), from which
   any observer computes \( m A = e^{-1}(s_m A - A_{ps}) \), a candidate
   test on \( m \) needing neither the second signature point nor any
   issuer key (review finding R2).  Folding \( b_t G \) into the same
   commitment is what removes the test.

** Theorem R-sound (Special Soundness)

   *Statement.*  From two accepting transcripts with the same
   \( (A, B, pk, R, C, C_1, T_C, T_R, T_{key}, \mathrm{ctx}) \) and
   challenges \( e \neq e' \), one computes

   \[
   m^\ast = \frac{s_m - s'_m}{e - e'}, \quad
   b^\ast = \frac{s_b - s'_b}{e - e'}, \quad
   r^\ast = \frac{s_r - s'_r}{e - e'}, \quad
   sk^\ast = \frac{s_{sk} - s'_{sk}}{e - e'}
   \]

   such that \( pk = sk^\ast G \), \( (R, C) = (r^\ast G,\ m^\ast G + r^\ast pk) \),
   and \( e(A,\; X + m^\ast Y) = e(B - b^\ast Y_1,\; g_2) \).  Hence
   \( (A,\ B - b^\ast Y_1) \) is a plain PS signature on the \( m^\ast \)
   encrypted in \( E \), under a key whose secret is \( sk^\ast \).

   *Proof.*  Dividing the two instances of (a') and raising to the power
   \( (e - e')^{-1} \):
   \( e(m^\ast A + b^\ast G, Y)\, e(A, X) = e(B, g_2) \), i.e.
   \( e(A, X + m^\ast Y)\, e(b^\ast G, Y) = e(B, g_2) \).  Since
   \( Y_1 = y G \) (enforced at =trustIssuer=),
   \( e(b^\ast G, Y) = e(b^\ast Y_1, g_2) \), which rearranges to the
   stated equation; \( A \neq O \) by (g).  Relations (b), (c), (k)
   extract \( r^\ast, sk^\ast \) and the same \( m^\ast \) exactly as in
   Theorem 4 of Part II.  \( \blacksquare \)

   This is the only place \( Y_1 \) enters the argument; the verifier
   never multiplies by it.

** Theorem R-comp (Composition of the Credential, Ciphertext and Key Relations)

   *Statement.*  Relations (a'), (b), (c), (k) under one challenge form
   a sigma protocol for the conjunction

   \[
   \exists\, (m, b, r, sk):\quad
   B = xA + y(mA + bG),\quad
   (R, C) = (rG,\ mG + r\,pk),\quad
   pk = sk\,G,
   \]

   with special soundness and honest-verifier zero-knowledge.  In
   particular an accepting registration proves that the /same/ \( m \)
   is certified by the presentation and encrypted in \( E \), and that
   the registrant holds \( sk \) for \( pk \).

   *Proof.*  AND-composition of sigma protocols under a common
   challenge preserves both properties.  The shared nonce \( m_t \)
   makes the \( m \) extracted from (a') and from (b) the same scalar
   \( (s_m - s'_m)/(e - e') \); the shared \( r_t \) does the same for
   \( r \) between (b) and (c).  The simulator of Theorem R-HVZK already
   simulates all four commitments from the same responses.
   \( \blacksquare \)

** Theorem R-unforge (Registration Unforgeability, from PS-CM)

   *Statement.*  Let \( \mathcal{A} \) be an adversary in RU making
   \( q_H \) random-oracle queries and \( q_P \) presentation queries,
   whose accepting output has extracted \( m^\ast \notin Q \) with
   probability \( \varepsilon \).  There is a PS-CM forger
   \( \mathcal{B} \) with success probability at least
   \( \varepsilon^2 / q_H - (q_P + q_H + 1)/q \), up to the forking
   lemma's constants.

   *Proof.*  \( \mathcal{B} \) receives the PS-CM public key
   \( (X, Y, Y_1) \) and a signing oracle \( \mathcal{S} \), and forwards
   the key to \( \mathcal{A} \).

   /Sign queries./  Forwarded to \( \mathcal{S} \); \( \mathcal{B} \)
   records \( Q \).

   /Presentation queries./  \( \mathcal{B} \) must answer
   \( \mathsf{Present}(m, \mathrm{ctx}) \) for identities it holds no
   credential on.  It queries \( \mathcal{S} \) once, on a message
   \( m_d \) chosen uniformly at random, obtaining
   \( \sigma_d = (h_d,\ (x + m_d y) h_d) \).  For each query it samples
   fresh \( a, b, sk, r \) and sets

   \[
   A = a h_d, \quad
   B = a \sigma_{d,2} + b Y_1 = x A + y (m_d A + b G), \quad
   P := m_d A + b G, \quad
   pk = sk G, \quad
   E = (r G,\ m G + r\,pk),
   \]

   using the real \( m \), which the adversary holds anyway, and
   simulates \( \pi \) with the simulator of Theorem R-HVZK, programming
   \( H \) at the transcript.

   /Forgery./  When \( \mathcal{A} \) outputs an accepting transcript
   whose challenge was the \( i \)-th oracle query, \( \mathcal{B} \)
   rewinds \( \mathcal{A} \) to that query with a fresh answer (the
   forking lemma[fn:forklemma]); with probability about
   \( \varepsilon^2 / q_H \) it obtains a second accepting transcript
   with the same commitments and a different challenge, extracts
   \( (m^\ast, b^\ast) \) by Theorem R-sound, and outputs
   \( (m^\ast,\ (A^\ast,\ B^\ast - b^\ast Y_1)) \).

   /Indistinguishability of the simulation./  In a real
   \( \mathsf{Present} \) answer, \( A \) is uniform nonzero, \( P \) is
   uniform on \( G_1 \setminus \{m A\} \), and \( B = x A + y P \).  In
   the simulation \( A \) is again uniform nonzero (a rerandomization of
   \( h_d \)), \( P \) is uniform on \( G_1 \setminus \{m_d A\} \), and
   \( B = x A + y P \).  The two differ by at most \( 1/q \) per query
   (Theorem 2').  The ciphertext is formed identically.  The proof is
   simulated, which by Theorem R-HVZK is exact given
   \( (A, B, P, pk, E, \mathrm{ctx}) \) up to the programming abort.  The
   relation "\( P = m A + b G \) for the \( m \) inside \( E \)" is not
   checkable by anyone without \( b \), since every \( m \) admits some
   \( b \).  So nothing in \( \mathcal{A} \)'s view distinguishes the
   simulated world from the real one beyond \( q_P/q + q_H/q \).

   /Validity of the forgery./  By Theorem R-sound the pair verifies as a
   PS signature on \( m^\ast \).  \( \mathcal{A} \) wins only if
   \( m^\ast \notin Q \).  The one message \( \mathcal{B} \) queried that
   \( \mathcal{A} \) does not see is \( m_d \); since
   \( \mathcal{A} \)'s view is independent of \( m_d \) up to the
   statistical distance above, \( \Pr[m^\ast = m_d] \le (1 + q_P)/q \).
   Hence the output is a forgery on a message never queried to
   \( \mathcal{S} \).  \( \blacksquare \)

   *What this closes.*  A party that has learned \( m \) from a receipt
   and holds every registration of the target (review finding R3); a
   party that copies a registration to another address or chain
   (\( \mathrm{ctx} \) is in the transcript, and a fresh proof needs
   \( sk \) and \( b \) anyway); and a party with no credential at all.
   Identity disclosure therefore stops conferring registration authority
   under an honest issuer.  The observation that stripping \( B \) needs
   \( b Y_1 = y (b G) \) from \( b G \) and \( Y_1 \), a CDH instance,
   remains true but is not load-bearing.

   *What it does not close.*  The issuer, who can sign.  Two credentials
   on the same identity issued to different people are a
   certification-policy problem, not a cryptographic one.

** Theorem R-blind (Issuer-Blindness of the Registration Transcript)

   *Statement.*  Every PPT adversary in IB has advantage at most
   \( \mathrm{Adv}_{\mathrm{DDH}} + 1/q + q_H/q \) per registration.
   This is the credential lock the main paper's issuer-blindness theorem
   needs; its plaintext lock is the DDH term.

   *Proof (hybrids).*  \( H_0 \): the real game with bit \( \beta \).
   \( H_1 \): replace \( \pi \) by the simulated proof from
   \( (A, B, P, pk, E, \mathrm{ctx}) \); identical up to the programming
   abort (Theorem R-HVZK).  \( H_2 \): replace \( (A, B, P) \) by
   \( (U_1,\ x U_1 + y U_2,\ U_2) \) with \( U_1 \) uniform nonzero and
   \( U_2 \) uniform, at statistical distance at most \( 1/q \)
   (Theorem 2'), computable by the challenger since the adversary
   supplied \( (x, y) \).  In \( H_2 \) the only object that depends on
   \( \beta \) is \( E = (r G,\ m_\beta G + r\,pk) \) with
   \( pk = sk\,G \) for secret \( sk, r \): the ElGamal encryption of a
   known plaintext under a fresh key, whose indistinguishability is the
   plaintext-checking problem, i.e. deciding whether
   \( (G, pk, R, C - m_\beta G) \) is a DDH tuple.  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Scope.*  The theorem is about the registration transcript.  It says
   nothing about the issuer's address (which identifies the anonymity
   set), about timing or gas, or about identity-derived Note keys.

** Proposition R-fresh (Randomness Reuse Is a Break)

   Each of the following is an explicit break if the wallet reuses a
   value:

   - \( b \) reused across two registrations of one identity:
     \( P_1 - P_2 = m (A_1 - A_2) \), so a holder of a candidate
     \( m_i \) tests \( P_1 - P_2 - m_i (A_1 - A_2) = O \).  Links the
     accounts and identifies the identity (sanity check, step 8).
   - \( a \) reused with the same credential: \( A_1 = A_2 \); links the
     accounts.
   - \( m_t \) reused across two proofs: \( s_m - s'_m = (e - e') m \)
     reveals \( m \) to everyone.
   - \( b_t \) reused: reveals \( b \), after which \( B - b Y_1 \) is a
     verifiable signature on \( m \), i.e. the credential is published.
   - \( r_t \) or \( sk_t \) reused: reveal \( r \) (hence \( M \) from
     \( C - r\,pk \)) and \( sk \).

   The wallet draws all six from the platform CSPRNG (=rand_scalar=).
   Deriving the four nonces deterministically from the witness and the
   transcript, in the style of RFC 6979, would remove the RNG from the
   trusted base for \( m_t, b_t, r_t, sk_t \); \( a \) and \( b \) must
   still be fresh per registration.

** What the Verifier Checks, and Why

   Each item is present in =src/IdentityRegistry.sol= with the revert
   string given.

   - \( A \neq O \) (=A=O=; critical).  With \( A = O \) the term
     \( e(eA, X) \) vanishes and (a') reduces to
     \( e(s_b G - C_1, Y) = e(eB, g_2) \), which \( B = b Y_1 \),
     \( C_1 = b_t G \) satisfy for every \( m \): a registration with no
     credential.  Reachable by choosing \( a = 0 \).
   - \( B \neq O \) (=B=O=; defensive).  \( B = O \) means
     \( x A = -y P \); producing such a pair needs \( x/y \).  Rejected
     so that no degenerate encoding is accepted.
   - \( pk \neq O \) (=pk=O=; critical).  Relation (k) is vacuous at
     \( pk = O \): no account-key ownership.
   - \( R \neq O \) (=R=O=; critical).  \( r = 0 \) makes \( C = m G \),
     publishing the identity point.
   - Canonical scalars (=bad scalar=).  Two encodings of one proof would
     otherwise be accepted; hygiene, since \( \mathrm{ctx} \) already
     prevents replay.
   - On-curve and subgroup.  \( G_1 \) has prime order on BN254, so
     on-curve suffices for \( G_1 \) inputs; the pairing precompile
     rejects \( G_2 \) points outside the subgroup, and \( X, Y \) are
     governance-set.
   - \( Y_1 \) consistency at =trustIssuer= (=Y1=O=, =Y1 inconsistent=).
     Theorem R-sound's last step needs \( Y_1 = y G \).  A wrong
     \( Y_1 \) cannot help a forger, because the verifier never
     multiplies by \( Y_1 \); it would only make honest presentations
     fail, which the check prevents.
   - Fiat-Shamir coverage (=bad FS challenge=).  The transcript hashes
     every public point of the statement, all four commitments, the
     registrant, the chain id, the registry address and the versioned
     domain.  The issuer address is /not/ hashed: the proof is bound to
     the issuer's key through equation (a') itself, so it cannot verify
     under a different key, and two issuers publishing the same key are
     indistinguishable by construction.
   - Registrant \( = \) =msg.sender= (or the target contract for
     binding).  The proof is useless to any other address.
   - Nonzero caller-supplied accumulator leaves are rejected
     (=unchecked identity leaf=); see the accumulator note in Part IV.
   - Storage, not calldata, for later use of \( E \) and \( pk \).
     Downstream proofs (approve, spend) read the registered values.

** Sanity Check (py_ecc on BN254)

   The block keeps \( m, x, y, X, Y, G_1, G_2 \) and =rand= defined for
   the later blocks.  It samples \( h \) as a known multiple of \( G \)
   so that step 3 can exhibit the second opening of Theorem 2'; a real
   issuer never reveals that logarithm.  Step 7 uses SHA-256 as a
   stand-in random oracle; the point is only that the simulated
   challenge is not the hash of the simulated commitments.

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   #+BEGIN_SRC python :session proofs :results output
     import secrets, hashlib
     from py_ecc.bn128 import bn128_curve as bc
     from py_ecc.bn128 import pairing, FQ12

     ORDER = bc.curve_order
     G1, G2 = bc.G1, bc.G2
     multiply, add, neg, eq = bc.multiply, bc.add, bc.neg, bc.eq

     def rand():
         return secrets.randbelow(ORDER - 1) + 1

     # Issuer key pair: (X, Y) in G2 as before, plus the G1 image Y1 = y G
     x, y = rand(), rand()
     X, Y = multiply(G2, x), multiply(G2, y)
     Y1 = multiply(G1, y)

     # Alice's identity and PS credential sigma = (h, (x + m y) h).  This check
     # knows log_G h (it samples h as a multiple of G) and uses it only for the
     # perfect-hiding witness in step 3; a real issuer keeps that log secret.
     m = int(hashlib.sha256(b"Alice Johnson | Alberta | AIC-2026").hexdigest(), 16) % ORDER
     log_h = rand()
     h = multiply(G1, log_h)
     sigma = (h, multiply(h, (x + m * y) % ORDER))

     def present(sigma, a, b):                 # (A, B) = (a sigma_1, a sigma_2 + b Y1)
         return (multiply(sigma[0], a), add(multiply(sigma[1], a), multiply(Y1, b)))

     def masked_verify(A, B, m, b):            # Theorem 1': e(B, g2) == e(A, X) e(mA + bG, Y)
         P = add(multiply(A, m), multiply(G1, b))
         return pairing(G2, B) == pairing(X, A) * pairing(Y, P)

     def raw_verify(S1, S2, m):                # the pre-A' candidate test: e(S1, X + mY) == e(S2, g2)
         return pairing(add(X, multiply(Y, m)), S1) == pairing(G2, S2)

     # --- 1. Theorem 1': the honest presentation satisfies the masked equation ---
     a, b = rand(), rand()
     A, B = present(sigma, a, b)
     assert not eq(A, bc.Z1) and not eq(B, bc.Z1)
     assert masked_verify(A, B, m, b), "Theorem 1' FAILED"
     print("Theorem 1' (masked completeness):    PASS")

     # --- 2. Lemma 2 and R1: the raw test identifies t*sigma but not (A, B) ---
     t = rand()
     sigma_p = (multiply(sigma[0], t), multiply(sigma[1], t))
     assert raw_verify(sigma_p[0], sigma_p[1], m), "Lemma 2: rerandomized pair must verify"
     assert not raw_verify(A, B, m), "R1: the presentation must NOT verify as a signature on m"
     print("Lemma 2 + R1 (raw candidate test):   PASS  (t*sigma identified; (A, B) not)")

     # --- 3. Theorem 2': another identity m' opens the SAME (A, B, P) ---
     m_prime = rand()
     log_A = (a * log_h) % ORDER
     b_prime = (b + (m - m_prime) * log_A) % ORDER
     P = add(multiply(A, m), multiply(G1, b))
     assert eq(P, add(multiply(A, m_prime), multiply(G1, b_prime))), "Theorem 2': P differs"
     assert masked_verify(A, B, m_prime, b_prime), "Theorem 2': (A, B) must verify for m' too"
     print("Theorem 2' (perfect hiding):         PASS  (same (A, B, P) opens to m and m')")

     # --- 4. Theorem R-sound: two transcripts sharing C1 extract (m*, b*) ---
     m_t, b_t = rand(), rand()
     C1 = add(multiply(A, m_t), multiply(G1, b_t))
     e1, e2 = rand(), rand()

* Part II: Chaum-Pedersen Re-Encryption Correctness

** Setup

   Alice holds a credential whose ElGamal ciphertext is registered on
   chain and was verified at registration time against her PS signature:

   \[
   E_{\text{alice}} = (R_a, C_a) = (r_a \cdot G,\; M + r_a \cdot pk_{\text{alice}}),
   \qquad pk_{\text{alice}} = sk_{\text{alice}} \cdot G.
   \]

   To identify herself to Bob she re-encrypts under \( pk_{\text{bob}} \)
   with fresh randomness \( r_b \):

   \[
   E_{\text{bob}} = (R_b, C_b) = (r_b \cdot G,\; M + r_b \cdot pk_{\text{bob}}).
   \]

   She then produces a non-interactive Chaum-Pedersen proof that
   \( E_{\text{alice}} \) and \( E_{\text{bob}} \) encrypt the /same/
   message point \( M \) -- without revealing \( M \), \( m \),
   \( sk_{\text{alice}} \), or \( r_b \).  The BUCK contract reads
   \( E_{\text{alice}} \) from storage (not calldata) and verifies the
   proof.

** The Statement

   Alice proves knowledge of \( (sk_{\text{alice}}, r_b) \) satisfying the
   three simultaneous relations

   \begin{align}
   pk_{\text{alice}} &= sk_{\text{alice}} \cdot G \tag{S1} \\
   R_b               &= r_b \cdot G \tag{S2} \\
   C_a - sk_{\text{alice}} \cdot R_a &= C_b - r_b \cdot pk_{\text{bob}}. \tag{S3}
   \end{align}

   (S1) binds her to the registered public key; (S2) binds \( r_b \)
   to the ciphertext she submits; (S3) states that the ElGamal-decrypt
   of \( E_{\text{alice}} \) equals the ElGamal-decrypt of
   \( E_{\text{bob}} \) under their respective secret keys.

** Protocol (Fiat-Shamir)

   *Commit.*  Prover samples \( k_1, k_2 \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q \) and computes

   \[
   T_1 = k_1 \cdot G, \qquad T_2 = k_2 \cdot G, \qquad T_3 = k_2 \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} - k_1 \cdot R_a.
   \]

   *Challenge.*

   \[
   e = H\!\left(R_a, C_a, R_b, C_b,\; pk_{\text{alice}},\; pk_{\text{bob}},\; T_1, T_2, T_3,\; \texttt{msg.sender},\; \texttt{spender},\; \texttt{chainid},\; \texttt{registry},\; \mathrm{keccak}(\tau_{\text{approve}})\right),
   \]

   with \( \tau_{\text{approve}} \) = =AlbertaBuck/FiatShamir/IdentityRegistry/Approve/v2=.

   *Respond.*  \( s_1 = k_1 - e \cdot sk_{\text{alice}} \pmod q,\qquad s_2 = k_2 - e \cdot r_b \pmod q \).

   *Verify.*  The contract recomputes \( e \) from the public inputs
   and the prover-supplied commitments, then checks

   \begin{align}
   s_1 \cdot G + e \cdot pk_{\text{alice}} &\stackrel{?}{=} T_1 \tag{V1} \\
   s_2 \cdot G + e \cdot R_b               &\stackrel{?}{=} T_2 \tag{V2} \\
   s_2 \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} - s_1 \cdot R_a + e \cdot (C_b - C_a) &\stackrel{?}{=} T_3. \tag{V3}
   \end{align}

** Theorem 3 (Completeness)

   *Statement.*  An honestly generated transcript satisfies (V1), (V2),
   (V3).

   *Proof.*  (V1) and (V2) are standard Schnorr identities:

   \begin{align*}
   s_1 G + e \cdot pk_{\text{alice}} &= (k_1 - e \cdot sk_{\text{alice}}) G + e \cdot sk_{\text{alice}} G = k_1 G = T_1, \\
   s_2 G + e \cdot R_b               &= (k_2 - e r_b) G + e r_b G = k_2 G = T_2.
   \end{align*}

   For (V3), substitute \( s_1, s_2 \) and rearrange:

   \begin{align*}
   s_2 \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} - s_1 \cdot R_a + e (C_b - C_a)
     &= (k_2 - e r_b) pk_{\text{bob}} - (k_1 - e \cdot sk_{\text{alice}}) R_a + e (C_b - C_a) \\
     &= \bigl[k_2 pk_{\text{bob}} - k_1 R_a\bigr] + e \bigl[sk_{\text{alice}} R_a - r_b \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} + C_b - C_a\bigr] \\
     &= T_3 + e \bigl[-\,(C_a - sk_{\text{alice}} R_a) + (C_b - r_b \cdot pk_{\text{bob}})\bigr] \\
     &= T_3 + e \cdot \bigl[-M + M\bigr] \\
     &= T_3.
   \end{align*}

   where the penultimate line uses the witness relations from the
   honest setup, and the final line uses (S3).  \( \blacksquare \)

** Theorem 4 (Special Soundness / 2-Extractability)

   *Statement.*  Given two accepting transcripts
   \( (T_1, T_2, T_3,\; e,\; s_1, s_2) \) and
   \( (T_1, T_2, T_3,\; e',\; s'_1, s'_2) \) with \( e \neq e' \) (and
   identical public inputs), there is an efficient algorithm that
   outputs a witness \( (sk^\ast, r^\ast) \) satisfying (S1), (S2),
   (S3).

   *Proof (extractor).*  Subtract (V1) for the two transcripts:

   \[
   (s_1 - s'_1) G + (e - e') \cdot pk_{\text{alice}} = O
   \quad \Longrightarrow \quad
   pk_{\text{alice}} = \frac{s'_1 - s_1}{e - e'} \cdot G.
   \]

   Set \( sk^\ast := (s'_1 - s_1)(e - e')^{-1} \bmod q \); this is
   well-defined because \( q \) is prime and \( e \neq e' \).  By
   construction (S1) holds.  Similarly from (V2),
   \( r^\ast := (s'_2 - s_2)(e - e')^{-1} \bmod q \) satisfies
   (S2): \( R_b = r^\ast G \).

   For (S3), subtract (V3) for the two transcripts:

   \[
   (s_2 - s'_2) \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} - (s_1 - s'_1) \cdot R_a + (e - e')(C_b - C_a) = O.
   \]

   Substitute \( s_2 - s'_2 = -(e - e') r^\ast \) and
   \( s_1 - s'_1 = -(e - e') sk^\ast \):

   \[
   -(e - e') r^\ast \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} + (e - e') sk^\ast \cdot R_a + (e - e')(C_b - C_a) = O.
   \]

   Dividing through by the nonzero scalar \( (e - e') \):

   \[
   sk^\ast \cdot R_a - r^\ast \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} + (C_b - C_a) = O
   \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
   C_a - sk^\ast \cdot R_a = C_b - r^\ast \cdot pk_{\text{bob}},
   \]

   which is (S3).  Therefore \( (sk^\ast, r^\ast) \) is a valid
   witness, and the extractor runs in the time of four field inversions
   and a constant number of group operations.  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Remark (implication for cheating provers).*  Special soundness
   lifts, via the forking lemma[fn:forklemma], to a reduction from any
   efficient prover that convinces the verifier on a false statement
   to an efficient solver for discrete logarithms on \( G_1 \).  Under
   the DLog assumption on \( \text{alt\_bn128} \), no such prover
   exists.  In particular, Alice cannot produce a valid proof for
   \( E_{\text{bob}} \) encrypting any \( M' \neq M \) -- not merely
   "hard to compute," but infeasible under a standard assumption
   Ethereum already requires.

** Theorem 5 (Honest-Verifier Zero-Knowledge)

   *Statement.*  There is an efficient simulator \( \mathcal{S} \) that,
   given only the public inputs and a challenge \( e \) drawn from the
   verifier's distribution, outputs a transcript
   \( (T_1, T_2, T_3, e, s_1, s_2) \) distributed /identically/ to an
   honest prover's transcript conditioned on that challenge.

   *Proof (simulator).*  \( \mathcal{S} \) samples
   \( s_1, s_2 \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q \) uniformly, then /defines/

   \begin{align*}
   T_1 &:= s_1 \cdot G + e \cdot pk_{\text{alice}}, \\
   T_2 &:= s_2 \cdot G + e \cdot R_b, \\
   T_3 &:= s_2 \cdot pk_{\text{bob}} - s_1 \cdot R_a + e \cdot (C_b - C_a).
   \end{align*}

   Equations (V1), (V2), (V3) hold by construction, so the transcript
   is accepting.

   *Distribution match.*  In the real protocol, \( k_1, k_2 \) are
   uniform on \( \mathbb{Z}_q \); the honest responses
   \( s_i = k_i - e \cdot w_i \) are therefore uniform on \( \mathbb{Z}_q \)
   (shift by a constant).  The commitments \( T_1, T_2, T_3 \) are
   /deterministic functions/ of the \( s_i \) and the public inputs:
   (V1), (V2), (V3) each uniquely determine one \( T_i \) from the
   corresponding equation.  The simulator samples \( s_i \) uniformly
   and computes the \( T_i \) via the same equations -- the joint
   distribution over \( (T_1, T_2, T_3, s_1, s_2) \) is identical.
   \( \blacksquare \)

   *Corollary (NIZK via Fiat-Shamir in the ROM).*  Model \( H \) as a
   programmable random oracle.  The Fiat-Shamir transform replaces the
   verifier's challenge with \( e = H(\text{public inputs}, T_1, T_2, T_3, \text{context}) \).
   Standard arguments[fn:fs] promote:

   - HVZK \( \Rightarrow \) NIZK (the simulator programs \( H \) so that
     \( H(\text{public inputs}, T_1, T_2, T_3, \ldots) = e \) for the
     \( (T_i) \) it produced);
   - special soundness \( \Rightarrow \) proof-of-knowledge soundness
     (the forking lemma rewinds the oracle).

   The binding of \( \texttt{msg.sender}, \texttt{spender}, \texttt{chainid} \)
   into the hash input is standard domain separation that prevents
   cross-context replay.

   KYC ceremony:   issuer signs m     -->  sigma on m                 (PS-CM unforgeability)

   Registration:   masked-PS NIZK     -->  E_alice bound to same m    (Theorems R-sound, R-comp)
   approve(Bob):   Alice re-encrypts  -->  E_bob                      (Theorem 3)
                   Chaum-Pedersen     -->  verified on chain          (Theorems 3, 4, 5)
   Decryption:     Bob computes M     -->  M = C_b - sk_bob * R_b     (ElGamal correctness)
   Two-channel:    Bob receives       -->  m(identity_data) * G == M  (tagged hash commitment)
   #+end_example

** Theorem 6 (End-to-End Re-Encryption Correctness)

   *Statement.*  Suppose the BUCK contract:
   1. holds a registered credential \( E_{\text{alice}} = (R_a, C_a) \)
      together with \( pk_{\text{alice}} \), accepted at registration
      time with a presentation \( (A, B) \) and a registration NIZK
      that verified against a trusted issuer public key
      \( (X, Y, Y_1) \);
   2. accepts a Chaum-Pedersen proof \( \pi \) supplied by
      \( \texttt{msg.sender} = \) Alice, with spender \( pk_{\text{bob}} \)
      and submitted ciphertext \( E_{\text{bob}} = (R_b, C_b) \).

   Then, under the DLog assumption on \( G_1 \) and in the ROM, there
   exists \( M = m \cdot G \) such that
   \[
   C_a - sk_{\text{alice}} \cdot R_a \;=\; M \;=\; C_b - sk_{\text{bob}} \cdot R_b,
   \]
   and the scalar \( m \) is an identity scalar the issuer certified.

   *Proof sketch.*  By Theorems R-sound and R-comp, an accepting
   registration yields (through the forking lemma) a single witness
   \( (m, b, r, sk) \) with \( C_a - sk_{\text{alice}} R_a = m \cdot G \)
   and \( (A, B - b Y_1) \) a plain PS signature on \( m \) under
   \( (X, Y) \); PS-CM unforgeability ensures \( m \) is a scalar
   the issuer signed.  The verifier never sees \( m \): it checks the
   masked equation (a'), not a signature against a visible message.
   The contract reads \( E_{\text{alice}} \) from storage, not
   calldata, so the value is frozen.

   Theorem 4 (special soundness) plus the forking lemma reduction to
   DLog gives: from any adversary producing an accepting \( \pi \)
   with non-negligible probability there is an extractor outputting
   witnesses \( (sk^\ast, r^\ast) \) with
   \( C_a - sk^\ast R_a = C_b - r^\ast pk_{\text{bob}} \).  Since
   \( pk_{\text{alice}} = sk^\ast \cdot G = sk_{\text{alice}} \cdot G \)
   and \( G \) is a generator, \( sk^\ast = sk_{\text{alice}} \); so
   both sides equal the registered \( M \).  Therefore
   \( E_{\text{bob}} \) decrypts under \( sk_{\text{bob}} \) to the
   same \( M \) that the issuer's PS signature attests to.
   \( \blacksquare \)

** Why This Suffices for Every Downstream Use Case

   - *=approve= / =transfer= / =transferFrom= (Identity):*  Bob decrypts
     \( E_{\text{bob}} \) and obtains \( M \); combined with off-chain
     =identity_data= delivery and the check \( \mathrm{keccak}(\tau_{\text{ID}} \Vert \text{identity\_data}) \cdot G = M \),
     he learns Alice's real identity with cryptographic assurance.  A BUCK
     =transfer= / =transferFrom= is gated on this =approve= handshake against
     /both/ parties' registered records, so a value move carries an account
     \( \leftrightarrow \) Identity binding for /each/ counterparty -- the
     bilateral mutual decryptability formalised in the EOA mirror below.

   - *Notes spend predicates (Part IV):*  The same re-encryption / decryption
     algebra underlies BUCK Notes.  At mint the issuer addresses the recipient's
     receiving key and a mint binding forces the ciphertext over the issuer's
     /own/ registered \( M_{\text{iss}} \) (Theorem 7).  At spend, relation (3)
     of the folded gate is this Part's statement -- the depositing account's
     registered credential decrypts to the Identity \( m_{\text{rec}} \) -- and
     the gate ties that Identity to the reading key through a certified
     accumulator leaf (Theorem 8); the bearer B1 path instead binds the
     depositor's /own/ Identity \( M_{\text{dep}} \) for the public issuer
     (Theorem 9).  The binding is to the /Identity/, not a mint-time keypair --
     /recovery, not finality/ on key loss -- yet identity laundering through
     the note pool remains infeasible.

   - *Identity Guardians (=approveFor=, recovery, cosigning):*  The Schnorr
     proof of knowledge of \( sk \) is a one-statement specialization of the
     above (only (S1) with a Fiat-Shamir binding to
     \( (\texttt{msg.sender}, \texttt{principal}) \)).  Theorems 3-5 apply
     verbatim with the second and third statements dropped.

** The EOA Transfer Mirror (=transfer= / =transferFrom=)

   A private \( \to \) private BUCK =transfer= achieves the /same/
   mutual-decryptability invariant as a Notes spend, but /without/ a SNARK and
   with collusion-resistance for free -- precisely because the transfer is /not/
   anonymous: both counterparties are named at one moment.  The bilateral
   =approve= handshake reads /both/ registered records in a single call and runs
   the re-encryption of this Part in each direction; the value move is gated on
   it.

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Step                        | Cryptographic step                                                                                    | Who is named  | Guarantee                                           |
   |-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
   | =register=                  | store \( (pk,\ E_{\text{addr}} = \mathrm{Enc}(M, pk)) \) + PS credential                               | --            | Identity \( \leftrightarrow \) KYC (Theorem 6)      |
   | =approve= (bilateral)       | re-encrypt \( E_{A\to B} = \mathrm{Enc}(M_A, pk_B) \) + Chaum-Pedersen \( \equiv E_{\text{addr}}[A] \) | both          | sound re-encryption under the /named/ key (Thm 3-6) |
   | =transfer= / =transferFrom= | value \( A \to B \), gated on the approve                                                              | both (linked) | mutual decryptability established                   |
   | receipt                     | =verifiable_decrypt= \( E_{X\to\text{me}} \to M \)                                                     | --            | either party names the other; 3rd-party-checkable   |
   #+LATEX: }

   *Corollary (EOA transfer mutual decryptability).*  For a =transfer= /
   =transferFrom= gated on bilateral =approve= proofs, each counterparty can
   recover the other's registered Identity point ( \( B \) decrypts
   \( E_{A\to B} \) under \( sk_B \) to \( M_A \); \( A \) decrypts
   \( E_{B\to A} \) under \( sk_A \) to \( M_B \) ), each can prove it under
   compulsion (verifiable decryption), and no third party lacking either secret
   key recovers \( M_A \) or \( M_B \) (ElGamal IND-CPA, hence DDH).  This is the
   BUCK mutual-decryptability invariant in its /cheapest/ form; the Identity-M
   Notes (Part IV) reconstruct the /same/ invariant across an anonymising
   mint \( \to \) spend detour, which is exactly what forces the extra apparatus
   there (Theorems 7-11).

   *Proof.*  Each direction is Theorem 6 applied to the respective ordered
   pair: an accepted Chaum-Pedersen proof certifies that \( E_{A\to B} \)
   re-encrypts \( A \)'s registered, PS-certified \( M_A \) under \( pk_B \),
   so \( C_{A\to B} - sk_B R_{A\to B} = M_A \) by ElGamal correctness;
   symmetrically for \( B \to A \).  Third-party secrecy is the IND-CPA
   security of ElGamal on \( G_1 \): deciding whether a ciphertext encrypts a
   candidate \( M \) is a DDH decision (see /Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later/ for
   the quantum caveat).  \( \blacksquare \)

* Part IV: BUCK Notes Spend Predicates (A1, A2, B1)

This Part proves what an accepted Notes spend establishes about the Identities on either side of
it.  An addressed note (A1, A2) is encrypted to the recipient's /receiving key/, which is
independent of its Identity, and its deposit is ONE folded Groth16 proof over one witness.  A
bearer note (B1) is spent with a sigma protocol and a membership proof that share one committed
point.  The clear-text reference for the folds is =alberta_buck/wallet/deposit_fold.py=; the
architecture, and the attack each relation refuses, are in
[[file:doc/review/notes-receiving-key.org][the receiving-key architecture]].

** Setup

   *Pool.*  =noteTree=, a depth-20 Poseidon tree of leaves
   \( cm = \mathrm{Poseidon}_6(T_{CM}, \mathrm{flavor}, v, \rho, \mathrm{idHash}, \mathrm{predicate}) \)
   (\( \mathrm{idHash} \) digests the per-flavor identity payload), whose empty leaf is the field
   tag of =AlbertaBuck/Notes/Zero/v2=; a =nullifiers= set; a 30-deep ring of recent roots.  Mint
   is the batch rollup of /alberta-buck-notes-rollup-mint/ -- a Groth16 proof of incremental
   insertion of \( N \) fresh leaves with in-circuit range checks
   (\( v_i, \texttt{totalFace} \in [0,2^{128}) \)) and value conservation
   (\( \sum_i v_i = \texttt{totalFace} \)); the contract pins =oldRoot= / =nextLeafIndex= and
   pulls =totalFace= BUCK.  Demurrage is orthogonal.

   *Identity accumulator.*  Membership is a path through two trees.  Each authority keeps
   subtrees: an identity registry's are private, of depth 12, with salted leaves; an institution's
   are public, with unsalted ones.  The aggregator's tree, of depth 20, has subtree roots for
   leaves, and its root \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) is posted on chain into a ring of 256 records, each
   with its posting time.  A consumer declares the oldest root it accepts -- Notes, seven days --
   and a spend names the root it proved against.  Every level folds with the same Poseidon, so a
   membership proof is one path of 32 levels.  Each leaf function leads with its own v2 field tag
   \( \tau \):
   \[
     \ell_R(m, k, s) = \mathrm{Poseidon}(\tau_R, m, k, s), \quad
     \ell_S(M, s) = \mathrm{Poseidon}(\tau_S, M.x, M.y, s), \quad
     \ell(M) = \mathrm{Poseidon}(\tau_I, M.x, M.y).
   \]
   \( \ell_R \), the receiving leaf, pairs an Identity with a receiving key; \( \ell_S \) is the
   salted identity leaf; \( \ell \) the public one.  A salt derives from a wallet secret, never
   from \( m \), so a party holding every certified identity scalar and a whole published subtree
   decides no membership (Poseidon as a random oracle).  =register= refuses a caller-supplied
   leaf: admission is the authority's certification, not a by-product of the hiding presentation,
   whose leaf would otherwise reveal \( M \) to whoever computed it.

   *Identity primitives (recap, Parts I-III).*  A registered account is
   \( (\text{addr},\ pk = sk\,G,\ E_{\text{addr}} = (rG,\ M + r\,pk)) \): an ElGamal encryption
   of its issuer-certified Identity point \( M = mG \) under its own key.  The Identity Fountain
   issues unlimited fresh accounts \( (sk_i, pk_i, E_{\text{addr},i}) \) on the /same/ \( M \).

   *Receiving key.*  The recipient holds a receiving secret \( k \), derived from wallet seed
   material and independent of \( m \), with \( pk_{\text{recv}} = kG \); its registry certifies
   \( \ell_R(m_{\text{rec}}, k, s) \).  The identity scalar is disclosed to every counterparty by
   design (Part II), so nothing is encrypted under \( M \): a key everyone you pay learns cannot
   also read your mail.

   *Note addressing (mint).*
   \[
     \text{A1: } eNote = (r_nG,\ vG + r_n\,pk_{\text{recv}}),\quad
                 eRec = (r'G,\ M_{\text{rec}} + r'\,pk_{\text{recv}});
   \]
   \[
     \text{A2: } eNote = (r_nG,\ vG + r_n\,pk_{\text{recv}}),\quad
                 eIss = (R_i, C_i) = (r'G,\ M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,pk_{\text{recv}}).
   \]
   A1's issuer is public: it signs the batch with a Schnorr proof checked at mint, and
   \( \mathrm{idHash} = \mathrm{Poseidon}_6(T_{ID}, eNote, m_{\text{iss}}) \).
   A2's issuer is private: a recipient-blinded re-encryption binding (=verifyIssuerReenc=)
   publishes \( Q = pk_{\text{recv}} + \beta H,\ U = r'H,\ T = r'\,pk_{\text{recv}} + \gamma H \),
   and \( \mathrm{idHash} = \mathrm{Poseidon}_{11}(T_{ID}, eNote, eIss, T) \); \( \gamma \) reaches the
   recipient wrapped in the delivery.  B1 is bearer: no recipient at mint, the public issuer signs
   the batch, and the depositor encrypts its /own/ Identity for the issuer at spend (Theorem 9).
   Throughout this Part \( H = H_{\text{PED}} \).

   *Nullifier.*  Flavor-agnostic: \( nf = \mathrm{Poseidon}_3(T_{NF},\ \rho,\ \mathrm{idHash}) \).
   Each note hash leads with its own v2 domain word (\( T_{CM}, T_{ID}, T_{NF} \)), so no two of
   them, and none of them and any other Poseidon in the protocol, share a preimage.

   *Transcripts.*  Every sigma protocol's Fiat-Shamir challenge ends in its own v2 domain word,
   so no accepting transcript of one protocol is an accepting transcript of another.

** Theorem 7 (Mint Addressing and Anti-Framing)

   *Statement.*  Let the recipient hold \( (m_{\text{rec}}, k) \) and the issuer a registered
   credential \( E^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}} = (R_{\text{reg}}, C_{\text{reg}}) \) decrypting
   under \( sk_{\text{iss}} \) to its certified \( M_{\text{iss}} \).  For fresh
   \( r_n, r' \):

   1. *(Decryptability.)*  \( k \) decrypts \( eNote \) to \( vG \), \( eRec \) to
      \( M_{\text{rec}} \), and \( eIss \) to \( M_{\text{iss}} \) -- from whichever account the
      recipient uses.
   2. *(Anti-framing.)*  An accepted mint binding forces the encrypted point to be the issuer's
      /own/ registered Identity: =verifyIssuerSchnorr= for a public issuer; for A2,
      =verifyIssuerReenc= forces \( eIss = (r'G,\ M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,pk_Q) \) with
      \( M_{\text{iss}} \) the plaintext of \( E^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}} \) at =msg.sender= and
      \( pk_Q = Q - \beta H \).
   3. *(Issuer ignorance, unlinkability.)*  The issuer learns \( pk_{\text{recv}} \), and
      \( M_{\text{rec}} \) off chain, but neither \( k \) nor \( m_{\text{rec}} \).
      \( Q, U, T \) are uniform, and two mints to one recipient are indistinguishable under DDH.

   *Proof.*  (1) ElGamal correctness under \( pk_{\text{recv}} = kG \):
   \( C - kR = P + r'kG - kr'G = P \).  (2) Schnorr EUF-CMA for the public binding.  For A2,
   special soundness of the five-relation Okamoto sigma extracts
   \( (r', \beta, sk_{\text{iss}}, \gamma) \) with
   \[
     \text{L1: } R_i = r'G,\quad
     \text{L2: } U = r'H,\quad
     \text{L3: } T = r'Q - \beta U + \gamma H,\quad
     \text{L4: } pk_{\text{iss}} = sk_{\text{iss}}G,\quad
     \text{L5: } C_{\text{reg}} + T - C_i = sk_{\text{iss}} R_{\text{reg}} + \gamma H.
   \]
   L2 and L3 give \( T = r'\,pk_Q + \gamma H \); L5 then gives
   \( C_i = (C_{\text{reg}} - sk_{\text{iss}} R_{\text{reg}}) + r'\,pk_Q = M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,pk_Q \).
   (3) The issuer handles group elements only; \( \beta \) and \( \gamma \) are one-time pads on
   \( Q \) and \( T \), \( U \) is uniform for uniform \( r' \), and distinguishing two mints is
   IND-CPA.  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Remark (which key).*  (2) binds \( eIss \) to \( pk_Q \), a key the minter chose, and not
   necessarily to \( pk_{\text{recv}} \).  An ElGamal ciphertext does not commit to its key, so
   the same bytes can open under \( k \) to a different registered Identity.  Theorem 12 closes
   this at spend.

** Theorem 8 (Folded Deposit Gate -- A1, A2)

   Spending an addressed note needs two facts about two DIFFERENT secrets: "I can read this note"
   (\( k \)) and "I am this registered Identity" (\( m_{\text{rec}} \)).  Proved side by side they
   say nothing about their owner -- a thief holding a stolen payload answers the first with the
   stolen \( k \) and the second with its own Identity, and both are true.  So the gate is one
   proof over one witness, and the tie is a relation.

   *Statement.*  Suppose =Notes= accepts an addressed spend (=spendCoupledA1= /
   =spendCoupledA2=): the spend proof (\( cm \) under =noteRoot=, \( nf \) well formed, flavor
   bound to the entry point); \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) accepted for the Notes consumer; and the fold
   proof, whose public inputs the adapter derives on chain -- \( nf \), (A1) the face \( v \),
   \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \), the supplied \( eEnc = (R_e, C_e) \), and the depositor's
   \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) and \( E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d) \), read from the registry at
   =msg.sender=.  Under SNARK knowledge soundness and Poseidon collision resistance there is an
   extractor producing a witness with:

   0. *(Same note.)*  \( nf = \mathrm{Poseidon}_3(T_{NF}, \rho, \mathrm{idHash}) \) for the
      \( (\rho, \mathrm{idHash}) \) the spend proof opened, and \( \mathrm{idHash} \) opens to the
      flavor's payload: A1 \( (eNote, m_{\text{iss}}) \); A2 \( (eNote, eIss, T) \).
   1. *(Reading.)*  A1: \( eNote = (r_nG,\ (v + r_nk)G) \).  A2:
      \( eIss = (r'G,\ M_I + (r'k)G) \) for a point \( M_I \).
   2. *(The spend's ciphertext.)*  A1: \( eEnc = (tG,\ (m_{\text{rec}} + tk)G) \).  A2:
      \( eEnc = (tG,\ M_I + (tk)G) \).
   3. *(Account.)*  \( pk_{\text{dep}} = sk_{\text{dep}}G \), \( R_d = r_EG \),
      \( C_d = (m_{\text{rec}} + sk_{\text{dep}} r_E)G \): the depositing account's registered
      credential decrypts to \( M_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{rec}}G \).
   4. *(Tie.)*  \( \ell_R(m_{\text{rec}}, k, s) \) has a 32-level path to \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \).
   5. *(A2: issuer registered.)*  \( \ell_S(M_I, s_{\text{iss}}) \) has a 32-level path to the
      same \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \).
   6. *(A2: key tie.)*  \( T = (r'k)G + \gamma H \).

   *Proof.*  Knowledge soundness extracts the witness, and each clause is a constraint.  The fold
   and the spend proof share only \( nf \), and Poseidon collision resistance makes the fold's
   \( (\rho, \mathrm{idHash}) \) the spend's.  The clauses are joined by signal identity rather
   than by inference across proofs: one \( m_{\text{rec}} \) signal enters (3) and the leaf of
   (4), and one \( k \) enters (1), (2), (4) and (6).  Every witnessed limb is range-checked.  A1
   folds its point sums into scalar sums and adds no points; A2's three additions each assert
   distinct \( x \)-coordinates, the precondition of the incomplete addition formula.
   \( \blacksquare \)

   *Implications.*

   - *Only the addressed Identity spends.*  A thief holding a stolen payload has \( \rho \), the
     openings and \( k \), and satisfies (3) only with its own account, whose Identity is
     \( m' \neq m_{\text{rec}} \).  Clause (4) then needs \( \ell_R(m', k, \cdot) \) certified,
     and no authority certified that pair.  Nor can it bring its own certified pair
     \( (m', k') \), because the committed payload pins \( k \): in A1, \( eNote \) and the public
     \( v \) fix both \( r_n \) and \( r_nk \); in A2, \( T \) fixes \( (r'k)G \) up to
     \( \gamma H \), and moving to \( k' \) needs \( r'(k - k')G = \delta H \) for a known
     \( \delta \), a discrete logarithm.
   - *Recovery, not finality.*  Any registered account of \( M_{\text{rec}} \) deposits (clause
     3), and \( k \) is recovered from the wallet seed.  Losing an account key loses no note.
   - *Collusion closed (A2).*  An issuer that keys \( eIss \) to a throwaway point leaves the
     recipient decrypting an \( M_I \) with no certified leaf; (5) has no witness and the deposit
     reverts.  No spendable-but-unnameable A2 note exists.
   - *The issuer cannot self-redeem.*  It knows \( pk_{\text{recv}} \) and \( M_{\text{rec}} \),
     not \( k \) or \( m_{\text{rec}} \), and (4) needs both -- unless it /is/ the recipient.
   - *Revocation latency is the consumer's age.*  A holder cleared from its subtree proves against
     no root posted after the clearance, and Notes accepts a root for seven days; that age is the
     protocol's statement of how long a revoked holder can still spend.
   - *No third-party leakage.*  No public input names an Identity: \( eEnc \) is a fresh
     encryption under \( pk_{\text{recv}} \), which is never public; the account's key and
     credential were public already; no leaf, subtree slot or path is revealed.  \( eEnc \) is a
     re-encryption of the note's ciphertext, not the ciphertext itself, so the spend presents
     nothing the mint published.

** Theorem 9 (Depositor-Binding Soundness -- B1)

   B1 needs no fold.  Its two facts -- "my payout account is bound to this Identity" and "this
   ciphertext encrypts that Identity for the issuer" -- rest on ONE secret, \( m_{\text{dep}} \),
   so the sigma's shared Fiat-Shamir nonce is a genuine tie.

   *Statement.*  Suppose =Notes= accepts a bearer spend (=spendCoupledB1=): the spend proof,
   =verifyDepositorBinding(depositor, issuer, eDepForIss, pi)=, \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) accepted,
   and the =identity_membership_b1= proof over \( pi.P_{\text{dep}} \).  Under SNARK knowledge
   soundness, special soundness of the Okamoto sigma, Schnorr EUF-CMA[fn:schnorr], and DLog with
   \( H \) hashed to the curve, extractors produce \( (m_{\text{dep}}, sk_{\text{dep}}, r, b) \)
   from the sigma and \( (M, b', s) \) with a path from the SNARK such that:

   1. \( cm = \mathrm{Poseidon}_6(T_{CM}, B1, v, \rho, \mathrm{idHash}, \cdot) \) lies under =noteRoot=,
      and the prover knows \( \rho \) (the bearer authorization).
   2. *(Account.)*  \( pk_{\text{dep}} = sk_{\text{dep}}G \),
      \( C_d = m_{\text{dep}}G + sk_{\text{dep}}R_d \).
   3. *(Encrypted for the issuer.)*
      \( eDepForIss = (rG,\ m_{\text{dep}}G + r\,pk_{\text{iss}}) \), the same
      \( m_{\text{dep}} \) as (2).
   4. *(Committed.)*  \( P_{\text{dep}} = m_{\text{dep}}G + bH = M + b'H \).
   5. *(Registered.)*  \( \ell_S(M, s) \) has a 32-level path to \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \), and
      \( M = M_{\text{dep}} \).
   6. The public issuer is named by \( m_{\text{iss}} \) and an EUF-CMA Schnorr over the batch.

   *Proof.*  The sigma's single response for \( m_{\text{dep}} \) couples (2), (3) and the first
   opening of (4) to one Identity (Theorem 4 shape).  The circuit computes \( b'H \) from \( b' \)
   rather than accepting it, and the contract feeds the same \( P_{\text{dep}} \) to both
   verifiers.  Equating the two openings gives \( M - M_{\text{dep}} = (b - b')H \).  A reduction
   that issued or extracted (Theorem R-sound) every registered scalar knows \( \log_G M \), so
   \( M \neq M_{\text{dep}} \) yields \( \log_G H \), which no one knows: \( H \) is hashed to the
   curve.  Schnorr EUF-CMA gives (6).  \( \blacksquare \)

   *Implications.*

   - *The public issuer names the bearer depositor.*  From the =SpentCoupledB1= event the issuer
     decrypts \( eDepForIss \) under \( sk_{\text{iss}} \) to \( M_{\text{dep}} \) --
     collusion-resistantly, since a substituted ciphertext breaks (3), and privately from third
     parties (IND-CPA).
   - *Collusion closed.*  A bogus \( M_{\text{dep}} \) has no certified leaf: (5) fails and the
     deposit reverts.  No one cashes a B1 note un-nameably.
   - *Why the generator must have no known logarithm.*  With \( H = hG \) for a public \( h \), a
     depositor holding any registered scalar \( m' \) -- which counterparties hold by design --
     sets \( b' = b + (m_{\text{dep}} - m')/h \), and the membership proof speaks of \( m' \)
     while the sigma speaks of its own unregistered \( m_{\text{dep}} \).  Hashing \( H \) to the
     curve leaves no \( h \) to shift by.
   - *Bearer race is civil-trust.*  Whoever publishes \( \rho \) first wins; the issuer who chose
     \( \rho \) can race but is identified at the moment of theft.  The cryptography places no
     prior restraint -- the legal system does, exactly as for a paper cashier's cheque.

** Theorem 10 (Nullifier and Double-Spend Prevention)

   The nullifier is flavor-agnostic,
   \( nf = \mathrm{Poseidon}_3(T_{NF},\ \rho,\ \mathrm{idHash}) \), over the spend proof's opening
   (\( \mathrm{idHash} \) digests the identity payload), so one =nullifiers= set serves every
   flavor.

   1. *(Determinism / double-spend.)*  \( nf \) is a deterministic function of
      \( (\rho, \mathrm{idHash}) \); two accepting spends of the same \( cm \) yield the same
      \( nf \), and the contract's \( nf \notin \texttt{nullifiers} \) check rejects the second.
   2. *(Third-party unlinkability.)*  Modelling Poseidon as a random oracle, \( nf \) is a PRF
      over the secret opening; without \( (\rho, \mathrm{idHash}) \) -- \( \rho \) a uniform
      \( q \)-element -- inverting \( nf \) to its \( cm \) is pre-image resistance.  So /no party
      lacking the opening/ links a spend to its mint.  The two counterparties who hold the
      opening already know the note exists; the nullifier protects the transaction /graph/ from
      outside observers -- exactly what bulk-trace resistance requires.

   *Proof.*  (1) is syntactic; (2) is the standard RO/PRF pre-image argument.
   \( \blacksquare \)

** Theorem 11 (Mutual Decryptability and Transaction-Graph Privacy)

   *Statement.*  For every accepted spend, /both/ counterparties can name the other as a
   /registered/ Identity, while no third party can name either or link the mint to the spend.

   *Recipient names the issuer.*
   - /A1 (public)/: the note carries \( m_{\text{iss}} \) and an EUF-CMA Schnorr; the recipient
     reads \( M_{\text{iss}} \) directly.
   - /A2 (private)/: the recipient decrypts \( eIss \) under \( k \) to \( M_{\text{iss}} \)
     (Theorem 7.1).  Theorem 8(5) certifies it registered, and Theorem 12 makes it the minter's
     own -- the name is right, not merely registered.

   *Issuer names the depositor.*
   - /A1, A2 (addressed)/: the issuer chose \( pk_{\text{recv}} \) and knows
     \( M_{\text{rec}} \).  The mailbox leaf
     \( \mathrm{Poseidon}(\tau_M, M_{\text{rec}}, pk_{\text{recv}}, s') \), checked against a
     posted root before paying, tells it the key belongs to that Identity, and Theorem 8(3)-(4)
     make that Identity's accounts the only ones that spend.
   - /B1 (bearer)/: the issuer decrypts \( eDepForIss \) from =SpentCoupledB1= under
     \( sk_{\text{iss}} \) to \( M_{\text{dep}} \) (Theorem 9), certified registered.

   Both directions resolve to a registered Identity point and -- via the off-chain
   identity_data path (Theorem 6) -- the real-world KYC identity.  This is the BUCK
   mutual-decryptability invariant, preserved across the pool detour and /enforced/: the
   membership relations make an un-nameable counterparty /un-spendable/, so the invariant holds
   against a colluding pair, not merely an honest one.

   *Privacy corollary (what Mallory cannot do).*  The chain shows an opaque \( cm \), a fresh
   \( nf \), a public \( (face, recipient) \), and ciphertexts and commitments that are IND-CPA or
   uniform: \( eIss \), \( Q, U, T \) at mint; \( eEnc \), \( eDepForIss \) and the perfectly
   hiding \( P_{\text{dep}} \) at spend.  The published subtrees' leaves are salted.  Lacking any
   counterparty secret, Mallory learns no Identity, no \( cm \leftrightarrow nf \) link
   (Theorem 10), and no link between two notes to one recipient (Theorem 7.3).  Bilateral
   disclosure between the two parties is preserved; bulk harvest is defeated.  \( \blacksquare \)

** Theorem 12 (The Key Tie -- A2 Naming)

   Theorem 7's remark leaves one freedom: \( eIss \) is bound to the minter's own Identity under a
   key \( pk_Q \) the minter chose.  A minter holding the recipient's \( pk_{\text{recv}} \) and a
   registered sock puppet \( M_B \) sets
   \( pk_Q = pk_{\text{recv}} + r'^{-1}(M_B - M_{\text{iss}}) \); then
   \( eIss = (r'G,\ M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,pk_Q) = (r'G,\ M_B + r'\,pk_{\text{recv}}) \).  L1-L5
   hold, the recipient decrypts a registered \( M_B \), (5) holds, and an honest recipient would
   name the puppet.  Relation (6) is what refuses it.

   *Statement.*  Suppose an A2 note was minted under an accepted =verifyIssuerReenc= (Theorem
   7.2) and deposited under an accepted fold (Theorem 8).  Then under DLog, with \( H \) hashed to
   the curve, the Identity the fold certifies is the minter's: \( M_I = M_{\text{iss}} \).  A
   receipt that checks
   \[ M_{\text{named}} = C_i - T + \gamma H \]
   names the same point.

   *Proof.*  Theorem 7's extractor gives \( R_i = r'G \), \( T = r'\,pk_Q + \gamma H \) and
   \( C_i = M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,pk_Q \).  Theorem 8's gives, over the same committed
   \( (eIss, T) \) (clause 0), the same \( r' \) -- discrete logarithms in a prime-order group
   are unique -- with \( C_i = M_I + r'kG \) and \( T = r'kG + \gamma_m H \).  Subtracting,
   \( M_{\text{iss}} - M_I = r'(kG - pk_Q) = (\gamma - \gamma_m)H \).  Both points are
   registered, so a reduction that issued or extracted every registered scalar knows both
   logarithms, and \( M_I \neq M_{\text{iss}} \) yields \( \log_G H \).  Hence
   \( M_I = M_{\text{iss}} \), then \( \gamma_m = \gamma \) and \( pk_Q = kG \), and
   \( C_i - T + \gamma H = M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,pk_Q - r'\,pk_Q = M_{\text{iss}} \).
   \( \blacksquare \)

   *Implications.*

   - *The split is refused where it matters.*  At spend, by the fold, because the chain cannot
     look anything up there: \( cm \) and \( \mathrm{idHash} \) are hidden and the nullifier
     reveals nothing, so the only channel from mint to spend is \( \mathrm{idHash} \), which is
     why it commits \( T \).  A receipt repeats the check off chain with the \( \gamma \) the
     delivery carried.
   - *Why the blind is on \( H \).*  Blinded on \( G \), (6) binds nothing: \( \gamma \) absorbs
     any difference of Identities.  A prime-order group has no rearrangement that repairs a
     known-logarithm blind.
   - *Whose Identity, and whether registered, are separate facts.*  Relation (5) says the
     decrypted point is REGISTERED; the tie says it is the MINTER'S.  Registration cannot supply
     the second, since =register= deliberately refuses a caller-supplied leaf and so ties no
     credential's plaintext to any accumulator leaf.  Both relations are needed.

** Worked Example: An A2 Spend End to End

   Theorems 7-12 in one A2 transfer: Alice issues privately to Bob, who deposits from an account
   no one could have pinned at mint.  Executable reference: =alberta_buck/wallet/unilateral_a2.py=,
   =delivery.py= and =deposit_fold.py=, with =test_unilateral_a2.py=, =test_delivery.py= and
   =test_receiving_key.py=, and on chain =NotesA2Tie.t.sol= and =NotesE2E.t.sol=.  The relation
   trace:

   1. *Register* (Parts I-III).  Alice and Bob each hold a PS-certified Identity \( (m, M) \) and
      registered accounts.  Bob's registry certifies \( \ell_R(m_{\text{rec}}, k, s) \), his
      Identity paired with his receiving key; Alice's certifies a naming leaf
      \( \ell_S(M_{\text{iss}}, s_{\text{iss}}) \).
   2. *Mint* (Theorem 7).  Alice learns \( M_{\text{rec}} \) and \( pk_{\text{recv}} \) off chain
      and checks the mailbox leaf, then mints \( eNote \) and \( eIss \) with the binding
      \( (Q, U, T) \).  The leaf
      \( cm = \mathrm{Poseidon}_6(T_{CM},\ A2,\ v,\ \rho,\ \mathrm{Poseidon}_{11}(T_{ID}, eNote, eIss, T),\ 0) \)
      is folded into =noteTree=.
   3. *Deliver and check* (Theorems 11, 12).  The payload -- \( \rho \), \( r' \), \( \gamma \),
      \( s_{\text{iss}} \) and the openings -- travels wrapped to \( pk_{\text{recv}} \).  Bob
      decrypts \( eIss \) under \( k \) to \( M_{\text{iss}} \), checks
      \( C_i - T + \gamma H = M_{\text{iss}} \), and rebuilds the path to
      \( \ell_S(M_{\text{iss}}, s_{\text{iss}}) \) from the published subtree before accepting the
      note.
   4. *Deposit* (Theorems 8, 10, 12).  From any registered account, Bob submits =spendCoupledA2=
      naming a posted \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \): the spend proof (\( cm \) under =noteRoot=, \( nf \))
      and one fold proof over the six relations.  The pool pays =face= BUCK.
   5. *Receipt* (Theorem 11).  Bob's receipt names himself and Alice, and the check of step 3 makes
      the naming the minter's.

   No on-chain value reveals an Identity; mint and spend are unlinkable to Mallory (Theorem 10);
   a throwaway \( eIss \) fails step 3 and would revert at step 4 (Theorem 8(5)); and a split
   \( eIss \) fails the tie at step 4 (Theorem 12).

** Mint-Circuit Obligations

   Three correctness obligations fall on the mint circuit rather than the spend.  The batch mint
   (=circuits/mint_batch.circom=, deployed as per-N pinned variants =mint_batch_n${N}.circom=; see
   [[https://perry.kundert.ca/images/alberta-buck-notes-rollup-mint.pdf][alberta-buck-notes-rollup-mint.org]]) discharges them.

*** Obligation 1 (Field-Overflow / Value-Conservation Range Check) -- /Discharged in circuit/

    Value conservation \( \sum_{i=1}^{N} v_i \;===\; \texttt{totalFace} \) is an equation in
    \( \mathbb{F}_r \) with \( r \approx 2^{254} \) and therefore admits integer-overflow
    witnesses unless each \( v_i \) is bounded.  =mint_batch.circom= applies =Num2Bits(128)= to
    every \( v_i \) and to =totalFace= (constraint family /(R)/ in the circuit source).  With
    \( N \leq 2^{20} \) (the tree-depth cap) and 128-bit per-leaf face, the integer sum cannot
    overflow: \( N \cdot 2^{128} \leq 2^{148} \ll 2^{254} = r \).  No spend can release more BUCK
    than was escrowed through an integer-versus-field mismatch on the mint side.

*** Obligation 2 (Single =transferFrom= \(\leftrightarrow\) \( N \) Commitments) -- /Discharged by SNARK binding/

    The =totalFace= public input of the mint SNARK is both (i) the value passed to
    =buck.transferFrom= and (ii) the value the circuit forces to equal \( \sum_i v_i \).  The
    single =Transfer= event of size =totalFace= is therefore bound to the Groth16 proof's public
    inputs -- which include the entire =cms[]= array on the per-N pinned verifiers (see
    [[https://perry.kundert.ca/images/alberta-buck-notes-rollup-mint.pdf][alberta-buck-notes-rollup-mint.org]], "Calldata delivery").  No batch-identifier storage slot is
    needed for soundness; analytics that want a transfer \(\leftrightarrow\) batch correspondence
    recover it from the =Minted= event's =cms= and the simultaneous =Transfer=.

*** Obligation 3 (Membership, Nullifier, Value) -- /Split between mint and spend/

    1. *Merkle membership (mint side).*  =mint_batch= performs an in-circuit dual Merkle walk per
       leaf (constraint family /(M)/ in the circuit source): the empty-seat walk constrains every
       witness sibling against the rolling prior root, and the filled-seat walk advances the
       rolling root with \( cm_i \) inserted.  After all \( N \) leaves the final rolling root is
       constrained to equal the public =newRoot=, which the contract accepts into its root ring;
       spends prove inclusion against any root in that window.
    2. *Nullifier determinism (spend side).*  =circuits/spend.circom=, the one note proof every
       spend reuses, recomputes \( nf = \mathrm{Poseidon}_3(T_{NF},\ \rho,\ \mathrm{idHash}) \) from
       the committed opening (Theorem 10); the folds recompute it too, which is how they are
       joined to the spend.
    3. *Value conservation (spend side).*  The spend circuit outputs =faceReleased= equal to the
       committed \( v \), the same 128-bit-bounded quantity range-checked at mint.  The mint-side
       range check (Obligation 1) is load-bearing here: a spend circuit that trusts \( v \)
       without re-bounding it relies on the mint circuit having done so.

    The leaf layout is
    \( \mathrm{Poseidon}_6(T_{CM},\, \mathrm{flavor},\, v,\, \rho,\, \mathrm{idHash},\, \mathrm{predicate}) \)
    over BN254 at tree depth 20, pinned by the per-N verifiers.  The A2 mint additionally exposes
    each leaf's \( eIss \) and \( T \) and recomputes
    \( \mathrm{idHash} = \mathrm{Poseidon}_{11}(T_{ID}, eNote, eIss, T) \) in circuit, so the binding
    =Notes.mint= checks is the one the note commits: \( 7N + 4 \) public signals per batch.

* Implementation Notes

** What the Contract Must Enforce

   The theorems assume a well-formed verifier.  Four conditions on
   the on-chain code are load-bearing:

   1. *Registration: the guards of Part I.*  \( A \neq O \) is
      critical (at \( A = O \) the masked equation holds for every
      \( m \): the trivial presentation attack, the Identity doc's
      Attack 9 / Link 1 with \( a = 0 \) in place of \( t = 0 \));
      \( pk \neq O \) and \( R \neq O \) are critical (no key
      ownership; \( C = mG \) published); \( B \neq O \) is
      defensive; the \( Y_1 \) consistency check at =trustIssuer=
      is what Theorem R-sound's extraction needs.  Part I's
      "What the Verifier Checks, and Why" gives each with its revert
      string.

   2. *Registration: \( G_2 \) subgroup check.*  The =ecPairing=
      precompile (post-EIP-197) validates that \( X, Y \) lie in the
      correct prime-order subgroup of \( G_2 \).  Without this,
      small-subgroup attacks on the twist curve could forge pairings.
      Theorem 1 is stated over the correct subgroup; enforcement is
      the precompile's responsibility.

   3. *Verification: \( E_{\text{alice}} \) read from storage, not
      calldata.*  Theorem 6's reduction routes through the
      registration-time binding of \( E_{\text{alice}} \) to
      issuer-certified \( m \).  If Alice supplies \( E_{\text{alice}} \)
      via calldata the binding is severed and Theorem 6 does not apply.

   4. *Notes: every fold input derived on chain.*  Theorem 8's clause
      (3) is about a registered account only because the adapter reads
      \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) and \( E_{\text{dep}} \) from the registry at
      =msg.sender= and derives \( nf \), the face and \( eEnc \)'s limbs
      itself; the identity root must be one the registry accepts for the
      Notes consumer; and the B1 membership proof must receive the same
      \( P_{\text{dep}} \) the sigma verified.  A caller-supplied copy of
      any of them severs the clause it feeds.

** What the Prover Must Enforce

   - *Uniform, fresh randomness for \( a, b, m_t, b_t, r_t, sk_t, k_1, k_2, r_b \).*
     Theorems 2', R-HVZK and 5 depend on uniform distributions, and
     Theorem 2' additionally on \( b \) never being reused across
     accounts.  Proposition R-fresh lists the explicit breaks for each
     reused value; RFC 6979-style derivation of the four proof nonces
     is the recommended hardening.  A biased RNG converts the hiding
     into a detectable correlation and converts perfect HVZK into
     approximate HVZK with an adversarial advantage proportional to
     the bias.
   - *Never publish \( \sigma \) or a rerandomization of it.*  Lemma 2's
     remark: any such pair is a candidate test on \( m \).  Backups,
     receipts and demos must carry the presentation or nothing.

   - *Fiat-Shamir domain separation.*  The hash input must include
     all public inputs and context (\( \texttt{msg.sender}, \texttt{spender}, \texttt{chainid},
     \texttt{registry} \)) and end in the protocol's v2 domain word.
     Omitting any one opens a cross-context replay path; the forking
     lemma reduction is unaffected, but the NIZK is no longer bound
     to the intended transaction, or to the intended protocol.

** Scope Not Covered Here

   - /Knowledge soundness/ of the registration-time NIZK that binds
     \( \sigma' \) to \( E_{\text{alice}} \): this is a standard
     Schnorr-family sigma protocol over a linear pairing relation
     (see =alberta-buck-identity.org=, "One-Time (PS Signature + NIZK
     Verification at Registration)") and follows the same
     completeness / soundness / ZK template as Theorems 3-5.  A
     separate write-up at the same level of detail is the natural
     next step.

   - /Circuit soundness/ of the BUCK Notes spend: Part IV proves that the
     folded deposit gates (A1/A2), the depositor-binding sigma (B1) and its
     membership proof imply the desired guarantees /assuming/ knowledge
     soundness of the underlying SNARK system, and that each circuit carries
     the relations its clear-text reference states.
     Soundness of the /proof system itself/ (Groth16 / PLONK / STARK)
     depends on the chosen proof system's trusted setup (if any) and
     circuit correctness; this is orthogonal to the contents of this
     document.

   - /Collision-resistance and hiding/ of the Poseidon commitment
     hash \( H_{\text{cm}} \) and pseudorandomness of the Poseidon
     nullifier PRF.  Poseidon is modelled as a random oracle in the
     SNARK circuit for the double-spend and commitment-binding
     arguments; its concrete security on BN254 has been independently
     studied and is assumed here.  See the Poseidon-hash
     cryptanalysis literature for current best attacks and parameter
     choices.

** The Long-Horizon Threat: Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later

   Every unlinkability guarantee in this document that rests on /ElGamal/
   -- cross-account unlinkability, the /issuer-blindness/ of the identity
   layer (a compelled or breached issuer cannot map its issued identities
   to chain accounts), and the third-party non-disclosure of A2 Notes --
   is /computational/, reducing to DDH (hence DLog) on \( \mathbb{G}_1 \).
   Only Theorem 2' (the hiding of the presentation) is
   information-theoretic and survives unconditionally; the pre-A'
   published pair was candidate-testable classically, by anyone
   holding \( m \), with no break at all.  This asymmetry has
   a sharp consequence against a powerful, patient adversary (a state or
   military actor with archival access).

   The on-chain credentials \( E_{\text{addr}} = (rG,\ M + r\,pk) \) are
   permanent public history.  An adversary who learns the /plaintext/
   identity points \( \{M_i\} \) -- paradigmatically a *compelled or
   breached issuer*, whose KYC records /are/ exactly that set -- cannot
   link them to chain accounts /today/: testing whether a given \( E \)
   encrypts a known \( M_i \) is a plaintext-checking attack, i.e. deciding
   whether \( (G,\ pk,\ R,\ C - M_i) \) is a DDH tuple, infeasible under
   DDH (this is the load-bearing observation behind the Identity doc's
   privacy-boundary table and the paper's /Issuer-blindness/ theorem).  But
   a future discrete-log break -- in particular a cryptographically
   relevant quantum computer running Shor against \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) --
   collapses that DDH decision into a cheap test, retroactively
   de-anonymising /all past/ identity and A2-Note traffic from the
   harvested \( \{M_i\} \) plus the archived chain.  The presentation's
   hiding is unaffected, but it does not hide the
   \( M \leftrightarrow E_{\text{addr}} \) relation; only the ElGamal
   layer does, and only computationally.

   Two points are worth recording for the threat model:

   1. *Revocation does not bound it.*  Clearing a holder from its registry's
      subtree ends its liveness, but the harvest target here is the
      persistent \( M \leftrightarrow E_{\text{addr}} \) link, which a quantum
      DLog break recovers wholesale, revoked or not.

   2. *The migration is encryption-layer, not pairing-layer.*  The
      BLS12-381 path (EIP-2537, "alt_bn128 Security Horizon" in the
      Identity doc) hardens /pairing/ security for PS verification; it does
      nothing for the ElGamal confidentiality that DDH underwrites.
      Post-quantum unlinkability requires replacing the \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)
      ElGamal credential with a post-quantum encryption (a lattice-based
      KEM/commitment, with a matching re-encryption-equality argument to
      preserve the =approve= handshake) -- a substantial identity-layer
      redesign, and the right place to invest if the threat model includes
      a quantum adversary with archival access to the issuer's records.

   This is a property of /every/ DDH/DLog-based confidential-payment system,
   not of Alberta Buck specifically; it is recorded here because the
   issuer-breach corpus makes the harvest concrete and names the one party
   whose compromise hands an adversary the full plaintext set
   \( \{M_i\} \).
