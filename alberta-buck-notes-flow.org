* Abstract

This document is the /narrative/ companion to [[file:alberta-buck-notes.org][alberta-buck-notes.org]] (architectural
overview), [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][alberta-buck-proofs.org]] (formal correctness), and [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][alberta-buck-ethereum.org]]
(Solidity surface).  It walks one concrete example -- Alice mints a 1,200-BUCK transfer
into six notes of denominations =[1000, 100, 25, 25, 25, 25]= -- end-to-end through the
Phase-6 Groth16-verified mint pipeline, and then walks the /inverse/ flow: Bob redeems
his 1,000-BUCK note via the Phase-7 spend circuit, receives 1,000 BUCK from the pool,
and leaves no chain-observable link back to =cm_0= (the specific commitment he opened).
Every actor, every artifact, and every check is named.  Where the example bumps up
against an open question (Phase-7 spend circuit not yet in code, mint-circuit range
bounds), the gap is flagged inline rather than papered over.

The aim is to show, by walking the path, that the system delivers five concrete
guarantees:

1. *Atomic conservation* -- if the transaction succeeds, the BUCK balance leaves Alice
   exactly once and matches the public total of the new notes.  If the proof fails, no
   BUCK moves and no commitments are appended.
2. *Issuer-bound, recipient-blinded artifacts* -- the public commitments name the issuer
   (=msg.sender=) but reveal nothing about per-note values or recipients.
3. *Per-note disclosure to recipients only* -- each recipient receives an /opening/
   off-chain that lets them reconstruct exactly their own note's value and the issuer's
   verifiable identity, plus the fact that N siblings exist whose hidden values sum to
   =totalFace= -- and nothing more.
4. *Offline receipt reconstruction* -- given only the mint transaction ID, anyone holding
   an opening can independently verify the note against the chain, with no help from
   Alice or any indexer.
5. *Adversarial-prover soundness* -- even if Alice runs a maliciously patched EVM and an
   arbitrarily hostile witness generator, the honest validators executing the deployed
   =Notes.sol= and =MintGroth16Verifier.sol= reject every batch that does not satisfy
   the mint relation.  The on-chain truth is what governs.

The remaining caveats (a known field-overflow gap on =v[i]= and the deferred spend
circuit) are addressed at the end and cross-referenced into the proofs and
implementation docs.

* Cast of Characters

The example uses concrete names so the message flow is easy to track:

- *Alice* -- the issuer.  She holds 1,200 BUCK and wants to break it into six notes.  She
  is registered with the [[file:alberta-buck-identity.org][IdentityRegistry]] so that her =msg.sender= address resolves to a
  cryptographically attested identity.
- *Bob, Carol, Dave, Eve, Frank, Grace* -- six recipients.  Bob will get the 1,000-BUCK
  note; Carol the 100-BUCK note; Dave/Eve/Frank/Grace one 25-BUCK note each.  Recipients
  do /not/ need to be on-chain known to Alice -- the recipient identity binding is a
  field inside the commitment, not an on-chain registration step at mint.
- *The honest EVM* -- every Ethereum validator running stock client software.  This is
  the only EVM whose verdict matters.  It executes the deployed =Notes.sol=,
  =MintGroth16Verifier.sol=, =MintVerifierAdapter.sol=, and =Buck.sol= bytecode.
- *Alice's hostile EVM (Eve-EVM)* -- a fictional entity Alice can run locally.  She may
  patch any contract logic she wants, run any witness generator, and produce any proof
  bytes.  The point of the design is that nothing she runs locally affects what the
  honest EVM accepts.
- *A passive observer (Mallory-the-watcher)* -- someone scanning the public chain after
  the fact, trying to learn /which/ note went to /whom/ for /how much/.  Mallory has
  full chain history and unlimited compute.

* The Promise

By the end of the mint transaction Alice will have:

- A single Ethereum transaction =T_mint= on-chain whose effects are:
  - 1,200 BUCK debited from Alice and credited to the =Notes= pool address.
  - Six opaque =uint256= commitments =[cm_0..cm_5]= appended to =Notes.commitments[]=.
  - One =Minted(issuer=Alice, totalFace=1200, startIndex=K, count=6)= event.
  - One =Transfer(Alice, Notes, 1200)= ERC-20 event.
  - One Groth16 proof in calldata, accepted by the on-chain verifier.

- Six off-chain artifacts, one per recipient, each containing the /opening/ of exactly
  one commitment plus the mint-transaction reference.

The key invariant: looking at the chain alone, Mallory sees that Alice escrowed 1,200
BUCK and posted six hashes.  She cannot tell which hash hides which value, who any
recipient is, or even whether two of the recipients are the same person.  Looking at
his off-chain artifact, Bob sees his 1,000 BUCK is real, was minted by Alice in =T_mint=,
and that five other notes exist whose hidden values sum to 200 BUCK -- but he does not
learn what those values are or who holds them.

* What's Inside a Note (Conceptually)

A "note" is just a five-tuple known by Alice and the recipient:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
opening_i = ( flavor_i, v_i, rho_i, idHash_i, predicate_i )
cm_i      = Poseidon5( flavor_i, v_i, rho_i, idHash_i, predicate_i )
#+END_SRC

- =flavor_i= -- which note variant (A1 identified cheque, A2 private cheque, B1 bearer
  cheque); see [[file:alberta-buck-notes.org][Notes]] for the menu.
- =v_i= -- the face value in BUCK base units (18-decimal scaled integer).
- =rho_i= -- a fresh, uniformly random nonce.  This is the source of unlinkability:
  swapping =rho_i= changes =cm_i= to a uniformly fresh hash even if every other field
  is identical.
- =idHash_i= -- a Poseidon-hashed handle on the recipient's identity (e.g. a
  Poseidon-hash of the recipient's registered ElGamal credential, or zero for a pure
  bearer note).
- =predicate_i= -- a domain-separating tag for any optional spending condition (escrow,
  time-lock, etc.).  Zero in the simplest case.

The Poseidon-5 hash gives the commitment two cryptographic properties that make the
whole flow work:

- *Hiding*.  Without =rho_i=, the commitment leaks no information about the other
  fields.  Mallory sees =cm_i= and gains nothing about =v_i=, =idHash_i=, or
  =flavor_i=.
- *Binding*.  No prover, hostile or honest, can find two distinct openings hashing to
  the same =cm_i= (collision-resistance of Poseidon on BN254, modelled as a random
  oracle in [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][alberta-buck-proofs.org]] Part IV).

The on-chain contract stores only =cm_i=.  Openings live exclusively off-chain.

* The Mint, Step by Step

** Step 0: Pre-flight (Off-Chain, In Alice's Wallet)

Alice's wallet has done all the slow work before any transaction is broadcast:

- She owns 1,200 BUCK on-chain.
- She has decided on the denomination split: =[1000, 100, 25, 25, 25, 25]=.  N=6, but
  the Phase-6 circuit is pinned at =N=2= (see [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][Phase 6]]); for the example we proceed
  /as if/ a future =Mint(6)= circuit were already in place.  The structure of every
  step is identical -- only the circuit constant changes.
- She has chosen an =idHash_i= for each recipient (typically a Poseidon hash of the
  recipient's IdentityRegistry-bound ElGamal credential, derived once at out-of-band
  introduction time).
- Her CSPRNG produces six fresh =rho_i= nonces.

She now has six opening tuples in memory.

** Step 1: Build Commitments

For =i = 0..5= her wallet computes

#+BEGIN_SRC text
cm_i = Poseidon5( flavor_i, v_i, rho_i, idHash_i, predicate_i )
#+END_SRC

This is pure off-chain Poseidon arithmetic in =alberta_buck.wallet.notes=.  Six
=uint256= field elements drop out.  Their order in the eventual on-chain array is fixed
by Alice's local list -- the contract preserves whatever order she submits.

** Step 2: Generate the Groth16 Proof

Alice's wallet runs =scripts/snark/prove_mint.js= (or the production equivalent).  The
witness consists of:

- Public inputs: =totalFace = 1200e18=, =cm = [cm_0, cm_1, cm_2, cm_3, cm_4, cm_5]=.
- Private witnesses: =flavor[i], v[i], rho[i], idHash[i], predicate[i]= for each =i=.

The =mint.circom= circuit checks two relations across the witness:

1. *Per-note opening.*  For each =i=, ~cm[i] === Poseidon5(flavor[i], v[i], rho[i], idHash[i], predicate[i])~.
   Six Poseidon evaluations, six equality constraints.
2. *Sum conservation.*  ~sum(v[0]..v[5]) === totalFace~.  One linear constraint.

The witness is processed by snarkjs's Groth16 prover.  A few hundred milliseconds later
the wallet has a fixed-size proof object: =(pi_A: G1, pi_B: G2, pi_C: G1)=.  Encoded as
ABI-static =(uint256[2], uint256[2][2], uint256[2])= it is exactly 256 bytes.

*Observation.*  The Groth16 proof reveals /nothing/ about the witness.  Soundness
guarantees that no =proofBytes= can satisfy the verification equation unless a witness
exists; zero-knowledge guarantees that the verification equation reveals nothing about
which witness was used.  Mallory, even with the proof in hand, cannot back out
=v[i]=, =rho[i]=, =idHash[i]=, =flavor[i]=, or =predicate[i]=.

** Step 3: Submit =Notes.mint= On Chain

Alice now broadcasts a single transaction =T_mint= calling

#+BEGIN_SRC solidity
notes.mint(proofBytes, [cm_0..cm_5], 1200e18);
#+END_SRC

The honest EVM walks =Notes.mint=:

#+BEGIN_SRC solidity
require(cms.length > 0, "Notes: empty mint");
require(mintVerifier.verifyMint(proof, totalFace, cms, msg.sender),
        "Notes: bad mint proof");
require(buck.transferFrom(msg.sender, address(this), totalFace),
        "Notes: transfer failed");
// ... append cms[i] to commitments[], assert no duplicates,
//     emit Minted(...).
#+END_SRC

Three things happen in strict order, and *all of them are atomic* (one transaction, one
EVM state transition):

a. The =MintVerifierAdapter= unpacks the 256-byte =proofBytes= into Groth16 form,
   builds the public-signal vector =[totalFace, cm_0..cm_5]=, and invokes the
   =MintGroth16Verifier=.  If the verifier returns false, the entire transaction
   reverts -- nothing is written, no BUCK moves.
b. The BUCK ERC-20's =transferFrom= debits =totalFace= from Alice and credits the
   =Notes= pool.  This is a normal allowance-driven pull, gated by Alice's prior
   =approve(notes, 1200e18, ...)=.  Because the =Notes= pool is registered as
   /system-public/ in the IdentityRegistry, the bilateral identity check on the
   transfer leg succeeds without a Chaum-Pedersen receipt.
c. For each =cm_i=: the contract checks =!commitmentExists[cm_i]=, marks it true,
   appends to =commitments[]=, emits =Appended(cm_i, startIndex+i)=.  Finally a single
   =Minted(Alice, 1200e18, startIndex, 6)= event closes the call.

If any of (a), (b), or (c) fails -- bad proof, insufficient BUCK, missing approval,
duplicate commitment -- the entire transaction reverts and the chain state is
indistinguishable from no submission at all.  *Alice gets one shot per transaction*; she
cannot retry a partial state.

** Step 4: Off-Chain Note Delivery

After =T_mint= confirms, Alice transmits each opening to its intended recipient by any
out-of-band channel: an encrypted message, a printed QR code, a USB stick.  The payload
to recipient =i= is:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
note_artifact_i = {
  txHash:    T_mint,                # Ethereum transaction ID
  index:     startIndex + i,        # position in Notes.commitments[]
  cm:        cm_i,                  # for cross-checking against the chain
  opening:   ( flavor_i, v_i, rho_i, idHash_i, predicate_i ),
  meta:      { issuer: Alice's IdentityRegistry handle, ... },
}
#+END_SRC

The chain itself never sees =opening=.  =cm_i= is already on-chain; the artifact
includes it only as a convenience so the recipient does not have to recompute it
before locating his entry in =commitments[]=.

** Step 5: Recipient Verification (Offline, Independent)

Bob receives his artifact for the 1,000-BUCK note.  Without trusting Alice and without
any help from an indexer, he runs:

1. *Recompute and check.*  Bob computes
   ~cm_check = Poseidon5(flavor, 1000e18, rho, idHash, predicate)~ and asserts
   ~cm_check == cm~.  If this fails, Alice lied about the opening; Bob discards the
   artifact and demands a real one.  (Binding of Poseidon makes substitution
   impossible.)
2. *Look up the mint transaction.*  Bob reads =T_mint= on-chain, parses the call data
   into =(proofBytes, cms[], totalFace, msg.sender)=.
3. *Confirm =msg.sender= is Alice.*  Cross-check =msg.sender= against the
   IdentityRegistry: it must resolve to Alice's PS-credentialed identity.  If Bob
   wanted an A1 identified cheque, the issuer field of his opening must match.
4. *Confirm membership.*  Read =Notes.commitments(index)= via an =eth_call= and assert
   it equals =cm=.  Bob now has chain-anchored evidence that =cm= is officially a
   commitment in the pool.
5. *Confirm batch totals.*  Read =totalFace= from the call data.  The chain's
   acceptance of =T_mint= is itself a proof that the Groth16 verifier accepted -- which
   means ~sum(v[0..5]) === totalFace~ holds in the witness.  Bob does not need to
   re-verify the proof; the EVM already did.  But if he wants belt-and-suspenders, he
   can re-run =MintGroth16Verifier.verify(proofBytes, [totalFace, cms...])= locally
   against the same on-chain bytecode, and get the same accept.

After (1)-(5) Bob knows:

- His note encodes exactly 1,000 BUCK.  (Step 1.)
- It was minted in =T_mint= by Alice.  (Steps 2, 3.)
- It is one of exactly six commitments produced by that mint, the others being
  =cms[0..5] \ cm=.  (Step 4.)
- The hidden values of the other five sum to exactly =1200 - 1000 = 200= BUCK.  (Step 5
  + the per-note opening relation: he knows /his/ value, the chain attests to the
  /sum/.)
- He learns nothing else about the other notes -- not their individual values, not
  their recipients, not their flavors.  Each =cm_j= is hiding under a fresh =rho_j= he
  does not have.

This is the unlinkability promise made concrete: each recipient can pin down their own
slice and the batch total, without learning anything about siblings.

* Why Alice Cannot Cheat (Even With a Hostile EVM and Prover)

Alice runs whatever software she wants on her laptop.  She can fork the snarkjs
witness generator, patch the circom compiler, run Anvil with a malicious
=MintGroth16Verifier= that always accepts.  None of it matters, because the truth on
the public chain is determined by what /the honest validators/ run.  Three pillars
prop up the soundness story:

** Pillar 1: The On-Chain Verifier Is Fixed Code

=MintGroth16Verifier.sol= was emitted from the Groth16 verification key produced by
the trusted setup ([[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][Phase 9]] for production; dev-only entropy in =scripts/snark/setup.sh=
today).  Its bytecode is deployed once and immutable.  Validators run whatever bytecode
sits at that address; Alice cannot tamper with it without consensus-breaking the chain.

Knowledge soundness of Groth16 means: for every =proofBytes= the verifier accepts on
public inputs =[totalFace, cm_0..cm_N-1]=, there exists a witness
=(flavor[i], v[i], rho[i], idHash[i], predicate[i])_i= such that:

- =cm[i] = Poseidon5(flavor[i], v[i], rho[i], idHash[i], predicate[i])= for all =i=
- =sum(v[i]) = totalFace=

Alice is free to /find/ such a witness any way she likes -- she's the prover.  But she
cannot find a witness for a /false/ statement; that's what soundness prohibits.

** Pillar 2: BUCK Escrow Is Inside the Same Atomic Call

The proof check, the BUCK pull, and the commitment append all live in the same
=Notes.mint= function.  An EVM transaction is all-or-nothing: the only two outcomes are
"the entire call's state changes commit" or "the entire call's state changes revert".

This means Alice cannot, for instance:

- Verify a proof, then refuse to pay -- the verifier acceptance and the
  =transferFrom= live in the same call, gated by the same =require=.
- Pay 1,200 BUCK but then get away with appending only one =cm= and pocketing the
  delta -- the loop appending commitments runs after =transferFrom=, in the same call,
  and a revert at any point unwinds the transfer too.
- Trick the contract into appending a duplicate =cm= -- =commitmentExists[cm]= is
  checked per item.

** Pillar 3: Public Inputs Are Sealed Into the Proof

The Groth16 verifier consumes the public-signal vector
=[totalFace, cm_0, .., cm_N-1]=.  The proof was generated against the specific values
Alice committed to before submission.  If Alice tries to submit the proof with a
different =totalFace= or with the =cms= permuted, the verifier rejects.

This is the bedrock of the test suite in =test/MintVerifier.t.sol= -- four explicit
tamper paths (=totalFace + 1=, =cm[0] ^ 1=, wrong batch size, malformed proof bytes)
each revert with =Notes: bad mint proof= or an ABI decode error.  Soundness is not a
proof of /absence/ of attack, but every documented attack surface is closed by a test
that triggers a verifier reject.

** Putting the Pillars Together: The Adversarial Walk

Suppose hostile-Alice tries to mint two *contradictory* batches that both claim to be
the canonical breakdown of her =T_mint= transfer.  She would need:

- Two different =proofBytes= and/or =cms[]=, both passing the on-chain verifier.

That requires either:

- Two valid Groth16 witnesses for the same public inputs (allowed -- but they yield
  the same =cms=, so they are not /contradictory/), or
- Two valid Groth16 witnesses with /different/ public inputs.  Allowed too, but each
  one demands its own =transferFrom= for its own =totalFace= -- *that* is the
  duplicate-spend problem ERC-20 solves.  Alice cannot transfer the same 1,200 BUCK
  twice without holding 2,400 BUCK to start with.

So a single 1,200-BUCK escrow yields exactly one accepted batch.  The only freedom
Alice has is /which/ batch she lays down -- which denominations and which recipients --
and that freedom is hers to keep, by design.

What about hostile-Alice trying to mint /more/ commitment value than she escrowed?
She would need a witness whose =sum(v[i])= equals the integer =totalFace= /that the
EVM sees/, but whose individual =v[i]= values /actually/ sum to more.  This is
[[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Open Question 1 in the proofs document]]: in =F_r= with =r ~ 2^254= and no =Num2Bits=
range check on =v[i]=, the constraint ~sum(v[i]) === totalFace~ is satisfiable by
witnesses whose integer sum differs from =totalFace= by a multiple of =r=.  Currently
this attack is /dormant/ because Phase 2 has no spend path -- Alice would have to
escrow =totalFace= up front and then never be able to release more than =totalFace=
back, since no spend mechanism exists.  /This dormant gap closes the moment Phase 7
ships/, and the fix (=Num2Bits(128)= per =v[i]=) is tracked as a Phase-7 prerequisite
in [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][alberta-buck-ethereum.org]].

* Why Outside Observers Cannot Link

Mallory has full read access to the chain after =T_mint= confirms.  What does she see?

#+BEGIN_SRC text
T_mint:
  from:  Alice (a registered IdentityRegistry account)
  to:    Notes (a system-public contract)
  value: 0 (BUCK is ERC-20, not ETH)
  data:  Notes.mint(proofBytes, [cm_0..cm_5], 1200e18)

events emitted (in order):
  Buck.Transfer(Alice, Notes, 1200e18)
  Notes.Appended(cm_0, K+0)
  ...
  Notes.Appended(cm_5, K+5)
  Notes.Minted(Alice, 1200e18, K, 6)
#+END_SRC

What can Mallory infer?

- That Alice (publicly) escrowed 1,200 BUCK.  *Yes* -- her address is =msg.sender=.
- That six new commitments entered the pool.  *Yes* -- she sees the indices.
- /Which/ commitment is worth /how much/.  *No* -- each is a fresh =Poseidon5= output
  hidden by an independent =rho_i=.  Without a =rho_i=, distinguishing the 1,000-BUCK
  hash from the 25-BUCK hash is computationally equivalent to inverting Poseidon on a
  uniform field element, modelled here as a random oracle.
- /Who/ holds each note.  *No* -- =idHash_i= is also inside the hash.
- Whether two commitments share a recipient.  *No* -- two notes addressed to the same
  =idHash= but with distinct =rho_i= produce uniformly independent =cm_i=, so the
  chain shows nothing distinguishable from independent draws.

What about /linking back to a specific note artifact/ that Bob later possesses?  Mallory
sees =cm_0..cm_5= published by Alice in =T_mint=.  Bob's artifact references some
=cm_i= and =T_mint=, but the artifact lives off-chain.  Mallory cannot get the artifact
unless Bob (or Alice) shows it to her, and the chain alone gives her no way to single
out =cm_i= as "the one Bob holds" against the other five.

The remaining linkability surface is the issuer.  =msg.sender= is publicly Alice, so
every commitment in the batch carries a public *issuer* tag.  This is intentional and
desired: a recipient must be able to verify who minted his note.  The privacy goal is
unlinkability of recipients and amounts, not of issuers.

** Spend-Time Unlinkability (Phase 7 Forward Reference)

When the spend circuit ships ([[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][Phase 7]]), Bob will be able to deposit his note without
revealing /which/ =cm= in the on-chain set he opened.  The spend SNARK proves
"I know an opening to /some/ commitment in the published Merkle tree that hashes to
this nullifier", with =cm= as a private witness rather than a public input.

That is when the unlinkability of /spend events/ to /mint events/ becomes a true
chain-observable property.  Today (Phase 6) Bob still has to point at a specific =cm=
when redeeming -- because no redemption path exists yet at all.  The full walkthrough
of the Phase-7 redemption flow is in *The Redemption (Spend), Step by Step* below.

* Atomicity, No Double-Mint, No Replay

Three properties together rule out duplicated or partial mints:

** Atomicity

Already covered above: =Notes.mint= performs proof verification, BUCK pull, and
commitment appends in one EVM call.  Either all happen or none.

** No Double-Mint From One Transfer

The transfer and the commitments are bound in the same call: there is no
"pre-deposit, then mint" or "mint, then deposit later" flow that an attacker could
desynchronize.  Each =Notes.mint= invocation does its own =transferFrom=; each
=transferFrom= debits Alice's BUCK balance once.  Two minting transactions require two
=transferFrom= debits, which require Alice to actually hold 2 * =totalFace= BUCK.

** No Replay

A literal replay -- broadcasting the exact same =T_mint= calldata in a new transaction
-- fails for two independent reasons:

a. *Commitment uniqueness.*  =commitmentExists[cm_i]= is set true on the first append.
   The second submission would revert at the first =require(!commitmentExists[cm_i])=
   check.  (Replay-of-cm with a different =rho= is structurally different and is
   simply a fresh mint, not a replay.)
b. *Allowance and balance.*  Even if the prior call's state were somehow reverted,
   the second mint would re-debit Alice's BUCK and re-consume her allowance to the
   =Notes= pool.

** Cross-Chain or Cross-Deployment Replay

The =MintGroth16Verifier= verification key is fixed by the trusted-setup ceremony for
that deployment.  A proof generated against the dev-key in
=scripts/snark/setup.sh= will not verify against a production-key contract on
mainnet, and vice versa.  Public inputs are also sealed into the proof: a proof valid
on Sepolia for =totalFace=1200= cannot be reused on mainnet because the on-chain
verifier address (and key) differ.

The only cross-context vulnerability would be a /chain-id collision/ on identical
verification keys, which the production setup ceremony precludes by construction.

* Receipt Construction From the Transaction ID Alone

A practical question: months later, Bob wants to prove to Carol that his note was
legitimately issued.  He needs no help from Alice and no live indexer:

1. Bob fetches =T_mint= from any Ethereum archive node.
2. He decodes the call data into =(proofBytes, cms, totalFace)= using the standard
   =Notes.mint= ABI.
3. He notes =msg.sender= and resolves it to Alice's identity via the
   IdentityRegistry on the same chain (a single =eth_call=).
4. He locates his =cm= in =cms[]= at the index recorded in his artifact and asserts
   equality.
5. He recomputes ~Poseidon5(flavor, v, rho, idHash, predicate) == cm~ from the opening.
6. (Optional) He re-runs =MintGroth16Verifier.verify(proofBytes, [totalFace, cms...])=
   locally to convince himself the EVM did its job.

The "receipt" Carol receives is therefore =(T_mint, opening, idHash mapping)=.  Its
validity is independently checkable against the public chain.  Nothing here requires
ongoing cooperation from Alice -- which is exactly the property a receipt should have.

* The Redemption (Spend), Step by Step (Phase 7 Design)

This section walks the /inverse/ of the mint.  Bob holds the opening for the 1,000-BUCK
note =cm_0=; he wants to redeem it for 1,000 BUCK credited to his on-chain account, and
he wants no public observer to be able to tell /which/ commitment in the pool he
consumed.  The section describes Phase-7 design intent -- the spend circuit, the
on-chain =Notes.spend= entry point, and the Merkle-root machinery it depends on are
not yet in the code tree.  Everything here follows directly from Theorems 8-10 of
[[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][alberta-buck-proofs.org]] and the spend-circuit sketches in
[[file:alberta-buck-notes.org][alberta-buck-notes.org]] §§2270-2340.

Bob's note is of flavor /A1/ (addressed, identified cheque).  The A-spend circuit is
described in detail; the B-spend (bearer cheque) collapses to a simpler nullifier rule
and is covered as a one-paragraph delta at the end.

Two properties of the flow are worth stating up front, because they shape every step:

- *The anonymity set is the full matching-flavor pool, not only same-denomination.*
  The spend proof takes =cm= as a /private/ witness under a public =noteRoot= -- the
  chain-observable anonymity set is every unspent commitment of the matching flavor
  in the tree.  The *face value is a public output*, because the BUCK contract must
  know how much to pay out; amount-level linkability narrows the effective set to
  commitments whose hidden =v= could equal the revealed =face=.  Same-denomination
  issuance is therefore the strongest-privacy regime, but the underlying
  cryptographic hiding is over the whole tree, not over a denomination class.
- *The BUCK ERC-20 does not verify the SNARK itself.*  The authorization chain is:
  valid SNARK + fresh nullifier + root match \(\to\) =Notes.spend= proceeds \(\to\)
  =Notes.spend= invokes a BUCK transfer out of the pool.  The proof is a capability
  token for entry into =Notes.spend=; the resulting ERC-20 movement is a routine
  pool-to-recipient call gated by that entry.  Since the =Notes= pool is a system-
  public contract in the [[file:alberta-buck-identity.org][IdentityRegistry]], the pool's outbound transfer does not
  itself require a Chaum-Pedersen receipt -- the recipient's own registration (for
  A-spends) or a Schnorr signature over =(chainid, nullifier, recipient)= (for
  B-spends) is what binds the payout destination to the proof.

** Step 0: Bob Holds a Verified Note

From the mint walk (*Step 5: Recipient Verification*) Bob already has:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
note_artifact_0 = {
  txHash:    T_mint,
  index:     K + 0,
  cm:        cm_0,
  opening:   ( flavor_A1, 1000e18, rho_0, idHash_bob, predicate_0 ),
  meta:      { issuer: Alice, ... },
}
#+END_SRC

He has independently confirmed that =cm_0= is in =Notes.commitments[]= at index =K=
and that =Poseidon5(opening)= reproduces it.  The opening has been sitting in his
wallet for weeks, months, or years -- addressed A-notes are cryptographically safe for
indefinite holding ([[file:alberta-buck-notes.org][Notes]]).

** Step 1: Fetch the Commitment Tree and Build a Merkle Path

The Phase-7 =Notes= contract maintains an incremental Poseidon Merkle tree of depth
~20, whose current root is exposed as =noteRoot()=.  Two things happen:

a. Bob (or, more practically, any light-node helper running off-chain) fetches the
   =Appended(cm, index)= event log from genesis to the current block, reconstructs the
   tree, and computes his Merkle path for =cm_0= -- a sequence of sibling hashes up to
   the root.  The path is purely public information; everyone can reproduce it, and
   Bob does not have to trust any indexer because the event log is canonical.
b. Bob reads the current =noteRoot()= via =eth_call= and confirms it matches the root
   he reconstructed.  Any divergence means the local event log is out of sync; in no
   case does it weaken privacy, only liveness.

Phase 7 also stores a short window of /recent/ historical roots on-chain (e.g. the
last 128 blocks of root updates), so that a spend transaction assembled against a
slightly-stale root is not forced to re-prove against every new mint between
assembly and inclusion.  This is standard zk-rollup hygiene (Zcash, Tornado Cash);
the concrete window size is a deployment parameter.

** Step 2: Compute the Nullifier

The A-spend nullifier is

\[
  nf \;=\; \mathrm{Poseidon}_{\mathrm{nf}}\!\bigl(\mathrm{flavor}_A \;\|\; M_{\mathrm{rec}} \;\|\; \rho_0\bigr),
\]

where \( M_{\mathrm{rec}} = H(\mathrm{identity}_{\mathrm{bob}}) \cdot G \) is the
identity point Bob's wallet recovers by decrypting the opening's credential payload
under his registered secret key \( sk_{\mathrm{bob}} \).  Three properties follow:

- *Deterministic.*  The same =(flavor, M_rec, rho)= always gives the same =nf=.  So
  Bob can only spend =cm_0= once; a second attempt re-derives the same =nf= and is
  rejected by the nullifier-set check.
- *Unforgeable without the opening.*  An attacker who sees =cm_0= and the tree cannot
  compute =nf= without =rho_0= and =M_rec=, both of which live inside the opening.
- *Re-issuance-safe.*  If Bob's ElGamal key pair is rotated (new =sk_bob'=), his
  registered credential re-derives the /same/ =M_rec= on a canonical identity, so
  re-issuance does not create a second redemption path for the same note.  This is
  Theorem 10.1 in [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][the proofs]].

The nullifier is the chain-observable anchor of the spend.  Publishing it is what
marks =cm_0= as consumed from the tree's /semantic/ point of view, even though the
tree itself remains append-only and nothing is deleted.

** Step 3: Generate the Spend Proof

Bob's wallet runs =scripts/snark/prove_spend.js= (the Phase-7 analogue of
=prove_mint.js=).  The spend circuit's signal layout is:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
public  noteRoot;         # the canonical root Bob read in Step 1
public  nullifier;        # the nf computed in Step 2
public  face;             # 1000e18 -- the BUCK to release
public  recipient;        # Bob's on-chain address (the payout destination)
public  chainid;          # replay-domain separation

private cm;               # cm_0 -- the specific commitment being opened
private flavor, v, rho,
        idHash, predicate;  # the opening
private merkle_siblings[]; # depth-20 path
private merkle_indices[];  # left/right flags per level
private sk_dep;           # Bob's ElGamal secret key (A-spend only)
private E_note;           # the ElGamal credential baked into cm (A-spend only)
#+END_SRC

The A-spend circuit enforces five relations simultaneously:

1. *Opening binds.*  =cm === Poseidon5(flavor, v, rho, idHash, predicate)=.
2. *Merkle membership.*  Walking =merkle_siblings= with =merkle_indices= from =cm=
   reaches =noteRoot=.
3. *Nullifier derivation.*  =nullifier === Poseidon_nf(flavor || M_rec || rho)=,
   where =M_rec= is recovered in-circuit from =E_note= and =sk_dep= (Chaum-Pedersen
   decryption lifted into the circuit).
4. *Depositor is the rightful recipient.*  A CP-equality constraint proves that
   =M_rec= recovered from =E_note= under =sk_dep= equals the =M_reg= that Bob's
   IdentityRegistry credential registers on chain.  (Theorem 8 reduces the soundness
   of this constraint to Chaum-Pedersen + knowledge-soundness of the SNARK.)
5. *Value release matches.*  =face === v=.  Range-bounded by the same =Num2Bits(128)=
   check that closes Open Question 1 in the proofs.

Critically, =cm= is a *private* witness.  The proof convinces the verifier that
/some/ =cm= in the tree satisfies (1)-(5); it does not reveal /which/.

Bob's wallet serializes the Groth16 proof into the standard
=(uint256[2], uint256[2][2], uint256[2])= ABI-static tuple (256 bytes).

** Step 4: Submit =Notes.spend= On Chain

Bob now broadcasts the spend transaction =T_spend=:

#+BEGIN_SRC solidity
notes.spend(proofBytes, noteRoot, nullifier, 1000e18, bob);
#+END_SRC

The honest EVM walks =Notes.spend= (design intent; Phase 7 pending):

#+BEGIN_SRC solidity
require(isAcceptedRoot(noteRoot),        "Notes: stale root");
require(!nullifiers[nullifier],          "Notes: double spend");
require(spendVerifier.verifySpend(
    proof, noteRoot, nullifier, face, recipient, block.chainid
),                                       "Notes: bad spend proof");

nullifiers[nullifier] = true;            // mark spent before paying out
noteFaceSum          -= face;

require(buck.transfer(recipient, face),  "Notes: payout failed");
emit Spent(nullifier, face, recipient);
#+END_SRC

Four invariants are enforced in order:

a. =noteRoot= is current (or within the recent-roots retention window).  A root from
   outside that window is stale -- Bob must rebuild the proof against a fresher root.
b. =nullifier= has never been published before.  This is the double-spend check;
   Theorem 10.5 in [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][the proofs]] says it is sufficient.
c. The SNARK itself is valid against the =SpendGroth16Verifier=.  By soundness, a
   witness satisfying (1)-(5) exists.
d. Only after (a)-(c) pass does the contract mark the nullifier and invoke
   =buck.transfer(bob, 1000e18)= from its own pool balance.  Because the =Notes= pool
   is /system-public/, the outbound ERC-20 transfer skips the Chaum-Pedersen leg; the
   bilateral identity check degenerates to "recipient is a registered account or
   flavor is bearer".

If any of (a)-(d) reverts, the whole transaction reverts: no BUCK moves, the nullifier
bit is never set, the noteFaceSum is unchanged.  *Bob gets one attempt per root
window*; a failed spend can be retried with a refreshed proof, but never in a way
that consumes a nullifier without paying out the BUCK.

** Step 5: BUCK ERC-20 Pays Out

The =buck.transfer= call inside =Notes.spend= is a routine ERC-20 movement from the
pool's address to Bob's.  Nothing in the ERC-20 contract knows or cares that the
transfer originated from a SNARK-authorized redemption -- as far as =Buck.sol= is
concerned, the =Notes= pool is just another account making a transfer to a registered
recipient, satisfying the bilateral identity predicate because the pool is
system-public.

The result on-chain (ignoring demurrage for a moment -- see the next paragraph):

- =Buck.balanceOf(Notes)= decreases by 1,000 BUCK (plus the carried demurrage).
- =Buck.balanceOf(Bob)= increases by 1,000 BUCK (plus the carried demurrage).
- =Buck.Transfer(Notes, Bob, payout)= is emitted, where =payout= is face + carry.
- =Notes.Spent(nullifier, 1000e18, Bob)= is emitted (the Note's recorded =face=).
- =Notes.nullifiers[nullifier]= becomes =true=.

*Demurrage carry.*  The BUCK transfer in (d) is a /variant/ of the standard BUCK
ERC-20 transfer.  In a standard outgoing transfer of 1,000 BUCK, the demurrage that
has accrued on the transferred slice is deducted from the source account as a
separate fee (retained / burned by the BUCK contract), and the recipient receives
1,000 fresh BUCKs whose age clock starts anew and dilutes the recipient's average
demurrage BUCK-age.  The Note spend uses a different convention: the demurrage that
would normally have been deducted as a fee is instead /transferred to the
recipient/ along with the face amount, so Bob receives =face + carried_fee=.  The
Note pool's demurrage BUCK-age is unchanged on the outgoing leg (just as in a
standard transfer out), but the fee does not get burned -- it rides along with the
payout.  A further wrinkle dilutes the carry: the pool's actual BUCK balance =B=
may exceed the sum of outstanding note face values =F= (because anyone can donate
or burn BUCK to the pool by =buck.transfer(notes, amount)=), and the over-supply
=B - F= is distributed pro-rata across redeemers, effectively reducing the
demurrage owed per face-BUCK by the ratio =F/B=.  The full arithmetic, rationale,
and a proposal for exposing this variant as a first-class BUCK ERC-20 option are
in *Demurrage-Carrying Transfers: A BUCK ERC-20 Design Proposal* below.

** What the Observer Sees at Spend (Mallory Returns)

After =T_spend= confirms, Mallory can read:

#+BEGIN_SRC text
T_spend:
  from:  Bob (a registered IdentityRegistry account)
  to:    Notes (system-public)
  data:  Notes.spend(proofBytes, noteRoot, nullifier, 1000e18, Bob)

events:
  Buck.Transfer(Notes, Bob, 1000e18)
  Notes.Spent(nullifier, 1000e18, Bob)
#+END_SRC

What she infers:

- *That a note was spent.*  Yes -- the nullifier is fresh and the pool balance dropped
  by 1,000 BUCK.
- *That the spend was for 1,000 BUCK to Bob.*  Yes -- =face= and =recipient= are
  public inputs (they must be, to tell the ERC-20 whom to pay).
- /Which commitment in the tree was consumed?/  *No* -- =cm= is a private witness to
  the SNARK.  Mallory's anonymity set is every unspent commitment of flavor =A= in
  the pool whose hidden =v= could equal 1,000 BUCK.  At deployment scale this is
  potentially the entire pool minus prior nullified entries; in a same-denomination
  regime (many 1,000-BUCK notes in circulation) it is exactly the 1,000-BUCK
  subset.
- /Which mint did the spent note come from?/  *No* -- there is no cross-reference
  to =T_mint= in =T_spend=.  The only link back to any specific mint would be the
  nullifier, but nullifiers are Poseidon-PRF outputs over secret material; inverting
  one to recover =(flavor, M_rec, rho)= is exactly the pre-image resistance we
  assume.
- /Who held the note before Bob?/  *No*.  If Alice gave the note to Carol who handed
  it to Dave who sold it to Bob, none of those transfers touched the chain.  The
  chain sees only "at mint Alice deposited, later Bob withdrew" -- the intermediate
  holders are invisible.

The residual linkability surfaces are (i) =face= (the revealed amount) and (ii)
=recipient= (the on-chain payout address).  Both are unavoidable at the SNARK/ERC-20
boundary -- the contract must know where and how much to pay.  The community-
standardized mitigation is fixed-denomination notes (so revealing =face= does not
subdivide the anonymity set) and batched, relay-mediated payouts (so revealing
=recipient= need not reveal the /true/ beneficiary).  Both are orthogonal to the
core spend circuit and can be layered on top.

** Why Alice Cannot Steal Bob's Note (Even Between Rooms)

The analogue of the mint-time adversarial walk, flipped: can /any/ actor other than
Bob -- including Alice-the-issuer, any prior holder, or a random Mallory -- redeem
=cm_0=?  No, for three independently sufficient reasons:

1. *Opening secrecy.*  The spend circuit requires =(flavor, v, rho, idHash,
   predicate)= as a private witness.  Without the opening, the circuit is
   unsatisfiable.  Alice may remember =rho_0= -- she generated it -- but she does
   /not/ have Bob's =sk_bob=, which is required to decrypt =E_note= in-circuit
   (relation 3 of the spend circuit).  She cannot produce =M_rec= and therefore
   cannot compute a valid =nullifier=.
2. *Recipient-binding in the nullifier.*  Even with the full opening, the A-spend
   nullifier is keyed on =M_rec= -- the recipient's identity point.  Any prover
   other than an actor who can prove registration under that same =M_rec= via
   CP-equality is blocked by relation 4.  This is Theorem 8 in [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][the proofs]]: only
   the registered depositor can assemble an accepting A-spend proof.
3. *Recipient-binding at the payout site.*  The spend circuit takes =recipient= as a
   public input -- so if a front-runner intercepts Bob's =proofBytes= and tries to
   retarget the payout to themselves by re-submitting with a different =recipient=
   argument, the SNARK immediately rejects (the proof was generated against Bob's
   address in the public signals, not the attacker's).  The Chaum-Pedersen binding
   additionally forces =recipient= to be an account registered to the same =M_rec=
   that keys the nullifier.

For /B-spend/ (bearer) notes, protection (2) collapses -- the nullifier is publicly
derivable from =rho= alone.  Whoever publishes the nullifier first wins the payout,
which is the civil-trust semantics of bearer cash.  Protection (3) still applies,
because the spend circuit still takes =recipient= as a public input and still
signs it into the proof (via a Schnorr PoK of =sk_dep= over
=chainid || nullifier || recipient=) so the payout destination cannot be rewritten
in-flight.

** The B-Spend One-Paragraph Delta

If Bob's note were flavor /B1/ (bearer), Steps 1 and 4 are unchanged; Steps 2-3
shrink.  The nullifier is =nf = Poseidon_nf(flavor_B || rho_0)= -- publicly
derivable from the opening, no secret key involved.  The spend circuit drops
relations 3 and 4 and replaces them with a single Schnorr PoK: the prover
demonstrates knowledge of the secret key of the /payout/ address =recipient= by
signing =(chainid, nullifier, recipient)= in-circuit.  This is cheaper (~6,000
vs ~8,000 constraints, per [[file:alberta-buck-notes.org][Notes]]) but has weaker holder-binding -- if two
parties hold copies of the same B1 opening (e.g. a paper note photographed and
forwarded), whichever submits =T_spend= first wins.  That is the intended civil-
trust semantics of a bearer cheque, not a bug.

** Recapping Property C (Only Valid Notes Redeem)

The original Property C question -- /subsequently only those N valid notes can be
deposited/ -- becomes concrete at this layer.  Claim: no actor can redeem a
commitment =cm= unless

a. =cm= was appended to =commitments[]= by a prior =Notes.mint= transaction whose
   Groth16 proof verified (i.e., =cm= is part of an honest mint batch), /and/
b. The actor holds the opening of =cm=, /and/
c. For A-spends, the actor is the depositor keyed by =M_rec= in that opening.

Proof sketch.  The spend circuit's relation 2 (Merkle membership) rejects any =cm=
not in =noteRoot=.  The mint circuit's relations 1-2 (opening binding and sum
conservation) are what places a =cm= in =noteRoot= in the first place, and by the
mint-side soundness story in *Why Alice Cannot Cheat* every such =cm= corresponds to
a well-formed opening whose =v= is included in the mint's =totalFace= escrow.  The
spend circuit's relation 5 (=face === v=) then ensures the BUCK released equals
exactly the share of the escrow that =cm= was allocated.  Summing over every
accepted spend of a given mint batch, the cumulative BUCK released equals at most
the =totalFace= escrowed -- property C follows from mint-side value conservation
plus spend-side nullifier uniqueness.

The outstanding caveat is Open Question 1 (missing =Num2Bits= range check on =v[i]=
at mint time), which is /load-bearing/ here: without range bounds on =v[i]=, the
mint-side =sum(v[i]) === totalFace= holds only modulo =r=, so an adversarial mint
could leave a =cm= whose opening has =v = totalFace + r= in the tree -- and relation
5 of the spend circuit would happily release =totalFace + r= BUCK against it.
Phase 7 /cannot ship/ until that range check lands.

* Demurrage-Carrying Transfers: A BUCK ERC-20 Design Proposal

The Note-spend payout in Step 5 invoked a /variant/ of =buck.transfer= whose
semantics differ from the standard BUCK ERC-20 transfer in one targeted respect:
the demurrage due on the transferred amount is *carried with* the transfer to the
recipient rather than *deducted from* the source account as a fee.  This section
unpacks the mechanic in detail and proposes an approach to making it a
first-class, per-account option of the BUCK ERC-20 itself -- suitable for the
Note pool and a small family of similarly-shaped "transitory" accounts.

** Standard vs. Fee-Carrying Transfer

BUCK balances accumulate demurrage over time.  The age-per-BUCK accounting lives
in [[file:alberta-buck-notes.org][alberta-buck-notes.org §Demurrage Interaction]] and the ERC-20 surface in
[[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][alberta-buck-ethereum.org §BUCK: ERC-20 Token]]; the headline property is that a
BUCK held for longer has a higher accrued demurrage fee, and that fee settles at
the next transfer.

- /Fee-deducting transfer (the default)./  Alice transfers 1,000 BUCK to Bob.
  Demurrage accrued on Alice's account for the transferred slice, computed at
  transfer time, is a separate deduction from Alice's remaining balance -- the
  BUCK contract retains it (effectively a burn against the source's balance,
  since the fee is not credited to any other account).  Bob receives 1,000 BUCK
  /fresh/: its age clock starts anew in Bob's account, diluting Bob's average
  demurrage BUCK-age.  From the ERC-20 perspective, Alice's balance drops by
  =1,000 + fee=, Bob's balance rises by =1,000=, and total supply drops by
  =fee=.
- /Fee-carrying transfer (the proposed variant)./  The source contract transfers
  a face amount of 1,000 BUCK to Bob, /plus/ the demurrage that would have been
  deducted as a fee.  Bob receives =face + carried_fee= BUCK in one movement.
  The source's balance drops by =1,000 + fee= -- identical to the deducting
  case -- but instead of the fee being burned, it is credited to the recipient.
  Total supply is preserved; the "cost" of the transfer, accounting-wise, is
  absorbed by the recipient rather than by the network.

The two variants are balance-symmetric on the source side.  The difference is
entirely on the destination side: is the fee burned or forwarded?

** Why the Note Pool Needs the Fee-Carrying Variant

The Note pool is the BUCK contract's own account.  Its balance holds the aggregate
face value of every outstanding note, plus any BUCK donated or burned into the
contract.  Three structural features make the fee-deducting default impractical:

1. *Per-note age opacity.*  When Alice mints six notes, the pool records only six
   opaque Poseidon commitments -- there is no per-note age clock that the
   redeeming Bob could query, because every per-note field except =cm= is hidden
   off-chain.  The pool cannot deduct "the demurrage /Alice's specific note/
   owes" because the chain does not distinguish Alice's fraction of the pool from
   Carol's, Dave's, and the rest.  Any per-note fee would have to be computed
   off the pool aggregate, which would force Bob to pay demurrage fees that the
   pool's other depositors owed.
2. *Face-to-payout identity.*  =face= is the public SNARK output and the value
   the mint circuit committed to.  A fee-deducting pool transfer would deliver
   =face - fee= to Bob while the proof claimed =face=.  This would break the
   mint-to-spend value-conservation invariant: the SNARK's promise would not
   match the ERC-20 ledger movement, and the spend circuit would have to become
   aware of demurrage accounting to compensate, defeating the clean separation
   between privacy (the SNARK) and settlement (the ERC-20).
3. *Transitory nature of the pool./*  The pool is not a long-term BUCK holder with
   a business reason to retain demurrage fees.  Every BUCK that enters the pool
   is earmarked for exactly one future redeemer; the pool is a bookkeeping
   buffer, not an economic actor.  Charging it fee-deducting transfers would
   skim value from the pool's balance that the pool has no natural mechanism to
   replenish -- over time, the pool would accumulate a cumulative fee deficit
   and insolvency.

The fee-carrying variant solves all three: per-note demurrage collapses to a
pool-level calculation at spend time, Bob receives exactly the face the SNARK
promised (plus a fee bonus), and the pool never bleeds value into uncredited
fees.

** Over-Supply and the Subsidy Dilution

A second, orthogonal mechanism compounds the fee-carrying behaviour.  The pool's
actual BUCK balance =B= can exceed the sum of outstanding note face values =F=
for two reasons:

- /Donated BUCK./  Anyone can =buck.transfer(notes, amount)= to gift BUCK to
  the pool.  The pool does not track donations as separate ledger entries; the
  donated BUCK becomes indistinguishable from the pool's aggregate balance.
- /Burned BUCK./  Equivalently, BUCK sent to the contract with no matching
  mint commitment is lost to the sender but retained in the pool.

The over-supply =B - F= is distributed across all outstanding note holders
pro-rata at redemption time.  When Bob redeems a 1,000-face note out of an
outstanding =F = 10,000= and pool balance =B = 10,100= BUCK, the 100-BUCK
over-supply subsidises every redemption at a rate of =(B-F)/F = 1%= of face:

\[
  \mathrm{payout\_face} \;=\; \mathrm{face} \;\cdot\; \frac{B}{F}
\]

so Bob's face-equivalent payout is 1,010 BUCK before any demurrage carry.  The
carried fee itself is then computed on the /subsidised/ face: demurrage at the
pool's aggregate age-per-BUCK, applied to =payout_face=, and diluted by the
ratio =F/B= (because the over-supply is, by construction, younger than the
face-backed fraction of the pool -- donations enter as fresh BUCK).  Net:

\[
  \mathrm{payout} \;=\; \mathrm{face} \cdot \frac{B}{F} \;+\; \mathrm{fee}(\mathrm{face}, \text{pool age}) \cdot \frac{F}{B}
\]

The chain-observer's view collapses this to a single =Transfer(Notes, Bob,
payout)= event.  The arithmetic is internal to =Notes.spend= and is identical
for every spender of the same face, keeping the anonymity set intact.

** A Proposal: Per-Account Fee-Treatment Mode

Fee-carrying transfers are useful beyond the Note pool.  A BUCK account benefits
from the variant whenever:

- The true beneficiary of the BUCK is /not/ the account-of-record but some
  downstream recipient the account forwards to (escrow, payroll, bridge, pool).
- Per-depositor age bookkeeping is either opaque (commitment-pool privacy) or
  inconvenient (batched flows, cross-chain delays).
- The account is /transitory/: it has no durable business reason to retain
  demurrage fees that its depositors would normally pay on their own.

This document proposes a per-account /fee-treatment mode/ in the BUCK ERC-20,
exposed as:

#+BEGIN_SRC solidity
enum FeeTreatment { Deducting, Carrying }

mapping(address => FeeTreatment) internal _feeMode;     // default: Deducting

event FeeModeSet(address indexed account, FeeTreatment mode);
event FeeCarried(address indexed from, address indexed to,
                 uint256 face, uint256 carriedFee);

/// @notice Opt a BUCK-derived contract into demurrage-carrying transfers.
///         Callable only by governance; callable only for addresses whose
///         bytecode exposes the BuckDerived() marker (prevents EOAs).
function setFeeMode(address account, FeeTreatment mode) external;
#+END_SRC

Transfer semantics branch on the /source/ account's mode:

- =Deducting= (default).  Standard behaviour; every existing account is in this
  mode.  A 1,000-BUCK outgoing transfer debits =1,000 + fee=, credits =1,000=,
  and burns =fee=.
- =Carrying=.  The variant.  A 1,000-BUCK outgoing transfer debits =1,000 + fee=
  from the source (same debit as Deducting), credits =1,000 + fee= to the
  recipient, and additionally emits =FeeCarried(from, to, 1000, fee)= so that
  the recipient's wallet can correctly attribute the "bonus" portion to
  demurrage carry rather than an overpayment.

Three guard conditions constrain the =Carrying= mode:

1. *Governance-only assignment.*  =setFeeMode= is callable only by the BUCK
   governance address (the same governance path that trusts issuers and upgrades
   the IdentityRegistry).  Account creation via the standard registration path
   never sets =Carrying=; an explicit post-deployment governance call is
   required.
2. *Derivation whitelist.*  The governance call requires the target address's
   bytecode to expose a =BuckDerived()= marker (a view function returning a
   sentinel constant).  EOAs, which have no bytecode, can never be in
   =Carrying= mode even by governance misconfiguration.  Externally-authored
   contracts that do not declare themselves BUCK-derived are equally ineligible.
3. *Recipient disclosure.*  The =FeeCarried= event is emitted /before/ the
   standard =Transfer= event within the same call, so any wallet that indexes
   transfers in order sees the fee-mode signal before the balance movement.
   Wallets that understand the event attribute the carry correctly; wallets
   that do not still see a strictly-correct =Transfer(from, to, amount + fee)=
   event, so total-supply and per-account balance accounting remain consistent
   -- only downstream per-account age bookkeeping needs the event to stay
   precise.

** Examples Beyond Notes

The pattern generalises cleanly:

- /Escrow pools for delivery-vs-payment./  A contract holding BUCK pending a
  condition (oracle decision, off-chain proof, cross-chain bridge settlement)
  shares the Note pool's per-depositor age opacity.  Setting the escrow to
  =Carrying= lets the eventual beneficiary receive the intended face amount
  plus accrued fee without the escrow contract running a demurrage deficit.
- /Batched payroll./  A payroll contract pulling from an employer and
  distributing to N employees, if in =Carrying= mode, delivers the gross face
  amount with demurrage carry.  Each employee absorbs their proportional share
  of the fee, rather than the employer pre-funding it out of a separate
  balance.
- /Cross-chain bridges./  A BUCK-side bridge escrow holding funds pending
  withdrawal on the other chain can carry demurrage to the withdrawer, so
  bridge latency does not eat into user-visible amounts.
- /Transitory utility accounts./  Any contract that routes BUCK between an
  inbound party and an outbound party within a bounded lifetime -- a prediction
  market escrow, a subscription-period holding vault, a donation-matching
  intermediary -- fits the pattern.  The /inconvenience of computing exact
  fees due between deposit and spending/ is the tell that =Carrying= is the
  right mode.

In every case the shared invariant is: the account-of-record is transitory, its
depositors are the true beneficiaries of the held BUCK, and the contract has no
durable reason to retain demurrage.

** Restrictions: EOAs and External Contracts Stay Deducting

Regular BUCK-holding addresses -- EOAs, externally-authored contracts, unregistered
wallets -- *cannot* be set to =Carrying= mode.  Three reasons:

1. *Consent.*  Recipients of an EOA transfer cannot be expected to interpret
   the =FeeCarried= event as a known quantity; wallets would have to prompt for
   acceptance on every ingestion, which is a UX disaster.  BUCK-derived contracts
   are programmatic, their API contracts are documented, and their recipients
   know in advance what to expect.
2. *Adversarial bookkeeping./*  A malicious EOA in =Carrying= mode could claim
   large outgoing "fees" to friendly recipients as a covert value-transfer
   channel, avoiding demurrage they owed.  The derivation whitelist closes this
   by limiting =Carrying= to bytecode that has been reviewed alongside the BUCK
   governance process.
3. *Total-supply integrity./*  The BUCK contract's global demurrage accounting
   assumes regular accounts deduct fees (burn).  If EOAs could carry, the
   aggregate demurrage burn rate would depend on per-account mode settings in
   ways that governance would have no principled way to calibrate.
   Restricting =Carrying= to a small whitelist keeps the macro-level demurrage
   model tractable.

** Open Design Questions

1. *Mode-switching with non-zero balance./*  Can a contract switch from =Deducting=
   to =Carrying= while holding BUCK?  The cleanest answer is no -- require
   =balanceOf(account) == 0= as a precondition -- to avoid discontinuities in
   age-per-BUCK accounting across the switch.  An upgrade path (drain, switch,
   refill) covers the rare legitimate use case.
2. *Interaction with the Chaum-Pedersen approve receipt./*  The fee-carrying leg
   does not change the CP-equality obligation on the sender (the source's
   identity is still bound by the receipt).  What changes is the /amount/ the
   recipient sees credited; the CP receipt commits to a face, not a
   face-plus-fee.  Acceptable as long as the receipt's =amount= field is
   interpreted as "what the source committed to pay" rather than "what the
   recipient netted".
3. *Event-ordering discipline./*  =FeeCarried= before =Transfer= keeps wallets
   compatible.  A conformance test in =test/Buck.t.sol= should assert the order,
   so a future refactor cannot silently flip it.
4. *Gas cost./*  Each =Carrying= transfer adds one SLOAD for the source's mode
   lookup (~100 gas) plus one event emission (~375 gas).  Negligible compared to
   the ~475 KB Groth16 verify in the Notes case.

This proposal is referenced from [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][alberta-buck-ethereum.org]]'s *Open Work Items*
section as a concrete Phase-7 deliverable: the Note spend cannot ship without it,
and the =Carrying= mode is the minimum-surface mechanism that makes the spend's
value-conservation invariant hold at the ERC-20 layer.

* Putting the Guarantees Side-by-Side

The mint walkthrough established five guarantees; the spend walkthrough adds four
more that become concrete only once the redemption path exists.

  #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
  | Stage | Guarantee                              | Mechanism                                                                                       |
  |-------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
  | Mint  | Atomic conservation                    | Single EVM call: proof verify, =transferFrom=, append-cms all-or-nothing                        |
  | Mint  | Issuer-bound, recipient-blinded        | =msg.sender= is public; =cm_i= is Poseidon-hidden over =(flavor, v, rho, idHash, pred)=         |
  | Mint  | Per-note disclosure (recipient only)   | Off-chain opening contains exactly one note's witness fields                                    |
  | Mint  | Offline receipt reconstruction         | =T_mint= calldata + opening + on-chain Identity lookup is sufficient and self-checkable         |
  | Mint  | Adversarial-prover soundness           | Groth16 knowledge soundness on fixed on-chain verifier; ERC-20 balance/allowance integrity      |
  | Spend | Redemption unlinkable to specific =cm= | =cm= is a /private/ witness under public =noteRoot=; only nullifier + face + recipient revealed |
  | Spend | Double-spend prevented                 | Deterministic nullifier + one-bit =nullifiers= mapping; rejected on reuse                       |
  | Spend | Recipient-bound payout                 | =recipient= is a public SNARK input; proof rejects if re-targeted in-flight                     |
  | Spend | Value conservation across mint<->spend | Range-bounded mint =v[i]= + spend =face === v=; cumulative payouts \le =totalFace= per batch    |
  #+LATEX: }

  The mint-stage rows are enforced by code already in the tree: Phase 5 (=Notes.sol=,
  =StubMintVerifier=) for the contract surface, Phase 6 (=mint.circom=,
  =MintGroth16Verifier=, =MintVerifierAdapter=) for the SNARK pipeline, the
  [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][IdentityRegistry]] for issuer attestation, and the [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][formal proofs]] (Theorems 7-11) for
  the cryptographic backstop.  The spend-stage rows are Phase-7 design intent: the
  spend circuit, the incremental Merkle root, and =Notes.spend= are not yet in the
  tree, and the mint-circuit range check (Open Question 1) is a prerequisite for the
  value-conservation row.
  
* Open Questions and Forward-Looking Caveats
  
The example assumed properties that the Phase-6 deployment does not yet fully
discharge.  Each of these is tracked in greater depth elsewhere; they are listed here
so the reader is not misled about the current state.

1. *Range bounds on =v[i]=.*  The constraint ~sum(v[i]) === totalFace~ holds in
   =F_r= and the circuit lacks per-=v= range checks.  An attacker today could craft
   a witness whose integer-sum overshoots =totalFace= by a multiple of =r= -- but
   cannot extract that surplus, because Phase 2 has no spend path.  The fix
   (=Num2Bits(128)= per =v[i]=) is a Phase-7 prerequisite.  See [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Open Question 1]] in the
   proofs and the [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][Open Work Items]] section in the implementation doc.
2. *Implicit transfer<->batch binding.*  The chain emits =Transfer= and =Minted= in
   the same transaction, but no on-chain batch identifier ties the BUCK transfer to
   the specific =cm= range.  Off-chain indexers reconstruct the binding from event
   correlation.  Whether to add an explicit =batchId= storage slot (and a matching
   public input to the spend circuit) is an open design question; see [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Open Question 2]].
3. *Spend circuit not yet built.*  Bob's note is currently not redeemable -- there is
   no =Notes.spend= function in the tree.  The Phase-7 design is walked in
   *The Redemption (Spend), Step by Step* above, and open questions on its
   integration with the mint-circuit commitment schema are in [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Open Question 3]] of the
   proofs.  Until that circuit ships, the system is "mint-only" and the
   unlinkability story is an at-rest property of the published commitments rather than
   an end-to-end chain-observable property of the spend.  See also the
   [[file:alberta-buck-ethereum.org][Phase 7 and Beyond]] section of the implementation doc.
4. *Phase-6 batch size is pinned at =N=2=.*  The walk above used =N=6= for narrative
   clarity.  The shipped =mint.circom= is templated at =N=2= and the
   =MintVerifierAdapter= hard-codes =MINT_BATCH_SIZE=2=.  Splitting 1,200 BUCK into
   six notes in the Phase-6 deployment requires three sequential =Notes.mint= calls of
   two notes each (which weakens the unlinkability story for that particular issuer,
   since the three calls are linkable as a sequence by =msg.sender=).  Variable-=N=
   support is deliberately deferred until the spend circuit pins the eventual public-
   signal arity.
