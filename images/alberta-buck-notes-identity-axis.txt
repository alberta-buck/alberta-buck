                       NOTES ON THE IDENTITY AXIS
             Unifying A1, A2, and B1 Notes around KYC-Bound
                               Identity M

                             Perry Kundert
           ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━


                               2026-06-04


<https://perry.kundert.ca/images/dominion-logo.png>

A Note's authority axis is the KYC-bound Identity point \( M \), never
the Ethereum EOA or contract address: a Note /names/ the issuing and
receiving Identity, and accounts are interchangeable envelopes that need
only /match/ the expected \( M \).  This document shows that making that
axis explicit – by adding a *registry-Identity Merkle accumulator* (root
on chain, a fresh root committed /before/ a newly issued \( M \) can be
used) – collapses the entire Notes system onto a *single gadget*: prove
a re-encryption of a /registered/ Identity (a membership in the
accumulator) under a target key.  The three flavours are one gadget,
instantiated by /who encrypts, when, and under what key/: *A2* (the
issuer encrypts its Identity under the recipient identity point, at
mint), *A1* (a public issuer, in the clear), and *B1* (the depositor
encrypts /its own/ Identity under the public issuer's key, at spend).
The A2 and B1 bindings are exact duals, and *both are implemented and
on-chain-verified* (`IdentityRegistry.verifyDepositCoupling',
`verifyDepositorBinding').  The B1 dual answers the open question
directly: a public bearer issuer gains /collusion-resistant/ knowledge
of the unencrypted Identity of every depositor of its notes – unknown at
mint – while Mallory learns nothing. ([Notes], [Unilateral A2],
[Decryptability])

1 The Authority Axis Is Identity, Not the Account
═════════════════════════════════════════════════

  The shipped Notes design is /account-centric/: the A-spend pins the
  note to a specific mint-time account via a Chaum-Pedersen equality
  that forces \( pk_{\text{rec}} = pk_{\text{dep}} \) (so key loss
  strands the note), and the B-spend carries /no/ in-circuit Identity at
  all (the depositor-naming is an optional on-chain step, "sound for an
  honest depositor" only).  Two divergent spend circuits, two binding
  mechanisms, and an account that is load-bearing.

  But a Note is not a transfer between accounts; it is a claim by one
  /Identity/ on the commons, redeemable by another /Identity/.  The
  unilateral-A2 result (/alberta-buck-notes-unilateral/) made this
  concrete: encrypt under the recipient's Identity /point/ \(
  M_{\text{rec}} \) and the note is redeemable by /any/ Fountain account
  bound to \( m_{\text{rec}} \).  Pushed to its conclusion, the account
  drops out of the design and one structure remains.


2 The Registry-Identity Accumulator
═══════════════════════════════════

  The /identity accumulator/ is a Poseidon Merkle tree whose leaves are
  registered Identity points, \( \ell(M) = \mathrm{Poseidon}(M.x, M.y)
  \), maintained by the `IdentityRegistry' on every `register' /
  `bindContract'.  Its root \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) is published on
  chain; the full tree lives off chain (the wallet's Holochain layer –
  no size limit).  Membership of \( M \) in \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) is
  the statement "\( M \) is a genuine, KYC-registered Identity."
  (`alberta_buck.wallet.unilateral_a2.IdentityTree'.)

  *Commit-before-use.* A newly issued \( M \) must be folded into the
  on-chain root /before/ it is used as a Note counterparty: the issuance
  is final only once \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) reflects it.  This is the
  standard incremental-accumulator discipline (Tornado / Semaphore):
  membership proofs reference a finalised root, and an unregistered \( M
  \) is structurally unusable.  Maintenance is an on-chain incremental
  Poseidon root (gas per registration) or an off-chain-computed root
  proven by a small SNARK; the tree body stays off chain.


3 The One Gadget
════════════════

  Every binding in the system is one relation, parameterised:

  /(Registered re-encryption.)/ Given a target key \( K \) and a
  published ciphertext \( E = (R, C) \), prove knowledge of a scalar \(
  m \) (and randomness \( r \)) such that
  \begin{equation*}
    R = r\,G, \qquad C = m\,G + r\,K, \qquad \ell(m\,G) \in \mathsf{rt_{id}}
  \end{equation*}
  i.e. \( E \) encrypts, under \( K \), a /registered/ Identity \( M =
  mG \) the prover controls – revealing neither \( m \) nor \( M \).

  The EC half (\( R, C \)) is a cheap Okamoto sigma over EIP-196; the
  membership half (\( \ell(mG) \in \mathsf{rt_{id}} \)) is a /native/
  Poseidon-Merkle proof.  Three substitutions of \( (K,\ \text{who
  proves},\ \text{when}) \) give the three flavours.

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Flavour  \( K \) (target key)                         Who proves  When   Effect                                                    
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   *A2*     recipient identity point \(M_{\text{rec}}\)  issuer      mint   recipient decrypts -> names the (private) issuer          
   *A1*     – (issuer \(M_I\) in the clear + signature)  issuer      mint   issuer named directly; recipient proves identity at spend 
   *B1*     public issuer key \(pk_{\text{iss}}\)        depositor   spend  issuer decrypts the event -> names the (bearer) depositor 
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  A2 and B1 are /duals/: the same gadget, run by opposite parties,
  toward opposite keys.  Both are implemented and on-chain-verified.


4 A2 – The Recipient Names the Private Issuer (recap)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  The issuer encrypts its own registered Identity under the recipient
  identity point, \( eIss = (r'G,\ M_I + r'M_{\text{rec}}) \), and the
  depositor proves eligibility with the deposit coupling
  (`verifyDepositCoupling'), all Identities hidden:

  \begin{align*}
    &\textbf{(E4)} & pk_{\text{dep}} &= sk_{\text{dep}} G; \\
    &\textbf{(E2)} & C_d &= m_{\text{rec}} G + sk_{\text{dep}} R_d; \\
    &\textbf{(E3)} & C_e - P_I &= m_{\text{rec}} R_e - bH.
  \end{align*}

  The recipient /alone/ decrypts \( M_I = C_e - m_{\text{rec}} R_e \)
  and produces a plaintext bilateral receipt; membership of the
  decrypted \( M_I \) closes collusion.  Full treatment and tests:
  /alberta-buck-notes-unilateral/ (`unilateral_a2.py', 11 Python + 7
  Solidity).


5 B1 – The Public Issuer Names the Bearer Depositor
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════

  This is the dual, and it answers the standing question: /can a B1
  issuer (public Identity) learn the unencrypted Identity of a depositor
  unknown at mint?/ Yes – collusion-resistantly, and privately from
  Mallory.


5.1 The construction
────────────────────

  At deposit the depositor \( D \) (identity \( m_{\text{dep}} \),
  registered payout account \( (pk_{\text{dep}}, E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d,
  C_d)) \)) re-encrypts /its own/ Identity under the public issuer's key
  and publishes it in the `SpentB' event:

  \begin{equation*}
    E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}} \;=\; (R_f, C_f) \;=\;
       \bigl(r\,G,\ \; M_{\text{dep}} + r\,pk_{\text{iss}}\bigr).
  \end{equation*}

  Only the issuer (holding \( sk_{\text{iss}} \)) recovers \(
  M_{\text{dep}} = C_f - sk_{\text{iss}} R_f \); to Mallory it is
  IND-CPA noise.  The depositor proves – on chain, hiding every Identity
  (`verifyDepositorBinding') – knowledge of \( (m_{\text{dep}},
  sk_{\text{dep}}, r) \):

  \begin{align*}
    &\textbf{(E4)} & pk_{\text{dep}} &= sk_{\text{dep}}\,G
      &&\text{— the real payout-account key;}\\
    &\textbf{(E2)} & C_d &= m_{\text{dep}}\,G + sk_{\text{dep}}\,R_d
      &&\text{— the account is bound to } M_{\text{dep}} = m_{\text{dep}}G;\\
    &\textbf{(F1)} & R_f &= r\,G
      &&\text{— event-ciphertext randomness;}\\
    &\textbf{(F2)} & C_f &= m_{\text{dep}}\,G + r\,pk_{\text{iss}}
      &&\text{— } E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}} \text{ encrypts } M_{\text{dep}}.
  \end{align*}

  The /shared witness \( m_{\text{dep}} \) couples (E2) and (F2)/: the
  Identity bound to the payout account is exactly the one encrypted for
  the issuer – so a colluding depositor cannot substitute a different
  (or junk) Identity.  The membership half (\( \ell(M_{\text{dep}}) \in
  \mathsf{rt_{id}} \)) confirms it is genuine – though for B1 it is
  /also/ implied, since the payout account is itself a registered record
  (the dual is even tighter than A2: the depositor is named, so the
  account /is/ the membership witness).


5.2 The issuer-unilateral receipt
─────────────────────────────────

  The issuer scans `SpentB', decrypts \( E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}} \)
  with \( sk_{\text{iss}} \), and produces a plaintext receipt naming
  /both/ parties – /alone/, the mirror of the A2 recipient receipt.  A
  `verifiable_decrypt' proof (a Chaum-Pedersen DLEQ) makes it
  third-party-checkable: anyone confirms \( E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}}
  \) decrypts under \( pk_{\text{iss}} \) to \( M_{\text{dep}} \), and
  \( M_{\text{dep}} \in \mathsf{rt_{id}} \).  (Tested:
  `test_issuer_receipt_names_depositor'.)


5.3 Why it is collusion-resistant and private
─────────────────────────────────────────────

  /(B1 depositor naming.)/ If `verifyDepositorBinding' accepts a B1
  spend, the issuer recovers, from the event, a registered Identity \(
  M_{\text{dep}} \) the depositor controls; no colluding depositor can
  cash a B1 note while remaining un-nameable, and no third party learns
  \( M_{\text{dep}} \).

  /Soundness/: (E2)+(F2) force \( E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}} \) to
  encrypt the /same/ \( M_{\text{dep}} \) the registered payout account
  is bound to; a substituted ciphertext breaks (F2) (tested:
  `test_collusion_substituted_ciphertext_rejected', and on chain
  `test_vector_substitutedCiphertext_rejected').  /Privacy/: the sigma
  is HVZK and \( E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}} \) is IND-CPA under \(
  pk_{\text{iss}} \), so Mallory cannot read \( M_{\text{dep}} \) nor
  link the depositor's account to the issuer's mint (tested:
  `test_mallory_cannot_decrypt', `test_two_deposits_unlinkable').


6 The Unified Spend Relation
════════════════════════════

  One spend circuit, parameterised by flavour, replaces the divergent
  `spend_a.circom' (CP-pinned) and `spend.circom' (bearer):

  \begin{align*}
   &\text{(opening)}  && cm = \mathrm{Poseidon}(\text{flavor}, v, \rho, idHash, pred),\ cm \in noteRoot;\\
   &\text{(identity)} && M = mG,\quad \ell(M) \in \mathsf{rt_{id}}\ \text{(payout account bound to } M);\\
   &\text{(flavor)}   &&
      \begin{cases}
        \textbf{A2:} & M_I = C_e - m\,R_e\ \text{(decrypt issuer)}\\
        \textbf{A1:} & m = m_{\text{rec}}\ \text{matches the addressed identity; } M_I\ \text{plaintext}\\
        \textbf{B1:} & \text{know } \rho;\ E_{\text{dep}\to\text{iss}} = (rG, MG + r\,pk_{\text{iss}})
      \end{cases}\\
   &\text{(nullifier)} && nf = \mathrm{Poseidon}(\rho, idHash, \text{tag}).
  \end{align*}

  The /identity/ and /flavor/ lines are the one gadget in its three
  forms.  The EC parts are EIP-196 sigmas (`verifyDepositCoupling' for
  A2, `verifyDepositorBinding' for B1, both shipped); the membership and
  the opening are the native Poseidon-Merkle circuit pieces.

8 Key-Management Semantics
══════════════════════════

  Moving the authority to Identity \( M \) changes what a spend
  /requires/: knowledge of the Identity scalar \( m = H(\text{id}) \)
  plus a KYC-registered account bound to \( M \).  Two consequences,
  stated honestly:

  • *Recovery, not finality.* An addressed note survives the loss of any
    single account's key: re-register a fresh account on the same \( M
    \) (the KYC issuer re-certifies the real person) and deposit.  The
    current account-pinned model treats loss as terminal; the
    identity-axis makes it recoverable, with the KYC issuer as the
    recovery anchor.  Both can coexist (offer an account-pinned opt-in
    for self-custody finality).
  • *No PII-grind shortcut.* Although \( m = H(\text{id}) \) is
    recoverable from \( \text{id} \), guessing it does /not/ enable
    theft: spending also requires a /registered/ account bound to \( M
    \) (constraint E2/E4), and an attacker cannot make the KYC issuer
    certify someone else's \( M \).  The non-grindable-identities
    assumption (\( H \) a random oracle) and the KYC gate together close
    the shortcut.

9.1 Migration
─────────────

  This supersedes the account-pinned `spend_a' CP path and the
  in-circuit-less B-spend with the unified relation, rotated in via the
  governance verifier slots (`setMintVerifier' / `setA2MintVerifier').
  The two EIP-196 bindings shipped here are forward-compatible: they are
  the EC halves of the unified spend, usable on-chain today and inside
  the unified circuit tomorrow.
