              THE ALBERTA BUCK - DEMURRAGE AND THE JUBILEE
                                  FUND

                             Perry Kundert
           ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━


                               2026-10-27


Held BUCK pay demurrage on the BUCK remaining in the account. Each
BuckCredit position earns Jubilee relief on the BUCK it actually issued,
proportional to issuance and elapsed time. The total gross demurrage
basis is all outstanding BUCK, including BUCK held by Jubilee. The fund
receives the charges and may invest its assets; it pays the relief when
issuance is retired. Gross demurrage includes charges paid by Jubilee to
itself, so cumulative gross demurrage and BUCK actually held by the fund
are distinct.

1 Status

  This is the intended monetary design, clarified on 2026-10-27. The
  executable [Python reference model] and its tests exercise the
  mathematics below. The current Solidity contract and Rust mirror still
  use a linear charge on a holder's gross raw balance and advance
  Jubilee on issuance; they do not yet implement the model in this
  document. Their existing tests establish the old behavior, not
  conformance with the new one. The detailed implementation audit is

  Earlier versions of this document described linear demurrage on the

2 Issuance, holdings, and relief
════════════════════════════════

  A BuckCredit authorizes credit; only the BUCK actually issued into
  existence forms the demurrage base and earns relief. Undrawn credit is
  not itself BUCK.  While issuance remains outstanding, all of those
  BUCK are held somewhere: ordinary accounts, pool or other service
  accounts, or Jubilee. The underlying asset's insured coverage and a
  user's unused credit headroom are different quantities from the number
  of BUCK actually issued.

  Let `I(t)' be total outstanding BUCK issuance, `r' the nominal annual
  rate, and `P_j(t)' the outstanding issuance attributable to credit
  position `j'.  As issuance changes, the system checkpoints the old
  base and starts integrating the new base. Over an interval,

  ┌────
  │ BUCK-years issued       = integral(I(t) dt)
  │ gross demurrage         = r * integral(I(t) dt)
  │ position j BUCK-years   = integral(P_j(t) dt)
  │ position j gross relief = r * integral(P_j(t) dt)
  └────

  BUCK-years are the integral of outstanding issued BUCK, not merely the
  age of an open but unused credit limit. When an issuance position is
  reduced, its appropriate share of accrued relief is paid from Jubilee
  and the remaining principal is repaid from the account. In the
  reference model, a partial reduction takes a pro-rata share of that
  position's accumulated BUCK-years.  Separate positions retain separate
  issuance histories. Relief on a reduction is capped at the amount
  being reduced. For issuance held for 50 years at a nominal 2% per
  year, the position's liability can therefore be entirely relieved; it
  does not become a negative liability after 50 years.

  An account's actual BUCK holding, the outstanding issuance it owes,
  and its unused credit are not interchangeable. Relief reduces a
  liability only when it is paid. A quoted relief entitlement and the
  assets presently available to pay it must each be visible.


3 Demurrage on remaining balances
═════════════════════════════════

  Demurrage already transferred to Jubilee no longer belongs to the
  former holder and is not charged again to that holder. For an idle
  account with a remaining balance `H' and no other flows, the Python
  reference uses a nominal continuous rate:

  ┌────
  │ dH/dt = -r H
  │ H(t)  = H(0) * exp(-r t)
  └────

  The reference uses continuous compounding because the contract has a
  per-second nominal rate and a result must not depend on the frequency
  of permissionless settlement. If a future implementation instead
  chooses an effective annual rate or another schedule, that convention
  and its rounding must be specified and used consistently in contracts,
  models, and tools.

  New incoming BUCK join the remaining balance at their arrival
  time. Outgoing BUCK leave the balance at their outgoing
  time. Subsequent charges apply to the resulting net holdings, never to
  fees already transferred out. For example, an account with 0.5 BUCK
  remaining incurs approximately 0.009901 BUCK of continuous demurrage
  over the next year at 2%; it does not pay 2% of a much larger
  historical raw balance.

  The fund's BUCK also experience demurrage. If the fund holds BUCK
  directly, its demurrage is transferred to the fund itself: it counts
  in total gross demurrage, but the self-transfer has no net effect on
  fund assets. A fund position in a basket or desk requires separate
  accounting for the underlying BUCK, beneficial ownership, realizable
  asset value, and its returns. It cannot assume that a mark or a
  cumulative demurrage figure is immediately spendable BUCK.


4 The 100 BUCK, 50-year example
═══════════════════════════════

  One credit issues 100 BUCK to an ordinary wallet. Nothing is
  transferred, invested, retired, or added for 50 years. At a nominal
  continuous 2% rate, the Python reference produces, ignoring rounding:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Years  Wallet balance  Jubilee BUCK  Gross demurrage  Jubilee self-demurrage  Relief claim
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
       0      100.000000      0.000000         0.000000                0.000000      0.000000
       1       98.019867      1.980133         2.000000                0.019867      2.000000
      25       60.653066     39.346934        50.000000               10.653066     50.000000
      49       37.531110     62.468890        98.000000               35.531110     98.000000
      50       36.787944     63.212056       100.000000               36.787944    100.000000
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  The 100 BUCK gross demurrage is the sum of 63.212056 transferred from
  the wallet and 36.787944 paid by Jubilee to itself. The latter is
  counted as a charge, but does not create an additional 36.787944 BUCK
  in the fund. Wallet plus fund still hold 100 BUCK, matching the
  outstanding issuance. Crediting Jubilee with all 100 *in addition* to
  leaving 36.787944 in the wallet would create 36.787944 BUCK without a
  source.

  The full relief claim at year 50 is 100, but the fund directly holds
  only 63.212056 in this no-return, no-transfers example. It needs
  another 36.787944 BUCK to pay the entire claim. Acquiring that amount
  from the wallet, for example by selling other assets or realizing
  returns, would leave 100 in the fund; a 100 BUCK Jubilee payment can
  then extinguish the credit. The external consideration and market
  price are not represented by the closed BUCK ledger. No specific
  return or access to liquidity is guaranteed by the arithmetic.


5 Fund investments and liquidity
════════════════════════════════

  Jubilee may put its assets to work in the BUCK-maximizing BuckBasket
  and, when available, desk implementations that seek other assets such
  as USDC.  A useful accounting implementation must distinguish:

  • cumulative gross demurrage, including Jubilee's self-payments;
  • liquid BUCK owned by the fund;
  • deployed positions, their acquisition cost, current mark and
    realizable proceeds after liquidity, price impact and transaction
    costs;
  • other assets and the BUCK they can actually acquire;
  • each credit position's accrued and payable relief; and
  • the cash and positions reserved for anticipated relief payments.

  When a BuckCredit position is reduced, the fund may need to redeem a
  basket position or exchange other assets for BUCK. A modeled
  investment return must have a source, such as a transfer from a
  counterparty, a realized external asset sale, or new, separately
  recorded issuance. It must not appear solely because the fund paid
  demurrage to itself. The reference includes an idealized fund strategy
  that holds a 1:1 BUCK claim and liquidates at par, plus returns
  transferred explicitly from a counterparty. It does not model actual
  basket prices, collateral values, losses, transaction costs, delay, or
  market depth.

  The 50-year example therefore confirms the desired gross fee and
  liability arithmetic while exposing an unfunded cash obligation under
  its stated no-return assumptions. The protocol must specify what
  happens if fund assets cannot be realized in time or are insufficient:
  a payout, unpaid remainder, quote, and subsequent position state must
  not silently create BUCK or discard a claim. Positive ROI is a
  possible source of funds, not a mathematical proof of solvency. An
  individual claim can fit within the fund's assets while all
  outstanding claims together exceed them; individual quotes alone
  cannot establish solvency. Without a reservation or allocation rule,
  paying some creditors first can leave later creditors underfunded.


6 Signed accounts and carrying services
═══════════════════════════════════════

  The present contract uses one signed raw account balance and a
  timestamped `BUCK-seconds' accumulator. Its positive side represents
  gross held BUCK and currently uses the accumulator for the holder's
  accrued fee; its negative side represents actual credit drawn and uses
  the accumulator to integrate the outstanding lien for relief. Zero
  must have zero accumulated seconds. A sign change must settle the old
  interpretation before recording the new one. A negative raw balance is
  an actual liability, not unused credit and not an exhausted positive
  holding. The signed view of an ordinary positive holding must never
  turn negative merely because its accumulated fee exceeds its raw
  amount.

  The existing Carrying mode lets service contracts, including AMM
  pools, report nominal raw balances to preserve ERC-20 reserve
  accounting, while passing a proportional accumulated fee with a
  transfer. These nominal amounts are not necessarily the same as
  unencumbered economic value. The corrected model must preserve a clear
  distinction between nominal pool balances, remaining value,
  transferred fee attribution, and the fund's actual assets.  The
  current sign encoding and Carrying rules are implementation details to
  validate or replace; they do not justify charging an already
  transferred fee again.

  Delegated demurrage also needs an explicit net-balance treatment: its
  payer must have enough actual remaining value, and the outcome must
  remain conserved when a payer is depleted or multiple sponsees settle
  in different orders. See the implementation audit for the existing
  lazy behavior.


7 Run and verify
════════════════

  ┌────
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--years 50 --json"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--years 50 --acquire-all"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--deploy-fund --strategy-return 10"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-test
  └────

  The reference is an event-driven, floating-point monetary model, not
  an EVM or fixed-point implementation. Its tests cover 1 to 100-year
  idle holdings, changes to outstanding issuance, proportional
  reductions, separate positions, small net balances,
  settlement-frequency independence, fund self-demurrage, strategy
  returns and liquidation, conservation, and insufficient funds.  Before
  contract deployment, implement and cross-check fixed-point precision
  and rounding, supply and claim conservation, settlement and retirement
  transitions, pool and delegated-fee semantics, fund liquidity, and
  migration of any existing account history. The five-year economy and
  sandboxes must subsequently use the implemented rules; existing runs
  use the old contract and do not validate this design.
