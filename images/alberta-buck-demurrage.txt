              THE ALBERTA BUCK - DEMURRAGE AND THE JUBILEE
                                  FUND

                             Perry Kundert
           ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━


                               2026-10-27


Held and issued-but-unspent BUCK pay demurrage on the BUCK remaining in
the account. Each BuckCredit position earns Jubilee relief on the BUCK
it actually issued, proportional to issuance and elapsed time. The total
gross demurrage basis is all outstanding BUCK, including BUCK held by
Jubilee. The fund receives the charges and may invest its assets; it
pays the relief when issuance is retired. Gross demurrage includes
charges paid by Jubilee to itself, so cumulative gross demurrage and
BUCK actually held by the fund are distinct.

1 Status
════════

  This is the intended monetary design, clarified through
  2026-10-29. The executable [Python reference model] and its tests
  exercise the mathematics below. The current Solidity contract and Rust
  mirror still use a linear charge on a holder's gross raw balance and
  advance Jubilee on issuance; they do not yet implement the model in
  this document. Their existing tests establish the old behavior, not
  conformance with the new one. The detailed implementation audit is
  [Net-balance demurrage: audit and redesign]. The proposed division of
  responsibility among Buck, each credit NFT, Jubilee and each insurer's
  pool is in [Buck, credit facilities, Jubilee, and insurer pools].

  Earlier versions of this document described linear demurrage on the
  holder's raw balance and contained older storage sketches. Those
  formulas are superseded; the previous text remains available in the
  Git history.

2 Issuance, holdings, and relief
════════════════════════════════

  A BuckCredit authorizes credit; BUCK actually issued into existence
  form the demurrage base and earn relief. When a facility issues BUCK
  into an account's issued-but-unspent balance, they are already issued
  and pay demurrage even before they are transferred. Merely potential
  capacity that has not been issued is not BUCK. While issuance remains
  outstanding, the corresponding net BUCK are in issued-but-unspent
  balances, ordinary accounts, pool or other service accounts, or
  Jubilee. The underlying asset's insured coverage and a user's
  not-yet-issued capacity are different quantities from outstanding
  issued BUCK.

  Let `I(t)' be total outstanding BUCK issuance, `r' the nominal annual
  rate, and `P_j(t)' the outstanding issuance attributable to credit
  position `j'.  As issuance changes, the system checkpoints the old
  base and starts integrating the new base. Over an interval,

  ┌────
  │ BUCK-years issued       = integral(I(t) dt)
  │ gross demurrage         = r * integral(I(t) dt)
  │ position j BUCK-years   = integral(P_j(t) dt)
  │ position j target relief = r * integral(P_j(t) dt)
  └────

  BUCK-years are the integral of outstanding issued BUCK, including
  issued but unspent BUCK, not the age of a potential credit limit. When
  an issuance position is reduced, its target relief is computed from
  its BUCK-years. Jubilee first redeems a proportion of its investment
  positions and computes the actual relief from its available full-value
  BUCK; the account repays the remaining principal.  Actual relief can
  be lower or higher than the target, but is bounded by the principal
  being reduced and by the available net BUCK. In the reference, a
  partial reduction takes a pro-rata share of that position's
  accumulated BUCK-years.  Separate positions retain separate issuance
  histories. For issuance held for 50 years at a nominal 2% per year,
  the target reaches the entire position; the actual payment depends on
  the fund and cannot exceed the position.  Gross issuance-seconds
  continue accumulating for system demurrage after the target reaches
  that cap. Claimable position seconds are capped before new principal
  is added, so excess age beyond 50 years cannot immediately relieve
  newly issued BUCK. The reference keeps one average age within each
  NFT; after new issuance, the combined principal accrues toward its
  combined cap.

  An account's received/held BUCK, issued-but-unspent BUCK, and the
  outstanding issuance of each NFT it owns are not interchangeable. Its
  ERC-20 `balanceOf' reports the full spendable balance: held plus
  issued-but-unspent net BUCK for an ordinary account. Separate views
  can expose each amount. Spending issued BUCK does not issue it again,
  and receiving BUCK does not automatically retire an NFT. Relief
  reduces a liability only when it is paid. The target and the actual
  relief computed after liquidation must each be visible; a target
  shortfall is not an additional guaranteed claim.


3 Demurrage on remaining balances
═════════════════════════════════

  Demurrage already transferred to Jubilee no longer belongs to the
  former holder and is not charged again to that holder. Held and
  issued-but-unspent BUCK follow the same net-balance rule. For an idle
  account with a remaining balance `H' and no other flows, the Python
  reference uses a nominal continuous rate:

  ┌────
  │ dH/dt = -r H
  │ H(t)  = H(0) * exp(-r t)
  └────

  The reference uses continuous compounding because the contract has a
  per-second nominal rate and a result must not depend on the frequency
  of permissionless settlement. If a future implementation instead
  chooses an effective annual rate or another schedule, that convention
  and its rounding must be specified and used consistently in contracts,
  models, and tools.

  New incoming BUCK join the remaining balance at their arrival
  time. Outgoing BUCK leave the balance at their outgoing
  time. Subsequent charges apply to the resulting net holdings, never to
  fees already transferred out. For example, an account with 0.5 BUCK
  remaining incurs approximately 0.009901 BUCK of continuous demurrage
  over the next year at 2%; it does not pay 2% of a much larger
  historical raw balance.

  The fund's BUCK also experience demurrage. If the fund holds BUCK
  directly, its demurrage is transferred to the fund itself: it counts
  in total gross demurrage, but the self-transfer has no net effect on
  fund assets. A fund position in a basket or desk requires separate
  accounting for the underlying BUCK, beneficial ownership, realizable
  asset value, and its returns. It cannot assume that a mark or a
  cumulative demurrage figure is immediately spendable BUCK.


4 The 100 BUCK, 50-year example
═══════════════════════════════

  One credit issues 100 BUCK into an ordinary account's unspent
  balance. Its ordinary held balance starts at zero and `balanceOf'
  reports the full spendable unspent amount. Nothing is transferred,
  invested, retired, or added for 50 years. At a nominal continuous 2%
  rate, the Python reference produces, ignoring rounding:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Years  Unspent / balanceOf  Jubilee BUCK  Gross demurrage  Jubilee self-demurrage  Target relief
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
       0           100.000000      0.000000         0.000000                0.000000       0.000000
       1            98.019867      1.980133         2.000000                0.019867       2.000000
      25            60.653066     39.346934        50.000000               10.653066      50.000000
      49            37.531110     62.468890        98.000000               35.531110      98.000000
      50            36.787944     63.212056       100.000000               36.787944     100.000000
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  The 100 BUCK gross demurrage is the sum of 63.212056 transferred from
  the unspent balance and 36.787944 paid by Jubilee to itself. The
  latter is counted as a charge, but does not create an additional
  36.787944 BUCK in the fund.  Unspent balance plus fund still hold 100
  BUCK, matching outstanding issuance.  Crediting Jubilee with all 100
  *in addition* to leaving 36.787944 unspent would create 36.787944 BUCK
  without a source.

  The target relief at year 50 is 100, but the fund directly holds only
  63.212056 in this no-return, no-transfers example. It needs another
  36.787944 BUCK to pay the full target. Acquiring that amount from the
  owner, for example by selling other assets or realizing returns, would
  leave 100 in the fund; a 100 BUCK Jubilee payment can then extinguish
  the credit. The external consideration and market price are not
  represented by the closed BUCK ledger. Under the available-relief rule
  the actual relief is computed from the fund's net liquid BUCK after
  any proportional redemption; the borrower pays the remainder. No
  separate fixed 100 BUCK payout is assumed.


5 Fund investments and liquidity
════════════════════════════════

  Jubilee may put its assets to work in the BUCK-maximizing BuckBasket
  and, when available, desk implementations that seek other assets such
  as USDC.  A useful accounting implementation must distinguish:

  • cumulative gross demurrage, including Jubilee's self-payments;
  • liquid BUCK owned by the fund;
  • deployed positions, their acquisition cost, current mark and
    realizable proceeds after liquidity, price impact and transaction
    costs;
  • other assets and the BUCK they can actually acquire;
  • each credit position's accrued and payable relief; and
  • the cash and positions reserved for anticipated relief payments.

  For the fund's cash ledger, separate cumulative 256-bit counters can
  record full-value demurrage received, relief paid, BUCK invested, and
  net BUCK withdrawn. With no other flows, liquid net BUCK equals
  received demurrage plus investment withdrawals, less relief payments
  and investments. Other inflows and outflows are recorded
  separately. Demurrage paid by the fund to itself cannot be added to
  net incoming cash a second time. A running average cost basis on the
  pooled investment additionally identifies gains or losses only as the
  corresponding shares are withdrawn. The net of either pair of
  cumulative counters can be negative after gains; unsigned totals must
  not be subtracted without signed or otherwise guarded arithmetic.

  When a BuckCredit position is reduced, the fund computes relief from
  the selected fraction of its net cash and marked basket receipt,
  without selling the receipt during that transaction. The calculated
  net relief must be no more than 10% of the available net cash,
  otherwise the reduction reverts. The borrower supplies the difference;
  if the borrower lacks that amount, the reduction cannot complete. A
  separate keeper can redeem basket shares to replenish the cash
  reserve. A modeled investment return must have a source, such as a
  transfer from a counterparty, a realized external asset sale, or new,
  separately recorded issuance. It must not appear solely because the
  fund paid demurrage to itself. The reference includes an idealized
  BUCK-only strategy that pays demurrage into fund cash and liquidates
  at its current mark, plus returns transferred explicitly from a
  counterparty. The strategy's mark falls as its BUCK pay fees, while
  the same fee increases fund cash: counting the mark decrease alone as
  a loss of total fund value would be misleading. The reference does not
  model actual basket prices, collateral values, transaction costs,
  delay, or market depth.

  An earlier available-relief experiment scales the target by
  post-redemption net cash divided by a 10% reserve target, capped at
  net cash and principal.  That signal can mistake uninvested cash or
  redemption proceeds for returns.  In a two-facility idle example it
  lets the first reducer receive the entire fund, leaving the second
  unable to close. It is retained only as a comparison, not a safe
  contract rule.

  A second earlier rule allocated the fraction `f' of pre-sale cash plus
  actual proceeds from selling fraction `f' of the position, capped by
  principal. It remains an explicit reference comparison, not the
  current contract policy.  The marked rule allocates the same fraction
  of net cash plus the receipt's marked value; if an actual sale
  realizes exactly the mark with no costs, the two rules agree. A mark
  is not a guarantee of liquidity or realized price.  See the
  [marked-relief analysis] for the comparison and remaining audit work.

  The simulator retains fixed-2%, reserve-ratio and proportional-sale
  rules as comparison diagnostics. Its command-line retirement defaults
  to the marked rule. Credits of different ages make their share of
  outstanding relief very different from their share of issued BUCK. The
  reference sums its positions to obtain the benchmark; the experimental
  claim book maintains the capped aggregate incrementally, with bounded
  permissionless processing when facilities reach 50 years.

  The experimental replacement does not sell the fund's basket position
  inside a reduction. It allocates the selected fraction of the fund's
  *net liquid BUCK plus the net marked value of its basket receipt*, and
  reverts if that relief exceeds 10% of liquid net BUCK. The selected
  fraction is the reduced facility's capped BUCK-seconds divided by all
  capped outstanding BUCK-seconds, including any matured principal
  assumed by Jubilee and not yet burned. Consequently the effective rate
  is the nominal 2% multiplied by the whole-fund mark divided by the
  aggregate relief benchmark, subject to the principal cap. The isolated
  Solidity reduction and renewal paths implement this rule; their
  oracle, mark-to-market and executable-liquidity assumptions still
  require further review and simulation. See the [marked-relief
  analysis] for a numerical example, the 50-year aggregate and valuation
  limitations.

  At 50 years of a facility's average issuance age, its owner can
  instead release the lien without providing BUCK. The prototype burns
  the NFT and records the unburned principal as Jubilee's obligation,
  retaining it in outstanding supply and the benchmark until the fund
  later burns it. A premium deposit remains owed by the original insurer
  pool in nominal BUCK; if the pool cannot immediately return it, the
  refund can be retried after lien release. Deferred burns are limited
  to 10% of current net fund cash per operation. A strict limit can
  leave a small residual obligation if fewer than ten raw BUCK units are
  liquid.

  If an owner leaves an already mature facility open, its claim does not
  grow beyond its principal, but the BUCK it issued remains in
  circulation and continues to pay demurrage. For example, with 100 BUCK
  issued and held in an ordinary account, after 50 years that account
  still has approximately 36.79 BUCK and Jubilee has approximately 63.21
  net BUCK in the no-investment example. The 100-BUCK target does not
  imply that 100 BUCK are already liquid in Jubilee. At release the fund
  must burn what it can under the liquidity rule and retain the rest as
  an obligation until BUCK are acquired or accrue.  Value exceeding a
  facility's principal remains available to other claims; it may result
  from later demurrage as well as investment returns.

  In both examples the *gross* demurrage assessed over those 50 years is
  the full 100 BUCK. The difference is that, without an investment
  return, the approximately 36.79 BUCK of fees paid by the fund to
  itself do not add net value to its own account a second time. A
  separate closed-ledger comparison models the entire fund earning 2%
  annualized: the return is transferred from another holder's existing
  BUCK, so Jubilee accumulates 100 net BUCK at year 50 and total supply
  remains 100. No new credit is necessary, though the return has a real
  economic source and is not guaranteed. The fund in that comparison is
  fully invested; a liquid reserve does not itself earn that investment
  return. Run `make nix-venv-demurrage-returns' to reproduce the
  comparison, or add `DEMURRAGE_ARGS'"–json"= for its rows.

6 Account state and carrying services
═════════════════════════════════════

  The present contract uses one signed raw account balance and a
  timestamped `BUCK-seconds' accumulator. Its positive side represents
  gross held BUCK and currently uses the accumulator for the holder's
  accrued fee; its negative side represents actual credit drawn and uses
  the accumulator to integrate the outstanding lien for relief. Zero
  must have zero accumulated seconds. A sign change must settle the old
  interpretation before recording the new one. This is a description of
  the current code, not the proposed model: the current account sign
  cannot represent held BUCK, issued-but-unspent BUCK and multiple
  independent NFT liabilities at once. The proposed design stores the
  BUCK and credit-facility responsibilities separately and attributes
  each NFT's relief to that NFT. The signed view of an ordinary positive
  holding must never turn negative merely because its accumulated fee
  exceeds its raw amount.

  The existing Carrying mode lets service contracts, including AMM
  pools, report nominal raw balances to preserve ERC-20 reserve
  accounting, while passing a proportional accumulated fee with a
  transfer. These nominal amounts are not necessarily the same as
  unencumbered economic value. The corrected model must preserve a clear
  distinction between nominal pool balances, remaining value,
  transferred fee attribution, and the fund's actual assets.  For
  Jubilee's own Carrying balance, a relief payment must deliver its full
  net value. If the fund has 100 nominal BUCK with 20 BUCK of carried
  fee, its net balance is 80. To deliver 40 net BUCK under proportional
  continuous fee allocation, it sends 50 nominal BUCK carrying 10 of
  fee; 50 nominal and 10 of fee remain, or 40 net. The fund's
  already-credited self-demurrage must not be credited again on
  payout. The actual on-chain rounding must follow the chosen
  accumulator representation; the Python integer example is not yet a
  derivation for Solidity's buck-seconds rounding.  The current sign
  encoding and Carrying rules must be replaced or adapted; they do not
  justify charging an already transferred fee again.

  Each upstream insurer has its own Carrying premium pool. An initial
  deposit of 10 full-value BUCK is refunded as 10 nominal BUCK, with
  that pool's average carried fee. This differs from Jubilee relief,
  which must deliver full net value. A pool can invest and redeem its
  positions before paying; an insurer's deposit and refund must not be
  mixed with another insurer's pool. A renewal can settle an NFT's
  actual relief, update insurance terms, and optionally add issuance
  atomically without first releasing its old coverage.

  Delegated demurrage also needs an explicit net-balance treatment: its
  payer must have enough actual remaining value, and the outcome must
  remain conserved when a payer is depleted or multiple sponsees settle
  in different orders. See the implementation audit for the existing
  lazy behavior.


7 Run and verify
════════════════

  ┌────
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--issued 1000000 --years 1"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--years 50 --json"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--years 50 --acquire-all"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--deploy-fund --strategy-return 10"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--deploy-fund --wipe-strategy --retire 50 --redeem-fraction 0.25"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--retire 50"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-reference DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--retire 50 --payout-policy reserve"
  │ make nix-venv-demurrage-test
  └────

  The reference is an event-driven, floating-point monetary model, not
  an EVM or fixed-point implementation. Its tests cover 1 to 1,000-year
  idle holdings, changes to outstanding issuance, proportional
  reductions, separate NFT positions, full spendable balances,
  insurer-pool refunds and atomic renewals, small net balances,
  settlement-frequency independence, fund self-demurrage, strategy
  returns and liquidation, zero proceeds, grossed-up Carrying relief,
  conservation, and insufficient debtor funds.  Before contract
  deployment, implement and cross-check fixed-point precision and
  rounding, supply and claim conservation, settlement and retirement
  transitions, pool and delegated-fee semantics, fund liquidity, and
  migration of any existing account history. The five-year economy and
  sandboxes must subsequently use the implemented rules; existing runs
  use the old contract and do not validate this design.
