              THE ALBERTA BUCK - DEMURRAGE AND THE JUBILEE
                                  FUND

                             Perry Kundert
           ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━


                               2026-04-29


1 Overview
══════════

  Alberta Buck imposes a flat 2%/yr demurrage on every BUCK in
  existence.  The fee is *not* a transfer or a burn – it is a view-time
  computation against per-account state.  The locked fees stay inside
  each account as inaccessible BUCKs, visible to the holder via
  `balanceOfFees(a)'.  Aggregate demurrage is expressed via the Jubilee
  fund, an on-chain account whose raw ERC-20 balance grows ONLY at
  user-driven `mint()' / `burn()' events; between such events neither
  the Jubilee fund nor any user account experiences any state change due
  to demurrage.

  The model has three load-bearing properties:

  1. *Linear in time.* A BUCK held still earns demurrage at a linear
     rate on its raw balance: BUCK$100 sitting unmoved for one year
     reports `balanceOf' = BUCK$98.  After 50 years it reports BUCK$0.
     No exponential decay; no compounding.
  2. *No demurrage burn.* No transfer or accrual event ever burns from a
     holder's `_balanceof'.  `totalSupply' is mutated only by
     user-initiated `mint()' and `burn()' calls (and by Jubilee accrual
     that fires on those same calls).
  3. *Conservation of effective spendable.* At any block, the sum of
     `balanceOf(a)' over every account (including Jubilee) plus the sum
     of `balanceOfFees(a)' equals `totalSupply' – no BUCKs are created
     or destroyed by demurrage; they are simply locked and slowly
     redistributed to the Jubilee fund at mint/burn checkpoints.

  The system supports two transfer flavours, dispatched automatically by
  the sender's registered identity type:

  • *Non-Carrying* (sender's `IdentityRegistry.isCarrying' flag is false
    – the default for EOAs registered via `register()').  The sender's
    accumulated BUCK-age is crystallized into its locked-fees pool; the
    recipient gets fresh BUCKs (no inherited fee debt).
  • *Carrying* (sender's `isCarrying' flag is true – typical for service
    contracts bound via `bindContract()').  The sender does not
    crystallize on outflow; the carried portion's fee debt is added to
    the recipient's locked fees.  Recipients accept Carrying transfers
    in advance via the standard identity-bound `approve(carryingAccount,
    0, ...)' call – the same CP-receipt mechanism that gates any
    identity-bound transfer is also the consent to receive carried fees.

  There is no separate `transferCarrying' function; both `transfer' and
  `transferFrom' dispatch through the registry flag.  A contract has no
  will of its own – it executes on behalf of an operator and holds BUCK
  temporarily for others.  The Carrying flavour pushes the demurrage on
  those temporarily-held BUCKs to the parties that ultimately collect
  them, in exchange for whatever value the contract delivers.

  Carrying-only contract accounts (e.g., the Jubilee fund, the Notes
  pool, AMM pools) do not pay demurrage from their own holdings – they
  deploy funds via ordinary `transfer' calls that the registry resolves
  to Carrying.  Their goal is to keep only a "hot" working balance, so
  the demurrage on idle Carrying-account balance is a second-order
  effect.

  The `isCarrying' flag is set at registration / binding time and is
  *immutable once any counterparty has approved the address*.  The
  freeze prevents an operator from flipping a wallet's flavour after
  recipients have already consented to receive from it.  Pre-approval
  reconfiguration (e.g., a freshly-deployed service contract changing
  its mind before it goes live) is permitted; once a single CP receipt
  names the address as spender, the flag is locked.


2 Per-account state
═══════════════════

  Every account `a' – EOAs, contract accounts, and the Jubilee fund –
  carries three storage values:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Field            Meaning                                                                                 
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   `_balanceof[a]'  Raw ERC-20 balance.  Inherited from OZ ERC20.  Mutated only by user-initiated           
                    balance changes (mint, burn, transfer in, transfer out).  Demurrage does not modify it. 
   `_demurrage[a]'  Cumulative locked fees accrued up through `_timestamp[a]'.                              
   `_timestamp[a]'  Block timestamp of the most recent balance-mutating event for `a'.  All fees            
                    accrued through this instant are already folded into `_demurrage[a]'.                   
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  A freshly created account has `_balanceof = _demurrage = 0' and
  `_timestamp' unset (treated as zero, but no fee accrues against zero
  raw so the value is irrelevant until raw becomes non-zero).


3 Live fee calculation
══════════════════════

  Three view functions expose demurrage state.  Let `BASE_RATE_PER_SEC'
  = 2%/yr / (365.25 days), and let `now' be `block.timestamp'.

  ┌────
  │ function feeOwing(a):
  │     elapsed     = now - _timestamp[a]
  │     pending_fee = _balanceof[a] * BASE_RATE_PER_SEC * elapsed
  │     return _demurrage[a] + pending_fee
  │ 
  │ function balanceOfFees(a):
  │     return min(feeOwing(a), _balanceof[a])
  │ 
  │ function balanceOf(a):
  │     return _balanceof[a] - balanceOfFees(a)
  └────
  Three behaviours fall out of these definitions:

  1. `balanceOf' is what the holder can spend right now.  It decreases
     linearly as time passes against a fixed raw balance.  At BUCK$100
     raw and `BASE_RATE' = 2%/yr, after one day `balanceOf' =
     $99.99452…, after one year = $98, after fifty years = $0.
  2. `balanceOfFees' is the locked dust currently inaccessible to the
     holder.  At any moment `balanceOf + balanceOfFees = _balanceof'.
  3. `feeOwing' can in principle exceed `_balanceof' for an account that
     has been idle long enough; `balanceOfFees' caps the reported value
     at `_balanceof' (an account is never short more than it actually
     holds).

  The view functions are O(1) and require no on-chain state mutation – a
  wallet can compute live demurrage at any moment without spending gas.


4 Crystallization
═════════════════

  Whenever an account's raw is about to change – mint into, burn from,
  transfer in, or transfer out – the contract calls a helper
  `_crystallize(a)' before the change:

  ┌────
  │ function _crystallize(a):
  │     elapsed       = now - _timestamp[a]
  │     pending_fee   = _balanceof[a] * BASE_RATE_PER_SEC * elapsed
  │     _demurrage[a] = _demurrage[a] + pending_fee
  │     _timestamp[a] = now
  └────
  The function is idempotent in time: calling it twice in the same block
  yields the same state as calling it once.  After the call,
  `feeOwing(a) == _demurrage[a]' and the second term in the view is zero
  – the live fee is fully captured in storage.

  Crystallization fires at the start of every event that touches an
  account's `_balanceof'.  Pure read calls (`balanceOf',
  `balanceOfFees') never crystallize – they read storage and compute the
  view.


5 Transfers
═══════════

  The public surface is the standard ERC-20 `transfer(to, value)' and
  `transferFrom(from, to, value)'.  `Buck._update' reads
  `IdentityRegistry.isCarrying(from)' and dispatches:

  ┌────
  │ function _update(from, to, value):
  │     if from == 0 || to == 0:
  │         // mint / burn -- handled separately, see "Mint and burn"
  │         return _mintBurnUpdate(from, to, value)
  │ 
  │     if identity.isCarrying(from):
  │         _carryingTransfer(from, to, value)
  │     else:
  │         _nonCarryingTransfer(from, to, value)
  └────
  The flavour is determined entirely by the sender's registered identity
  type – it is never a per-call option.  A user wanting to receive aged
  BUCK from a service contract approves that contract once via the
  existing identity-bound CP receipt; from then on every transfer that
  contract pushes to the user is Carrying.


5.1 Non-Carrying transfers (EOA sender)
───────────────────────────────────────

  When the sender is an EOA (or any address with `isCarrying' == false),
  the transfer crystallizes the sender's fees and lands fresh BUCKs at
  the recipient:

  ┌────
  │ function _nonCarryingTransfer(from, to, value):
  │     require(value <= balanceOf(from))
  │     _crystallize(from)
  │     _crystallize(to)
  │     _balanceof[from] = _balanceof[from] - value
  │     _balanceof[to]   = _balanceof[to]   + value
  │     // _demurrage[from] retains the pre-existing locked fees.
  │     // _demurrage[to]   is unchanged: recipient gets fresh BUCKs.
  │     // Both _timestamp[*] are set to `now` by crystallization.
  └────
  Operational invariants:

  • The sender's residual BUCKs continue to age from `now' forward (the
    fresh `_timestamp[from]' starts a new linear accrual against the
    smaller raw).
  • The sender's accumulated locked fees stay locked in the sender's
    `_demurrage[from]'.  They are NOT moved to the recipient and they
    are NOT moved to the Jubilee fund.
  • The recipient's incoming BUCKs land at age 0 – the recipient's
    `_demurrage[to]' does not change.

  The check `value <= balanceOf(from)' prevents the sender from spending
  its locked fees: only `balanceOf(from)' is available for outflow.  The
  OZ super `_update' guards on raw, so the extra spendable check sits in
  front.


5.2 Carrying transfers (Carrying-flagged sender)
────────────────────────────────────────────────

  When the sender's `isCarrying' flag is true (typically a service
  contract: the Jubilee fund, a Notes pool, an AMM pool), the same
  `transfer' / `transferFrom' dispatches through the internal Carrying
  path.  The transferred portion's demurrage is pushed onto the
  recipient's locked fees; the sender's basis is left untouched.

  ┌────
  │ function _carryingTransfer(from, to, value):
  │     require(value <= _balanceof[from])
  │     age_basis_seconds = now - _timestamp[from]
  │     carried_fee       = value * BASE_RATE_PER_SEC * age_basis_seconds
  │     _crystallize(to)
  │     _balanceof[from]   = _balanceof[from] - value
  │     _balanceof[to]     = _balanceof[to]   + value
  │     _demurrage[to]    = _demurrage[to]  + carried_fee
  │     // _timestamp[from] and _demurrage[from] are unchanged --
  │     // the residual continues to age from the original basis.
  └────
  Operational invariants:

  • The sender's `_timestamp[from]' and `_demurrage[from]' do not
    change.  The residual BUCK in `_balanceof[from]' continues to accrue
    fee at the same age basis – no fresh clock for the remaining
    balance.
  • The recipient's `_demurrage[to]' absorbs the carried fee:
    `balanceOf(to)' immediately reflects the inherited debt.  The
    recipient's `_balanceof[to]' grows by the gross `value'.
  • The Carrying transfer is its own balance-mutating event for `to', so
    the recipient is crystallized first; the carried fee is then added
    to the just-refreshed `_demurrage[to]'.

  A Carrying transfer is only valid when the recipient has explicitly
  consented (see "Approve and consent" below).  This prevents a
  malicious Carrying account from dumping unwanted fee debt on an
  unconsenting counterparty.


5.3 Algebraic identity: total spendable is preserved
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Both transfer flavours preserve `sum_a balanceOf(a) + sum_a
  balanceOfFees(a) = totalSupply' block-by-block.  The distribution of
  locked fees across accounts differs:

  • Non-Carrying: the sender's locked grows; the recipient lands fresh.
  • Carrying: the recipient's locked grows by the carried amount; the
    sender's locked stays at whatever it was.

  Both keep `totalSupply' unchanged and conserve the system fee debt.


6 Mint and burn
═══════════════

  Only `mint()' and `burn()' can mutate `totalSupply'.  Both trigger
  Jubilee accrual before performing the user's intent.


6.1 Sequence on `Buck.mint(amount)'
───────────────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ function mint(amount):
  │     require(identity.isVerified(msg.sender))
  │     require(amount + _balanceof[msg.sender] <= storedLimit[msg.sender])
  │     _accrueJubilee()                  // see below
  │     _crystallize(msg.sender)
  │     premium = _computePremium(msg.sender, amount, storedLimit[msg.sender])
  │     super._mint(msg.sender, amount - premium)
  │     if premium > 0:
  │         _crystallize(insurancePool)
  │         super._mint(insurancePool, premium)
  └────


6.2 Sequence on `Buck.burn(amount)'
───────────────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ function burn(amount):
  │     require(amount <= balanceOf(msg.sender))
  │     _accrueJubilee()
  │     _crystallize(msg.sender)
  │     super._burn(msg.sender, amount)
  └────


6.3 Jubilee accrual
───────────────────

  The Jubilee fund's raw balance grows ONLY when `_accrueJubilee()'
  runs, and `_accrueJubilee()' runs ONLY at the start of `mint()' or
  `burn()'.

  ┌────
  │ uint256 internal _jubileeLastUpdate;   // initialised to genesis
  │ 
  │ function _accrueJubilee():
  │     elapsed = now - _jubileeLastUpdate
  │     if elapsed == 0:
  │         return
  │     delta = totalSupply() * BASE_RATE_PER_SEC * elapsed
  │     _crystallize(address(this))
  │     super._mint(address(this), delta)
  │     _jubileeLastUpdate = now
  └────
  The accrual mints fresh BUCK to `address(this)' equal to the 2%/yr
  rate applied to the *current* total supply across the elapsed interval
  (the supply is piecewise constant between mint/burn events, so the
  integral is just `totalSupply * elapsed').  `totalSupply' grows by
  `delta'; the new BUCK is backed by the locked fees that already exist
  in user accounts.

  After the accrual:
  • `_balanceof[address(this)]' is larger.
  • `_timestamp[address(this)]' is `now' (the crystallization step folds
    Jubilee's prior age into its `_demurrage[]' before the new BUCKs
    land).
  • The new BUCKs themselves are at age 0; they will accrue Jubilee's
    own self-demurrage going forward.


6.4 Why the Jubilee fund cannot be a pure view
──────────────────────────────────────────────

  Earlier sketches considered making `balanceOf(jubilee)' a computed
  view (`BASE_RATE * area_under_supply' minus a self-demurrage haircut)
  with no actual ERC-20 storage.  That fails when the Jubilee must
  transfer out: a BuckCredit lien redemption needs to *burn* BUCKs from
  the Jubilee account, and you cannot burn from a virtual balance.  The
  Jubilee therefore holds real ERC-20 storage that grows at mint/burn
  checkpoints.


7 The Jubilee fund
══════════════════

  The Jubilee fund lives at `address(this)' (the Buck contract itself).
  It is a Carrying account: it does not pay its own demurrage from its
  own holdings on outflow; instead it pushes funds via Carrying
  transfers, and recipients absorb the proportional fee debt.

  Holdings and operations:

  • `_balanceof[address(this)]' grows at every mint/burn via
    `_accrueJubilee()'.
  • `balanceOf(address(this))' = `_balanceof[address(this)] -
      feeOwing(address(this))'.  Just like any other account, the
      Jubilee accrues its own demurrage on its idle balance.
  • `balanceOfFees(address(this))' is the Jubilee's locked
    self-demurrage.  These BUCKs stay in the Jubilee until carried out
    via a future transfer to a consenting recipient.
  • The Jubilee deploys funds via the ordinary `Buck.transfer(to,
    amount)' called by an authorised governance process; because
    `isCarrying[address(this)]' is true at binding time, `Buck._update'
    dispatches through the Carrying path automatically.  Recipients must
    have consented via the identity-bound `approve(jubilee, 0, ...)'.

  Practical posture: the Jubilee should be lean.  Funds it collects are
  intended to be deployed promptly to the insurance pool, BUCK_K
  stabilization mechanisms, public goods, etc., so that the "hot"
  balance held idle is small and self-demurrage on it is negligible.
  Long idleness produces a real but bounded degradation in
  `balanceOf(jubilee)' that is recovered via Carrying transfer to
  consenting recipients.


8 Worked example: one BUCK$100 mint, no other events
════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  Setup at `t=0':

  ┌────
  │ mint(100) by alice
  │ state:
  │   alice:    _balanceof=100, _demurrage=0,    _timestamp=0
  │   jubilee:  _balanceof=0,   _demurrage=0,    _timestamp=0
  │ totalSupply: 100
  └────
  After exactly 1 year (no other events):

  ┌────
  │ state (storage unchanged):
  │   alice:    _balanceof=100, _demurrage=0,    _timestamp=0
  │   jubilee:  _balanceof=0,   _demurrage=0,    _timestamp=0
  │ totalSupply: 100
  │ 
  │ views:
  │   feeOwing(alice)        = 100 * 2%/yr * 1yr = $2
  │   balanceOf(alice)       = $100 - $2 = $98
  │   balanceOfFees(alice)   = $2
  │ 
  │   feeOwing(jubilee)      = 0 * 2%/yr * 1yr = $0
  │   balanceOf(jubilee)     = $0
  └────
  The "missing" BUCK$2 is locked in alice's account and will be
  effectively "swept" into the Jubilee fund at the next mint/burn event
  (the Jubilee fund will grow by the same value locked in alice's
  account).

  If at exactly `t=1yr' a second account does `mint(1)':

  ┌────
  │ 1) _accrueJubilee():
  │      elapsed = 1yr,  totalSupply = $100
  │      delta   = $100 * 2%/yr * 1yr = $2
  │      _crystallize(jubilee):     _demurrage += 0
  │      super._mint(jubilee, $2):  _balanceof += $2,  totalSupply = $102
  │      _timestamp[jubilee] = t1
  │ 2) _crystallize(alice):     _demurrage += $2,    _timestamp = t1
  │ 3) super._mint(bob, $1):    bob._balanceof = $1,  totalSupply = $103
  │ 
  │ state after:
  │   alice:    _balanceof=100, _demurrage=$2,  _timestamp=t1
  │   bob:      _balanceof=$1,  _demurrage=0,   _timestamp=t1
  │   jubilee:  _balanceof=$2,  _demurrage=0,   _timestamp=t1
  │ totalSupply: $103
  │ 
  │ views (instant after the mint):
  │   balanceOf(alice)   = 100 - 2 = $98
  │   balanceOf(bob)     = $1
  │   balanceOf(jubilee) = $2
  │ 
  │   sum balanceOf  = $98 + $1 + $2 = $101
  │   sum balanceOfFees = $2 (alice) + 0 + 0 = $2
  │   total          = $103 = totalSupply  ✓
  └────
  After a further year (no events between `t1' and `t1+1yr'):

  ┌────
  │ state (storage unchanged):
  │   alice:    _balanceof=100, _demurrage=$2,  _timestamp=t1
  │   bob:      _balanceof=$1,  _demurrage=0,   _timestamp=t1
  │   jubilee:  _balanceof=$2,  _demurrage=0,   _timestamp=t1
  │ 
  │ views at t1+1yr:
  │   feeOwing(alice)    = 2 + 100 * 2%/yr * 1yr = $4
  │   balanceOf(alice)   = $96
  │ 
  │   feeOwing(bob)      = 0 + 1 * 2%/yr * 1yr   = $0.02
  │   balanceOf(bob)     = $0.98
  │ 
  │   feeOwing(jubilee)  = 0 + 2 * 2%/yr * 1yr   = $0.04
  │   balanceOf(jubilee) = $1.96
  │ 
  │   sum balanceOf      = $96 + $0.98 + $1.96 = $98.94
  │   sum balanceOfFees  = $4 + $0.02 + $0.04 = $4.06
  │   total              = $103 = totalSupply  ✓
  └────
  The Jubilee held $2 raw at `t1', and at `t1+1yr' reports `balanceOf' =
  $1.96 – $2 raw minus its own $0.04 of self- demurrage on twelve months
  at age 1yr.  The earlier illustrative figure of $1.98 corresponds to a
  smoother continuous-accrual approximation in which the Jubilee's age
  is six-month average; in the actual implementation the average age
  depends on the spacing of mint/burn events.


9 Carrying accounts and the `isCarrying' flag
═════════════════════════════════════════════

  A Carrying account is any address whose
  `IdentityRegistry.isCarrying[a]' flag is true.  The account itself
  accrues demurrage like any other; the distinguishing behaviour is on
  outflow, where `Buck._update' dispatches the sender's transfer through
  the Carrying path automatically.


9.1 Setting the flag
────────────────────

  The flag lives in `IdentityRegistry' and is set at registration time:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Path              Default                  Override                                                    
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   `register()'      false (EOA)              not permitted – EOAs are always Non-Carrying.               
   `bindContract()'  true (service contract)  `bindContract(target, pk, E, isPublic, /isCarrying=false/)' 
                                              for contract wallets that should crystallize like an EOA;   
                                              multisigs operated by a single user, AA wallets, etc..      
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Carrying contracts are expected to be rare in practice – most contract
  bindings are service contracts (Notes pools, AMM pools, the Jubilee
  fund itself) where the flag defaults true.  Smart-contract wallets opt
  out at binding time.


9.2 Immutability after first approval
─────────────────────────────────────

  Once any counterparty issues an identity-bound approve naming this
  address as the spender (i.e., a CP receipt fragment is written for the
  (counterparty, `a') pair), the `isCarrying[a]' flag is locked.
  Pre-approval changes are permitted (a freshly- deployed contract may
  flip its mind before going live); post-approval changes are not
  (recipients have already consented to a specific Carrying flavour and
  the operator must not be able to retroactively change it).

  The lock is enforced by a per-address boolean `carryingFrozen[a]' and
  a `binderOf[a]' mapping in `IdentityRegistry', plus a one-time
  `setBuck()' governance hook that authorises the Buck contract to mark
  a spender frozen on approve:

  ┌────
  │ // Buck.approve(spender, amount, E_spender, pi_CP):
  │ //   ... existing CP-receipt machinery ...
  │ //   identity.markApproved(spender)        // freezes the spender's flag
  │ 
  │ function markApproved(address spender):       // IdentityRegistry, external
  │     require(msg.sender == buck, "only Buck")
  │     if !carryingFrozen[spender]:
  │         carryingFrozen[spender] = true
  │ 
  │ function setIsCarrying(address target, bool value):  // IdentityRegistry, external
  │     require(msg.sender == binderOf[target],     "not binder")
  │     require(!carryingFrozen[target],            "carrying frozen by approval")
  │     isCarrying[target] = value
  └────
  A re-bound (re-deployed) contract resets through the standard
  `bindContract()' flow; in-place flag changes after first approval are
  simply not supported.


9.3 Why Carrying for service contracts
──────────────────────────────────────

  A service contract holds BUCK temporarily on behalf of others (the
  Jubilee fund pre-deployment; a Notes pool holding face value until a
  note is spent; an AMM liquidity pool waiting to be paired).  If such
  an account were forced to crystallize on outflow like an ordinary EOA,
  it would have to surface its own accumulated demurrage and either
  absorb it (degrading the funds it manages) or push it onto the
  recipient as a non-Carrying gift of fresh BUCKs (mis-pricing the
  actual age-adjusted value).  The Carrying flavour preserves the age
  basis: BUCK that has been sitting in the Notes pool for six months
  arrives at the redeemer with six months of age-adjusted fee debt baked
  in, and the recipient sees a `balanceOf' less than the gross face
  accordingly.


9.4 Sender side of a Carrying transfer
──────────────────────────────────────

  The sender's `_timestamp' and `_demurrage' are unchanged.  Sender's
  `_balanceof' drops by `value'.  The residual continues to age from the
  same basis – it does NOT start a fresh clock.


9.5 Recipient side of a Carrying transfer
─────────────────────────────────────────

  Recipient is crystallized (any pending fees on its prior `_balanceof'
  are folded into `_demurrage[to]', `_timestamp' is reset to `now').
  Then the carried fee `value * (now - sender_timestamp) * BASE_RATE' is
  added to `_demurrage[to]', and `value' is added to `_balanceof[to]'.

  The instantaneous post-transfer effect:

  ┌────
  │ balanceOf(to)     = _balanceof[to] (post) - _demurrage[to] (post)
  │                   = (raw_old + value) - (existing_locked + carried_fee)
  │                   = balanceOf_old + (value - carried_fee)
  └────
  The recipient's spendable goes up by the *net* amount `value -
  carried_fee', exactly the age-adjusted value of the incoming BUCKs.


10 Approve and consent
══════════════════════

  The identity-bound `Buck.approve(spender, amount, E_spender, pi_CP)'
  serves a dual role under this model.  Its primary job is unchanged –
  it produces the CP receipt fragment that gates identity-bound
  transfers between the (sender, spender) pair.  Its second job is to
  express the counterparty's consent to receive Carrying transfers from
  `spender' if `spender' is (or becomes) Carrying-flagged.


10.1 One approve covers both directions
───────────────────────────────────────

  The CP receipt is checked symmetrically for any transfer between the
  two identities, regardless of which one ends up as `msg.sender'.  When
  the spender is a service contract (`isCarrying = true') and pushes
  BUCK to the recipient, the recipient's prior approve has already
  established the receipt; the contract's Carrying transfer proceeds
  through the same identity-bilateral check as any other identity-bound
  transfer.


10.2 Zero-allowance approves for receive-only consent
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  When a recipient simply wants to enable an incoming Carrying transfer
  (e.g., to receive future deployments from the Jubilee fund) without
  granting any spend allowance, they call:

  ┌────
  │ recipient calls:
  │   Buck.approve(carryingAccount, 0, E_carryingAccount, pi_CP)
  └────
  This creates the receipt fragment without granting allowance.
  `amount=0' is exactly the semantic the user wants: identity binding
  plus consent-to-receive without spending authorisation.


10.3 Approval freezes `isCarrying'
──────────────────────────────────

  Per the immutability rule above, the first approve naming
  `carryingAccount' as the spender freezes
  `isCarrying[carryingAccount]'.  A wallet UI presenting an approve
  confirmation should surface the current `isCarrying' value so the user
  can see whether they're consenting to a Carrying-flavour relationship;
  once they approve, the operator can no longer change the flavour.


10.4 Non-Carrying senders
─────────────────────────

  The existing identity-bound approve applies unchanged: a non-Carrying
  sender approves a spender with a non-zero allowance and a CP receipt;
  `transferFrom' checks both the allowance and the receipt.
  Non-Carrying transfers carry no fee debt to the recipient – the
  recipient receives fresh BUCKs – so no extra consent dimension is
  involved.


11 Lien redemption
══════════════════

  A BuckCredit NFT backs a BUCK mint via an off-chain insured asset.
  When the BuckCredit's "close" mechanic is invoked (asset returned,
  lien released), it must reverse the original mint by burning BUCK
  equal to the originally-insured face value.  The burn is sourced
  jointly from the original holder (`alice') and the Jubilee fund, in
  proportion to the elapsed fraction of the credit's lifetime.


11.1 Mechanics
──────────────

  For a BuckCredit insured at face value `F' with lifetime `L', closed
  at elapsed time `e' (with `e <= L'):

  ┌────
  │ function close(BuckCreditId):
  │     F = creditFace(id)
  │     L = creditLifetime(id)
  │     e = now - creditMintTime(id)
  │ 
  │     fromJubilee = F * min(e, L) / L
  │     fromAlice   = F - fromJubilee
  │ 
  │     Buck._accrueJubilee()                 // bring Jubilee current
  │     Buck._crystallize(alice)
  │     Buck._crystallize(jubilee)
  │ 
  │     require(_balanceof[alice]   >= fromAlice,   "alice underfunded")
  │     require(_balanceof[jubilee] >= fromJubilee, "jubilee underfunded")
  │ 
  │     Buck.super._burn(alice,   fromAlice)
  │     Buck.super._burn(jubilee, fromJubilee)
  │     // BuckCredit NFT then releases the asset to alice and self-burns.
  └────


11.2 Worked example: 50-year credit closed at year 50
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Alice mints BUCK$100 at `t=0' (let `F=100', `L=50yr', no premium for
  simplicity).  No further events for 50 years.

  At `t=50yr', BuckCredit calls `close':

  ┌────
  │ 1) _accrueJubilee():
  │      elapsed = 50yr
  │      delta   = 100 * 2%/yr * 50yr = $100
  │      _crystallize(jubilee):  no-op (raw=0 since genesis)
  │      super._mint(jubilee, $100):  _balanceof[jubilee] = $100
  │      totalSupply: $100 -> $200
  │ 
  │ 2) _crystallize(alice):
  │      pending_fee = 100 * 2%/yr * 50yr = $100
  │      _demurrage[alice] = $100,  _timestamp[alice] = t50
  │ 
  │ 3) Compute split:
  │      fromJubilee = $100 * 50/50 = $100
  │      fromAlice   = $100 - $100  = $0
  │ 
  │ 4) Burns:
  │      super._burn(alice,   $0):  no change
  │      super._burn(jubilee, $100): _balanceof[jubilee] = 0
  │      totalSupply: $200 -> $100
  │ 
  │ 5) BuckCredit releases the asset to alice and self-destructs.
  └────
  Alice's `_balanceof' remains $100, but `_demurrage[alice]' = $100 so
  `balanceOf(alice)' = $0.  The original mint has been fully redeemed:
  alice paid the full $100 of demurrage over 50 years (it accrued in her
  `_demurrage[alice]' at lien-close time), and the Jubilee fund burned
  the matching $100 it had accumulated.


11.3 Worked example: closed at year 25 (half-elapsed)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Same setup, `close' at `t=25yr':

  ┌────
  │ 1) _accrueJubilee():
  │      delta = 100 * 2%/yr * 25yr = $50
  │      super._mint(jubilee, $50):  _balanceof[jubilee] = $50
  │      totalSupply: $100 -> $150
  │ 
  │ 2) _crystallize(alice):
  │      pending_fee = 100 * 2%/yr * 25yr = $50
  │      _demurrage[alice] = $50,  _timestamp[alice] = t25
  │ 
  │ 3) Compute split:
  │      fromJubilee = $100 * 25/50 = $50
  │      fromAlice   = $100 - $50   = $50
  │ 
  │ 4) Burns:
  │      super._burn(alice,   $50): _balanceof[alice]   = $50
  │      super._burn(jubilee, $50): _balanceof[jubilee] = 0
  │      totalSupply: $150 -> $50
  │ 
  │ 5) Asset returned, BuckCredit destroyed.
  └────
  After early closure alice retains `$50' of raw BUCK with
  `_demurrage[alice]' still $50 – giving `balanceOf(alice)' = $0
  immediately.  The implementation may choose to clear
  `_demurrage[alice]' as part of `close', in which case alice walks away
  with $50 spendable; this is a policy decision tied to BuckCredit's
  specific close terms.  (Discuss further with the BuckCredit design.)


11.3.1 Simplifications
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  The true Jubilee account accrues demurrage on its own holdings.  These
  examples ignore this for simplicity.  In practice, the Jubilee fund
  deploys its holdings to low-risk revenue generating contracts (AMM
  pools, parametric insurance, …), which earn at least the owed
  demurrage fees and return the profits to the pool.  So, the Jubilee
  fund ends up holding the required funds without demurrage fees owing.


11.4 Why no compound interest is required
─────────────────────────────────────────

  The integral identity that makes the redemption work cleanly:
  `BASE_RATE * totalSupply * elapsed' is exactly the amount Jubilee can
  offer at lien close, and it equals exactly what the holder has accrued
  in locked fees over that elapsed period.  Both sides sum to `F' over
  `L' for a credit that ages its full lifetime.  There is no need for
  any account to compound interest on demurrage; the rate is applied to
  raw `_balanceof' linearly, and Jubilee's accumulation tracks the same
  linear integral across all outstanding BUCK.


12 Conservation invariants
══════════════════════════

  Verifiable on every block, after every operation:

  1. *Raw conservation.*
     ┌────
     │ sum_a _balanceof[a] = totalSupply
     └────
     ERC-20 super-state.  `mint()', `burn()', and `_accrueJubilee()' are
     the only writes that move `totalSupply'.

  2. *Effective spendable identity.*
     ┌────
     │ sum_a balanceOf(a) + sum_a balanceOfFees(a) = totalSupply
     └────
     Direct from `balanceOf = _balanceof - balanceOfFees' and
     `balanceOfFees <= _balanceof'.

  3. *Demurrage rate identity.* Between any two adjacent mint/burn
     events at times `t_0' and `t_1' (`totalSupply' constant):
     ┌────
     │ d(sum_a balanceOfFees(a)) / dt = BASE_RATE * totalSupply
     └────
     The locked-fee total grows at exactly the demurrage rate times the
     supply.  Across multiple intervals this integrates to `BASE_RATE *
     area_under_supply', which is the cumulative Jubilee target.  When
     `_accrueJubilee()' materializes a delta, it shifts that growth from
     "locked in user accounts" to "live in `_balanceof[address(this)]'"
     without changing any sum.

  4. *Carrying-transfer invariant.* A transfer whose sender has
     `isCarrying = true' leaves both sides unchanged in `sum balanceOf'
     and `sum balanceOfFees' (apart from rounding).  The locked-fee
     total is conserved, with the carried amount moving from sender's
     implicit basis to recipient's explicit `_demurrage[to]'.

  5. *Non-Carrying transfer invariant.* A transfer whose sender has
     `isCarrying = false' leaves `sum balanceOf' unchanged;
     `balanceOfFees(from)' crystallizes into `_demurrage[from]' (no
     change in the reported view).  Sum totals are preserved.

  6. *isCarrying-immutability invariant.* For any address `a' such that
     `carryingFrozen[a]' is true, `isCarrying[a]' cannot be modified by
     any subsequent transaction.  `carryingFrozen[a]' becomes true the
     first time `Buck.approve' names `a' as the spender (via the
     registry's `markApproved(a)' hook).


13 Comparison with a "pure-view Jubilee" model
══════════════════════════════════════════════

  An earlier draft considered a model in which the Jubilee fund had no
  ERC-20 storage at all; `balanceOf(address(this))' would be a computed
  view (`BASE_RATE * area_under_supply' minus a self-demurrage haircut),
  and no `_accrueJubilee' would mint fresh BUCK.  Reasons that model was
  rejected:

  • *Lien redemption.* `BuckCredit.close' must burn BUCKs from the
    Jubilee.  A virtual balance cannot be burned without inventing a
    parallel supply concept; making the Jubilee a real ERC-20 holder is
    operationally simpler and matches user intuition.
  • *Carrying transfers from Jubilee.* A Carrying transfer pushes
    `_balanceof[jubilee]' down by `value' and the recipient's
    `_balanceof[to]' up.  Both are real ERC-20 movements; both sides
    must be backed by real raw.
  • *Inflation matches lockedness.* The fresh BUCK minted to Jubilee at
    every checkpoint exactly equals the amount of locked fees that have
    accumulated in user accounts over the same interval.  `totalSupply'
    grows; user =balanceOf=s shrink; the gap appears as Jubilee's raw
    balance.  The bookkeeping is local and inspectable.

  The current model accepts `totalSupply' growth between `mint=/=burn'
  events as the cost of having a real Jubilee account that can transact.
  The growth is bounded by the locked-fee identity above and is fully
  unwound at lien close.


14 Implementation sketch
════════════════════════

14.1 `Buck.sol' storage
───────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ mapping(address => uint256) internal _demurrage;
  │ mapping(address => uint64)  internal _timestamp;
  │ uint64                      internal _jubileeLastUpdate;
  └────
  Per-account `_balanceof[a]' continues to be inherited from OZ ERC20.
  The previous `_indexAtLastTouch[a]' mapping (cumulative- index style)
  is replaced by `(_demurrage[a], _timestamp[a])' per the model above.
  The previous `_areaAcc', `_areaLastUpdate' and `_cumIndex' storage is
  no longer needed: the Jubilee accrual reads `totalSupply()' and the
  elapsed time directly.


14.2 `IdentityRegistry' storage additions
─────────────────────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ mapping(address => bool)    public isCarrying;
  │ mapping(address => bool)    public carryingFrozen;
  │ mapping(address => address) public binderOf;
  │ address                     public buck;             // set once via setBuck()
  └────
  `isCarrying[a]' is set at registration / binding time and read on
  every `Buck._update' to dispatch the transfer flavour.
  `carryingFrozen[a]' is set to true the first time `a' appears as the
  spender on an identity-bound `approve()' call; once frozen,
  `isCarrying[a]' cannot be changed.  `binderOf[a]' records the
  `msg.sender' of the `bindContract()' call (zero for EOAs, which
  therefore can never be targets of `setIsCarrying').  `buck' is a
  one-time governance setter naming the authorised `Buck' contract – the
  only address permitted to call `markApproved'.


14.3 `Buck.sol' public/external view surface
────────────────────────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ function feeOwing(address a)          external view returns (uint256);
  │ function balanceOfFees(address a)     external view returns (uint256);
  │ function balanceOf(address a)         public  view override returns (uint256);
  │ function rawBalanceOf(address a)      external view returns (uint256);
  │ function jubileeBalance()             external view returns (uint256);
  └────
  (`jubileeBalance()' is just an alias for `balanceOf(address (this))' –
  there is no separate "target" view because target no longer exists as
  a distinct concept; it is implicit in the locked-fee sum.)

  There is *no* `transferCarrying' public function.  The two transfer
  flavours are dispatched internally based on
  `IdentityRegistry.isCarrying(msg.sender)' (or `from' for
  `transferFrom').


14.4 `Buck.sol' internal helpers
────────────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ function _crystallize(address a) internal;
  │ function _accrueJubilee()        internal;
  │ function _nonCarryingTransfer(address from, address to, uint256 value) internal;
  │ function _carryingTransfer(address from, address to, uint256 value)    internal;
  └────


14.5 ERC-20 hook dispatch
─────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ function _update(address from, address to, uint256 value) internal override {
  │     if (from == address(0) || to == address(0)) {
  │         // mint / burn -- _accrueJubilee then super._update; see "Mint and burn".
  │         _accrueJubilee();
  │         if (from != address(0)) _crystallize(from);
  │         if (to   != address(0)) _crystallize(to);
  │         super._update(from, to, value);
  │         return;
  │     }
  │ 
  │     if (identity.isCarrying(from)) {
  │         _carryingTransfer(from, to, value);
  │     } else {
  │         _nonCarryingTransfer(from, to, value);
  │     }
  │ }
  └────


14.6 `IdentityRegistry' API additions
─────────────────────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ // One-time governance setter for the authorised Buck contract.
  │ // Subsequent attempts revert; only callable while buck == address(0).
  │ function setBuck(address _buck) external;
  │ 
  │ // register() leaves isCarrying[msg.sender] = false (EOAs are always
  │ // Non-Carrying); binderOf[msg.sender] is left unset, so the EOA can
  │ // never be the target of setIsCarrying.
  │ //
  │ // bindContract() takes an explicit isCarrying_ flag.  Service
  │ // contracts pass true; user-controlled smart-contract wallets pass
  │ // false.  The caller is recorded in binderOf[target].
  │ function bindContract(
  │     address target,
  │     BN254.G1Point calldata pk,
  │     ElGamalCT calldata E,
  │     bool isPublicIdentity_,
  │     bool isCarrying_
  │ ) external;
  │ 
  │ // Pre-approval reconfiguration of the carrying flag.  Reverts if
  │ // the target has already been frozen by a counterparty's approve.
  │ function setIsCarrying(address target, bool value) external;
  │ 
  │ // Called from Buck.approve() to freeze the spender's flag.  Reverts
  │ // unless msg.sender == buck.  Idempotent across multiple approvers.
  │ function markApproved(address spender) external;
  └────
  Receipt fragments and identity binding (existing `IdentityRegistry'
  flow) are layered on top unchanged: the identity check for both
  flavours of transfer happens at the public function boundary, before
  the demurrage logic runs.  `Buck.approve' calls
  `identity.markApproved(spender)' immediately after writing the CP
  receipt so the spender's `isCarrying' flag becomes immutable from that
  moment on.


15 Open questions and policy choices
════════════════════════════════════

  These are decisions to be settled in companion documents or in the
  implementation:


15.1 Should `close' clear `_demurrage' on the redeeming account?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  After early closure (`e < L') the holder's `_demurrage[a]' still
  reflects the locked fees from before the close.  The holder retains
  some raw, but `balanceOf(a)' reads as zero until those fees
  re-amortize.  The implementation may choose to zero `_demurrage[a]' as
  part of `close', freeing the residual immediately, or to leave it in
  place and let it re-amortize over the remaining "credit-free" period.
  This is a BuckCredit policy choice, not a Buck demurrage primitive.


15.2 What happens if a Carrying account is unfunded at close time?
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  If the Jubilee fund's `_balanceof' is insufficient to satisfy
  `fromJubilee' (because someone else minted between the
  `_accrueJubilee()' step and the burn – in practice impossible within a
  single transaction, but worth specifying), the close should revert and
  the BuckCredit should be re-tried in a later block.  The
  mint/burn-only Jubilee accrual is sufficient to keep the fund solvent
  at the moment of any close.


15.3 Multiple BuckCredits and shared Jubilee
────────────────────────────────────────────

  The Jubilee fund is a single pool serving all BuckCredits
  simultaneously.  Its accrual rate is on `totalSupply', not on any
  per-credit notion.  A close consumes Jubilee funds in proportion to
  the elapsed fraction of the closing credit's lifetime; the fund is
  replenished at every subsequent `mint=/=burn' elsewhere in the system.
  The model degrades gracefully under heavy redemption load: if many
  credits close at once, `_accrueJubilee()' simply tracks the cumulative
  `totalSupply * elapsed' integral and Jubilee's `_balanceof' covers the
  joint demand.


15.4 Long-idle Carrying accounts
────────────────────────────────

  A Carrying account whose policy is to hold BUCK indefinitely will
  accumulate self-demurrage that depresses its `balanceOf' relative to
  its raw.  This is intended behaviour – it incentivises Carrying
  accounts to deploy funds rather than hoard.  However, very long idle
  periods can produce `balanceOf(carrying) << _balanceof(carrying)', and
  a Carrying transfer of the full raw would push a large carried fee
  onto the recipient.  The contract enforces only the technical
  constraint `value <= _balanceof'; recipient consent (via the
  zero-allowance approve) is the social control.


15.5 Premium routing and the insurance pool
───────────────────────────────────────────

  The mint `premium' computed by `_computePremium' is routed to
  `insurancePool', a non-Carrying account.  The insurance pool accrues
  demurrage on its holdings like any other account; when it deploys
  funds it does so via standard non-Carrying `transfer'.  No special
  handling is required.
