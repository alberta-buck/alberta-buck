The Alberta Buck /Wormhole/ ([Wormhole]) and /Cash/ ([Cash]) designs
both need a privacy-preserving bridge from an on-chain balance to a
later on-chain mint.  In the current series both designs supply their
own mechanics: the Wormhole uses per-certificate burn addresses and
`_mint_noincrease'; Cash uses a Zerocash-style commitment tree with Mode
A/B/C spend predicates (bearer, stealth-targeted, programmable).  The
overlap is nearly complete – both reduce to "burn into a pool, mint out
by SNARK" – and maintaining two architectures doubles the verifier
surface, the circuit count, and the audit scope without buying
additional functionality.

This document collapses the two into one primitive.  A single Merkle
tree of note commitments and a single nullifier set replace both the
burn-address family and the Cash note pool.  Three /Identity bindings/
parameterise the commitment:

• *Type 1 – Encrypted Issuer Identity.* Spend predicate requires the
  depositor's registered ElGamal credential to encrypt the same identity
  point the issuer's credential encrypts.  Subsumes the v1 Wormhole: the
  issuer and the eventual depositor must share an Identity scalar, but
  may use unlinkable key pairs.
• *Type 2 – Public Issuer Identity.* Spend predicate requires the
  depositor to be any registered identity; the issuer's public identity
  is bound to the note in plaintext.  A cheque whose maker is known,
  whose bearer is not.
• *Type 3 – Stealth-Targeted to Recipient.* Spend predicate requires the
  depositor to hold the secret of a stealth public key derived from the
  recipient's registered identity.  A cheque made out to a specific
  recipient, retrievable only by them.

Demurrage needs no new machinery.  The Note Pool /is/ simply the BUCK
ERC-20 contract's own balance – a regular BUCK account accumulating
BUCK-age like any other account.  Mint: the user transfers \( v \) BUCK
to the contract (a standard ERC-20 transfer; sender pays their own
outgoing demurrage), incrementing both the contract's balance \( B \)
and a scalar \( F \) (sum of face values of live notes).  Redemption is
an /age-preserving transfer/ unique to the Notes contract's single
egress path: the recipient's nominal balance rises by the full face
value \( v \), and their BUCK-age rises by \( v \cdot A/B \), where \( A
\) and \( B \) are the pool account's accumulated BUCK-age and balance
in the existing ERC-20 ledger.  That is: the pool's age-per-BUCK follows
the redeemed value to the recipient, who then bears the pool's share of
demurrage on their /next/ outgoing transfer via the standard BUCK
accounting.  This is exactly the cost a user would have paid had they
held \( v \) BUCK personally through the pool's residence period.
Donations to the contract dilute \( A/B \) (donations add balance with
no age), directly lowering the age-share absorbed by all subsequent
redemptions.  Conservation is automatic: balance and age are each
exactly transferred, nothing is created or destroyed; the Jubilee
continues to grow autonomously at \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \).
No face discount, no division in the delivered amount, no \( F \) in the
formula – only a single new primitive (age-preserving transfer),
localized to one contract function.

The consequence most relevant to users: only notes where the issuer
cannot later race the holder to spend are safe for long-term holding.
This is *Type 1* (issuer = depositor, same Identity, no third party
knows the note secret) and *Type 3* (the issuer's knowledge of the note
secret does not suffice to spend – only the stealth-derived secret can).
Type 2's public-issuer attribution comes at the cost of retained
issuer-spend capability, restricting it to short-lived use cases
(cheques presented within hours or days, not stored for years).  This is
an intrinsic cryptographic property, not a policy choice. ([PDF],
[Text])

1 The Redundancy Between Wormhole and Cash
══════════════════════════════════════════

  The v1 Wormhole ([Wormhole]) and the BUCK Cash proposal ([Cash]) both
  solve the same core problem by different mechanical routes.


[Wormhole]
<https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-wormhole>

[Cash] <https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-cash>

1.1 The Problem Both Solve
──────────────────────────

  Each requires a cryptographic pipeline that:
  1. Accepts a burn of an existing on-chain balance
  2. Creates a durable, privacy-preserving token (certificate or note)
     referencing the burn
  3. Permits a later on-chain mint that can be proved (in zero
     knowledge) to correspond to some prior burn
  4. Prevents double-spending via a global nullifier
  5. Preserves BUCK's demurrage economics (the minted amount must
     reflect accumulated demurrage across the burn-to-mint interval)
  6. Composes with the existing Identity system (so that the mint lands
     in a properly registered, KYC-verified account)


1.2 The Two Current Mechanisms
──────────────────────────────

  *Wormhole v1 (burn-address model).* Each certificate is anchored by a
  distinct EVM address that receives a fixed-denomination transfer.  The
  burn is an ordinary `transfer' call; the ZK proof binds the
  destination mint to the source burn through the `_mint_noincrease'
  primitive that transfers balance and BUCK-age into the destination
  without adjusting `totalSupply'.  Each burn address costs an identity
  registration, an `approveFor' handshake, and a state-trie slot.

  *BUCK Cash (note-pool model).* A contract-managed Merkle tree of note
  commitments replaces the per-burn-address state anchor.  A single burn
  transaction appends \( N \) commitments to the tree and emits the
  summed value into the contract reserve.  Each spend is a SNARK proving
  inclusion of some commitment and emission of a unique nullifier.  The
  spend /predicate/ inside the commitment distinguishes bearer (Mode A),
  stealth-targeted (Mode B), and programmable (Mode C) notes.


1.3 The Overlap
───────────────

  Functionally these are nearly identical.  Both provide commit-nullify
  privacy; both bind the burn to a later mint through a ZK relation;
  both produce one nullifier per unit redeemed; both depend on the
  Identity system for the minting-account side.  The divergence is
  cosmetic:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Wormhole v1 anchor                               Cash note-pool anchor                              
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   `addr_burn' (state trie leaf)                    `cm_i' (Merkle tree leaf)                          
   `_mint_noincrease' to destination                `mint' directly (supply unchanged by note pool)    
   Per-burn `approveFor' + `approve'                No per-note approval (spend predicate)             
   Fixed-denomination cohort anonymity set          Global pool anonymity set                          
   Spend relation: CP re-encryption (Theorems 3-6)  Spend relation: predicate-specific (Theorems 4, 7) 
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  What neither design fully expresses is that the /spend relation/ is
  orthogonal to the pool structure: Chaum-Pedersen re-encryption,
  stealth-address Schnorr, and arbitrary predicates can /all/ coexist as
  different spend circuits on the same note pool.  If we take this
  seriously, burn addresses and `_mint_noincrease' become redundant: the
  note pool absorbs the Wormhole as one spend-predicate family among
  many.


1.4 Maintenance Cost of Duality
───────────────────────────────

  Keeping both mechanisms in production doubles:
  • *Verifier code.* Two Merkle-style lookups, two nullifier-set
    discipline rules, two edge-case sets for the auditor.
  • *SNARK circuits.* The Wormhole CP-re-encryption circuit and the Cash
    bearer/targeted/programmable circuits do not share skeleton code;
    the proof systems compile separately.
  • *Demurrage bookkeeping.* The Wormhole's per-certificate BUCK-age is
    computed against EVM storage; the Cash note's per-commitment \(
    (t_{\text{print}}, age_0) \) is computed in the SNARK.  Two distinct
    conservation invariants.
  • *Identity integration.* Wormhole requires `approveFor' against the
    burn-address pseudo-account; Cash predicates compose differently
    with Schnorr authorisations.
  • *Documentation.* Two designs to teach, two attack tables to
    maintain, two sets of open questions.

  A unified architecture reduces all of this to one audit, one circuit
  family, one invariant.


2 The Single Primitive: Notes in One Global Pool
════════════════════════════════════════════════

  The unified design replaces both mechanisms with a single primitive.


2.1 The Note Contract State
───────────────────────────

  The BUCK contract gains exactly three new pieces of state:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   State          Type                         Purpose                                         
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   `noteTree'     Merkle tree of commitments   Cryptographic anchor for every outstanding note 
   `nullifiers'   Set of used nullifiers       Double-spend prevention                         
   `noteFaceSum'  \( F \) in \( \mathbb{Z} \)  Sum of face values of all outstanding notes     
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  That is all.  The pool's nominal BUCK balance \( B \) and accumulated
  BUCK-age \( A \) are not tracked as separate state: /the pool is just
  a BUCK account/, specifically the ERC-20 contract's own address, and
  \( B, A \) are exactly the balance and BUCK-age the ERC-20 ledger
  already maintains for it.  No lazy integration, no shadow aggregates,
  no separate `poolT' timestamp.  `noteFaceSum' is a pure audit scalar –
  the total face-value liability outstanding – and does not appear in
  the redemption formula itself.

  BUCK transferred to the contract address (via mint, donation, or
  accident) joins \( B \) through the normal ERC-20 path.  BUCK
  transferred out of the contract on redemption uses a /single new
  primitive/: an age-preserving transfer that moves both balance and
  proportional BUCK-age from the contract to the recipient.  This is the
  only non-standard BUCK operation in the design, analogous to
  `_mint_noincrease' in v1 Wormhole.


2.2 The Note Commitment
───────────────────────

  A note commitment is a Poseidon hash of the fields the SNARK will
  later open:

  \[ cm \;=\; H_{\text{cm}}\!\left(\text{type},\; v,\; \rho,\;
     \text{id}_{\text{payload}},\; \text{predicate}\right) \]

  with:

  • *type* \( \in \{1, 2, 3\} \): the Identity-binding mode
  • *v* \( \in \mathbb{Z}_q \): face value
  • *\( \rho \)*: per-note randomness (source of the nullifier)
  • *\( \text{id}_{\text{payload}} \)*: Identity-binding data (see next
     section)
  • *predicate*: optional additional spend predicate (for future Mode C
    programmability – not elaborated in this document; a single bit
    `predicate =' None= keeps the SNARK to the three base types)

  No per-note timestamp or baked-in BUCK-age: demurrage is computed at
  redemption time from the /pool's/ account state \( (B, A) \), not from
  the individual note's life history, so no per-note age fields need to
  ride in the commitment.

  The commitment is inserted as a leaf of `noteTree' at mint time.


2.3 The Note Nullifier
──────────────────────

  The nullifier is a pseudo-random function of the note's secret witness
  material:

  \[ nf \;=\; \text{PRF}_{\text{key}}(\rho) \]

  where `key' is the spend-side secret specific to the note type (the
  issuer's \( sk \) in Type 1, any registered spender's \( sk \) in Type
  2, the stealth \( sk^\ast \) in Type 3).  The PRF is instantiated via
  Poseidon in the SNARK-friendly setting.

  The nullifier is emitted on deposit; double-spend is rejected by
  set-membership check.


2.4 Lifecycle
─────────────

  ┌────
  │ mint (burn):
  │   # Standard ERC-20 transfer from caller to contract address.
  │   # Caller pays their own outgoing-transfer demurrage through the
  │   # normal BUCK accounting path.  Contract balance B gains v; its
  │   # accumulated BUCK-age A starts earning on the new v from here.
  │   transfer v BUCK from caller to contract
  │   for each of {cm_1, ..., cm_N}:
  │      append cm_i to noteTree
  │   noteFaceSum += sum(v_i)
  │ 
  │ deposit (mint-out):
  │   verify SNARK:
  │     - Merkle inclusion of some cm in noteTree
  │     - Knowledge of (v, rho, id_payload, predicate)
  │     - Predicate satisfied by witness (type-specific)
  │     - nullifier correctness
  │     - v is a public output of the SNARK
  │   require nf not in nullifiers
  │   nullifiers += nf
  │   # Read pool state from the ERC-20 ledger:
  │   (B, A) = (balanceOf(contract), buckAgeOf(contract))
  │   ageShare = v * A / B
  │   # Age-preserving transfer (the one new primitive):
  │   #   contract.balance -= v;   contract.age -= ageShare
  │   #   recipient.balance += v;  recipient.age += ageShare
  │   transferPreservingAge(contract, recipient, v, ageShare)
  │   noteFaceSum -= v
  └────

  Full face value is delivered: the recipient's nominal balance goes up
  by exactly \( v \).  The pool's demurrage cost rides along as the
  BUCK-age \( v \cdot A/B \) added to the recipient's account.  The
  recipient will bear that demurrage on their /next/ outgoing transfer
  through the ordinary BUCK accounting path – the same path any holder
  travels, but on an age they acquired by accepting the note.  No
  Jubilee payment is emitted from inside the deposit; the Jubilee grows
  autonomously at \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \), which continues
  to include both \( B \) (before redemption) and \( v \) (after, as
  part of recipient's balance).


3 Three Identity Bindings
═════════════════════════

  Every note commits to one of three /Identity-binding/ payloads.  The
  binding determines who can spend the note and what observers learn
  about the issuer.


3.1 Type 1: Encrypted Issuer Identity ("Wormhole")
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

  *Identity payload.* \[ \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)} \;=\;
  E_{\text{issuer}} \;=\; (R_i, C_i) \;=\; (r_i G,\; M_{\text{issuer}} +
  r_i \cdot pk_{\text{issuer}}) \]

  where \( pk_{\text{issuer}} \) is the issuer's own identity public key
  at mint time, \( M_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \), and
  \( r_i \) is fresh randomness.  This is a standard ElGamal encryption
  of the issuer's Identity point under their own key – the same
  structure registered credentials use.

  *Spend predicate.* A depositor with registered credential \(
  E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d) \) under their own \( pk_{\text{dep}} \)
  submits a SNARK proving the Chaum-Pedersen relation \( C_i -
  sk_{\text{issuer}} R_i = C_d - sk_{\text{dep}} R_d \).  That is, the
  two credentials decrypt to the same Identity point.

  This is Theorem 6 of the proofs document ([Proofs]) lifted verbatim
  into the SNARK; a SNARK-verified instance of the CP relation implies
  \( m_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{dep}} \), i.e., /same underlying
  Identity/.

  *Consequence.* Only accounts whose underlying Identity scalar matches
  the issuer's can spend a Type 1 note.  This is the Wormhole semantics:
  Alice mints a Type 1 note from address \( A_1 \) and later deposits at
  address \( A_2 \), where both addresses carry Alice's Identity
  (different registered credentials, same \( M \)).  Observers cannot
  link the two addresses beyond the Identity-constant relation they
  already have.


[Proofs] <file:alberta-buck-proofs.org>


3.2 Type 2: Public Issuer Identity ("Signed Cheque")
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  *Identity payload.* \[ \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(2)} \;=\;
  (m_{\text{issuer}},\; \sigma_{\text{issuer}}) \]

  where \( m_{\text{issuer}} \in \mathbb{Z}_q \) is the issuer's
  Identity scalar /in plaintext/ and \( \sigma_{\text{issuer}} \) is a
  Schnorr signature on \( cm \) under the issuer's registered \(
  pk_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \) (or a signed
  commitment to the note; details equivalent).

  Anyone reading the commitment openings learns who issued the note: the
  Identity point \( M_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \) is
  in the clear, and since this is a public-identity account, its
  real-world identity is world-readable from the IdentityRegistry.

  *Spend predicate.* The SNARK proves:

  1. Knowledge of \( \rho \) such that \( nf = \text{PRF}_\rho(\cdot) \)
  2. \( \sigma_{\text{issuer}} \) verifies against the registered \(
     pk_{\text{issuer}} \) in IdentityRegistry (SNARK-inside signature
     verification)
  3. The depositor is /some/ registered identity: Schnorr proof of
     knowledge of \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for a \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) that
     IdentityRegistry attests to (anonymous-valid) or whose public
     identity is published (public)

  No CP-equality is required – the issuer's identity is already known,
  the depositor's identity is anyone properly registered.

  *Consequence.* Any registered identity can deposit.  The issuer
  retains the right to spend their own note (by virtue of knowing \(
  \rho \)), so holders of Type 2 notes bear /printer-race risk/: if the
  issuer kept \( \rho \), they can race the holder to deposit.  Type 2
  is appropriate for transactions that close within hours or days, not
  long-term holding.  See section "Long-Term Holding Safety".


3.3 Type 3: Stealth-Targeted to Recipient ("Addressed Cheque")
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  *Identity payload.* \[ \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(3)} \;=\; (R_e,\;
  pk^\ast) \]

  where \( R_e = r_e G \) is an ephemeral published with the note and

  \[ pk^\ast \;=\; H_{\text{st}}(r_e \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}}) \cdot
     pk_{\text{recipient}}.  \]

  \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \) is /not/ in the payload – it would reveal
  the target to observers.  Only the recipient, scanning notes with
  their own \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \), can recognise a note as
  addressed to them: for each candidate note they compute \( s =
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \cdot R_e \) and check whether \(
  H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \) equals the note's \(
  pk^\ast \) (see Corollary 3 of Theorem 7 in [Proofs]).

  *Spend predicate.* The SNARK proves knowledge of a scalar \( \alpha \)
  with \( \alpha \cdot G = pk^\ast \).  By Theorem 7 this implies the
  prover knows the stealth secret \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \), which reduces to knowing \(
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \) (the issuer cannot compute \( sk^\ast \) from
  \( r_e, pk_{\text{recipient}} \) alone without breaking DLog).

  The depositor also produces a Schnorr PoK of knowledge of \(
  sk_{\text{dep}} \) for their own registered \( pk_{\text{dep}} \),
  binding the deposit to a compliant mint-out identity.  Optionally, the
  recipient's credential \( E_{\text{recipient}} \) can be used
  directly: the SNARK proves CP-equality between the decrypted \(
  M_{\text{recipient}} \) and the depositor's registered \(
  E_{\text{dep}} \), making the deposit identity-binding.  The
  minimal-privacy variant omits this and lets any registered depositor
  submit the stealth-spend (suitable when the recipient wants
  pseudonymous redemption to a fresh account).

  *Consequence.* Only the recipient – the holder of \(
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \) – can spend the note.  The issuer /cannot/
  spend what she printed (Theorem 7, Corollary 1 – "printer
  non-fungibility").  Third parties who see the published note learn
  nothing about \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \).  Type 3 is safe for
  long-term holding because the issuer's knowledge of \( r_e \) does not
  translate into spending power.


[Proofs] <file:alberta-buck-proofs.org>


3.4 Comparison of the Three Bindings
────────────────────────────────────

     cheque" primitive: a public issuer commits to a transfer that any
     registered holder can realise.  This is the natural form for
     payroll issuance, charitable disbursements, and
     attribution-preserving payments that current BUCK flow lacks.

     decides which of their public accounts to deposit to.

     visibility at mint time and the depositor's account visibility at
     deposit time are chosen independently.  The matrix above shows the
     /depositor/ dimension; a full 3x2x2 enumeration would duplicate
     most cells without adding structural insight.

5 Demurrage at the Pool Level
═════════════════════════════

  The central simplification of this design: *the Note Pool is just a
  regular BUCK account.* Specifically, it is the BUCK ERC-20 contract's
  own balance.  BUCKs transferred to the contract's address join the
  pool; BUCKs transferred out (as note redemptions) leave the pool.  The
  contract account accumulates BUCK-age like every other account and
  participates in the ordinary demurrage accounting with no special-case
  code.  The only piece of additional state is a single scalar \( F \),
  the sum of face values of live notes.


5.1 Reminder: How BUCK Demurrage Actually Works
───────────────────────────────────────────────

  Demurrage is /not transferred/ on any per-account basis.  It is an
  accounting entry maintained on each account's state:

  • Every block, the Jubilee account's balance grows by \( \kappa \cdot
    \text{totalSupply} \cdot \Delta t \).  This is a single global
    operation; the sum of all other accounts' nominal balances is held
    constant.
  • Each account \( X \) has a running BUCK-age \( A_X \) that grows at
    rate \( B_X \) (its nominal balance) per unit time.
  • When account \( X \) transfers \( v \) BUCK out, its spendable
    fraction is \( (B_X - \kappa A_X)/B_X \); the outgoing transfer
    costs the account \( v \cdot B_X / (B_X - \kappa A_X) \) of its
    nominal balance, and the corresponding \( \kappa A_X \cdot v / (B_X
    - \kappa A_X) \) demurrage "owed" is simply extinguished from \(
    (B_X, A_X) \) against the Jubilee's autonomous growth.

  System-wide invariant (preserved by every transfer):

  \[ \sum_{\text{all accounts } X} B_X \;+\; B_{\text{Jubilee}} \;=\;
     \text{constant}.  \]

  The Jubilee balance reflects the aggregate accrual \( \kappa \int_0^t
  \text{totalSupply}(s)\,ds \); individual accounts' \( \kappa A_X \)
  quantities track /their share/ of that total.


5.2 The Pool Account's Demurrage
────────────────────────────────

  Treat the BUCK contract address as an ordinary account.  Let:

  • \( B \) = the contract's ERC-20 balance (= sum of all BUCK
    transferred in via mints and donations, minus all BUCK transferred
    out via redemptions, modulo the standard demurrage adjustment on
    outgoing transfers).
  • \( A \) = the contract's accumulated BUCK-age, as maintained by the
    ERC-20 ledger.

  \( B \) and \( A \) are already on chain; the Notes design queries
  them, it does not compute them.  The pool's /effective spendable
  value/ at any moment is

  \[ E \;=\; B - \kappa \cdot A.  \]

  This is the amount the contract would be able to transfer out if it
  emptied itself in a single call – exactly the same definition that
  applies to any other account.


5.3 The Age-Preserving Redemption
─────────────────────────────────

  When a note of face \( v \) is redeemed, the contract performs an
  /age-preserving transfer/ of \( v \) BUCK from itself to the
  recipient:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Side       Balance change  BUCK-age change    
  ───────────────────────────────────────────────
   Pool       \( -v \)        \( -v \cdot A/B \) 
   Recipient  \( +v \)        \( +v \cdot A/B \) 
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Full face value is delivered; the pool's BUCK-age /proportional share/
  – one average age-per-BUCK times face \( v \) – moves along with the
  balance to the recipient.  The recipient's account absorbs that age
  just as if they had personally held \( v \) BUCK through the pool's
  residence period.

  The pool's /own/ age-per-BUCK ratio \( A/B \) is preserved across the
  redemption: \( A_{\text{new}}/B_{\text{new}} = (A - vA/B) / (B
  • v) = A(B - v) / (B(B - v)) = A/B \).  The ratio drifts only
  through time (age accrues at rate \( B \)) and through mints (fresh
  BUCK arrives age-free, diluting the ratio).

  Contrast with a standard BUCK transfer, where the sender bears the
  demurrage on their side (balance reduced by \( v \cdot B/(B - \kappa
  A) \)) and the recipient receives a clean \( v \) with no age.  The
  age-preserving variant simply /follows/ the balance with its
  proportional age, deferring the demurrage reckoning to the recipient's
  next outgoing transfer.  This is an economically natural choice for a
  Note recipient: they have accepted the note in lieu of a direct BUCK
  holding, and in return accept the demurrage cost that goes with having
  held that BUCK.


5.4 Donations Integrate Smoothly
────────────────────────────────

  Any BUCK transferred to the contract address /without/ minting a
  corresponding note increases \( B \) without adding BUCK-age (the
  sender's age stays with the sender per standard ERC-20 semantics).
  The pool's ratio \( A/B \) drops; subsequent redemptions carry a
  /smaller/ age-share per face unit.  Over a sequence of redemptions the
  donation is consumed as a demurrage subsidy for live noteholders.

  Four origin cases, all handled identically:

  1. *Accidental transfers.* A user fat-fingers the contract as a
     recipient.  Their BUCK is not burned; it dilutes \( A/B \), and
     future redeemers bear marginally less age.
  2. *Philanthropic donations.* A user deliberately sends BUCK to the
     contract to subsidise noteholders.  The subsidy spreads across all
     future redemptions until \( A/B \) drifts back up.
  3. *Recovered collateral.* Mechanism-level refunds (e.g., an issuer's
     collateral unlock) sent to the contract address.
  4. *Rounding / dust.* Integer-arithmetic residuals.  Self-healing.

  After all notes are eventually redeemed, any remaining donation
  principal sits at \( F = 0, B = D_{\text{residual}}, A = 0 \) –
  unclaimable by redemption, but available to subsidise a fresh round of
  mints if the pool is used again.


5.5 Conservation is Automatic, and Tight
────────────────────────────────────────

  The age-preserving transfer conserves both quantities /exactly/:

  • \( \Delta B_{\text{pool}} + \Delta B_{\text{recipient}} = 0 \).
  • \( \Delta A_{\text{pool}} + \Delta A_{\text{recipient}} = 0 \).

  Total BUCK in circulation is unchanged by redemption; total
  accumulated BUCK-age is unchanged by redemption.  The Jubilee
  continues to grow autonomously at rate \( \kappa \cdot
  \text{totalSupply} \) per block; this total is unaffected by whether
  the \( v \) BUCK sits in the pool or in the recipient's account.  And
  whether the demurrage on that age is eventually paid out by the pool's
  (hypothetical) transfer or by the recipient's real outgoing transfer,
  the same \( \kappa \cdot v \cdot A/B \) amount of spendable value will
  be reconciled against the Jubilee.

  No pool-specific conservation theorem is needed beyond the system-wide
  \( \sum_X B_X + B_{\text{Jubilee}} = \text{const} \) (automatic) and
  the local observation that the age-preserving transfer is two-sided
  balanced (evident from its definition).


5.6 Numeric Example
───────────────────

  Suppose the pool holds \( B = 10\,000\,000 \) BUCK with \( A =
  5\,000\,000 \) BUCK-years (average age 0.5 yr), and \( F =
  10\,000\,000 \) BUCK of live notes outstanding.

  Alice redeems a note of face \( v = 1{,}000 \):
  • Alice's balance rises by 1,000 BUCK.
  • Alice's BUCK-age rises by \( 1{,}000 \cdot 5{,}000{,}000 /
    10{,}000{,}000 = 500 \) BUCK-years.  In nominal units: her 1,000
    incoming BUCK has an effective /age/ of 0.5 yr attached to it.
  • When Alice next spends 1,000 BUCK from this account, the outgoing
    transfer reconciles its share of her total BUCK-age against the
    Jubilee: roughly \( 0.02 \cdot 500 = 10 \) BUCK worth of demurrage
    is paid out of her balance (the exact amount depends on her other
    holdings' age).  Net effect: she receives 1,000, pays ~10 when she
    spends them, nets 990.  This matches the previous-model result where
    she received 990 at redemption – same economic total, different
    accounting.

  Now suppose an anonymous donor sends 500,000 BUCK to the contract
  address.  \( B \) rises to \( 10{,}500{,}000 \); the donor's BUCK-age
  stays with them.  The pool's \( A/B \) drops from \( 0.5 \) to \(
  5{,}000{,}000 / 10{,}500{,}000 \approx 0.476 \).  Alice's next
  redemption absorbs a smaller age-share per face – a direct subsidy
  from the donor.

  No Jubilee payment was emitted by the pool during any of this.  The
  Jubilee's growth is unchanged: it keeps accruing \( \kappa \cdot
  \text{totalSupply} \) per block regardless of who holds which BUCK.


5.7 Who Bears the Demurrage, Really
───────────────────────────────────

  The answer is now sharply localized: *the Note recipient bears the
  pool's accumulated share of demurrage on the face value they received,
  and they pay it when they next spend from that account.* The recipient
  has accepted the note, and with it has accepted the demurrage cost of
  the BUCK sitting in the pool while the note was outstanding.

  This is the "price" the recipient pays for accepting BUCK Notes
  instead of a direct BUCK transfer: they inherit the note's
  pool-residence demurrage, prorated across all live notes at the moment
  of redemption.  The trade-off is explicit – and visible in the
  resulting \( (B, A) \) state on the recipient's account.

  Noteholders (while holding) bear no demurrage: they hold a
  cryptographic note, not a BUCK balance, and the BUCK they implicitly
  own sits in the pool where its age is borne collectively.  The issuer
  (at mint time) bears their own outgoing-transfer demurrage to deposit
  into the pool, as for any ERC-20 transfer.  Third parties bear
  nothing.  The Jubilee keeps receiving the BUCK-wide demurrage stream
  \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \) regardless of how BUCK sloshes
  between accounts.


6 Long-Term Holding Safety: The Printer-Trust Distinction
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  Pool-rate demurrage makes every note type equally cheap to /hold/.
  What distinguishes them for long-term storage is not demurrage but the
  /printer-trust/ model: who, besides the intended holder, retains
  information that could let them spend the note?


6.1 The Printer-Trust Model
───────────────────────────

  At mint time, the issuer knows:
  • The note's commitment preimage \( (v, \rho,
    \text{id}_{\text{payload}}, \text{predicate}) \)
  • Whatever additional secret is packed into \(
    \text{id}_{\text{payload}} \) (the issuer's \( sk_{\text{issuer}} \)
    for Type 1, the ephemeral \( r_e \) for Type 3, etc.)

  The question is whether this knowledge enables the issuer – or anyone
  else with the same knowledge – to spend the note later.


6.2 Type 1 (Encrypted Issuer Identity): Safe
────────────────────────────────────────────

  The spend predicate requires a Chaum-Pedersen proof of equality with
  the depositor's /registered/ credential.  The issuer is the intended
  eventual depositor (possibly via a different registered account
  sharing the same \( M \)).  No third party can spend: even with full
  knowledge of \( \rho \), a non-issuer's registered credential does not
  encrypt \( M_{\text{issuer}} \), so the CP relation fails.

  *Long-term holding is safe.* The only person who can spend is the
  issuer, and the issuer is the intended holder.


6.3 Type 2 (Public Issuer Identity): Unsafe for Long-Term Storage
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The spend predicate requires only knowledge of \( \rho \) plus any
  registered identity.  The issuer minted the note, so the issuer knows
  \( \rho \).  If the issuer retains any copy of \( \rho \) after
  printing the physical note – in a database row, a printed backup, a
  backup transaction – the issuer can race the holder to deposit.

  This is not a cryptographic failure; it is a policy failure.  A
  trustworthy issuer /could/ destroy \( \rho \) after printing.  But the
  /holder has no way to verify that destruction/.  The note is only as
  safe as the issuer's operational discipline.

  *Long-term holding is unsafe.* Type 2 notes should be cashed promptly
  (hours to days).  They are a cheque, not a savings instrument.  In the
  failure mode where the issuer does retain \( \rho \), legal recourse
  is via the issuer's registered Identity – which is the point of making
  the issuer /public/: they cannot deny having printed the note, and the
  registered Identity is subpoena-able.

  For use cases that need long-term safety with public attribution
  (e.g., an inheritance instrument where the issuer's identity must be
  known), the natural solution is to use Type 3 (stealth-targeted) and
  separately publish the issuer's identity in a signed accompanying
  statement rather than bake it into the commitment.


6.4 Type 3 (Stealth-Targeted to Recipient): Safe
────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The spend predicate is a Schnorr PoK of the stealth secret \( sk^\ast
  = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot sk_{\text{recipient}} \).  Computing \(
  sk^\ast \) requires \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \), which only the
  recipient possesses.  The issuer knows \( r_e \) and can compute \( s
  = r_e \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \) and \( H_{\text{st}}(s) \), but
  /cannot/ compute \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \) without \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \) (Theorem
  7, Corollary 1 – "printer non-fungibility").

  *Long-term holding is safe.* The issuer's knowledge of every mint-time
  secret does not enable them to spend.  Only the intended recipient,
  through their own \( sk \), can redeem.


6.5 Summary: Which Notes Can Be Saved?
──────────────────────────────────────

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Note type  Issuer can spend later?  Long-term holding safe?  Comparable instrument              
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Type 1     Only to themselves       Yes                      Savings account (self-owned)       
   Type 2     Yes (knows \( \rho \))   No                       Uncashed cheque (needs to clear)   
   Type 3     No (Theorem 7)           Yes                      Registered bond (recipient-locked) 
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  The consequence: /only Type 1 and Type 3 notes are suitable for
  long-term holding./ A user printing a Type 2 cheque and placing it in
  a safe deposit box for a decade is, cryptographically, trusting the
  issuer's operational discipline for a decade; a user doing the same
  with Type 1 or Type 3 is trusting only the cryptography.

  This constrains how the note types map to use cases:

  • *Long-term savings, cold storage, estate, self-wormhole:* Type 1 (if
    you are your own beneficiary) or Type 3 (if a named heir is the
    beneficiary).
  • *Cheques, payroll, invoices:* Type 2, cashed promptly.
  • *Targeted gifts, remittances, inheritance:* Type 3.
  • *Wormhole-style cross-address transfers:* Type 1 exclusively.


7 Required Identity Cryptography
════════════════════════════════

  The unified design re-uses the existing Identity machinery
  ([Identity]) wherever possible, adding one new note-side primitive and
  one re-use of the Theorem 7 stealth-address construction.


[Identity] <file:alberta-buck-identity.org>

7.1 Components Already Present
──────────────────────────────

  The following are unchanged from Identity v1 and are used directly:

  1. *Pointcheval-Sanders issuer signature* \( \sigma \) on the Identity
     scalar \( m \).  Re-randomisation continues to be used for
     unlinkable credential generation (Identity Fountain).  No change.
  2. *ElGamal registered credential* \( E = (R, C) = (r G, M + r \cdot
     pk) \) where \( M = m \cdot G \).  Continues to be the on-chain
     identity witness.  No change.
  3. *Chaum-Pedersen equality of discrete logs*.  Used by Type 1 spend
     predicate for the same-\( M \) proof; identical to the `approve'
     flow's CP proof, just wrapped in a SNARK.  Theorem 6 applies
     unchanged.
  4. *Schnorr PoK of \( sk \) for registered \( pk \)*.  Used by Type 2
     and Type 3 spend predicates to bind the depositing account to a
     registered identity.  Single-statement case of CP, Theorems 3-5
     apply verbatim.
  5. *IdentityRegistry* mapping between on-chain addresses and
     registered credentials (PS signatures, ElGamal ciphertexts,
     public-claim flags).


7.2 New Primitive: The Note Commitment Hash
───────────────────────────────────────────

  A SNARK-friendly commitment hash \( H_{\text{cm}} \) (Poseidon) is
  needed to compute the commitment inside the spend circuit.  The hash
  is deterministic, collision-resistant over the note's field, and
  efficient in R1CS; Poseidon over the BN254 scalar field is the natural
  choice and is already used by the Aztec and Zcash Sapling circuits.

  No novel cryptographic construction; only a circuit-level primitive
  that is standard in modern SNARK systems.


7.3 New Primitive: The Nullifier PRF
────────────────────────────────────

  The nullifier PRF \( \text{PRF}_{\text{key}}(\rho) \) is also
  Poseidon-based, keyed differently per type:

  • *Type 1:* key = \( sk_{\text{issuer}} \) (which, per Theorem 6,
    equals the depositor's \( sk \) since same \( M \)).  Nullifier is
    therefore computable by the depositor and uniquely determined by \(
    \rho \) and the Identity scalar shared between issuer and depositor.
  • *Type 2:* key = \( \rho \) itself; nullifier = \(
    H_{\text{nf}}(\rho) \), publicly computable by anyone who knows \(
    \rho \).  This is the reason Type 2 is vulnerable to issuer-race:
    whoever knows \( \rho \) can compute the nullifier and race a
    deposit.
  • *Type 3:* key = \( sk^\ast \) (the stealth secret, known only to the
    recipient).  The nullifier is uniquely bound to \( (sk^\ast, \rho)
    \); only the intended recipient can compute it.

  Cross-type nullifier-space separation is enforced by a
  type-domain-separator byte in the PRF input, so collisions across
  types are avoided.


7.4 Re-Used Primitive: Theorem 7 (Stealth Address)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The stealth-address construction \( pk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot
     pk_{\text{recipient}} \) is proved sound in the proofs document
     (Theorem 7) under DLog on \( G_1 \) alone.  The same construction
     is used here verbatim for Type 3 notes.  No modification, no new
     assumption.


7.5 The Re-Encryption Operation at Mint
───────────────────────────────────────

  Type 1 and Type 3 require the issuer to perform a re-encryption
  operation at mint time:

  *Type 1 mint-time re-encryption.* The issuer takes their own
  registered \( M_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \) and
  produces \( E_{\text{issuer}}^{(\text{note})} = (r_i G,
  M_{\text{issuer}} + r_i \cdot pk_{\text{issuer}}) \) with fresh \( r_i
  \).  This is just a fresh ElGamal encryption under the issuer's
  existing pk; the same operation Alice performs at `approve' time.

  *Type 3 mint-time stealth derivation.* The issuer picks \( r_e
  \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \), computes \( R_e = r_e G \), computes
  the shared point \( s = r_e \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \) (the issuer
  can do this because she knows \( r_e \) and \( pk_{\text{recipient}}
  \)), and computes \( pk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot
  pk_{\text{recipient}} \).  She packs \( (R_e, pk^\ast) \) into the
  commitment.  She does /not/ compute \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s)
  \cdot sk_{\text{recipient}} \): she can't, because she lacks \(
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \).

  Both operations are single-curve-multiplication and single-hash
  operations on BN254 G_1.  No pairing, no new hardness assumption.


7.6 The Re-Encryption Operation at Spend (Type 1 Only)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A Type 1 depositor must produce a fresh ElGamal encryption \(
  E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d) = (r_d G, M_{\text{issuer}} + r_d \cdot
  pk_{\text{dep}}) \) and a SNARK-internal CP proof that \(
  E_{\text{issuer}}^{(\text{note})} \) and \( E_{\text{dep}} \) decrypt
  to the same \( M \).  This is Theorem 6 (end-to-end re-encryption
  correctness) lifted into the SNARK circuit: the statement proved is
  identical to the on-chain CP proof from `approve', just encoded as a
  SNARK witness rather than a Fiat-Shamir NIZK.

  The extractable witness is \( (sk_{\text{issuer}}, r_d) \), and since
  \( sk_{\text{issuer}} \) is bound (by registration) to \(
  M_{\text{issuer}} \), the proof establishes that the depositor owns
  the registered credential that encrypts the same \( M \) the issuer
  encrypted.


7.7 The Schnorr PoK Operation at Spend (Type 2 and Type 3)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Both Type 2 and Type 3 spenders include a Schnorr PoK of \(
  sk_{\text{dep}} \) for their registered \( pk_{\text{dep}} \),
  Fiat-Shamir bound to the spend transaction.  For Type 3, an
  /additional/ Schnorr PoK of \( sk^\ast \) for \( pk^\ast \) is
  required, as per Theorem 7.

  Both Schnorr proofs are standard; no novelty.

8 The SNARK Circuits
════════════════════

  Three spend circuits (one per note type) and one mint circuit are
  required.  Alternatively, a single circuit conditioned on a public
  `type' input subsumes all three spends; this is the more efficient
  deployment choice but costs a larger universal verifier.


8.1 Mint Circuit
────────────────

  *Public inputs.* `totalFace' (the total face value \( \sum v_i \) of
  the notes being minted), \( \{cm_1, \ldots, cm_N\} \) (commitments to
  be appended), and `issuerRegistryEntry' (a Merkle root or index into
  IdentityRegistry attesting to the issuer's registered credential).

  *Witnesses.* For each note \( i \): \( (type_i, v_i, \rho_i,
  \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}, \text{predicate}_i) \), plus the
  issuer's private material for whatever \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}}
  \) the note requires.

  *Constraints.*
  1. \( \sum v_i = \texttt{totalFace} \) (amount conservation).
  2. Each \( cm_i = H_{\text{cm}}(\text{type}_i, v_i, \rho_i,
           \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}, \text{predicate}_i) \) (opening
           binding).
  3. For each Type 1 note: \( \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}^{(1)} =
           (R_{i,i}, M_{\text{issuer}} + r_{i,i} \cdot
           pk_{\text{issuer}}) \) with \( R_{i,i} = r_{i,i} G \), and \(
           M_{\text{issuer}} \) matches the issuer's registered ElGamal
           decryption.
  4. For each Type 2 note: \( \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}^{(2)} =
           (m_{\text{issuer}}, \sigma_i) \) with \( \sigma_i \) a valid
           Schnorr signature on \( cm_i \) under \( pk_{\text{issuer}} =
           m_{\text{issuer}} G \).
  5. For each Type 3 note: \( \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}^{(3)} =
           (R_{e,i}, pk^\ast_i) \) with \( R_{e,i} = r_{e,i} G \) and \(
           pk^\ast_i = H_{\text{st}}(r_{e,i} \cdot
           pk_{\text{recipient},i}) \cdot pk_{\text{recipient},i} \),
           for each note's target recipient.

  *Contract side.* The contract verifies the proof, pulls `totalFace'
  BUCK from the caller (ordinary ERC-20 transfer, caller pays their own
  outgoing-transfer demurrage), appends each \( cm_i \) to `noteTree',
  and increments `noteFaceSum' by `totalFace'.  No other state is
  touched – the contract's \( (B, A) \) update through the normal
  transfer path.


8.2 Spend Circuit (Type 1 – Encrypted Issuer)
─────────────────────────────────────────────

  *Public inputs.* `noteRoot' (current Merkle root of `noteTree'),
  `nullifier', `faceValue' (the note's face \( v \), revealed),
  `depositorRegistryEntry' (pointer into IdentityRegistry for the
  depositor's credential).

  *Witnesses.* \( (v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)},
     \text{path}) \), \( sk_{\text{issuer/dep}} \) (shared, by Theorem
     6), the depositor's fresh encryption randomness \( r_d \), and the
     depositor's registered credential \(
     E_{\text{dep}}^{(\text{registry})} \).

  *Constraints.*
  1. \( cm = H_{\text{cm}}(1, v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)},
           \text{pred}) \) opens to a leaf of `noteTree' under `path'
           (Merkle inclusion).
  2. `nullifier' = \( H_{\text{nf}}(1 \| sk_{\text{dep}} \| \rho) \).
  3. \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)} = (R_i, C_i) \); the registered
     credential \( E_{\text{dep}}^{(\text{registry})} =
     (R_d^{\text{reg}}, C_d^{\text{reg}}) \) exists in IdentityRegistry;
     CP-equality \( C_i - sk_{\text{dep}} R_i = C_d^{\text{reg}} -
     sk_{\text{dep}} R_d^{\text{reg}} \) holds (Theorem 6).
  4. `faceValue' \( = v \) (witness \( v \) revealed as public input).

  The contract checks the nullifier is unused, reads its own \( (B, A)
  \) from the ERC-20 ledger, computes \( \texttt{ageShare} = v \cdot A/B
  \), performs the age-preserving transfer (recipient gains \( v \)
  balance and \( \texttt{ageShare} \) BUCK-age; pool loses the same),
  and decrements `noteFaceSum' by \( v \).  No demurrage arithmetic
  lives in the SNARK.


8.3 Spend Circuit (Type 2 – Public Issuer)
──────────────────────────────────────────

  *Public inputs.* `noteRoot', `nullifier', `faceValue', `issuerPkClaim'
  (the issuer's public-identity claim for human-readable verification,
  redundant with the circuit's own check but useful for wallets),
  `depositorRegistryEntry'.

  *Witnesses.* \( (v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(2)},
     \text{path}) \), \( sk_{\text{dep}} \), the depositor's Schnorr
     nonce \( k_{\text{dep}} \).

  *Constraints.*
  1. Merkle inclusion of \( cm \) under `path' (as Type 1).
  2. `nullifier' = \( H_{\text{nf}}(2 \| \rho) \) (publicly derivable
     from \( \rho \) – this is the first-come-first-served property of
     Type 2).
  3. \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(2)} = (m_{\text{issuer}},
           \sigma_{\text{issuer}}) \) with \( \sigma_{\text{issuer}} \)
           verifying against \( pk_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} G
           \) in IdentityRegistry.
  4. Schnorr PoK of \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) in
     IdentityRegistry, Fiat-Shamir bound to `msg.sender', `chainid',
     `nullifier'.
  5. `faceValue' \( = v \).

  The contract's delivery is identical to Type 1: an age-preserving
  transfer of \( v \) balance and \( v \cdot A/B \) BUCK-age from pool
  to recipient, computed outside the SNARK.


8.4 Spend Circuit (Type 3 – Stealth-Targeted)
─────────────────────────────────────────────

  *Public inputs.* `noteRoot', `nullifier', `faceValue',
  `depositorRegistryEntry'.  \( pk^\ast \) is /not/ a public input:
  revealing it would let an observer look up \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \)
  in IdentityRegistry and deanonymise the target.  Instead, \( pk^\ast
  \) is a private witness, and the Merkle inclusion proof binds it to
  the commitment.

  *Witnesses.* \( (v, \rho, R_e, pk^\ast, \text{path}) \), \(
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \), the stealth secret \( sk^\ast =
  H_{\text{st}}(sk_{\text{recipient}} \cdot R_e) \cdot
  sk_{\text{recipient}} \), \( sk_{\text{dep}} \), Schnorr nonces.

  *Constraints.*
  1. Merkle inclusion of \( cm = H_{\text{cm}}(3, v, \rho, (R_e,
           pk^\ast), \text{pred}) \).
  2. `nullifier' = \( H_{\text{nf}}(3 \| sk^\ast \| \rho) \).
  3. \( sk^\ast \cdot G = pk^\ast \) (Theorem 7 completeness).
  4. \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(sk_{\text{recipient}} \cdot R_e) \cdot
           sk_{\text{recipient}} \) (Theorem 7 setup; also binds \(
           pk_{\text{recipient}} \) indirectly via this relation).
  5. Schnorr PoK of \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) in
     IdentityRegistry.
  6. `faceValue' \( = v \).

  As with Types 1 and 2, the contract performs an age-preserving
  transfer of \( v \) balance and \( v \cdot A/B \) BUCK-age from pool
  to recipient, outside the SNARK.

  /Optional identity-binding./ If the recipient wishes to bind the
  deposit to their registered identity (for tax/audit purposes), the
  circuit additionally proves CP-equality between \(
  E_{\text{dep}}^{(\text{registry})} \) and a re-encryption of the
  Identity point \( M \) decrypted from \(
  E_{\text{recipient}}^{(\text{registry})} \), matching the recipient's
  own credential.  This variant is opt-in; the minimal-privacy variant
  simply requires /any/ registered depositor.

  • Poseidon hash wrapper
  • Schnorr PoK
  • Range proofs on field elements (for \( v \))

  Type-specific gadgets layer on top.  This is standard SNARK library
  architecture (Aztec Noir, Circom, Halo2 all support this layering).


9 Migration from v1 Wormhole and BUCK Cash
══════════════════════════════════════════

  The transition is orderly and preserves all existing cryptographic
  anchors.


9.1 Wormhole v1 to Notes
────────────────────────

  A v1 burn-address certificate \( (addr, v, age) \) maps to a single
  Type 1 note:

  ┌────
  │ migrate_v1_to_notes(addr, v, age):
  │   sk_addr    = recovered Schnorr key bound to addr
  │   pk_addr    = sk_addr * G
  │   M_addr     = registered M for addr (from IdentityRegistry)
  │   r_i        = fresh random
  │   R_i, C_i   = ElGamal(M_addr, r_i, pk_addr)
  │   rho        = fresh random
  │   cm         = Poseidon(1, v_effective, rho, (R_i, C_i), None)
  │   # v_effective = v - kappa * age folds the certificate's
  │   # accrued BUCK-age into its face value at migration time.
  │   submit mint proof: burn (addr, v, age), append cm to noteTree
  └────

  The v1 certificate's accumulated demurrage is discharged at migration
  (by computing the certificate's current effective value \(
  v_{\text{eff}} = v - \kappa \cdot \text{age} \) and using that as the
  note's face).  After migration the note participates in the pool as a
  normal Type 1 note; its subsequent demurrage is the pool-rate, not the
  per-certificate rate.  The v1 burn address is emptied; its BUCK (net
  of demurrage) flows into the contract balance \( B \) exactly as any
  other mint.


9.2 BUCK Cash to Notes
──────────────────────

  • *Cash Mode B (stealth-targeted)* maps one-to-one to Type 3; the
    cryptographic structure is identical.
  • *Cash Mode C (programmable)* can be expressed as a Type 1, 2, or 3
    note with a non-trivial `predicate' field in the commitment,
    evaluated by the spend circuit.  The three base types subsume the
    most common programmable variants (time locks are a predicate on \(
    t_{\text{mint}} \); oracle conditionals are a predicate on oracle
    state; multi-sig is a conjunction of multiple Schnorr PoKs).
  • *Cash Mode A (pure bearer)* does not map to any of the three types.
    See "What Gets Dropped" below.


9.3 v1 Coexistence During Transition
────────────────────────────────────

  The unified Notes architecture can be deployed alongside the v1
  Wormhole during a transition window.  The v1 burn-address pool remains
  a valid anonymity set; new users mint into the Notes pool; v1 holders
  migrate at their convenience.  The Notes contract's `noteFaceSum'
  reflects only Notes-pool activity; v1 certificates continue to accrue
  per-certificate demurrage as before, via their individual burn-address
  accounts.

  The final /old/ v1 certificate need not be migrated – it can continue
  to be redeemed against its burn-address anchor indefinitely, at the
  cost of the contract maintaining two verifier paths.  A practical
  deadline (e.g., 5 years from Notes launch) is reasonable.


10 What Gets Dropped: Pure Bearer
═════════════════════════════════

  The Cash document's /Mode A – bearer note with QR code sealed under a
  hologram, anyone who possesses the QR can deposit/ does not map
  cleanly onto the unified scheme.  The reason is the same as why Mode A
  in the Cash design required a physical hologram seal: a purely
  secret-based predicate (`know(rho)' with no identity hook) has no
  cryptographic mechanism preventing a malicious issuer from retaining
  \( \rho \) and racing the holder.


10.1 Why Mode A Is Absent
─────────────────────────

  Three routes considered:

  1. /Blind minting/ – the issuer does not know \( \rho \) at mint time.
     Requires the holder to participate in minting (giving a blinded
     commitment).  Incompatible with the "print a stack of bearer notes
     and hand them out" use case.

  2. /Trusted printer/ – hardware security module or audited print
     service that provably erases \( \rho \).  Works in principle;
     undermines the "trustless bearer instrument" pitch.

  3. /Short expiry/ – Mode A notes expire after a bounded period,
     limiting the issuer-race window.  Equivalent to Type 2 with a
     short-term contract; the use case is then cheque-like, not
     cash-like.

  None of these route options preserve the "$20 bill-equivalent"
  property that Mode A was designed for.  The unified scheme therefore
  /does not attempt/ to provide a pure-bearer cryptographic note, and
  defers that use case to a separate design dimension:


10.2 The Physical-Token Escape Hatch
────────────────────────────────────

  A pure-bearer instrument remains available via the /physical/ trust
  model that all physical bearer instruments already rely on.
  Specifically: an issuer operating a print service with a
  hardware-attested erasure of \( \rho \) can issue Type 2 notes with a
  public warranty that the service did in fact erase \( \rho \).  The
  warranty is an off-chain social/legal claim, not a cryptographic one,
  but it is identical in kind to the trust extended to the Bank of
  Canada when it issues physical polymer banknotes (whose security rests
  on BoC printing discipline, not on cryptography).

  This keeps the pure-bearer door ajar without requiring the unified
  scheme to pretend it solves the cryptographic problem of making a
  truly trustless bearer note.  BUCK Cash's original Mode A was arguably
  already conceding this point via the tamper-evident seal ("the seal
  makes it safe to display, but any unsealed QR is spendable by
  anyone"): the seal is a physical-world primitive, not a cryptographic
  one.


10.3 Use-Case Coverage Without Mode A
─────────────────────────────────────

  The canonical Mode A use cases re-route cleanly:

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Use case                    Cash Mode A           Unified scheme                             
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Tip jar                     Printed Mode A sheet  Public-issuer Type 2 cheques, held briefly 
   Walking-around money        Printed Mode A notes  Self-issued Type 1 in a hot wallet         
   Gifts to unknown recipient  Printed Mode A        Type 2 signed cheque, short expiry         
   Disaster preparedness cold  Printed Mode A hoard  Self-issued Type 1, long-term safe         
   Underbanked payment rail    Physical Mode A       Type 2 cheque from issuer's office         
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Every use case that truly requires "no identity link at all" is
  handled by the physical-token escape hatch above.  Every use case that
  tolerates an /encrypted/ identity link (which is most of them) gains
  stronger cryptographic guarantees via Types 1 and 3.

11.1 Demurrage Conservation as a Contract Invariant
───────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Because the pool is a regular BUCK account and redemption uses a
  two-sided-balanced age-preserving transfer, no Notes-specific
  demurrage invariant is needed.  The system-wide BUCK invariants

  \[ \sum_{\text{all accounts}} B_X \;+\; B_{\text{Jubilee}} \;=\;
     \text{const}, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\!\sum_{\text{all accounts}} A_X
     \;=\; \sum_{\text{all accounts}} B_X \]

  hold trivially through redemption: the \( v \) balance and \( v \cdot
  A/B \) age moved from pool to recipient appear on both sides of each
  sum.  The Notes contract only needs to maintain two auxiliary local
  invariants:

  1. \( F \geq 0 \) (no negative note face sum).
  2. Every \( v \) appearing in a deposit equals the \( v \) that
     appeared in the corresponding mint (enforced by the SNARK via the
     Merkle inclusion of \( cm \) binding \( v \)).

  Both are trivial.  The only new mechanism is the age-preserving
  transfer primitive, itself a simple state-machine operation on two
  accounts' \( (B, A) \) pairs.


12 Implementation Scope
═══════════════════════

  Rough engineering estimate, assuming the existing Alberta Buck
  contract skeleton and an existing SNARK toolchain (Aztec Noir or
  Circom/snarkJS):


12.1 Smart Contract Additions
─────────────────────────────

  • `noteTree' (Merkle tree with Poseidon nodes): ~1 week, bulk of code
    is existing Aztec/Zcash sapling implementations.
  • `nullifiers' mapping: trivial.
  • `noteFaceSum' scalar + mint/deposit updates: trivial (audit only).
  • Age-preserving transfer primitive (system-level hook writing to two
    accounts' \( (B, A) \) atomically): ~1 week, mirrors the v1
    `_mint_noincrease' pattern.
  • Three spend-circuit verifiers (Groth16-style on-chain): ~1 week
    integration per circuit, bulk is circuit-side.
  • Mint-circuit verifier: ~1 week.
  • Migration helper for v1 certificates: ~1 week.

  *Total:* ~8 weeks for an experienced Solidity engineer.


12.2 SNARK Circuits
───────────────────

  • Mint circuit: ~2-3 weeks (Poseidon commitments, ElGamal encryption,
    Schnorr signature verification inside the circuit).
  • Spend circuits (three, with shared gadgets): ~4-6 weeks.
  • Common gadgets library (Merkle, Poseidon wrappers, Schnorr, range
    checks): ~2 weeks.

  *Total:* ~10 weeks for an experienced SNARK engineer.


12.3 Wallet & UX
────────────────

  • Note scanning / recognition for Type 3 (stealth-address scan): ~1
    week.
  • Type 1 self-mint-to-private-address flow (Wormhole replacement): ~1
    week.
  • Type 2 cheque creation and deposit UX: ~2 weeks.
  • Type 3 targeted-note UX: ~2 weeks.
  • Prover service integration for thin clients: ~2-3 weeks.

  *Total:* ~8 weeks for a wallet team.


12.4 Cross-Checks and Audit
───────────────────────────

  • Cryptographic review of the unified spend circuits: 4 weeks,
    external auditor with SNARK-circuit expertise.
  • Economic model validation (pool-rate demurrage in stress scenarios:
    low turnover, high turnover, mint/deposit mismatch): 2 weeks.
  • End-to-end fuzz and property-based testing: 2 weeks.

  *Total:* ~8 weeks of external + internal audit.

  Overall program estimate: 6-9 months of focused work to ship a
  production-quality unified Notes system, subsuming Wormhole v1 and the
  BUCK Cash proposal into a single architecture.


13 Open Questions
═════════════════

  The unified design resolves most of the architectural redundancy but
  leaves a handful of economic and cryptographic questions worth
  sharpening before commitment:


13.1 Donation Incidence
───────────────────────

  Donations to the contract lower \( A/B \) and therefore lower the
  age-share every subsequent redeemer absorbs.  The benefit spreads
  across all future redemptions rather than accruing to a single lucky
  first-redeemer.  Because the age-share calculation uses the /current/
  \( A/B \) at each redemption, a donation immediately before a large
  redemption benefits that redemption more than a small one – but every
  subsequent redemption benefits in proportion.  This is reasonable
  behaviour without any special smoothing logic.

  Residual donation principal (after all notes drain) is not lost: it
  remains in \( B \) with \( A = 0 \), available to subsidise the next
  generation of mints (whose recipients will absorb an artificially low
  \( A/B \) at their redemption).


13.2 Stranded Value on Lost Notes
─────────────────────────────────

  A lost note contributes to \( F \) indefinitely.  The BUCK backing
  that note sits in the pool, accumulating BUCK-age.  Since the
  age-preserving transfer uses \( A/B \) (not \( A/F \)), the lost face
  value weighs on \( A \) through continued age accumulation, and \( B
  \) remains unclaimed.  Redemptions of live notes absorb an ever-larger
  \( A/B \) as the lost share's age dominates.  At 1%/yr loss and 2%/yr
  demurrage, the long-term tendency is that the lost note's implicit
  demurrage is paid instalment-style by subsequent live noteholders.

  A reclaim path is possible: after a very long period (say 99 years),
  the contract could allow the issuer to submit a zero-knowledge proof
  that a specific note commitment remains unspent, and reclaim the
  residual principal less accrued demurrage.  Requires issuer-side
  recordkeeping; deferred.


13.3 Integer-Arithmetic Rounding
────────────────────────────────

  Redemption involves two integer divisions (computing \( v \cdot A / B
  \) for the age-share).  Any residual from truncated division remains
  in the pool's \( (B, A) \) state, raising \( A/B \) slightly for the
  next redeemer – the rounding is self-healing rather than
  drift-accumulating.  Choice of rounding direction (floor vs. banker's)
  is a minor contract-level decision with no cryptographic impact.


13.4 Predicate Programmability vs. Circuit Simplicity
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Keeping three fixed types simplifies the circuit count.  Adding
  programmable predicates (time locks, oracle conditions, multi-sigs)
  re-introduces the complexity of Cash Mode C.  A compromise: support a
  /bounded/ predicate language in the circuit (time-lock +
  oracle-condition + up-to-3-sig) rather than a fully general one.  The
  bounded language covers 90%+ of the use cases Mode C was designed for
  while keeping the verifier compact.


13.5 Universal Spend Circuit vs. Three
──────────────────────────────────────

  Deploying three distinct spend verifiers costs gas per transaction
  (one verifier per call).  A universal verifier that takes `type' as a
  public input and branches internally costs a larger but single
  verifier.  Gas trade-off depends on production transaction mix; if
  Type 1 dominates (Wormhole-style), three verifiers are cheaper.
  Empirical question, resolvable at deployment time.


13.6 Regulatory Interaction with the Age-Preserving Transfer
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Jurisdictional regulators (FINTRAC, OSFI) have rules around bearer
  instruments and tax treatment of negative-interest accounts.  BUCK's
  on-chain demurrage was argued through separately, and the Notes
  contract inherits that treatment with no pool-specific novelty – the
  contract is just another BUCK account.

  The one new wrinkle is the /age-preserving transfer/ itself: ordinary
  BUCK transfers carry only balance (recipient gets "fresh" BUCK),
  whereas Note redemptions carry balance plus a proportional BUCK-age.
  This means a Note recipient's post-redemption spendable value is less
  than their nominal balance – the difference being the demurrage they
  will pay on their next outgoing transfer.  For tax and reporting
  purposes, whether the "face" or the "spendable" number should be used
  as the income figure is a question for tax counsel.  The on-chain
  state is fully transparent either way.


14 Summary
══════════

  The Wormhole and BUCK Cash designs converge naturally on a single
  note-pool primitive.  Three Identity-binding types – Encrypted-Issuer
  (Type 1, Wormhole-style), Public-Issuer (Type 2, signed cheque),
  Stealth-Targeted (Type 3, addressed cheque) – together span every use
  case in the existing series while removing the burn-address /
  `_mint_noincrease' machinery, the per-certificate Identity
  registration, and the separate Cash Mode A/B/C taxonomy.

  Demurrage needs no new machinery: the Note Pool is literally the BUCK
  ERC-20 contract's own balance – a regular BUCK account with its own \(
  B \) and \( A \) in the ERC-20 ledger.  The only Note-side state
  beyond the Merkle tree and nullifier set is one scalar \( F \), the
  total face value of live notes, kept as an audit quantity.  Redemption
  delivers the /full face value/ \( v \) to the recipient, together with
  the pool's proportional share of BUCK-age \( v \cdot A/B \); the
  recipient absorbs this age onto their own account and bears the
  demurrage cost on their next outgoing transfer through the ordinary
  BUCK accounting path.  This is exactly the demurrage they would have
  paid had they held \( v \) BUCK personally through the pool's
  residence period.  The entire mechanism requires a single new
  primitive, an age-preserving transfer from pool to recipient,
  localized to the Notes contract's one egress function.  Donations to
  the pool dilute \( A/B \) and cheaply subsidise all subsequent
  redeemers.  Conservation of balance, age, and Jubilee growth all fall
  out exactly.

  The printer-trust distinction produces a sharp partition of which note
  types are safe for long-term holding: only /Type 1/ (issuer =
  beneficiary) and /Type 3/ (stealth-targeted to beneficiary) can be
  held for arbitrary durations.  /Type 2/ is cryptographically a
  short-term instrument regardless of how long a user might wish to keep
  it, because the issuer retains the spending secret.  This is an
  intrinsic property that cannot be remedied by additional cryptography;
  it follows directly from the Type 2 identity payload carrying the
  issuer's public identity and no recipient-binding.

  Pure-bearer notes (Cash Mode A) are not replaced by any cryptographic
  Type; they remain the domain of physical trust models (tamper-evident
  seals, trusted printers).  This is a philosophical refinement of the
  Cash proposal rather than a retreat: Cash Mode A already relied on a
  physical hologram for its security, and the unified design makes the
  physical/crypto boundary explicit.

  No novel cryptographic assumption is introduced.  Theorems 6 and 7 of
  the Proofs document ([Proofs]) apply verbatim to the Type 1 and Type 3
  spend predicates, respectively; Type 2 reduces to Schnorr.  The only
  new circuit-level primitive is Poseidon, which is already standard
  across the SNARK ecosystem.  Implementation scope is 6-9 months for a
  focused team.

  The unification is, in the small, a refactor that removes redundancy.
  In the large, it crystallises the Alberta Buck design around a single
  cryptographic primitive (the note pool) whose Identity-binding
  dimension produces the full range of financial instruments the series
  aspires to, and whose economic dimension (pool-rate demurrage)
  elegantly solves the lost-note problem that motivated the original
  BUCK Cash demurrage discussion.
