Public money is going programmable, and programmability invites
surveillance: every proposed central-bank digital currency couples the
convenience of digital cash to a ledger its issuer can read, freeze, and
mine.  Physical cash resists this but offers the opposite extreme – no
accountability at all, no issuance trail, no recourse.  This paper
presents BUCK Notes, a bearer-instrument layer for an identity-aware
stablecoin that occupies neither pole.  /Bulk/ traceability is defeated
– no analytics firm or omnibus subpoena can reconstruct who paid whom –
while /bilateral/ disclosure is preserved: the two parties to any
payment always retain the cryptographic means to prove, under compulsion
and with *no trusted third party*, exactly who the counterparty was.
The load-bearing property is the *non-deniable-receipt invariant*: there
is no path, even for a /colluding/ payer and payee, to construct a
payment for which no court-compellable receipt naming the KYC-bound
counterparty exists.  We formalise the invariant, give a construction
meeting it for public-issuer notes (shipped and evaluated on Ethereum),
prove its security, and characterise precisely the boundary at which the
guarantee stops being free: when /both/ parties are anonymous at their
respective binding moments, a collusion-resistant receipt provably
requires in-circuit set-membership over the identity registry.  We give
the negative result that the obvious cheaper fix is vacuous, and an
incentive-compatible mechanism that bounds the residual risk to the
always-identifiable recipient.

1 Introduction
══════════════

  /FC-first framing.  Open on the public-money mission; the cryptography
  serves it./

  The choice a society makes about the privacy of its money is a choice
  about the balance of power between the individual and the institutions
  that watch over them.


1.1 Contributions
─────────────────

  • *The non-deniable-receipt invariant* (Def. 3.2): a security
    definition for payment systems whose operative, novel clause forbids
    a /colluding/ payer/payee pair from constructing a payment with no
    accepting receipt.  The accountability is *bilateral* and held by
    the counterparties; there is no system-wide opening authority.
  • *A construction meeting it for unlinkable bearer payments*
    (public-issuer notes, A1/B1), defeating bulk on-chain traceability
    while preserving court-compellable bilateral disclosure – *shipped
    and evaluated* on Ethereum.
  • *A forced taxonomy* (Thm. 5): the realisable design space is exactly
    three flavours; a private-issuer bearer note is structurally
    impossible.
  • *A characterisation of where accountability is free vs. expensive*
    (Sec. 7): the receipt is free exactly when at least one party is
    named at the binding instant; when both are anonymous, we show the
    obvious cheap coupling is /vacuous/ (Lem. 7.2) and that a
    collusion-resistant receipt requires registry set-membership ZK
    (Prop. 7.4).
  • *An incentive-compatible backstop* (Prop. 7.5): for the
    doubly-anonymous case, an economic mechanism bounds the residual
    risk to the always-KYC-identifiable recipient, obviating the
    expensive cryptographic fix under a stated threat model.
  • *A reproducible artifact*: every theorem ships an executable
    `py_ecc' witness on BN254; the contracts, circuits, and verifiers
    are open and test-covered.

3 Model, Adversaries, and the Invariant
═══════════════════════════════════════

  An /Identity/ is a KYC-certified point \( M \in \mathbb{G}_1 \) bound
  to a registered account by an issuer's Pointcheval-Sanders credential
  and stored ElGamal-encrypted as \( E_{\text{addr}}[X] = (R_X, C_X) =
  (r_X G, M_X + r_X\, pk_X) \) with \( pk_X = sk_X G \).  A /payment/
  moves value \( v \) from payer \( P \) to recipient \( D \).


3.1 Adversaries
───────────────

  Three properties, held against adversaries of increasing power:
  • *(Bulk-tracing resistance.)* /Mallory/, a passive observer with full
    chain history and unbounded compute but no party's secrets, cannot
    reconstruct who paid whom, how much, or link a mint to its spend(s).
  • *(Third-party non-disclosure.)* Nothing on chain reveals either
    party's Identity point \( M \) to a non-participant, even in
    aggregate across many payments – /including to the issuer/ (below).
  • *(Mutual decryptability / accountability.)* Each of the two
    participants, from data they hold plus public chain state, can
    produce a sound receipt naming the other's registered \( M \); and a
    /colluding/ pair cannot arrange a payment for which no such receipt
    exists.
  The first two properties must hold against the strongest realistic
  deanonymiser, the *Issuer*: the KYC trust anchor that holds, for every
  client it certified, the plaintext identity \( (\mathrm{id}_i, m_i,
  M_i) \) and the credential \( \sigma_i \) it issued – strictly more
  than Mallory knows.  We treat a /compelled or wholly breached/ issuer
  in Sec. 3.3.


3.2 The non-deniable-receipt invariant
──────────────────────────────────────


  /(Non-deniable receipt.)/ A protocol satisfies the
  *non-deniable-receipt invariant* if for every accepted payment there
  exist a receipt \( \mathcal{R} \) and an efficient public predicate \(
  \mathsf{RcptVerify} \) such that:
  • *(Completeness.)* The recipient \( D \) can compute \( \mathcal{R}
    \) from data they hold plus public chain state, and \(
    \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \) names a registered Identity
    \( M_P \) and value \( v \).
  • *(Soundness / non-frameability.)* No PPT adversary produces \(
    \mathcal{R}' \) with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}') = 1 \)
    naming an Identity that did not author the payment.
  • *(No deniability.)* No PPT payer can cause an accepted payment to \(
    D \) for which /no/ accepting receipt naming the payer exists – even
    in collusion with \( D \).

  The third clause is the operative one and the departure from prior
  art: it is the collusion-resistant form.  Completeness alone protects
  a self-interested recipient; the invariant additionally forbids a
  /willing/ pair from constructing an untraceable payment.


3.3 Issuer-blindness: accountability without a bulk opener
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The KYC issuer is the system's only trust anchor, and the natural
  objection to any /accountable privacy/ claim is that the issuer is a
  bulk-deanonymisation backdoor.  It is not.  Issuance is
  /address-blind/: the issuer signs the identity scalar \( m =
  H(\mathrm{id}) \) once (a Pointcheval-Sanders credential over \( m \)
  alone) and is thereafter done; the client derives each on-chain
  account /offline/ – a fresh key pair \( (sk, pk) \), a self-formed
  ElGamal credential \( E_{\text{addr}} = (rG,\ M + r\,pk) \), and a
  /rerandomised/ credential \( \sigma' \) – without the issuer ever
  seeing \( pk \).  The issuer's view is therefore, per certified client
  \( i \), the tuple \( (\mathrm{id}_i, m_i, M_i, \sigma_i) \) – the
  plaintext identity – plus public chain state; it holds /no/ chain
  addresses.


  /(Issuer-blindness.)/ Under statistical unlinkability of PS
  rerandomisation (Thm. 2 of the companion /Proofs/) and IND-CPA
  security of ElGamal on \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) (equivalently DDH), an
  issuer – even one compelled or wholly breached, in possession of \(
  \{(\mathrm{id}_i, m_i, M_i, \sigma_i)\}_i \) and the entire chain –
  has negligible advantage in matching any issued identity \( M_i \) to
  its on-chain registration \( (pk_j, E_j, \sigma'_j) \).  Compulsion or
  breach yields the /set/ of certified identities but no /map/ to chain
  accounts; deanonymisation remains per-account, requiring that
  account's \( sk \) or a counterparty receipt.

  Two locks.  /Credential lock./ \( \sigma'_j \) is a rerandomisation of
  some issued \( \sigma_i \); by statistical unlinkability the two carry
  zero mutual information, so even an unbounded issuer cannot recognise
  its own signature.  /Plaintext lock./ The issuer /knows/ \( M_i \), so
  its task is not decryption but /plaintext-checking/: does \( E_j = (R,
  C) \) encrypt \( M_i \)?  That is deciding whether \( (G, pk_j, R, C -
  M_i) \) is a DDH tuple, and an algorithm answering it for a chosen \(
  M_i \) breaks IND-CPA (submit \( m_0 = M_i \), \( m_1 \) random).
  Knowing the plaintext thus confers no advantage.  With both locks shut
  and no stored \( pk \), the issuer is reduced to an independent DDH
  decision per (identity, account) pair.

  This is the load-bearing form of /no trusted opener/: the closest
  thing to an authority cannot open transactions in bulk, even against
  itself – under compulsion it can only /verify/ a link some party
  surrenders (map a receipt-disclosed \( M \) to the real person), never
  /produce/ one.  Two caveats bound the theorem, and we state rather
  than bury them:

  • *Computational, not information-theoretic.* The plaintext lock is
    DDH; only the credential lock is statistical.  A discrete-log break
    – in particular a quantum computer running Shor against \(
    \mathbb{G}_1 \) – turns the harvested \( \{M_i\} \) plus the
    archived chain into a wholesale, retroactive deanonymisation of all
    /past/ identity and A2-Note traffic.  The breached or compelled
    issuer's KYC records are precisely that harvest corpus (companion
    /Proofs/, Implementation Notes: /Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later/).
  • *Retroactive, not prospective.* Address-blind issuance protects
    /past/ registrations unconditionally against /future/ issuer
    compromise; but a coerced issuer can change the protocol going
    forward (demand \( pk \) at KYC, record addresses, backdoor
    credentials).  Issuer-blindness is a statement about issuances
    already performed under the honest protocol.


4 Construction: A Note Is a Deferred Approve Handshake
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  A standard transfer between two registered identities is a synchronous
  bilateral `approve' handshake: each party re-encrypts their credential
  under the other's key and proves (Chaum-Pedersen) the re-encryption
  carries the same \( M \).  A Note reorganises the same handshake
  through a Tornado-style commitment pool, splitting it across time so
  that mint and spend are unlinkable, while preserving the bilateral
  decryptability.

6 Security of the Public-Issuer Construction (A1, B1)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  This is the complete result: for public-issuer notes the
  non-deniable-receipt invariant holds in all three clauses, with no
  open problem.  The issuer is bound at /mint/ by a Schnorr signature
  over the batch commitment under their registered key; the depositor's
  reverse leg is the `verifyApprove'-shaped re-encryption at /deposit/.

  /(Issuer-binding completeness.)/ An honestly generated public-issuer
  Schnorr proof verifies.

  /(Issuer-binding soundness.)/ If `verifyIssuerSchnorr' accepts, the
  batch commitments were authorised by the issuer's registered key.
  Reduction: Schnorr EUF-CMA / special soundness.

  /(Receipt completeness.)/ For every note accepted by `mint' and
  redeemed by `spend=/=spendACP', the recipient computes \( \mathcal{R}
  \) with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \) naming a registered
  Identity.  Links (a),(b),(d) hold in the shipped contracts; (c) holds
  by issuer-binding soundness.

  /(Receipt soundness / non-frameability.)/ No PPT adversary produces an
  accepting \( \mathcal{R}' \) naming an Identity that did not author
  the payment.  A forged public receipt breaks Schnorr EUF-CMA or the
  collision-resistance of the commitment hash.

  /(No deniability under collusion – public issuer.)/ No payer/recipient
  coalition can produce an accepted public-issuer payment with no
  accepting receipt: `mint' rejects any leaf lacking a valid issuer
  binding, so every spendable leaf carries one, recoverable by the
  holder of the opening.

  /(Mutual decryptability, public issuer.)/ Combining the issuer half
  (Schnorr binding at mint) with the depositor half
  (`verifyDepositorForIssuer' at deposit), both parties recover the
  other's registered Identity and each can prove it under compulsion –
  with no third party.


7 The Cost of Anonymity: the Private-Issuer Boundary (A2)
═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  The public-issuer result is free of any SNARK over identities because,
  at the binding instant, one party is /named/.  We now show that
  removing the last named anchor – making /both/ parties anonymous at
  their respective binding moments (the A2 setting: issuer named only at
  mint, recipient named only at spend, mint and spend unlinkable) – is
  exactly what makes collusion-resistance expensive, and we locate the
  boundary precisely.


7.1 The EOA-transfer mirror: the cost is the anonymity
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A direct private-to-private transfer achieves mutual decryptability
  with no SNARK and free collusion-resistance, because the mandatory
  Chaum-Pedersen-bound `approve' reads /both/ parties' registered
  records in a single call – both anchors co-present, both named.  A2
  splits the anchors in time by anonymity: the issuer's anchor exists
  only at mint, the recipient's only at spend.  No on-chain check reads
  both at once, so the A2 binding /blinds/ the recipient's key to bridge
  them – and blinding it is precisely what drops the "under the
  recipient's /registered/ key" guarantee.


7.2 The obvious fix is vacuous   *** NEW (rigorous, ready to formalise) ***
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────


  /(Vacuity of key-equality coupling.)/ Let an A2 mint constrain only
  that the issuer payload \( eIss \) and the note ciphertext \(
  E_{\text{note}} \) are encrypted under the /same/ key \(
  pk_{\text{rec}} \), with the recipient's identity point \(
  M_{\text{rec}} \) a free witness.  Then for any issuer-chosen \(
  pk_{\text{bogus}} \) the constraint is satisfiable while the note
  remains spendable by the intended recipient \( D \) and \( eIss \) is
  encrypted under \( pk_{\text{bogus}} \neq pk_D \).

  Set \( pk_{\text{rec}} := pk_{\text{bogus}} \) and \( M_{\text{rec}}
     := M_D + r_n(pk_D - pk_{\text{bogus}}) \).  Then \( E_{\text{note}}
     = (r_n G,\ M_{\text{rec}} + r_n\, pk_{\text{rec}}) = (r_n G,\ M_D +
     r_n\, pk_D) \), a valid encryption of \( M_D \) under \( D \)'s
     registered key, so `verifySpendCP' accepts and \( D \) spends;
     while \( eIss = (r'G,\ M_{\text{iss}} + r'\, pk_{\text{bogus}}) \)
     is under \( pk_{\text{bogus}} \), so a compelled \( sk_D \)
     decrypts \( eIss \) to \( M_{\text{iss}} + r'(pk_{\text{bogus}} -
     pk_D) \neq M_{\text{iss}} \).  The free witness \( M_{\text{rec}}
     \) absorbs the constraint.


7.3 Closing the clause: the coupled construction
────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Lemma 7.2 shows that binding \( eIss \) to /a/ key is worthless; the
  binding must be to the recipient's /registered/ key, and the issuer –
  who does not hold \( sk_D \) – can only assert that key /exists in the
  registry/.  This yields the coupled mint circuit \( \mathcal{C}^\star
  \).  Write the registry as an accumulator with public root \(
  \mathsf{rt} \) over leaves \( \ell_X = \mathrm{Poseidon}(
  \mathrm{addr}_X, pk_X, E_{\text{addr}}[X]) \); the issuer is \( I =
  \texttt{msg.sender} \) with public \( E_{\text{addr}}[I] = (R_I, C_I)
  \); the note ciphertext is \( E_{\text{note}} = (R_n, C_n) \) and the
  issuer payload \( eIss = (R_e, C_e) \) is a public output (the
  leaf-tie of Sec. 4).  Per A2 leaf, with private witnesses \( (sk_I,
  M_{\text{iss}}, pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D]=(R_D,C_D), \pi_D, r', r_n)
  \), \( \mathcal{C}^\star \) constrains:

  (C1) ties \( eIss \) to the issuer's /registered/ identity (the public
  \( E_{\text{addr}}[I] \)); (C2) certifies the recipient is a /real/
  registered account without revealing which (leaf private, root
  public); (C3) makes \( E_{\text{note}} \) a re-randomisation of that
  account's registered credential – hence an encryption of \( M_D \)
  under \( pk_D \); (C4) encrypts \( eIss \) under the /same/ \( pk_D
  \).  The spender's key is pinned to \( pk_D \) only later, at spend,
  by `verifySpendCP'; soundness is therefore a /composition/ of the mint
  membership proof and the spend equality proof – the only two moments
  at which the issuer's and the recipient's anchors are respectively
  live.

  The construction rests on one property of registration, already
  present in the identity layer for unrelated (determinism /
  unlinkability) reasons, and now load-bearing.


  /(Non-grindable identities.)/ Each registered identity point is \( M =
  H(\mathrm{id})\cdot G \), where \( \mathrm{id} \) is the KYC-verified
  attribute record and \( H \) (keccak256 over a canonical encoding) is
  preimage-resistant, modelled as a random oracle.  No PPT adversary
  registers an identity point of its choosing; in particular it cannot
  register an account whose \( M \) equals a prescribed target \( M_D +
  \delta G \) for an adversarially chosen \( \delta \neq 0 \).


  /(Sufficiency.)/ Under Assumption 7.3, with `verifySpendCP' enforced
  at spend, the coupled construction \( \mathcal{C}^\star \) closes the
  no-deniability clause (Def. 3.2) for A2: for every accepted
  mint-and-spend, a compelled spender recovers the issuer's registered
  \( M_{\text{iss}} \) from \( eIss \).

  By (C3), \( E_{\text{note}} \) is a re-randomisation of \(
  E_{\text{addr}}[D] \), so for every \( sk \), \( C_n - sk\,R_n = M_D +
  (r_D + r_n)(sk_D - sk)\,G \), where \( sk_D = \mathrm{dlog}\,pk_D \).
  At spend, `verifySpendCP' establishes that the spender holds \( sk_s
  \) with registered key \( pk_s = sk_s G \) and that \( M_s := C_n -
  sk_s R_n \) equals the spender's /registered/ identity.  Substituting,
  \( M_s = M_D + (r_D + r_n)(sk_D - sk_s)\,G \).  If \( sk_s \neq sk_D
  \), then \( M_s = M_D + \delta G \) with \( \delta =
  (r_D+r_n)(sk_D-sk_s) \neq 0 \); by Assumption 7.3 the probability that
  such a point is a registered identity distinct from \( D \)'s is
  negligible (the adversary would have to register an account at \( M_D
  + \delta G \), i.e. find KYC data hashing to \( m_D + \delta \),
  contradicting preimage resistance).  Hence \( sk_s = sk_D \) and \(
  pk_s = pk_D \) except with negligible probability.  By (C4), \( C_e -
  sk_s R_e = M_{\text{iss}} + r'(pk_D - pk_s) = M_{\text{iss}} \), and
  by (C1) \( M_{\text{iss}} \) is the issuer's registered identity.  The
  compelled spender therefore produces a receipt naming \( I \).


  /(Recipient privacy preserved.)/ In \( \mathcal{C}^\star \) the values
  \( pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D], M_{\text{iss}} \) are private witnesses;
  the only public artefacts are the fixed root \( \mathsf{rt} \) and the
  ciphertext \( eIss \) (semantically secure absent \( sk_D \)).  By
  Groth16 zero-knowledge and ElGamal semantic security, no third party
  learns the recipient's identity or key, and two A2 mints to distinct
  recipients are computationally indistinguishable.

  This sufficiency is what reserves A2 rather than abandons it: the
  construction exists and is sound, but its non-native \( \mathbb{G}_1
  \) arithmetic (C1, C3, C4 are emulated \( \mathbb{F}_p \) operations
  on the proof system's own curve) costs \( \sim\!10^6 \) R1CS per leaf
  (Sec. 8) – an order of magnitude beyond the public-issuer path, and
  the reason A2 ships under the mechanism of Sec. 7.5 in the interim.

  *Remark (the cost is prover-side; interactive A2).* The \( 10^6 \)
  R1CS is a /prover/ cost: the EVM verifies the resulting proof in
  constant gas, so the construction is expensive to /prove/ but cheap to
  /check/.  Where the wallet runs on a free-compute substrate that
  already holds the registry tree (here, a Holochain layer), the prover
  cost is absorbed off chain and the tree need not live on chain beyond
  its root.  A variant we note for completeness: when both parties are
  co-present off chain at note creation (/interactive A2/), they run the
  full bilateral handshake and prove it at mint – recovering the EOA
  transfer's free, collusion-resistant mutual decryptability (Sec. 7)
  while still depositing unlinkably – at the cost of an online
  recipient.  Neither observation relaxes Prop. 7.4 (the membership
  proof is still required); they relocate /where/ it is proved and
  confirm its on-chain verification was never the bottleneck.


7.4 Is the SNARK necessary?   *** NEW (conditional result + open problem) ***
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The sufficiency above is an upper bound.  The matching question –
  /must/ a collusion-resistant A2 receipt use registry set-membership? –
  is the paper's central open problem.  We resolve it under one
  restriction and leave the unconditional statement open.


  /(Conditional necessity.)/ Let \( \Pi \) be a protocol that, in the A2
  setting, (i) preserves bulk-tracing resistance and third-party
  non-disclosure (issuer named only at mint, recipient only at spend,
  mint and spend unlinkable), (ii) closes the no-deniability clause, and
  (iii) achieves the binding by referencing a registered party at a
  /single/ protocol step.  Then \( \Pi \) performs a zero-knowledge
  proof of membership in the identity registry – over the recipient set
  at mint, or the issuer set at spend.

  By Lemma 7.2, (ii) forces \( \Pi \) to certify that \( eIss \)
  decrypts, under the addressed recipient's registered key, to a
  registered /issuer/ identity – binding to some key alone does not
  close the clause.  This certificate references a registered party: the
  recipient's key \( pk_D \) (at mint), or the issuer's identity (at
  spend).  By (iii) it is a single step's reference, either in the clear
  or in zero knowledge.

  /In the clear is ruled out./ Revealing the referenced key or identity
  on chain names a party and makes them linkable across mint and spend,
  violating (i): naming \( pk_D \) at mint exposes the recipient, and
  revealing the decrypted \( M_{\text{iss}} \) at spend exposes the
  issuer.  Importing \( E_{\text{note}} \) (or a deterministic
  commitment to it) to the mint to bind there instead re-links mint and
  spend, also violating (i).

  /Zero-knowledge is set-membership./ A reference that certifies the
  referenced party is registered without revealing /which/ – the
  recipient's \( pk_D \) at mint, or the issuer that \( eIss \)
  re-encrypts at spend – is, by definition, a zero-knowledge proof of
  membership in the registry.  As (i) excludes the in-clear case, \( \Pi
  \) performs this.

  The restriction (iii) – a single binding step – is what the proof
  leans on, and relaxing it names the open frontier.  A binding
  amortised across steps, a two-party mint in which the recipient
  contributes a blinded witness without exposing \( E_{\text{note}} \),
  a witness-encryption construction, or an accountability mechanism that
  does not bind \( eIss \) at all (threshold escrow of \( M_{\text{iss}}
  \) to a regulator key – a weaker trust model) each escape the case
  analysis.  Whether any achieves collusion-resistant A2 naming without
  registry set-membership is open (Sec. 9); a clean impossibility would
  prove the SNARK necessary, a loophole would be a cheaper design.


7.5 A mechanism instead of a proof   *** NEW (model to formalise) ***
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────


  /(Recipient-burden incentive compatibility.)/ Model an A2 deposit by
  recipient \( D \): audit probability \( q \), AML penalty \( L \) for
  holding funds from an unaccountable source, delivery-verification cost
  \( \varepsilon \) (decrypt \( eIss \), run the \( \gamma \)-coupling),
  and collusion benefit \( b \).  If \( qL > b \) and \( \varepsilon \)
  is small, the unique sequential equilibrium is "verify at delivery,
  reject an un-nameable note", so accepted A2 notes are nameable with
  high probability.  By non-frameability the only off-equilibrium
  outcome is an un-nameable note (never a framed third party), and \( D
  \) – always KYC-identifiable – bears the penalty; the residual risk is
  therefore self-limiting to the recipient.
