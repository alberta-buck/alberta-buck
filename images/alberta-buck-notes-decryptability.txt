A monetary system can promise privacy or it can promise accountability;
the Alberta Buck promises both at once, along a single seam.  /Bulk/
traceability is defeated – no chain-analytics firm can reconstruct who
paid whom – while /bilateral/ disclosure is preserved: the two parties
to any payment always retain the cryptographic means to prove, under
compulsion, exactly who the counterparty was.  The load-bearing half of
that promise is the *non-deniable-receipt invariant*: there must be no
path by which someone pays BUCKs to another and keeps cryptographic
deniability of the act.  Concretely, a recipient who is compelled by a
court, and who chooses to comply, must be able to produce a
cryptographically sound receipt naming the payer's /decrypted, KYC-bound
Identity/.

This document shows that BUCK Notes do not yet satisfy that invariant,
defines it formally, and specifies the fix.  The shipped spend path
verifies the /depositor's/ identity (`IdentityRegistry.verifySpendCP'),
but the mint path verifies /nothing/ about the /issuer's/: the per-leaf
`issuerData' is folded into the commitment's `idHash' and never checked
– exactly the "flavor-specific witness verification (Schnorr for A1/B1,
ElGamal re-randomization well-formedness for A2)" that `mint.circom'
marked "deferred to a later revision" and that was never re-introduced.
A dishonest or colluding issuer can therefore mint a note that pays a
recipient while leaving no recoverable payer Identity.  The remedy is to
make the /deferred approve handshake/ symmetric and fully verified: bind
a checked issuer identity at mint (a Schnorr binding for public issuers;
a recipient-hiding re-encryption proof for the private A2 issuer), and
add the `E_depositor_for_issuer' payload to B-note deposits.  Each leg
is stated as a theorem with an executable `py_ecc' check, and the circom
/ EVM formal-verification tooling that can mechanize the soundness
arguments is catalogued. ([Notes], [Proofs])

1 The Non-Deniable-Receipt Invariant
════════════════════════════════════

  The Alberta Buck identity layer is built so that /every/ BUCK transfer
  leaves both endpoints holding decryptable proof of the other's
  Identity.  For a direct `transfer', the bilateral `approve' handshake
  establishes this: each party re-encrypts their KYC-certified
  credential under the other's key and proves (Chaum-Pedersen) that the
  re-encryption carries the same Identity point \( M \).  BUCK Notes
  defer and reorganise that handshake through the commitment pool, and
  the invariant must survive the reorganisation.


1.1 Statement
─────────────

  Let an /Identity/ be the KYC-certified point \( M \in \mathbb{G}_1 \)
  bound to a registered account by an issuer's Pointcheval-Sanders
  credential and stored (ElGamal-encrypted) as \( E_{\text{addr}}[X] =
  (R_X, C_X) = (r_X G,\ M_X + r_X \cdot pk_X) \), with \( pk_X = sk_X G
  \).  A /payment/ moves value \( v \) from a payer \( P \) to a
  recipient \( D \).

  /(Non-deniable receipt.)/ A protocol satisfies the
  *non-deniable-receipt invariant* if for every accepted payment there
  exists a /receipt/ \( \mathcal{R} \) and an efficient public predicate
  \( \mathsf{RcptVerify} \) such that:
  • *(Completeness.)* The recipient \( D \) can compute \( \mathcal{R}
    \) from data they hold plus public chain state, and \(
    \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \) names a registered Identity
    \( M_P \) and value \( v \).
  • *(Soundness / non-frameability.)* No PPT adversary can produce \(
    \mathcal{R}' \) with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}') = 1 \)
    naming an Identity \( M_{P'} \) that did not author the payment.
  • *(No deniability.)* No PPT payer can cause an accepted payment to \(
    D \) for which /no/ accepting receipt naming the payer exists – even
    in collusion with \( D \).

  The third clause is the operative one: it is the collusion-resistant
  form.  A self-interested recipient who simply wants a record is
  protected by completeness alone; the invariant additionally forbids a
  /willing/ pair from constructing an untraceable BUCK payment.


1.2 Why the asymmetry between flavors
─────────────────────────────────────

  Who can be named, and how, depends on what is known at mint time –
  which is exactly the axis that defines the note flavors:

  • *B (bearer).* The depositor is unknown at mint, so the note cannot
    carry a recipient-targeted ciphertext.  The /issuer/ must therefore
    be a *public* Identity: their \( M \) is in the clear and the
    recipient names them directly.  A bearer note from an /encrypted/
    issuer is unnameable and must be impossible to mint.
  • *A (addressed).* The issuer knows the recipient at mint and has
    already re-encrypted material the eventual depositor can open.  The
    issuer may stay /private/ (A2) by re-encrypting their /own/ Identity
    under the recipient's key (`E_iss-for-rec'), which only the
    recipient can decrypt, or /public/ (A1) by attaching it in the clear
    with a signature.

  In all cases the recipient must end up holding a path to a /verified/
  issuer Identity.  The next section shows the shipped code does not
  provide one.


2 The Receipt as a Proof Chain
══════════════════════════════

  For the recipient to prove "I received \( v \) BUCKs issued by
  Identity \( M_P \)", the receipt must chain four links, each
  independently checkable against public chain state:

  ┌────
  │ (a) OPENING     I hold the opening O; cm = Poseidon5(flavor, v, rho, idHash, predicate)
  │ (b) MINTED      cm appears in the calldata cms[] of mint tx T            [reveals cm]
  │ (c) ISSUER      from O (+ my key), recover AND authenticate M_P
  │ (d) PAID        nullifier = Poseidon3(rho, idHash, tag) in a Spent/SpentA
  │                 event whose pool->recipient Transfer delivered v to me
  └────

  Links (a), (b), (d) are already sound in the shipped contracts.  (b)
  holds because `cms[]' live in the mint transaction's calldata, which
  is immutable chain history; the recipient reveals \( cm \) to anchor
  it (accepting the spend\(\leftrightarrow\)mint linkage, which is the
  price of complying).  (d) holds because the nullifier is a
  deterministic function of the opening and is emitted by `Notes.Spent'
  / `Notes.SpentA' alongside the ERC-20 `Transfer' from the pool.

  *The invariant lives or dies on link (c)* – and (c) is precisely the
  step the implementation never enforces.


3 What the Shipped Implementation Binds
═══════════════════════════════════════

  The spend side verifies the depositor's Identity; the mint side
  verifies nothing about the issuer's.  This asymmetry is the whole bug.

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   Leg                  Shipped check                                                           Guarantee                                       
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   Depositor (A-spend)  `IdentityRegistry.verifySpendCP(spender, recipient, E_n, pi)'           spender provably /is/ the addressed \(M_{rec}\) 
   Depositor (B-spend)  none (knowledge of \(\rho\) is the authorization)                       spender is /some/ registered recipient          
   Issuer  (any mint)   none – `idHash' folds `issuerData' via Poseidon-8, value never checked  *none*                                          
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  Concretely, from the source:

  • `circuits/spend_a.circom' constrains `idHash === Poseidon8(eNoteR.x,
        eNoteR.y, eNoteC.x, eNoteC.y, issuerData[0..3])', where
        `issuerData' is a /private witness/ holding A1 \(
        (m_{\text{iss}}, \sigma_R.x, \sigma_R.y, \sigma_s) \) or A2 \(
        (R_{\text{iss}}, C_{\text{iss}}) = E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \).
        The circuit /hashes it in/ but never verifies the signature or
        the encryption.
  • `circuits/spend.circom' (the shipped B-spend) leaves `flavor'
    unconstrained and binds /no/ issuer material at all; `idHash' is
    opaque.
  • `circuits/mint_batch.circom' public inputs are `[oldRoot, newRoot,
    nextLeafIndex, totalFace, cm]' with private `[flavor, v, rho,
    idHash, predicate, siblings]'.  No issuer key, no signature, no
    ElGamal well-formedness.  `mint.circom' says so in as many words:
    /"flavor-specific witness verification (Schnorr for A1/B1, ElGamal
    re-randomization well-formedness for A2) is deferred to a later
    revision."/
  • `src/Notes.sol' `mint()' records `Minted(issuer = msg.sender, ...)'
    but ties the minting account to the leaves only through transaction
    authorship, never cryptographically to the committed `issuerData'.


4 The Gap, Per Flavor
═════════════════════

4.1 A2 – addressed, private issuer
──────────────────────────────────

  The opening carries \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} = (R_{\text{iss}},
  C_{\text{iss}}) \), an ElGamal ciphertext the recipient decrypts with
  \( sk_{\text{rec}} \) to obtain \( M_{\text{iss}} \).  It is bound
  into `idHash' but *not* proven to re-encrypt the issuer's /registered/
  credential.  A malicious issuer commits a random \( (R_{\text{iss}},
  C_{\text{iss}}) \): the note mints and spends cleanly, the recipient
  decrypts to a meaningless point, and link (c) yields no registered
  Identity.  *The payer keeps full deniability.*


4.2 A1 – addressed, public issuer
─────────────────────────────────

  The opening carries \( (m_{\text{iss}}, \sigma) \).  The Schnorr
  signature \( \sigma \) is never verified, and nothing forces \(
  m_{\text{iss}} \) to equal the /minting/ account's registered \( M \).
  A malicious issuer can omit a valid \( \sigma \) (no receipt), or
  commit a /victim's/ \( m_{\text{iss}} \) and a forged \( \sigma \)
  (frame), and the note still works.


4.3 B1 – bearer, public issuer
──────────────────────────────

  `spend.circom' binds no issuer Identity whatsoever.  The only issuer
  signal is `msg.sender' in the `Minted' event.  A receipt is therefore
  constructible /only if/ the issuer minted from a *public* Identity –
  and the contract cannot enforce that, because `cms[]' are opaque and
  it cannot tell a bearer leaf from an addressed one.  A
  bearer-spendable note minted from an /encrypted/ Identity is
  unnameable.  *The payer keeps full deniability.*


4.4 Summary
───────────

  In every flavor the issuer Identity is, at best, /committed/ but never
  /verified/; in B1 it is not even committed.  The depositor half of the
  handshake is checked (`verifySpendCP'); the issuer half is missing.
  The fix is to add it.


5 The Fix: A Symmetric, Verified Handshake
══════════════════════════════════════════

  Restore the symmetry the "deferred approve handshake" framing always
  implied: the /mint/ is the issuer's half of approve and must be
  /verified/, exactly as the /deposit/ verifies the depositor's half.


5.1 Issuer half, bound at mint
──────────────────────────────

  At mint, the issuer is `msg.sender' – /not/ anonymous (they authored
  the tx).  So the issuer's registered credential \(
  E_{\text{addr}}[\text{issuer}] \) may be read as a public input; only
  the /recipient/ (for A2) must stay hidden.

  • *Public issuer (A1, B1).* Require `msg.sender' to be
    `isPublicIdentity', and verify a Schnorr signature by their
    registered key \( pk_{\text{iss}} \) over the batch commitment \(
    H_{\text{batch}} = \mathrm{keccak}(\texttt{cms}) \).  Bind \(
    m_{\text{iss}} = M_{\text{iss}} \) (the in-the-clear registered
    Identity) into `idHash'.  Because the registered Identity of a
    public account is in the clear, link (c) reduces to a registry read
    plus the verified signature.
  • *Private issuer (A2).* The issuer proves `E_iss-for-rec' re-encrypts
    their /own/ registered \( M_{\text{iss}} \) under the recipient's
    key \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) – the `verifyApprove' relation with
    `sender = issuer', `spender = recipient'.  But `verifyApprove' as
    shipped takes \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) as a verifier input and so
    /names the recipient/, which A2 must not do.  Two recipient-hiding
    realisations:

    1. *In-SNARK (mint circuit).* Extend `mint_batch.circom' so each A2
       leaf proves, with \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) and \( M_{\text{iss}} \)
       as private witnesses: \[ pk_{\text{iss}} = sk_{\text{iss}}
       G,\quad M_{\text{iss}} = C^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}} -
       sk_{\text{iss}} R^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}},\quad
       E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} = \bigl(r' G,\ M_{\text{iss}} + r'\,
       pk_{\text{rec}}\bigr), \] with \( E_{\text{addr}}[\text{issuer}]
       = (R^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}}, C^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}}) \) a
       /public/ input and \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) the same key that
       re-randomises the note ciphertext \( E_{\text{note}} \).  Cost: a
       handful of in-circuit \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) scalar muls per A2 leaf
       – the ~10x the original deferral feared, but bounded and paid
       only on A2 mints.
    2. *Recipient-blinded on-chain CP.* Keep verification on-chain
       (EIP-196, cheap) but blind \( pk_{\text{rec}} \): the issuer
       proves the re-encryption relation against a /Pedersen commitment/
       to \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) that also appears (identically
       committed) in \( E_{\text{note}} \), so the verifier never learns
       \( pk_{\text{rec}} \).  This is a new sigma protocol (Section
       /Provable Correctness/), avoiding in-SNARK \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)
       arithmetic at the cost of a bespoke proof.

  The in-SNARK route (1) is the recommended default: it reuses the
  trusted setup already in place for the batch mint and keeps the
  on-chain mint a single verifier call.


5.2 Depositor half, at deposit
──────────────────────────────

  • *A1, A2.* Already shipped: `verifySpendCP' proves the spender is the
    addressed \( M_{\text{rec}} \).  The issuer, who chose \(
    M_{\text{rec}} \) at mint, already holds the symmetric knowledge.
  • *B1.* Add `E_depositor_for_issuer' to the spend: the depositor
    re-encrypts /their/ registered \( M_{\text{dep}} \) under the public
    issuer's \( pk_{\text{iss}} \), with a `verifyApprove'-style CP
    proof (`sender = depositor', `spender = issuer', both
    public-or-named here so no blinding is needed), emitted in the
    `SpentB' event.  This closes the symmetric direction – the public
    bearer issuer can identify who cashed their note.


5.3 Enforcing bearer => public issuer
─────────────────────────────────────

  The contract cannot read `flavor' from an opaque `cm'.  Make the mint
  flavor-aware for the binding: the mint circuit exposes, per leaf, a
  public `issuerMode in {PUBLIC, PRIVATE}' and proves the matching
  binding (Schnorr-bound public \( M \), or in-SNARK re-encryption).
  `Notes.mint' requires every `PRIVATE' leaf to be an A-flavor
  (addressed) leaf – a bearer (B) leaf with `issuerMode = PRIVATE' is
  unsatisfiable – and requires `msg.sender' to be `isPublicIdentity'
  whenever any `PUBLIC' leaf is present.


6 Protocol Changes (Implementation Surface)
═══════════════════════════════════════════

6.1 IdentityRegistry
────────────────────

  ┌────
  │ // Public-issuer binding: Schnorr by msg.sender's registered key over H_batch.
  │ function verifyIssuerSchnorr(address issuer, bytes32 hBatch, SchnorrProof calldata s)
  │     external view returns (bool);
  │ 
  │ // Private-issuer binding (A2), recipient-blinded sigma variant (option 2):
  │ // proves E_iss_for_rec re-encrypts E_addr[issuer]'s M under a *committed* pk_rec
  │ // that the mint SNARK also binds to E_note, without revealing pk_rec.
  │ function verifyIssuerCPBlinded(address issuer, ElGamalCT calldata eIssForRec,
  │                                bytes32 pkRecCommit, IssuerCPProof calldata pi)
  │     external view returns (bool);
  │ 
  │ // B-note depositor->issuer (mirror of verifyApprove, both parties named):
  │ function verifyDepositorForIssuer(address depositor, address issuer,
  │                                   ElGamalCT calldata eDepForIss, CPProof calldata pi)
  │     external view returns (bool);
  └────

  Under the in-SNARK route the A2 binding is discharged inside the mint
  verifier and `verifyIssuerCPBlinded' is unnecessary; the registry then
  only gains `verifyIssuerSchnorr' and `verifyDepositorForIssuer'.


6.2 Notes.sol
─────────────

  • `mint(...)' gains per-leaf `issuerMode[]' (public input of the mint
    SNARK) and, for public leaves, a `SchnorrProof' over `keccak(cms)';
    it requires `msg.sender' public when any public leaf is present, and
    rejects `PRIVATE+bearer' leaves.  The A2 re-encryption binding rides
    inside the mint proof (option 1) or is checked per leaf via
    `verifyIssuerCPBlinded' (option 2).
  • `spend(...)' (B) gains `(E_depositor_for_issuer, CPProof)', calls
    `verifyDepositorForIssuer(msg.sender, issuer, ...)', and emits
    `SpentB(nullifier, face, recipient, eDepForIss)'.  The issuer
    address is recovered from the leaf's public-issuer binding revealed
    at spend.
  • `spendACP(...)' (A) is unchanged on the depositor side; the issuer
    guarantee now comes for free from the mint-time binding.


6.3 Circuits
────────────

  • `mint_batch.circom': add the per-leaf `issuerMode' public signal
    and, for A2 leaves, the re-encryption constraints above (option 1).
    For public leaves, constrain `idHash''s issuerData prefix to equal
    the registered public \( M \) so the in-clear identity is the one
    bound.
  • `spend.circom' (B): constrain `flavor ==' B1= and reveal enough of
    the leaf's public-issuer binding to recover the issuer address
    on-chain.
  • `spend_a.circom': unchanged (the A2 issuer binding moved to mint).


6.4 Wallet (`alberta_buck.wallet')
──────────────────────────────────

  • Mint: construct the issuer Schnorr proof (public) or the A2
    re-encryption witness (private), mirroring `notes.id_hash_a1/a2'.
  • Spend (B): construct `E_depositor_for_issuer' and its CP proof.
  • Receipt: implement `RcptVerify' as a standalone verifier (opening +
    mint-tx reference + spend-tx reference + issuer recovery) so a
    recipient can produce and a third party can check the receipt
    entirely off-chain from chain data.


7 Provable Correctness
══════════════════════

  We extend the companion /Proofs/ document (Theorems 1-11) with the
  issuer-binding results.  Each is accompanied – in the proofs
  document's house style – by an executable `py_ecc' sanity check on
  BN254.


7.1 Theorems
────────────

  /(Issuer-binding completeness.)/ An honestly generated public-issuer
  Schnorr proof, and an honestly generated A2 re-encryption proof
  (in-SNARK or blinded), verify.

  /(Issuer-binding soundness.)/ If `verifyIssuerSchnorr' accepts, the
  batch commitments were authorised by `issuer''s registered key.  If
  the A2 re-encryption proof accepts, \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \)
  decrypts under \( sk_{\text{rec}} \) to the issuer's /registered/ \(
  M_{\text{iss}} \) (the same \( M \) certified by the issuer's PS
  credential – /Theorem 6/).  Reduction: special soundness of the
  underlying sigma protocol (two accepting transcripts sharing
  commitments extract \( sk_{\text{iss}}, r' \)), as in /Theorems 4, 6/;
  or Groth16 knowledge-soundness for the in-SNARK route.

  /(Receipt completeness.)/ For every note accepted by the patched
  `mint' and redeemed by `spend=/=spendACP', the recipient can compute
  \( \mathcal{R} \) with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \)
  naming a registered Identity.  Proof: links (a),(b),(d) hold as
  before; (c) now holds by issuer-binding soundness (public: registry \(
  M \) + Schnorr; private: decrypt \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \),
  validity guaranteed by the accepted binding).

  /(Receipt soundness / non-frameability.)/ No PPT adversary produces an
  accepting \( \mathcal{R}' \) naming \( M_{P'} \) that did not author
  the payment.  Reduction: a forged public receipt breaks Schnorr
  EUF-CMA; a forged private receipt breaks re-encryption soundness
  (/Theorem 7/) or the binding hash (collision resistance of Poseidon-8
  in `idHash').

  /(No deniability under collusion.)/ No payer-recipient coalition can
  produce an accepted payment with /no/ accepting receipt: `mint'
  rejects any leaf lacking a valid issuer binding, so every spendable
  leaf carries one, and the binding is recoverable by the holder of the
  opening.

  /(Recipient privacy preserved.)/ The A2 issuer binding is
  zero-knowledge in \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) and \( M_{\text{iss}} \): the
  in-SNARK route reveals only the Groth16 proof; the blinded route
  reveals only a hiding Pedersen commitment to \( pk_{\text{rec}} \).
  No observer learns the recipient.  Reduction: HVZK of the sigma
  protocol (/Theorem 5/) / Groth16 zero-knowledge.

  /(Mutual decryptability.)/ Combining the issuer half (above) with the
  depositor half (`verifySpendCP' for A; `verifyDepositorForIssuer' for
  B), both parties can recover the other's registered Identity, and each
  can prove it under compulsion.  This is the Notes specialisation of
  the BUCK mutual-decryptability invariant (/Proofs/ Theorem 11), now
  /enforced/ rather than /assumed/.


7.2 Executable sanity checks (`py_ecc' on BN254)
────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Mirroring the proofs document, each theorem ships a small witnessed
  check that runs in CI alongside the existing `alberta_buck.wallet'
  vector generation:

  • *Issuer Schnorr*: sign \( H_{\text{batch}} \) under \(
    sk_{\text{iss}} \), assert verify; assert a tampered \(
    H_{\text{batch}} \) or wrong key fails.
  • *A2 re-encryption*: build \( E_{\text{addr}}[\text{issuer}] \),
    decrypt to \( M_{\text{iss}} \), form \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \)
    under \( pk_{\text{rec}} \), run the prover/verifier; assert the
    recipient's \( sk_{\text{rec}} \)-decryption equals \(
    M_{\text{iss}} \); assert a random \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \) is
    rejected (soundness witness) and that the transcript hides \(
    pk_{\text{rec}} \) (two runs with distinct \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) are
    indistinguishable to the verifier given the commitment).
  • *Receipt*: assemble \( \mathcal{R} \) from a synthetic mint+spend
    and assert `RcptVerify' = 1; assert a frame attempt (swap \( M_P \))
    fails.

  These vectors double as the canonical inputs the Solidity and circom
  tests load via `vm.readFile', keeping the on-chain verifier, the
  circuit, and the reference math in lock-step (the pattern already used
  for `verifySpendCP').


8 Formal-Verification Approaches
════════════════════════════════

  "Every step provably correct" spans three artifact classes – the
  sigma-protocol mathematics, the circom circuits, and the Solidity
  verifiers – each with its own tractable tooling.


8.1 The cryptographic mathematics
─────────────────────────────────

  • *Hand proofs + `py_ecc'*: the existing /Proofs/ methodology –
    completeness, special-soundness (2-extractability), and HVZK
    arguments, each pinned by an executable BN254 check.  Low-tech,
    high-confidence, already in the repo.
  • *EasyCrypt*: a proof assistant purpose-built for /game-based/
    cryptographic proofs; the sigma-protocol soundness and ZK lemmas can
    be mechanised as reductions, giving machine-checked versions of
    Theorems above.
  • *CryptoVerif* (computational) or *ProVerif* (symbolic):
    protocol-level analysis of the full handshake (mint binding +
    deposit binding) against the non-deniability and recipient-privacy
    goals stated as queries.


8.2 The circom circuits (soundness is the hard part)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  The danger in arithmetic circuits is /under-constraint/ – a witness
  slot that the prover can set freely, silently widening soundness (the
  exact failure mode of the unverified `issuerData').  Tooling that
  targets this directly:

  • *Picus* (Veridise): automated detection of under-constrained
    signals; run it on the patched `mint_batch.circom' to prove the new
    issuer-binding signals are /determined/ by the public inputs.
  • *Circomspect* (Trail of Bits): static analysis / linting for common
    circom pitfalls (unconstrained signals, `<--' without `<=',=
    signal-vs-constraint confusion).
  • *ecne* (0xPARC): proves R1CS systems compute deterministic functions
    of their inputs – a soundness witness for the binding constraints.
  • *Coda* / *circom-to-Coq* efforts: full functional-correctness
    extraction to a proof assistant for the security-critical templates
    (the re-encryption gate).
  • Discipline: keep the security-critical constraints (the \(
    \mathbb{G}_1 \) relations) in small, separately-audited
    sub-templates with explicit `Num2Bits' range checks, as the
    value-conservation bound already does.


8.3 The Solidity verifiers and Notes.sol
────────────────────────────────────────

  • *Halmos* (a16z): symbolic-execution property tests in Solidity –
    assert, for all inputs, e.g. "`mint' reverts unless every leaf
    carries a valid issuer binding" and "`spend' emits a recoverable
    issuer for every accepted note."
  • *Kontrol / KEVM* (Runtime Verification): K-framework formal proofs
    over EVM bytecode for the highest-assurance properties (the
    binding-or-revert invariant, nullifier non-reuse).
  • *Certora Prover*: CVL rules for state invariants (`noteFaceSum'
    conservation, "no accepted spend without a prior valid mint
    binding").
  • *solc SMTChecker*: lightweight, in-compiler assertions and overflow
    checks on the new code paths.
  • *Foundry*: fuzz + stateful invariant testing as the always-on
    backstop; negative tests below are expressed here first.


8.4 What "provably correct per step" buys
─────────────────────────────────────────

  The chain of guarantees is: =py_ecc=/EasyCrypt prove the /math/;
  Picus/ecne prove the /circuit/ faithfully and exhaustively enforces
  that math; Halmos/Kontrol prove the /contract/ admits no spend whose
  mint lacked the binding.  Composed, no accepted Note payment can exist
  without a recoverable, sound receipt – the invariant, mechanically
  checked end-to-end.


9 Test Plan
═══════════

9.1 Positive
────────────

  • A1/A2/B1 honest mint + spend; recipient runs `RcptVerify' and a
    third party re-checks it – all return 1 naming the correct
    registered Identity.
  • A2 recipient privacy: two mints to distinct recipients are
    observationally indistinguishable on chain.


9.2 Negative (these must now fail, and did not before)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  • A2 issuer commits a random `E_iss-for-rec': `mint' *reverts*
    (binding fails).
  • A1 issuer commits a victim's \( m_{\text{iss}} \) / a forged \(
    \sigma \): `mint' *reverts*.
  • Bearer leaf with `issuerMode = PRIVATE', or any public leaf from a
    non-`isPublicIdentity' minter: `mint' *reverts*.
  • Recipient attempts a forged receipt naming a non-issuer:
    `RcptVerify' = 0.


9.3 Cross-artifact
──────────────────

  • The `py_ecc' vectors drive both the circom witness tests and the
    Solidity verifier tests, asserting byte-identical `idHash' / proof
    encodings (the established `alberta_buck.wallet' -> `vm.readFile'
    pattern).
  • Picus run in CI gates merges on "no new under-constrained signals."


10 Phasing and Migration
════════════════════════

  1. *Public issuers first (A1, B1).* Schnorr binding +
     `isPublicIdentity' enforcement + `verifyDepositorForIssuer' for B.
     No new circuit setup; pure contract + wallet work.  Closes the gap
     for the public-issuer flavors, which are the audit-sensitive
     payroll / cashier-cheque cases.
  2. *Private issuer (A2).* The in-SNARK re-encryption binding: extend
     `mint_batch.circom', regenerate the per-N verifiers and trusted
     setup, wire `issuerMode'.  The larger lift; ship behind a
     governance-set verifier slot so the new mint verifier can be
     rotated in (`Notes.setMintVerifier').
  3. *Mechanisation.* Land Picus/Halmos in CI; mechanise the sigma
     proofs in EasyCrypt as time allows.

  Notes minted under the old (unbound) verifier remain spendable but do
  not carry the guarantee; governance announces a cutover root after
  which only bound mints are accepted, and wallets surface a `UNVERIFIED
  ISSUER' status for pre-cutover notes (a new wallet status alongside
  `VALID=/=DEAD=/=FAKE').


11 Open Questions
═════════════════

  1. *In-SNARK vs blinded-CP for A2.* The in-SNARK route is simpler to
     reason about (one verifier) but enlarges the trusted setup; the
     blinded-CP route keeps the SNARK small but adds a bespoke sigma
     protocol to audit.  The recommendation is in-SNARK, but the
     blinded-CP construction is worth a full soundness/ZK write-up
     before deciding.
  2. *Batch vs per-leaf issuer signature.* A single Schnorr over
     `keccak(cms)' is cheapest but binds the /whole batch/ to one public
     issuer; mixed-issuer batches need per-leaf signatures or per-issuer
     sub-batches.
  3. *Receipt non-repudiation scope.* `RcptVerify' proves the issuer
     /authored/ the note; tying that to a real-world legal identity
     still rests on the KYC binding of \( M \) (the PS credential).
     This document assumes that binding; it does not re-derive it.
  4. *Predicate-bearing notes.* The `predicate' field is out of scope
     here; a programmable predicate that itself moves value would need
     its own receipt analysis.
