The BuckBasket (see /The BuckBasket/) keeps itself roughly balanced for
free: redemptions draw from overweight pools, deposits and treasury
sweeps fill underweight ones, and the world's arbitrageurs do the rest.
What that leaves on the table is /timing/.  Basket constituents do not
wander at random – they trail the M2 money supply with measurable,
commodity-specific lags, so a constituent's share of basket value takes
long, slow excursions away from its target weight and then comes home as
the laggards catch up.

This article does four things.  First, it measures those lags and turns
them into a rebalancing policy: scale each constituent's rebalancing
effort by how far it is from proportion, /times/ how hard its delayed
moving average is turning back toward proportion – quench while the
excursion is still running, pour when it levels off.  Second, it
verifies the policy in simulation: the gated policies capture 75-90% of
the continuous-rebalancing premium at a quarter to a half of the
turnover, with by far the best per-trade timing.

Third, it changes coordinates.  A single window on a single
share-vs-target deviation harvests one timescale and carries the
numeraire's noise; working instead in /differential mode/ – pairwise log
ratios, which cancel the common mode exactly, across a seven-scale
filter bank – beats even continuous rebalancing's gross harvest, and is
what deploys.

Fourth – the heart of the matter – it encodes all of this as an EVM
state machine whose signal maintenance is /amortized across
activations/: every deposit, redemption, or bare keeper trigger carries
a small, bounded slice of the work, closed-form catch-up makes a poke
after a quiet week cost the same as a poke after a busy hour, and the
whole machine advises the basket through two O(1) reads: where deposits
should go, and which pool best satisfies a redemption.


1 The premise: money moves first, commodities follow

  The ordering is exactly the intuition: monetary, then energy and
  construction, then labour and food.  (Gold's fit is weak – it trades
  on real rates and crisis as much as on M2 – so it takes a fast
  "monetary asset" prior rather than a fitted lag.)

  The consequence for the basket: when M2 surges, the fast constituents
  overshoot their target share of basket value and stay overweight
  /until the laggards catch up/ – an excursion with a timescale set by
  the lag spread, roughly a year.  These excursions are the basket's
  harvest.  A rebalancer that reacts to instantaneous deviation starts
  selling the riser immediately and keeps selling all the way up –
  paying fees and bleeding against the trend.  One that waits for the
  excursion to /level off/ makes fewer, larger trades at maximum
  mispricing.


2 The policy: quench while diverging, pour on the turn
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  Per constituent \(i\), at a fixed sampling cadence (one epoch ~ one
  day):

  \begin{align*}
    \delta_i &= w_i / w^*_i - 1
      && \text{-- raw share deviation, right now};\\
    m_i &= \mathrm{EMA}_{X_i}(\delta_i)
      && \text{-- the delayed view (\(X_i\)-day window)};\\
    v_i &= \Delta m_i
      && \text{-- its velocity: receding / level / gaining};\\
    \dot{d}_i &= \mathrm{EMA}_{7\mathrm{d}}(\Delta\delta_i)
      && \text{-- the observed closure rate}.
  \end{align*}

  The rules, each of which was a /measured failure/ in the Python model
  before it was a rule (`alberta_buck/sim/rebalance_policy.py', `make
  nix-sim-policy'):

  1. *Direction and magnitude come from the raw deviation; the MA
     contributes only timing.* A lagging average that still reads
     "overweight" after the raw share has reverted through target must
     not trade.  Require `sign(m) == sign(delta)'.
  2. *The velocity of the MA classifies the regime.* Still receding
     (deviation growing): do nothing – don't fight a move that is still
     developing.  Level (turning): start, but not too fast.  Gaining
     (closing): complete the remaining rebalancing.
  3. *Size by rate-matching.* Trade at \(\rho\) times the closure rate
     the 1-week EMA actually observes – "the rate at which we'd lose
     half the distance to balance" – floored by a "half the gap in one
     window" prior (which alone applies while level): \[
     \mathrm{effort}_i \;=\; \min\bigl(\rho\,\hat{c}_i\,w^*_i,\
     \mathrm{cap}\bigr), \qquad \hat{c}_i \;=\;
     \max\Bigl(-\mathrm{sign}(\delta_i)\,\dot{d}_i,\ \tfrac{\ln
     2}{2X_i}\,\lvert\delta_i\rvert\Bigr). \] This is self-calibrating
     (no hand gain) and self-limiting: our own flow feeds the observed
     rate, so raising \(\rho\) saturates instead of overshooting.
     Effort peaks just after the turn – closure accelerating while
     deviation is still near maximum – and tapers as the gap closes.
  4. *A leash bounds the quench.* A secular trend that never levels off
     (BTC, 2020-25) would otherwise run the deviation away unboundedly.
     Past

     hysteresis release at 25%.  The factor times trades /within/ the

  5. *Proceeds recycle, but never wash.* Sell-side flow re-enters the

     donates the AMM fee.

  Every policy in that table reads one signal: a constituent's share of
  basket value against its target, on a single window.  The next section
  replaces that signal with a different one – and beats all four.  It is
  the engine the deployed contract actually runs, so the table above is
  best read as the argument for /gating/, not as the menu of what ships.


3 Differential mode: the basket as a polyphase machine
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  The policy above has two weaknesses, and both come from the
  /coordinate system/ rather than from the gating.

  First, *one window harvests one timescale.* Windows fitted to the M2
  lag spread catch the year-long excursions and concede every mid-size
  swing to whoever is rebalancing continuously.  Second,
  *share-of-basket-value is not a clean observable.* A constituent's
  share is measured from pool BUCK reserves, so it carries the
  numeraire's own noise: when BUCK moves against the dollar, every share
  moves, and the signal cannot tell that from a commodity actually
  getting rich.

  The reconception (2026-07-11) is to change coordinates.  In a
  three-phase machine one does not reason about the three line voltages;
  one splits them into a *common mode* (the zero sequence, which carries
  no torque) and the *differential modes* (which carry all of it).  The
  basket has exactly this structure:

        The profit lives in the differentials between commodity
        legs.  The common mode – BUCK's own valuation against the
        numeraire – is the K-controller's job, and it *cancels
        exactly* in cross-commodity log price ratios.

  So the `pairs' engine works pairwise and logarithmically.  For legs
  \(i,j\) the imbalance is \[ d_{ij} \;=\;
  \ln\frac{1+\delta_i}{1+\delta_j}, \] which is numeraire-free by
  construction: any factor common to both legs subtracts out.  A Uniswap
  /tick is already a log price/, so the engine reads ticks straight from
  `slot0' and never needs `ln()' on-chain.


3.1 A filter bank instead of a window
─────────────────────────────────────

  Each leg carries \(K=7\) EMAs of its normalized tick, on geometrically
  spaced windows (5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320 days).  Because the EMA is
  linear, /every pair's moving average, velocity and curvature at every
  scale is the difference of two legs' ladders/ – the full \(N(N-1)/2\)
  differential graph falls out of \(O(NK)\) state, with no pairwise
  storage at all.

  Differences of adjacent EMAs are band-pass filters, so the ladder is
  an octave filter bank: the short windows see mid-size swings, the long
  ones see the M2 excursions, and the same gating logic applies
  independently at each scale.


3.2 The quorum turn detector
────────────────────────────

  Per pair, each scale casts a vote, and it votes only when both of the
  single-scale rules hold at that scale: the MA gap agrees in sign with
  the raw pairwise imbalance, /and/ its curvature points back toward
  equilibrium.  The pair opens when votes reach a quorum (4 of 7 by
  default).  No single scale can fire alone, which is what makes a fast
  window safe to include.

  Effort is \(\kappa\,\lvert d_{ij}\rvert\,(\text{votes}/K)\), capped,
  under a pairwise leash with hysteresis (30% / 25%).  Trades are
  *matched pairs* – sell the rich leg, buy the poor leg, equal value –
  so they are self-financing, leave no cash residue, and are
  structurally wash-proof: the engine cannot sell a constituent and
  rebuy it in the same round, which is the failure the single-scale
  policy needed an explicit no-wash rule to avoid.  Progressive warmup
  lets the short windows vote from about day 20, so the engine is not
  silent for a 116-day window's warmup.

3.4 Which engine, and when
──────────────────────────

  The honest summary of the whole study is a regime split:

  • *`pairs' is the reverting-regime pick* – the regime the BuckBasket
    is built for, and the one the physics argument in /The BuckBasket/
    says is the default behaviour of the physical economy.
  • *`factor' remains the trend-regime pick* – best historical CAGR
    (19.3%) through the 2020-25 replay, where BTC ran x9 and reversion
    never arrived.

  Between the two engines that /exist on-chain/, though, the choice is
  not close: `pairs' dominates `vrate' on both the premium and the risk
  measure.  `vrate' concentrates its trades where observed closure is
  fastest, which in a trend means selling into sharp V-shaped pullbacks
  that then resume – its historical capture90 is the worst of any policy
  studied at -676bp, against `pairs' / `vel' at +270bp.  In the
  reverting regime that same concentration is maximally right (+514bp,
  the best of any policy), which is precisely what makes it the wrong
  default: it is the policy whose performance depends most on the regime
  being the one you hoped for.


4 Encoding it on-chain: work that amortizes across whoever shows up
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  The policy needs a clock – moving averages advance with time – but the
  EVM has no scheduler and nobody pays to run one.  The established
  answer is the pattern behind Compound's `accrueInterest', MakerDAO's
  per-collateral `drip', and Uniswap's oracle observations:

        *Lazy time-indexed accrual.* State is advanced on first
        touch, as a closed-form function of elapsed time – never
        per block.

  Three refinements make it fit a /multi-pool signal aggregator/:


4.1 Closed-form EMA catch-up
────────────────────────────

  Under sample-and-hold (the deviation observed now stands in for the
  quiet gap), \(n\) missed epochs of EMA recursion collapse exactly,
  with \(q = 1-\beta\) (and \(q_7\) the one-week EMA's decay):

  \begin{align*}
    m' &= \delta + (m - \delta)\,q^n
      && \text{-- the average};\\
    v' &= q^n\,v \;-\; (1-q)^2\,n\,q^{\,n-1}\,(m - \delta)
      && \text{-- its velocity};\\
    \dot{d}' &= q_7^{\,n-1}\bigl((\delta - \delta_{\mathrm{prev}})(1-q_7)
                + q_7\,\dot{d}\bigr)
      && \text{-- the closure rate};
  \end{align*}

  each computable in \(O(\log n)\) by binary exponentiation.  One poke
  after a quiet month costs the same as one poke after a busy hour, /and
  lands the signals in the same place/ n diligent pokes would.  This is
  not approximately true; it is an invariant the test suite fuzzes (256
  random gap/imbalance combinations) and enforces to fixed-point
  rounding.  (The velocity closed form matters: a naive "average dm over
  the gap" EMA update classifies the regime differently than the
  diligent recursion – a real bug the regime test matrix caught.)


4.2 The round-robin work wheel
──────────────────────────────

  N constituents, a persistent cursor, and `poke(maxWork)': each call
  advances at most `maxWork' stale constituents from the cursor on.
  Every basket activation – deposit, redeem, treasury sweep, or a bare
  keeper trigger – carries a small budget (1-2).  The gas story,
  measured (6-token mocks, via-IR, solc 0.8.28):

  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
   operation                           gas     
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
   poke one constituent, 1-epoch gap   ~36,500 
   poke one constituent, 50-epoch gap  ~19,900 
   poke when everything is fresh       ~3,100  
  ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Catch-up is /flat in gap size/ (the 50-epoch poke is cheaper than the
  1-epoch one purely from storage warmth), and the fresh-epoch guard is
  near-free.  So: *the more granular the triggering, the less work each
  agent performs* – and if nobody shows up for a week, whoever arrives
  next pays one closed-form update, not a week of them.  Work is
  conserved; granularity trades per-call gas against signal staleness,
  never total cost.


4.3 Cached observations with running sums
─────────────────────────────────────────

  A constituent's deviation needs /everyone's/ pool values (its share of
  the total).  Reading \(N\) pools per poke would defeat the sharding,
  so each poke reads only /its own/ pool – one `balanceOf' (the BUCK
  reserve \(bv_i\); for full-range positions pool value \(= 2\,bv_i\))
  and one `slot0' (spot, for the price-scaled target share \(s_i =
  a_i\,P_{0,i}^2/\mathrm{spot}_i\), the same fixed-quantity-index target
  the pro-rata redemption uses) – and folds the result into cached
  running sums by difference:

  \[ B \mathrel{{+}{=}} bv_i^{\mathrm{new}} - bv_i^{\mathrm{cached}},
     \qquad S \mathrel{{+}{=}} s_i^{\mathrm{new}} -
     s_i^{\mathrm{cached}}, \qquad \delta_i = \frac{bv_i / B}{\,s_i /
     S\,} - 1 . \]

  Deviations are computed against slightly-stale sums – which a
  moving-average policy is insensitive to by construction.  Decision
  data aggregates the same way: each poke stores its constituent's
  signed advisory effort, and the two decision reads – `depositHint()'
  (strongest buy-side effort) and `redeemHint()' (strongest sell-side) –
  are trivial scans over N cached int32s.


4.4 The trust model: hints, not authority
─────────────────────────────────────────

  The director holds no funds and executes nothing.  Its outputs are
  /advisory/: the basket's venue re-verifies everything material (the
  spot-vs-TWAP manipulation guard, liquidity bounds, the conversion-loss
  budget) on every trade it actually performs.  A stale, wrong, or even
  adversarial hint can re-order flows – route a deposit to the
  second-best pool – but cannot break solvency or extract value beyond
  what the venue's own guards already bound.  That is what makes it safe
  for the signal state to be lazily maintained by unauthenticated public
  pokes.


5 The contracts: two engines on a shared chassis
════════════════════════════════════════════════

  `RebalanceDirectorBase.sol' is the policy-agnostic chassis – epoch
  clock, `poke(maxWork)' work wheel, metadata sync, cached observations
  with running sums, binary exponentiation, and the `depositHint' /
  `redeemHint' / `effortOf' surface – and two signal engines plug into
  it behind the common `IRebalanceDirector' advisory interface:

  • `BasketRebalanceDirector.sol' (*vrate*) – the single-window
    share-vs-target policy derived above.
  • `PairsRebalanceDirector.sol' (*pairs*) – differential mode: per-leg
    7-window tick-EMA ladders, pair quorum votes on the confirmed turn
    (`vel'), arithmetic weight-ratio differential, pairwise leash
    bitmask, and `bestPair()' naming the top matched trade for keepers.
    Pair efforts are folded into per-leg /net/ efforts at poke time, so
    the chassis's advisory reads stay O(N) cached – fully API-compatible
    with `vrate'.

  *`pairs' at quorum 4 is what deploys*, and what the rebalancing
  simulation runs.  The rest of this section describes `vrate', whose
  state layout is the simpler illustration of the shared machinery.

  `src/basket/BasketRebalanceDirector.sol' (~2.5 slots of hot state per
  constituent, packed):

  ┌────
  │ struct Signal {
  │     int128  m;         // EMA_X of deviation (1e18)
  │     int128  vEma;      // EMA_X of per-epoch d(m)
  │     int128  ddotEma;   // 1-week EMA of per-epoch d(delta)
  │     int128  prevDelta;
  │     uint128 bv;        // cached pool BUCK reserve
  │     uint128 s;         // cached price-scaled target share
  │     int32   effortBp;  // signed advisory effort (bp of NAV per epoch)
  │     uint32  lastEpoch;
  │     uint32  firstEpoch;
  │     bool    leashed;
  │ }
  └────

  The API surface:

  • `syncConstituents()' – permissionless; pulls constituent metadata
    from the basket's public getters (re-callable: `addBasketToken'
    renormalizes).
  • `poke(maxWork)' / `pokeAll()' – the work wheel.  Skips fresh
    constituents for ~2 warm SLOADs; advances stale ones with the closed
    forms above and the vrate effort rule (regime gate, rate-matched
    size, leash, warmup).
  • `depositHint()' / `redeemHint()' / `effortOf(i)' / `effortsAll()' –
    the O(1)/O(N)-cached advisory reads.  A `type(uint256).max' hint
    means "no signal; use the default most-underweight routing".
  • `setParams(...)' – governance: epoch length, window, ρ, deadband,
    regime band, leash bounds, per-epoch cap.

  How `BuckBasketProRata' consumes it (the integration increments, in
  order of invasiveness):

  1. *Increment 0 – flow routing, zero new trades.* `_depositBuck' and
     `sweepTreasury' pass `director.depositHint()' as the `poolHint' to
     `investFromBucks' (falling back to most-underweight on the
     sentinel), and the single-token redeem path consults
     `redeemHint()'.  Every such activation also calls
     `director.poke(1)' – the bounded slice that keeps the signals
     alive.  The basket's ordinary deposit/redeem/sweep traffic then
     performs well-timed rebalancing /for free/.
  2. *Increment 1 – keeper rebalance steps.* A permissionless pair,
     `rebalanceSource(i)' (withdraw + convert from the strongest
     sell-side pool, bounded by `effortBp') and `rebalanceSink()'
     (invest the pending BUCK into the deposit hint), each one small
     transaction – the `sweepTreasury' pattern, directed by the signals.
  3. *Weighted draws.* `_allocateSellHigh' can tilt its per-pool draw
     weights by `effortsAll()' rather than spot overweight alone.


6 Verification

  Three layers, all runnable from the Makefile:

  (The model figures above regenerate via `make nix-sim-policy' then
  `make nix-sim-plot-article'.)

  • *Unit + fuzz (Forge)* – `make nix-test-director'.  Ten tests: the

  • *Parallel regime matrix* – `make nix-test-director-regimes'.  The
    same suite re-run across a window x rho grid (defaults: {6, 8, 16}
    epochs x {1.5, 3, 6}), one forge process per regime, in parallel.
    This matrix is what exposed the velocity catch-up approximation:
    windows of 6 classified a post-gap regime differently between the
    lazy and diligent paths until the closed form replaced the
    average-velocity shortcut.
  • *Anvil end-to-end* – `make nix-sim-director'.  The rebalancing
    scenario (~400 agents: arbs, LPs, direct-mint depositors, a whale)

    2-constituent budget each tick and executes its advice as 2-hop
    Universal Router swaps (sell-hint -> BUCK -> buy-hint), sized by the
    advised bp of basket NAV.  The output vector records `directorPokes'
    / `directorTrades' per day alongside the existing basket telemetry.


7 Variants worth investigating
══════════════════════════════

  Nothing in this section is measured.  It is the set of directions the
  machine's own structure suggests, each with the literature that would
  supply the theory, ordered by what looks like the best return on
  effort.  Two analogies organize them, and both are load-bearing rather
  than decorative.


7.1 Taking the machine analogy literally
────────────────────────────────────────

  The three-phase intuition that produced differential mode has more in
  it.  A polyphase drive does not stop at "separate the common mode"; it
  applies two specific transforms, and both have an exact basket
  counterpart:

  • The *Clarke transform* (abc to alpha-beta-zero) splits a three-phase
    vector into a zero sequence – which carries no torque – and two
    /orthogonal/ differential components.  The basket's log-weight
    vector splits the same way: one common mode (BUCK against the
    numeraire, the K-controller's business) and \(N-1\) differential
    modes.
  • The *Park transform* (alpha-beta to d-q) then rotates into a frame
    turning with the field, so that quantities which were sinusoidal
    become constant and a plain PI controller suffices.  Field-oriented
    control splits current into a torque-producing component and a
    flux-producing one, and regulates them separately.

  The basket's d-q split is the useful part: the component of an
  imbalance that is /harvestable/ (mean-reverting – torque) versus the
  component that is /secular/ (a trend that must be tolerated and
  bounded – flux).  The leash is today a crude version of exactly this
  distinction, applied after the fact and with a hand-set bound.

  And *slip* is the concept the engine already rediscovered.  Induction
  torque is proportional to slip – the /relative/ rate between field and
  rotor – and vanishes at zero slip, however fast both are turning.
  That is precisely the rate-matching rule: harvest scales with the rate
  of relative movement, not with absolute price motion.  The machine
  analogy retroactively validates the policy's central choice.


7.2 Taking the frequency analogy literally
──────────────────────────────────────────

  The EMA ladder is already a temporal decomposition: geometrically
  spaced windows, differences of adjacent EMAs acting as band-pass
  filters – an octave filter bank, which is a wavelet transform under
  another name.  What is /not/ decomposed is the cross-sectional axis.
  The full object is two-dimensional – *mode x timescale* – and the
  engine currently does one axis properly and the other by brute force.


7.2.1 1. Orthogonal modes instead of pairs
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  The sharpest available critique of the `pairs' engine is that its
  basis is redundant.  Six legs have *five* independent differential
  modes, but the engine tracks *fifteen* pairs.  The quorum therefore
  counts heavily overlapping evidence as if it were independent, and
  effort sizing double- and triple-counts the same underlying excursion.

  The fix is the Clarke transform generalized: diagonalize the
  covariance of leg log-returns and work in the eigenbasis.  PC1 is the
  common mode – which should be /discarded/ here, because it is the
  controller's job – and PC2..PCN are orthogonal, so each can be sized
  independently without double-counting, and a quorum over modes is a
  quorum over genuinely independent evidence.  This is the standard
  construction in statistical arbitrage (Avellaneda and Lee,
  /Statistical Arbitrage in the US Equities Market/, 2010: PCA
  eigenportfolios with mean-reverting residuals).

  Cost on-chain is an \(N \times N\) fixed-point rotation applied to the
  cached ladders.  At \(N=6\) that is small, and the eigenvectors are
  governance parameters refitted rarely – not per poke.  The
  cointegration literature (Johansen) gives the more principled version
  still: for constituents bound to each other by physics, the object to
  trade is the /stationary/ linear combination, not the pairwise raw
  imbalance.


7.2.2 2. Trade to the boundary, not to the target
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  Under proportional transaction costs the optimal policy is provably
  *not* to trade toward the target: it is a *no-trade region*, and one
  trades only enough to return to its boundary (Davis and Norman 1990;
  Shreve and Soner 1994).  The current engine sizes effort toward
  proportion and relies on the cap and deadband to stop it overshooting.
  Reformulating the deadband as a genuine no-trade wedge, with effort
  sized to reach the near edge rather than the centre, should cut
  turnover with no loss of premium – the cheapest change on this list,
  and the one with the firmest theory behind it.


7.2.3 3. Bands fitted to the reversion, not set by hand
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  The deadband is a flat 150bp and the leash a flat 30%.  For a
  mean-reverting spread the optimal entry and exit thresholds are known
  in closed form as a function of the reversion rate, the volatility and
  the cost (Bertram 2010; Leung and Li on optimal Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
  trading), and they scale like \(\sigma/\sqrt{2\theta}\).  Since the
  M2-lag study already fits a timescale per constituent, fitting
  \((\theta,\sigma)\) per /pair/ or per /mode/ is the same kind of
  exercise, and it replaces two hand-set constants with measured ones.


7.2.4 4. Weights are bounded, so bound them properly
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  "Strict upper and lower bounds" has an exact stochastic counterpart:
  weights live on a simplex, and the natural diffusion there is the
  *Jacobi (Wright-Fisher)* process, whose stationary distribution is
  Beta and whose first-passage behaviour is known.  Fitting it gives the
  leash a /quantile/ instead of a guess – "this excursion is at the 99th
  percentile of what this constituent's share does" is a defensible
  trigger in a way that "30%" is not.


7.2.5 5. A target-share family with a theorem attached
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  Constant-mix is one point in a family.  Fernholz's stochastic
  portfolio theory shows that a portfolio generated by a concave
  function of market weights decomposes into a generating term plus a
  drift term that is /non-negative/, and that *diversity-weighted*
  portfolios (\(w_i \propto \mu_i^{\,p}\), \(0<p<1\)) outperform the
  market portfolio under a diversity condition – which is exactly the
  bounded-weights premise the BuckBasket rests on.  The rebalancing
  premium this article measures /is/ Fernholz's excess growth rate.
  Exposing \(p\) as a governance parameter gives a continuous knob from
  equal-weight (maximum premium, maximum turnover) to cap-weight (zero
  of both), with a proof rather than a simulation behind the
  interpolation.


7.2.6 6. Phase, not just velocity
╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌╌

  "Wait for the turn" is a statement about /phase/: trade when an
  excursion is near the end of its cycle.  The engine currently infers
  this from the sign of a velocity or curvature, which is a one-bit
  reading of a continuous quantity.  The analytic-signal construction (a
  Hilbert transform, cheaply approximated by an EMA quadrature pair)
  yields instantaneous amplitude and phase directly, so effort could be
  a smooth function of how far through the excursion a pair actually is.

  For genuine periodicity – and energy and food have real seasonality –
  the *Goertzel algorithm* computes a single DFT bin with two state
  variables and O(1) work per sample, which is the one FFT-family
  primitive cheap enough to run on-chain unmodified.  Worth it only if a
  fitted seasonal component turns out to be material; worth /testing/
  for that reason.


7.3 What not to do
──────────────────

  Two attractive-sounding directions are traps, and the article's own
  data says so.  *Do not chase gross premium*: `curv' and quorum 3 both
  buy more harvest with more turnover, and the whole result of this
  study is that timing beats volume.  And *do not add a non-reverting
  constituent to sharpen the signal*: a machine whose every gear assumes
  reversion cannot be improved by feeding it something built not to
  revert – the argument is made in full in /The BuckBasket/, and the
  2020-25 replay is the measurement.


8 Limitations and next increments
═════════════════════════════════

  • The lazy catch-up is exact under sample-and-hold; a real gap has a
    price path.  More pokes give higher fidelity at the same amortized
    price – and the basket's own activity supplies them precisely when
    flows (and thus fidelity needs) are high.
  • The director reads whole-pool BUCK reserves; the venue nets out
    treasury liquidity.  The small bias is irrelevant to hint
    /ordering/.
  • Windows are governance parameters, seeded from the M2-lag analysis;
    the model says the premium is flat across 30-180d, so mis-tuning is
    forgiving.
  • The basket-side consumption (increment 0 wiring in
    `BuckBasketProRata', the `rebalanceSource/Sink' pair) is the next
    contract pass; the director itself, its tests, and the sim keeper
    are complete.
  • Keeper bounty economics (a few bp of each productive step, paid from
    treasury) are needed on a public network and are not yet modeled.
  • *The trend-regime pick has no on-chain engine.* `factor' wins the
    historical replay and is a Python policy only; the two Solidity
    engines are `vrate' and `pairs'.  Either the regime split is
    accepted as a deliberate bet on reversion – which the basket's whole
    thesis already is – or `factor' needs an engine so governance can
    switch.
  • The `pairs' basis is redundant (fifteen pairs over five independent
    modes at \(N=6\)); see /Variants worth investigating/.
