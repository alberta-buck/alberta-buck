* Purpose and lineage

The =rebalancing-eq= scenario family joins the two simulation programs
that had previously run apart: the *equilibrium campaign*
(=alberta_buck/sim/EQUILIBRIUM.md= -- the BUCK-K monetary loop on real
5-year macro data, judged by the parity-with-interior-K gate) and the
*rebalancing machinery* (the BasketRebalanceDirector and the BuckBasket
as a savings vehicle, previously exercised only on synthetic 3-token
regimes).  Every arm below runs the eq-baseline financial structure --
the 6-token M2-laggard weighted basket (LABR 2400 / CNST 2200 / FOOD
2000 / NRGC 2000 / PAXG 800 / cbBTC 600 bp), the real macro window,
prorata basket, K0 0.75 with rails (0, 0.95), seed 0xA1BC -- through
real contract deployments on an EVM (pyrevm), with the director keeper
active, at full rebalancing cadence (day_step 1, ticks 4: daily whale
pins, 6-hour PID samples).

Acceptance gate (unchanged from the campaign): tail (last 25%)
basketValueInBuck within 1.00 +/- 0.02, K never railed in the tail,
issuance/redemption channels alive.

* The family: variants, running, reproducing

Experiments: =alberta_buck/sim/experiments/rebalancing-eq-5yr*.toml=.
Variant extension names compose deltas left-to-right on three axes --
(1) knobs ([agents.*] and [deploy]), (2) trend regime (reserved), (3)

(no cross-file inheritance) and its resolved config is embedded in the

  =test/vectors/rebalancing-sim-eq-<ext>.json= +

* Model rulings (decisions of record)

These calibration and semantics decisions were made deliberately and
should not be silently re-litigated:

1. *Per-day agent rates.*  Rate knobs (saver base_rate, borrower
   retire/release rates, the funding factor's issue-rate signal) are
   per-DAY, scaled by =day_step/ticks_per_day= each activation, so
   daily flow is cadence-invariant.  The coarse macro mode (day_step
   10) is an approximation for slow dynamics only; the notorious
   cold-start buckUsd spike of early runs was substantially an artifact
   of 10-day PID sampling and lumpy per-act flows.

2. *Atomic refinance* (~refi_mode="atomic"~, this family only; the
   audited octl configs keep "paced").  A debtor evaluates the WHOLE
   mortgage conversion ex ante: quote the BUCK needed to net the full
   USDC mortgage after slippage and pool fee, check issuance capacity
   (spendable headroom + K-scaled unactivated face), gate the theta law
   on the all-in execution discount, then execute BuckCredit -> BUCK ->
   USDC -> full payoff in one act -- or do nothing (no mint, no premium
   paid, re-quote next month).  The paced model committed first and
   discovered the market later; it also made every deployed debtor a
   perpetual net BUCK absorber (monthly save-rate buffer accumulation
   with one-shot issuance), which is what strangled the early debtor
   arms.

3. *Admission frontier LTV <= K is expected.*  A mortgage above
   face * K cannot be fully funded by BUCK issuance; in reality the
   borrower would top up with supplemental USDC credit.  Those
   borrowers are IGNORED for now (declined debtors are fully inert).
   K is thereby the emergent admission gate on mortgage conversion --
   wide when the system wants supply, narrow when it does not.

4. *Housing-market distribution.*  The home (the insured asset) is the
   primary draw -- =home_k [200,1500]= LOG-uniform (median ~$530k),
   mirroring real estate's right skew -- and the rest derives: face =
   the appraised home value (the insurable value the BuckCredit
   attests; premium charged on it; creditLimit = face*K), mortgage =
   home * ltv with =ltv [0.05,0.95]= (mean ~0.49, the seasoned-ladder
   average), income = home * [0.20,0.35].  The legacy independent
   mortgage/face draws implied a maximally-leveraged civilization (LTV
   massed near 1.0 with underwater tails) and excluded ~88% of the
   population at the frontier.

5. *Deep exit route.*  The floating BUCK/USDC pool is $50M family-wide
   (~[deploy] target_buck_lp_m = 50~; deploy.py now sizes the pool off
   its documented knob).  A national-scale currency pair is deep, and a
   shallow pool was an artificial exit bottleneck; real best-cost
   routing would additionally spread exits across BUCK/TOKEN ->
   TOKEN/USDC legs.  The BUCK-unaware arbs keep the deep pool balanced.
   With atomic refinance, this depth is the literal admission control
   on credit adoption (~2% all-in on a ~$1M mortgage vs ~10% at $10M).

6. *Saver endowment* (~arrive_mode="endow"~): pre-window BUCK inventory
   transferred from SimLP against a zero-premium credit.  NB: minting
   on the saver's own credit endows K-scaled *headroom* (balanceOf
   counts unused creditLimit, which melts as K falls), not held BUCK;
   the third-party transfer is what makes it real inventory
   (totalSupply == sum of positive signed balances).

7. *Endogenous arrivals* (~arrive_mode="endog"~): debtor hazard =
   (K/k_ref) * (1 + arr_gain*(1-bvib)) capped at arr_max; depositor
   hazard = dev_ref / max(dev_ref, ewma|bvib-1|).  hazard == 1
   reproduces the open-loop "originate" stagger exactly, so the
   endog-vs-originate comparison isolates price-responsiveness itself.

* What each variant does (effect analysis)

** base -- the fine-cadence result
The control loop closes with no help: parity to 0.05% with K settled at
0.760.  K climbs during the FatCredit adoption S-curve and plateaus
exactly when cap_frac saturates -- the climb is the controller
compensating for *scheduled* capacity withholding, not chronic
deflation.  Residual weakness: the retirement channel is silent
(0.00) -- nothing in this cast ever wants to deleverage.

** capacity (savers2x demand + borrower face/funding headroom)
Buys the LOWEST resting K (0.634): with demand and capacity headroom
the plant answers the controller easily and the controller retains the
most unused authority.  The cost is the widest parity spread of the
non-basketeer arms (0.0266) -- strong saver demand is itself a
perturbation.  Choose this shape when rail margin is the objective.

** endow / deep / endow-deep -- steady-state initialization, attributed
At the original $10M floating pool, depth did nearly all the work.
With the family now at $50M, the attribution INVERTS: deep (extra
TOKEN/BUCK depth) is indistinguishable from base (K 0.757 vs 0.760),
while the saver endowment carries the whole remaining effect -- endow
and endow-deep are identical (K 0.715, retirement 0.54/0.55 vs 0.00).
Standing private sell-side inventory lowers the resting K by ~0.045 and
is the difference between a one-sided and a breathing channel.  Lesson:
liquidity depth and distributed inventory are complements, and once one
is adequate the other is the binding one.

** debtors (staggered arrival over the first year)
24 honest mortgage refinancers tighten parity (sd 0.0081, from 0.0107)
and open the retirement channel, at base-like K.  The conversion flow
itself is stabilizing: each atomic refinance is a supply event that
lands when the market can bear it (theta law + LTV<=K + quoted
slippage), and the ex-debtors' subsequent surplus investment adds
basket demand.

** debtors-mature (everyone converts at t0)
The "converted civilization": all 24 refinance immediately, and the
economy then runs on the refinanced supply.  K rests HIGH (0.925) --
the controller holds the admission gate wide -- but interior and
settled (dK ~ 0), with the second-tightest parity (0.0080) and a tail
that is pure voluntary deleveraging (iss 0.00 / ret 1.56M).  Under the
OLD rulings (paced, shallow, narrow-face) this exact arm railed 81% of
its tail; the reversal is entirely the three structural fixes.

** basketeers (+ DirectMint depositor population)
The basket finally exercised as a savings vehicle inside the loop: ~950
depositor round-trips, the first treasury accrual, the K-immune
100%-LTV tranche at ~10% of supply.  Cost: the loosest debtor-arm
spread (0.0244) -- depositor churn is real noise the director must
absorb -- but the gate still passes comfortably.  The stylized fear
that the K-immune tranche strangles the controller does NOT materialize
at this scale.

** originate (+ continuous debtor and depositor origination)
Both sides of the economy grow at roughly matched dollar rates; all 30
debtors convert; the K-immune tranche reaches 13.7M (~18% of supply)
with 1,158 round-trips -- and parity is the second-tightest in the
program (0.0072).  The caution: K rests at 0.942, EIGHT BASIS POINTS
of lever below the 0.95 rail.  A growing economy consumes controller
authority; see recommendations.

** endog (originate with price-responsive arrival hazards)
The headline theoretical result: the endogenous economy finds
essentially the SAME equilibrium as the hand-tuned calendar (bv 1.0002
vs 1.0006, sd 0.0068 vs 0.0072) with slightly MORE rail margin (K 0.924
vs 0.942), and nobody chose a growth rate.  Debtor arrivals accelerate
into lean markets, depositors wait out demonstrated instability, and
the on-chain brakes (funding-factor toll, pre-buy reserve, K-scaled
limits) price what arrivals may do.  Matched supply/demand growth is an
emergent property of the price system, as designed.

* The excursion program: poised capital vs. the whale (2026-08-22)

Short/mid-term stability is supposed to rest on profit-seeking
participants poised to capitalize on BUCK-vs-bvib excursions.  Two agent
classes test that thesis, plus an adversary.

** The excursion agents: methodology and assumptions

*ExcursionArbAgent* watches an EWMA of basketValueInBuck (halflife 5 d)
and acts once daily (tick 0).  Every trade is gated by an ex-ante
all-in quote in the atomic-refinance spirit: the closed-form
constant-product execution price (plus pool fee) at the impact-capped
size (50-150 bp of the pool side) must clear par by ~min_edge~ (1%) --
no computed edge at that size, no trade.  Two capital bases:

- ~capital="usdc"~ -- a budget war chest ($2-6M).  ENTRY: discount
  excursion (ewma > 1 + entry_dev, 0.03: BUCK cheap vs basket) -> buy.
  EXIT: the whole long is closed when the ewma re-enters
  1 + exit_dev (0.01) from above -- including a premium overshoot --
  booking realized P&L against the accumulated basis.
- ~capital="credit"~ -- the LATENT-CREDIT participant: a zero-premium
  BuckCredit face ($2-6M) it has never needed to draw.  ENTRY: premium
  excursion (ewma < 1 - entry_dev) -> mint against face*K and sell
  above par.  EXIT: a LATER DISCOUNT excursion (ewma > 1 + entry_dev,
  deliberately the entry-grade gate, not exit_dev) -> buy BUCK below
  par toward the drawn balance (the buy itself retires drawn credit;
  a best-effort burn deactivates coverage).  Selling a par obligation
  dear and retiring it cheap is the reversion profit; closing at par
  would only pay fees, so the position correctly WAITS -- possibly
  forever (the obligation melts on the Jubilee regardless).

Assumptions worth knowing when reading results: the signal is bvib
(the controller's own process variable), not buckUsd; quotes are
single-pool (the floating BUCK/USDC route only, no multi-hop); net
worth is par-marked (USDC + signed BUCK at 1.0); positions carry no
financing cost beyond demurrage; agents act independently, daily.

The 5-day halflife has a consequence the raid exposed: a 3-day
excursion is attenuated roughly 3x by the filter, so raid-speed events
barely graze the 3% entry gate -- the slow cold-start premium is what
the credit base sold into (nearly all recorded "entries"), while the
USDC base sat correctly idle for five years in the healthy arms.
"Zero exits" in the finals is therefore mostly the credit base's
designed patience, plus a filter too slow for raid-speed discounts;
a faster (or dual-timescale) signal is the obvious calibration
experiment.

*WhaleRaidAgent* is the adversary: accumulate a position quietly
(impact-capped daily slices), DUMP it all on the raid day
(deliberately uncapped -- that is the attack), then attempt to
re-accumulate at the depressed price.  Its ledger prices the raid
exactly: dump proceeds minus accumulation and re-acquisition costs.

this kind of comparison.

1. *Raids lose money, and defenders make them lose more.*  Against the
   poised cast the whale lost $1.00M; undefended, only $0.57M --
   because the defended market snapped back in 3 days, forcing
   re-accumulation at recovered prices, while the undefended market
   stayed depressed and sold the whale its position back cheap.
   Defense heals the market faster AND punishes the raider harder.
2. *The undefended economy takes permanent damage*: peak bv deviation
   16.7% (vs 8.8% defended), no recovery inside 60 days, and the arm
   fails its gate outright (K railed 37% of the tail).  The poised
   cast is the difference between a bruise and a wound.
3. *The excursion cast is the strongest K-relief measured*: resting K
   drops from the endog arm's 0.924 to 0.725 -- below K0 -- with the
   retirement channel open.  A standing two-sided private cast
   relieves the controller more than any initialization variant.
4. Caveat: excursion earnings (+$3.5-4.2M) are par-marked
   mark-to-model; the credit base's obligations remain open by design,
   so realized-vs-marked stays a reporting distinction.

* Portcast: the JS-port validation target (PASS)

~rebalancing-eq-5yr-portcast~ is the settled-physics cast -- every
agent in its decision-of-record embodiment, language-neutral keyed
RNG (~[scenario] rng = "keyed"~; recipe in TELEMETRY.md), and NO
FatCreditBorrowerAgent: if the household debtors + basket depositors
alone pass the gate, an entire complex class drops out of the JS
port.  Already learned from its smoke: WITHOUT FatCredit the
cold-start transient vanishes entirely (bv 0.993-1.009, K flat at
K0 = 0.750) -- the mature arms' violent openings were the Fat
borrowers' day-0 reserve prefunding and issuance all along.

Full-run verdict (2026-08-22): *PASS* -- bv 1.0004 (sd 0.0070), K
0.704 interior, 0% railed, channels alive through the bcd+dm fallback
(3.38M net issuance in the tail).  The no-FatCredit settled-physics
cast reproduces the success with the tightest spread of any
full-economy arm; the JS-port cast is CONFIRMED and FatCredit drops
out of the port.  It is also the base of the excursion catalogue.

* Cross-cutting findings

1. *Execution semantics and market microstructure matter as much as
   the controller.*  Nothing about the PID changed between the all-FAIL
   debtor matrix and the all-PASS one -- what changed was HOW agents
   execute (atomic vs paced), WHAT the exit route can bear ($50M vs
   $10M), and WHO the population is (housing distribution vs maximal
   leverage).
2. *K's resting level is the admission-frontier position.*  A converted
   or growing economy settles K high because the controller is holding
   the conversion gate open; that is a feature -- but it spends rail
   margin, which is the robustness budget.
3. *The K-immune basket tranche is tolerable at ~18% of supply.*  The
   fence's motivating problem did not bite at realistic scale; whether
   it bites at 30-50% remains the open fence-in-the-loop question.
4. *Private inventory is the retirement channel.*  Every arm whose
   savers hold standing BUCK shows two-sided channels; every arm with
   empty savers shows one-sided issuance.

* Recommendations: robustness against economic disruption

Ranked by expected information per unit of compute.

1. *Multi-seed sweep first.*  Everything above is seed 0xA1BC.  The
   equilibrium campaign's own history (3/5 seeds failed its plain
   baseline) is the warning: run the matrix's key arms (base, endow,
   debtors-mature, endog) across the campaign's 5 seeds via sweep.py
   before treating any conclusion as robust.
2. *Buy back rail margin for the growth arms.*  originate/endog rest
   0.008-0.026 below KMAX; any demand shock rails them immediately.
   Three levers, in order of principle: (a) implement the *top-up
   borrower* (BUCK for face*K, supplemental USDC for the remainder) so
   the admission frontier is not the only supply valve; (b) widen the
   collateral base (new insured asset classes = new face arriving
   independent of housing); (c) a KMAX policy study -- 0.95 was chosen
   to keep a railed K solvent and observable, but a converted economy
   may warrant a higher ceiling with an explicit solvency argument.
3. *Exercise the disruption axis.*  The naming scheme reserves it; the
   machinery exists (=[[interventions]]= price_shock, uptake_shock,
   retune).  Priority shocks against the winning arms: (a) a commodity
   spike (NRGC +30%, cbBTC +50% -- the campaign's shock-price recipe)
   mid-run; (b) a *demand run*: mass depositor exit (exit_ptick shock
   on the DM population) -- the basket-run scenario; (c) a rate-cycle
   shock (apr shift changes every theta threshold and the refinance
   arbitrage itself).
4. *Test the oversupply flood.*  The feared scenario -- massive
   issuance through unencumbered collateral driving K to its LOWER
   rail -- is still untested.  Add carry issuers (BuckIssuerArbAgent
   exists) or a debtor draw_mult beyond encumbrance matching, and
   measure: how deep K dives, where bv's discount equilibrates (the
   theta/ff boundary predicts ~theta*apr), and the recovery half-life
   (K throttles flow, not stock; only Jubilee melt and buy-backs
   shrink the overhang).
5. *Fence-in-the-loop.*  Swap =--basket fence= into the basketeer
   arms so basket issuance is K-scaled rather than K-immune, and
   compare stability AND depositor economics against these baselines.
   This is the designed join of the fence program with the monetary
   loop, and becomes urgent if the tranche share is pushed past ~30%.
6. *Slow-trend regimes.*  grow/decline commodity drifts (the trend
   axis) need a price-generation hook or staged PriceOverlay ramps --
   the reverting-vs-trending distinction mattered enormously in the
   synthetic program and is unexplored here.
7. *Population churn.*  Port the campaign's population-churn
   experiment (lost borrower + saver) to this family; the endog
   hazards should recruit replacements -- verifying that is exactly
   what they are for.
8. *Exit-route realism.*  The single deep pool abstracts best-cost
   multi-route exits (BUCK/TOKEN -> TOKEN/USDC).  Teaching the debtor
   quote (and the arbs) to split across routes would let depth be
   stressed without the abstraction absorbing it.

** The defender mixes (one agent, every quadrant)

~ExcursionArbAgent~ now holds wealth in two legs, BUCK and a NEUTRAL
asset, and rotates between them on the dual-timescale bvib signal
under the ex-ante-quote discipline.  ~neutral~ selects the non-BUCK
leg -- "usdc" (a war chest), "basket" (the constituents in the
basket's own ~basketAmount~ proportions, traded TOKEN<->BUCK in the
TOKEN/BUCK pools that DEFINE bvib, so the rotation pushes the
observable directly and pays no demurrage while parked), or "credit"
(the latent-credit issuer/retirer) -- and ~buck_frac~ (the rest-state
BUCK share) selects the quadrants served from day 0: 0 = absorb the
discount (Q1), 1 = supply the premium (Q3), 0.5 = two-sided.  Pinned
subclasses mix them in one roster: ExcursionArbAgent (usdc, Q1),
ExcursionBuckArbAgent (usdc, buck_frac 1, Q3), ExcursionCreditArbAgent
(Q4 issue / Q2 retire; buyback gate now its own knob ~cover_dev~),
ExcursionBasketArbAgent (basket, 0.5: the common-mode rotation agent,
roadmap item 1 realized as a parameterization, not a new class).
~CommodityRebalArbAgent~ (roadmap item 3) is the differential-mode
private trader: an EOA holding a TOKEN inventory in basket
proportions that sells the constituent dear relative to the basket
into the one cheap (most-over into most-under, common mode demeaned),
through the director's own two-hop route, when the spread beats
~band~ and the ex-ante quote leaves spread - cost >= min_edge; its
reversion expectation is either the MA deviation (~halflife~) or, with
~foresight_days~ > 0, the exogenous reference series itself -- the
commodity specialist who knows whether a shock is transient.  P&L is
marked against buy-and-hold of the initial inventory.  The excursion
population's DIRECTIONAL dollar flows are now booked per quadrant
(~exc_q~ = absorb / retire / supply / issue) -- the first cut of the
quadrant-flow telemetry (roadmap item 4).

** The response metric (eqmetrics, per injection window)

Windows are detected from the vector itself (raid phase 2 spans;
applied price_shock / other interventions).  For each: pre-window
baselines (30 d), signed peak bv deviation and its day, AUC of |bv-1|
(%-days), recovery days (first day after the injection end with

driver.  Verdicts of every historical vector are unchanged.

** Reading the grid

The arm x mix grid answers the program's two questions directly:
(1) the BOUNDS of equilibrium -- which injections a given mix survives
(verdict, peak, recovery) and at what intensity the bound breaks; (2)
the SIGNS of weak support from a quadrant -- an arm whose response is
poor under every mix lacking that quadrant and good under every mix
staffing it (a premium excursion uncontrolled without Q3/Q4 staffing,
a discount one without Q1/Q2, a differential one without the
rebalancer) isolates the missing quadrant.  Wave 1 (7 arms x 6 mixes,
scale 1) is in flight as of this writing; results will be recorded
here with the grid.

* Roadmap: the quadrant-balance program (2026-08-22)

The organizing model (Perry): K guarantees equilibrium in the long run;
short-term stability rests on profit-seeking agents covering four
quadrants in roughly equal strength -- BUCK issue/withdraw balanced by
accumulate/redeem, on two symmetry axes: the COMMON mode (BUCK vs the
neutral basket: sell BUCK rich into basket exposure, rotate back on the
discount) and the DIFFERENTIAL mode (sell commodities above their basket
proportion into those below).  Individual agents are modeled and tuned
for their own ROI first; the learnings then integrate into a sane
BuckBasket (which should perform the absorb service automatically when
BUCK is at a discount and K is rising -- BuckBasketOps Q1 with a
K-gradient condition).

In dependency order (status as of 2026-08-22 in brackets):

0. *The excursion catalogue* [DONE, wave 1 in flight] -- enumerate the
   excursions (axis x sign x speed), inject each on a controlled
   window, measure one response metric, grid it against defender
   mixes and intensities.  The falsifiable instrument the rest of the
   program is tuned against.
1. *BasketRotationAgent* [DONE as a parameterization: ExcursionArb
   neutral="basket", buck_frac 0.5] -- the common-mode rotation agent
   trading BUCK <-> the constituent basket in the bvib-defining pools.
   Completes symmetry axis 1.  A BuckBasket-RECEIPT neutral leg (LP
   exposure that earns the rebalancing premium) remains a possible
   fourth base.
2. Dual-timescale excursion signal [DONE]; credit-base buyback
   threshold exposed as ~cover_dev~ [DONE].
3. *CommodityRebalArbAgent* [DONE, minimal] -- MA-reversion or
   foresight signal, band, two-hop execution; to be tuned on the
   spike/step arms.
4. *Quadrant-flow telemetry* [PARTIAL] -- the excursion population's
   directional flows are booked per quadrant (~exc_q~); the remaining
   classes (Saver, debtors, DM) and the balance metric (strength
   ratios + symmetry index) are still to do.
5. *Per-class ROI reporting* -- telemetry-derived realized+marked ROI
   per class, so knob sweeps rank configurations by individual agent
   profitability as well as system stability.
6. *Balance-mix harness* -- one dial per quadrant (cohort counts and
   budgets), swept; plus a dedicated startup-imbalance arm (issuers
   front-loaded, opportunistic-wealth dial varied).
7. LAST: fold the winning knobs into the BuckBasketOps/Fence revision
   and gate it as a contract-level arm.
