Use [[file:README-build.org][the build guide]] first. =make help= lists the supported paths. The canonical economic model
is =alberta_buck/sim/experiments/economy.toml=; its household policies are implemented in both
Python and JavaScript. The Python engine is used for five-year evidence, JavaScript for browser
interaction and cross-language contract tests. They are not independent economic hypotheses.

Historical economy runs deployed legacy =Buck= (or =BuckWithBasketHooks=),
with signed-balance accounting. The canonical =Buck.sol= now uses compounded
cash and per-facility Jubilee accounting, but the primary household-economy and savings/equilibrium builders
still require migration to its five-argument constructor, linked libraries
and explicit cash/debt operations. Those commands are not yet restored under
the corrected model. Historical five-year results and household tests do not
validate that model. Newly saved vectors
record the actual BUCK artifact, address and accounting model in
=meta.buck_contract=; PDFs and campaign summaries show the recorded model.
Older vectors lacking that metadata are labeled =unrecorded=: check their
frozen source manifest instead of inferring an accounting model from the date.
The current sequence is in [[file:doc/review/bucknet-economy-implementation.org][the unified Buck accounting plan]]:
correct the existing Buck.sol using the BuckNet discoveries and preserve its
functionality, then migrate the existing economy. The token name alone does
not identify which accounting produced a result. The
[[file:doc/review/net-demurrage-simulation-migration.org][simulation migration map]] retains detailed legacy references.

* Demurrage and Jubilee reference

=make nix-venv-demurrage-reference= prints a 50-year closed-ledger example
with 100 BUCK issued into an ordinary account's issued-but-unspent balance.
The ordinary held balance starts at zero; =balanceOf= is the full spendable
held plus net unspent balance. Pass =--held= to issue directly to held BUCK
instead, or =--issued 1000000 --years 1= for the million-BUCK facility example;
=DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--years 50 --json"= produces JSON, and =--acquire-all=
models the fund acquiring the remaining spendable BUCK at the end. The latter is
an explicit transfer from the holder; non-BUCK consideration is not modeled.
=make nix-venv-demurrage-returns= compares no investment return with a 2%
annual return on the entire fund, sourced by transfers of existing BUCK from
the holder. Both cases issue exactly 100 BUCK, assess 100 gross BUCK of
demurrage over 50 years and conserve supply. The return case is idealized and
does not establish that the actual basket earns 2% or can liquidate at its mark.
Pass =DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--json"= for structured output.
=DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--deploy-fund --strategy-return 10"= puts fund cash into
an idealized BUCK-only strategy and attributes a 10 BUCK return to a transfer
from the holder. Strategy BUCK also pay demurrage into fund cash. This
illustrates fund deployment, sourced returns and
liquidation; it does not model real basket prices, impact or liquidity.
=DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--retire 5"= exercises the marked-portfolio payout without a
sale. The requested net relief must fit within 10% of liquid fund cash; a
larger request reports an error instead of silently reducing the payout.
For the earlier sale-based comparison, use
=DEMURRAGE_ARGS="--deploy-fund --wipe-strategy --retire 50 --redeem-fraction 0.25 --payout-policy proportional"=.
In that comparison the default redemption fraction is the requested baseline
relief divided by total outstanding baseline relief; =--redemption-basis issuance=
compares the share of total issued BUCK. =--payout-policy reserve= retains an
older reserve-ratio comparison. The isolated =BuckNet= and =JubileeFund=
prototype implements marked, cash-limited settlement, but still requires
oracle, leveraged-basket and full-system validation; the
[[file:doc/review/jubilee-marked-relief.org][marked-relief design]] explains the calculation and tradeoffs.
=make nix-venv-demurrage-test= tests issuance, independent facility histories,
atomic renewal, insurer-specific nominal refunds, proportional redemption,
Carrying fund payouts, long-idle accounts, and fund self-demurrage.
The reference is a mathematical specification, not a five-year economy run or
proof that the contract prototype is production-ready. See [[file:alberta-buck-demurrage.org][the demurrage design]] for the accounting and
the remaining policy and contract decisions. The proposed token, facilities,
fund, insurer pools, and basket changes are in the [[file:doc/review/credit-facility-accounting-design.org][contract design]].

=make nix-venv-net-demurrage-smoke= builds this checkout and deploys the
canonical =Buck= implementation in Python/Anvil and JavaScript/Tevm. It no
longer deploys the BuckNet facade. The explicit library graph is =BuckCash=,
=BuckTransfer=, =BuckFacilities= and =BuckRenewalOps=, followed by Buck with its
original four dependency meanings and a fifth, separate Jubilee address.
The fund, claim book, renewal consent and cash-only equity basket are real
contracts. These are accounting and integration checks, not a leveraged
household economy or a five-year acceptance run.

The checks cover one-year compounded cash and marked relief against the
independent reference; dynamic K and nonzero premiums from two insurer pools;
pre-issuance reserves, including decay between quote and execution; guarded
real V3 buys and Universal Router sells; and a separate confirmed payment to
a simulated mortgage bank. Transfers and purchases never count as NFT debt
retirement. Python and JavaScript use the same operation vectors. Real
private-identity Chaum-Pedersen approvals and receipt decryption run against
Buck. These are identity proofs, not restored Notes proof suites.

The canonical basket valuation checks are included in
=make nix-buck-accounting-test=; the focused suite is
=make nix-match-BuckCompoundBasketTest=. It tests separate cash/principal,
fee-aware Jubilee cross-holdings, entry dilution and the exclusion of unpaid

Public =openCredit(credit, face)= now supports canonical mode. It requires
governance, =enableNetAccounting=, a verified public ordinary basket identity,
the canonical token model and the token's own BuckCredit with its Buck hook
already wired. It creates a self-issued, zero-premium MARKED NFT with zero
activated coverage and principal. Opening alone changes no cash, supply or
issuance age; later upkeep marks and activates backing before explicit draws.

For a fresh basket, configure its venue and one existing upkeep facet
(=BuckBasketEquityWheel= or =BasketUpkeepFacet=). Governance calls
=authorizeIdentityBinding(registry, binder, pk, E)= at the *basket address*,
using =IBasketUpkeep= or the selected facet's ABI. The certified operator
then calls =registry.bindContract(basket, pk, E, true, false)=. Authorization
pins the exact binder, key, ciphertext and public/non-Carrying flags; only the
token's registry is accepted. Governance can revoke pending authorization
before deposits or credit opening. The deployed facet address is not the
target of this binding.

Enable canonical accounting once before any deposits or credit, configure
the existing fund/wheel graph, then call the shell's ordinary =openCredit=.
The shared =BasketCreditSetup= code is inherited by both upkeep facets and
adds no deployed initializer or storage fields. Its
=initializeEquityCredit= entry is a shell self-call; even governance must use
=openCredit= rather than invoke the initializer through the fallback.
Setup mutations are rejected during active position or credit operations.

=make nix-match-BasketCreditSetupTest= exercises actual production-registry
credential vectors, authorization/revocation, wrong flags, wrong registry,
foreign credit and model refusal. =make nix-match-BasketPublicLifecycleTest=
uses the unmodified basket shell for public opening, explicit issuance,
actual V3 LP placement, debt retirement and investor exit through both upkeep
facets. Its registry fixture supplies identity flags; the production
certification tests provide the separate binding evidence. Both suites are
included in =make nix-buck-accounting-test=. On a fresh checkout, build the
venue artifacts with =make nix-sim-build= before using individual match targets.

Both existing upkeep facets now use explicit canonical issuance: available
credit is a planning limit, while =balanceOf= remains actual wallet cash.
They draw only a purchase's cash shortfall and check the resulting net book.
The basket activates its free self-insured backing separately from that draw,
including when existing cash funds a purchase. Postchecks use actual activated
collateral, not the full appraised face. Free activation creates no currency or
claim age; insurance premiums, where applicable, remain real issued principal.
Sales and fee collections leave cash until a separate successful facility
reduction. =make nix-match-BuckCompoundBasketFlowTest= checks both real V3
paths, K/face/maturity limits, capacity quotes and transaction rollback.

The basket's =retireCredit(principal, maxCashCost, minimumReliefScaled)= is an
atomic governor/wheel action: principal and the cash ceiling are raw BUCK,
while minimum relief is net BUCK scaled by =1e36=. It settles the real NFT,
checks actual wallet cost and re-marks collateral. Fund liquidity limits still
revert the call; large reductions need smaller calls across blocks. It sells
no LP and is separate from deposit/redemption's position lock.
=make nix-match-BuckCompoundBasketRepaymentTest= covers bounds, fractional
net costs, supply destruction, block limits, reuse and callback rejection.

Canonical receipt and treasury exits require actual activated collateral to
cover unchanged principal both before and after payout. Cash and in-kind
withdrawals never repay that principal implicitly; an under-K exit reverts
even with ample cash. Separate liability retirement can restore eligibility.
Final marks use the receipt's final shares and basis, including fund ownership.
=make nix-match-BuckCompoundBasketExitTest= checks cash, real V3 in-kind,
fund-owned receipt conversion, low-K rollback, losses and treasury exits,
including 256 comparisons with the actual cash-exit credit boundary.

The separate =BasketDesk= now has explicit canonical mode, enabled before its
credit is opened. It requires a verified public ordinary identity and links
the same =BasketJubileeMath= library as the basket. =accountState()= reports
live cash, actual NFT principal, marked unpaid relief, bare collateral and
marked equity in raw BUCK. =refresh()= never issues, activates backing or
realizes relief. Marking and the capital floor exclude unpaid claims; an
unavailable relief mark need not block bare collateral or new issuance.

Existing cash is sold before new credit. Persistent supply advice can issue
only the cash being sold, within K, face, account/supply and programme caps.
Purchases measure actual net BUCK rather than nominal V3 output. Without a
liability they accumulate cash; with a liability they retire at most their
newly purchased whole BUCK principal through the real Jubilee fund, even for
nonpersistent advice, retaining the old credit-holder policy. Relief remains
recorded cash. Liquidity refusal rolls back the whole purchase; independent
=retireCredit= has the basket's raw-cost / scaled-relief bounds. Swaps consume
finite gross turnover; reversals and retirement never replenish it. A single
financial lock and final collateral checks protect the complete operation.
=make nix-match-BuckCompoundDeskTest= covers these paths, including actual V3
swaps, aged pools, callback rejection and 256 issuance-budget comparisons.
These contract checks do not wire the desk into the primary household world.

Both existing wheels now support the same =JubileeKind=, adding three slots
when configured: bounded claim-heap processing, deferred-principal burns,
then reserve maintenance. An optional fourth slot retires the basket's own
principal from cash above its liquidity target. This reuses the fund and its single
receipt. Heap processing does not close anyone's NFT. Deferred burns respect
both the governor's raw-BUCK cap and Buck's remaining shared block budget.
The fund's existing one-day half-life and 1%-of-target threshold control
investment. A newly observed excess starts that clock without earning a
wheel reward; it is not backdated to the last transaction.

Governance configures the basket's =wheel=, the wheel's =valuationSource=,
the fund's =valuationWheel= and =investmentKeeper=, then calls
=wheel.setJubilee(fund, policy)=. The policy supplies a maturity batch
(1..64 entries), maximum receipt fraction sold per job, net slippage bound
(2..500 bp), and raw deferred-burn cap. Every attempt rechecks those bindings;
revocation stops the fund jobs while other wheel work can continue.

Reserve sales are sized to the cash deficit and capped by the configured
receipt fraction. Positive aggregate net proceeds protect the fund's guarded
receipt mark after gain fees. In-kind routes use only registered constituent
pools, limit sqrt-price movement to half the configured price tolerance,
and sell only TOKEN newly received by the redemption. Both token orders are
tested. Positive entry-share bounds use the basket's TWAP share price;
volatility can cause a safe refusal and retry in a later block. Neither
these bounds nor an investment mark promise realizable liquidity.

Each fund job is attempted at most once per block, including failures.
=rearm()= cannot reset that limit. Failed trades roll back the complete job
and earn no reward; cash checkpoints also earn none. Actual heap progress,
principal burned or receipt shares changed count as work. Mutating wheel
scans and reserve funding reject callback reentry. The separate
=refreshValuation()= remains available under the token guard and runs no
portfolio work, regardless of which fund jobs are due.
The =JubileeAttempted= event records the job, success, work and revert selector
so a keeper can distinguish a refused transaction from completed maintenance.

=make nix-match-JubileeWheelTest= reproduces the canonical fund/basket/V3
cases; it is also included in =make nix-buck-accounting-test=.
For basket retirement, set the policy's raw =retirementMinimum= and
=retirementCap=; zero cap disables the fourth slot. The minimum controls
work rewards, while a complete smaller dust tail can clear without a reward.
The job reads actual NFT principal, preserves the recomputed cash target,
and limits actual cash cost through the existing =retireCredit= API. It quotes
the full facility to size a batch against the shared relief budget, then
requotes that batch before execution. Inverting a partial quote can select
another unaffordable amount because selected BUCK-seconds round down.
An exhausted budget neither authorizes nor counts a repayment. A fully aged
facility still requires its owner's separate release action. No LP sale is
forced by this job and cash on hand cannot itself make a low-K exit pass.
=make nix-match-BasketRetirementWheelTest= includes that rounding
counterexample, actual full/partial repayment and cash-floor/shared-budget
checks in both existing wheels.

World builders must still configure this maintenance and persist its actual
facet, wheel, fund and identity bindings. Before enabling funded BUCK rewards
or BUCK arbitrage, complete and test production identity binding for the
canonical wheel. Its current API lacks target-controlled binding authorization;
the cash-only platform smoke does not exercise that service role. The older
comparison wheel has only 75 runtime bytes spare. Prefer the canonical
machine wheel for the primary world and retire legacy variants only as their
distinct checks are covered. Public opening and wheel jobs do not migrate an
existing simulation or saved world by themselves.

BVIB retains its nominal V3 reference: newly issued ordinary BUCK enter a pool
at full value as that same number of nominal units. Net liquidation marks and
buybacks use =nominal quote * pool net / pool nominal= before swap costs.
An idle year can therefore leave nominal BVIB unchanged while one nominal
BUCK bought from a pool delivers about 0.9802 spendable BUCK. In the actual
test market, 50.000030 BUCK of TOKEN value bought 49.830073 nominal BUCK but
only 48.843352 net BUCK. A fresh paired LP input of the original size raised
the pool's net/nominal ratio from 0.9801983 to 0.9900991 without changing
nominal BVIB or issued supply.

=make nix-match-BuckCompoundPriceUnitsTest= reproduces the four vectors,
including a real direct-controller component reading the canonical basket.
The controller algorithm and gains are unchanged. These are denomination
checks, not five-year stability evidence. Household decisions must compare
actual net buyback cost with their basket/counterfactual reference, accounting
for carried fees, AMM fees and price impact; nominal BVIB above one can still
leave no profitable liability buyback after those costs.

The existing [[file:core/python/buck_core/net_world.py][Python]] and
[[file:core/js/src/net-world.js][JavaScript]] operations expose actual per-facility
principal/age, candidate premium and reserve checks, original-pool refund
estimates, bounded explicit retirement and receipt reconciliation. Their
[[file:src/BuckNetQuoter.sol][optional facility quoter]] keeps its transitional
artifact name but reads a neutral cash interface. Capacity is separate from
wallet cash; no held/unspent split is synthesized. The net swap guard counts
the Carrying pool's actual delivery separately from any sponsor restoration,
so a sponsor payment cannot satisfy a purchase's minimum BUCK output.

Unknown outcomes retain their transaction evidence and stop dependent work.
A confirmed bank payment is applied once; authorization alone is never a debt
repayment. Pending buybacks preserve their remaining principal target and
pre-purchase reserve, and later turns use already purchased BUCK before buying
again. Tevm snapshots do not preserve transaction receipts: a saved hash alone
cannot establish the outcome after restoration. Finality and full household
snapshot recovery remain part of the [[file:doc/review/bucknet-economy-implementation.org][unified accounting plan]].

* The household default

=adaptive= saves through BUCK deposits, reducing new purchases as the BUCK becomes expensive.
At a basket-relative discount after costs it retires liabilities with increasing urgency,
using suitable basket receipts and then spare cash. It retains the cash and BUCK funding needed
for a worthwhile issuance before discretionary saving. Credit savers can borrow at a premium;
mortgage borrowers refinance when reduced carrying cost justifies execution and switching costs.
Working balances, asset sales and arrival behavior are shared across policy comparisons.

Bootstrap depositors seed the TOKEN/BUCK pools in proportion to the basket's declared weights,
while retaining the same total opening seed capital. Day-zero observations follow that day's
trading; the opening price/mark transient still needs separate study.

The household basket return is a *marked open-position return*: remaining receipt basis and
current share value are in BUCK, and ROI is =(mark - basis) / basis=. The PDF aggregates those
positions; each household's =basket_at_risk=, =basket_mark= and fractional =basket_roi= are in
the vector. Successful partial redemptions reduce the remaining basis. The mark is before exit
charges and market impact; the ratio is not an annualized or realized return and changes when
the composition of open positions changes. A missing mark, or zero basis, produces no ROI.
For the machine, the allocation plot uses response weights including idle TOKEN and the machine's
leaned target; older basket types retain the explicitly labeled legacy pool-weight view.
Read-only Python and JavaScript helpers now obtain these marks from actual
cash-only =BuckNet= basket receipts at one block. Their tests use a test-only
registry binding; the net household and sandbox do not yet use them, and the
production-registry basket binding still needs implementation.

The comparison settings are =basket= (always invest spare cash, no discretionary paydown),
=retire= (retire liabilities regardless of parity) and =discount= (retire below the individual
discount threshold, otherwise retain cash). Asset-sale settlement still clears liabilities in
every policy. The exact sampled settings and arrival roster are recorded in each vector.

*Naming changed on 2026-10-25.* On the household-economy branch before this date, =basket=
already included price-sensitive saving and discounted paydown. That behavior is now called
=adaptive=. Earlier =adaptive= was a different premium/discount-band policy. Interpret saved
runs using their frozen source commit, not today's label. The older four-policy funding
campaign held parity on all policies and cleared every mortgage; it did not establish a
universally best policy. The newer default is the agreed behavioral baseline, not a promise
that every population mix passes.

=economy-smoke= uses the working tree and writes =build/sim/economy-smoke.{json,pdf}=.
=ECONOMY_SMOKE_OUT= changes the destination. It verifies integration, not five-year acceptance.
=ECONOMY_DAYS= shortens a campaign for infrastructure checks; give it a distinct campaign name.
=ECONOMY_SEED= selects a seed (or the seed used by =centre=). =JOBS= limits concurrent EVMs.
Commands run in the foreground; use a terminal multiplexer for long campaigns. Policy comparisons
reuse the adaptive centre cell names, so extending the same campaign to the grid skips those
already completed worlds. Use one runner per named campaign: extend it after its current run ends. =economy-report= may
read the completed results while the remaining workers run.

After changing the plotting code, use =sim-report= explicitly on the saved JSON paths to refresh
their PDFs. =economy-report= repairs missing or JSON-mismatched PDFs, but a matching data hash
alone does not identify which version of the renderer made an existing PDF.

The Python EVM batch runner saves JSON and a PDF for each completed run of at
least five years.
When =--out= is omitted, the runner chooses a unique name under
=build/sim/completed/= and prints both paths; concurrent runs do not overwrite
each other. A server-backed savings world uses the same directory and logs
the paths on completion. Its interim files are checkpoints until the vector's
=meta.run.completed= is true. The interactive TUI also saves a long run there;
its PDF identifies the run as diagnostic because it does not perform the
batch teardown or acceptance judgment. In the TUI, =[w]= retries a failed PDF
render from the saved data. A failed batch render leaves the completed JSON
and reports the command to render it again without an EVM rerun.

Supply scales debtor and saver arrival rates by 1/3, 1 or 3. Demand currently scales the pace
of adopting BUCK working balances: adoption takes three years, one year or four months. It
does *not* change eventual demand once adoption completes. This limits what the grid can say
about persistent demand differences; a balance-size axis is a separate proposed experiment.
History uses the recorded price path; revert uses the reverting path. Read =economy-plan= for
the actual overrides, rather than inferring parameters from a campaign's chosen name.

Stress arms apply one change on day 730: buy/sell pressure ($0.5M/$2M over 30 days), an energy
price fall (20%/50%), a mortgage-rate increase (2/4 percentage points), lower income for the
present cohort (25%/50% for 180 days), fewer arrivals (half/none), more arrivals (1.5x/3x), or
25%/75% of current investors exiting. These use the same baseline and reporting pipeline.
They are explicit diagnostics; adding an arm does not establish five-year coverage until it
has run and its relevant failure checks have been assessed. The raid, LP-exit and book-loading
actors still need equivalent arms. =all= means the baseline grid and its growth controls;
it intentionally does not add every diagnostic or policy comparison.

* Files, completion and acceptance

Each campaign lives in =build/sim/economy/NAME/=:

- =snapshot/= is the committed tree plus needed contracts, generated inputs and native kernels.
  =source-manifest.json= records its commit and content hashes. Resuming a named campaign uses
  that snapshot, even if the checkout has changed. Use a new name for a new model.
- =runs/CELL.log= is the worker log; =CELL.partial= is a checkpoint, not a final result.
  A successful worker produces =CELL.json= and a PDF tied to its SHA256 digest. =run-settings.json= fixes the horizon; a smoke campaign
  cannot silently become a five-year campaign under the same name.
- =status.json= records worker outcomes. A nonzero process exit fails the command, even if the
  vector was written. Missing or stale PDFs can be rebuilt from JSON without another EVM run.
- =index.html= links PDFs and JSON; =summary.json= contains the numerical acceptance results.
  Open the index directly in a browser. =economy-report= rebuilds it from completed data.

Five-year acceptance uses days 365--1826: mean absolute BVIB error below 1%, 99th-percentile
error below 5%, K at least 0.05 inside its limits on 95% of days, and no continuous rail visit
longer than 30 days. Household errors anywhere in the recorded run and unfunded exits fail.
Insured-asset exits taking more than 30 days also fail, even if they eventually finish. A household
reported as exited must have released its coverage and have no mortgage, net BUCK lien, activated
credit limit, credit deposit or basket receipt remaining. The final account marks verify these
conditions; daily =exit_started=, =exit_stage=, =exit_retries= and =exit_short= identify delays.
Older vectors without exit telemetry cannot establish these newer checks retrospectively.
Short or incomplete daily series cannot pass. A completed economic failure is valid evidence;
investigate it rather than changing thresholds to make it green.

BVIB is the basket's price in BUCKs: above one means the BUCK is cheap, below one dear. K is
credit availability, not an interest rate. Distinguish insured face, activated limit, spendable
headroom, outstanding liability and actual issuance. Basket shares are marked assets, not
guaranteed exit proceeds. Actual and counterfactual mortgage books use the same initial cash,
income and spending assumptions. Population telemetry distinguishes scheduled, arrived and
exited members; an arrival is not automatically an issuer.

The latest pre-consolidation grid (=release=, commit =d73f047=) passed 27 of 36 cells. All held
parity within the stated average and tail tolerances; nine low-supply cells failed K's interior
requirement. Those failures remain findings. Policy renaming alone does not resolve them.

The completed =consolidated-policies-20261025= campaign (=c7ef815=) has all 16 five-year vectors
and matching PDFs, with six full passes. All worlds meet the price-error tolerances. Retirement-only
fails K headroom in all four worlds; nine worlds record errors during household coverage release.
Those errors leave thirteen household instances unable to finish their asset sales and can cause
repeated buying and selling through still-open credit. Repair exits before drawing policy rankings
or treating these runs as release acceptance. The [[file:doc/review/household-release-readiness.org][October 26 review]] gives the measurements,
contract-retirement candidates, missing shock coverage and ordered merge/release work.

The October 26 exit repair refreshes the K-dependent release quote, verifies cancellation before
marking it complete, and sells only positively owned BUCKs. Partial basket claims remain recorded
until redeemed; a cash shortfall leaves settlement pending. Small positive execution reserves may
remain (up to one spendable BUCK per account and one dollar per pending TOKEN constituent); liabilities are
not forgiven. The 315-day replay of the first historical failure now closes the affected household
without errors. All sixteen corrected five-year policy runs have since completed without household
errors, but all fail the new exit audit: aged post-exit balances can appear negative despite a
positive raw balance. The bootstrap and the allocation/return plots have since been corrected;
these older vectors retain their original openings and can be re-rendered without an EVM run.

The control lab preserves component isolation, guards, response timing and phase effects that
an economic grid averages away. Its generated page is under =doc/control-lab/interactive/=.
The single-run vector viewer remains useful for inspecting individual accounts and trades;
PDFs are the portable campaign record. Avoid running all diagnostic tuning sweeps routinely.

The agreed simplification is coverage-based:

| Candidate | Replacement/check required before removal | Status |
|-----------+-------------------------------------------+--------|
| Old surplus naming and one-off October policy ablations | Explicit common policy settings and shared vectors | Replaced by the policy comparison cells |
| JS mortgage prototype and standalone income actor | Shared household books plus the real equilibrium-loop test | Removed; loop, CLI and memory benchmark use CreditHouseholdAgent |
| Separate savings-sandbox preset | Common economy with a dormant interactive shock actor | Removed; server and campaign load economy.toml |
| Standalone old/new savings runner | Machine-only routine campaigns; focused basket/contract tests | Retired; historical scripts and results remain in git |
| Old mature/equilibrium/retiree presets | Household baseline and corresponding stress cases | Superseded as the routine baseline; distinct stress cases still need matching coverage |
| Synthetic credit borrowers and older debtor decisions | Household cost, gate, reuse and mortgage tests | Current households inherit only shared account helpers; legacy consumers remain pending stress coverage |
| Catalogue attack actors | Named household-economy stress arms exercising each attack | Retain actors until the corresponding arms are tested |
| Control lab components | Component isolation and guard coverage | Retain; this is different detection capability |

The detailed decisions and historical results remain in
[[file:doc/review/household-economy-plan.org][the household-economy ledger]]. Legacy files are not another recommended baseline. Retirement
must remove their callers, tests that only restate obsolete behavior, Make entries and stale
documentation together; git history remains available without keeping every replay command.
