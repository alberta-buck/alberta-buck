* Overview

  Every operation in the Alberta Buck ecosystem has identity requirements.  Some
  operations require fully public identity (oracle claims, governance votes).
  Others require only that the participant is a verified identity, without
  disclosing who they are (BUCK transfers).  DeFi integrations (Uniswap pools,
  lending protocols) introduce contract addresses as counterparties, which also
  need identities associated with their deployers.

  This document tabulates the identity requirements for every operation, then
  walks through the cryptography of a DeFi swap transaction as a concrete
  example.

* Contract Identity: How DeFi Pools Become BUCK Counterparties

  A Uniswap pool contract is just an address.  For BUCK transfers to/from that
  address to pass ~isVerified~, the contract must have an identity in the
  IdentityRegistry.  But a contract can't do KYC.  The solution:

  *The pool deployer's identity becomes the contract's identity.*

  When someone creates a BUCK/USDT Uniswap pool:

  1. Deployer (a verified BUCK participant) calls the Uniswap factory to create
     the pool.  The factory deploys a new contract at a deterministic address.

  2. Deployer (or a registry agent) calls:
     ~identityRegistry.registerIdentity(poolAddress, deployer.onchainId, country)~

     This links the pool contract's address to the deployer's ONCHAINID.  The
     pool inherits the deployer's identity claims.

  3. Now ~isVerified(poolAddress)~ returns true.  BUCK transfers to/from the
     pool pass the identity check.

  4. Encrypted receipts for pool transactions list the deployer's identity as
     the counterparty, because the deployer is the legal operator of the pool.

  This means: if you deploy a BUCK DeFi pool, you are the identified legal
  operator.  Your identity is on the line for every transaction through that
  pool.  If a court subpoenas you, you can be compelled to decrypt the receipts
  (because your key is one of the encryption parties).


* The Receipt Problem: Who Constructs What, and When

  The fundamental constraint: the BUCK ERC-20 contract is *trusted code* -- when
  it emits a receipt, the fields (from, to, amount, ONBCHAINIDs) are reliable
  because the contract itself is executing the transfer.  But the contract needs
  counterparty identity information to include in the receipt, and this
  information may not be naturally available at the moment of transfer.

  There are three sub-problems:

  1. *The ECDH problem*: Solidity cannot practically perform ECDH on-chain
     (ecMul is expensive, ~6000 gas per scalar multiplication, and the full
     scheme requires multiple).  The contract constructs the receipt *plaintext*;
     the wallet layer encrypts it off-chain.

  2. *The counterparty discovery problem*: in a ~transferFrom~ call triggered by
     a DeFi pool, the contract knows the from/to addresses but needs the
     counterparty's ECDH public key to include in the receipt event so the
     wallet can later encrypt.  For public-identity accounts this is trivially
     readable from the ONCHAINID.  For private-identity accounts it is not.

  3. *The timing problem*: in DeFi, ~transferFrom~ is called by a router
     contract -- there is no standard way to pass additional receipt data
     through the AMM's call stack.  The identity exchange must happen *before*
     the transfer, at a point where the account holder is directly interacting
     with the BUCK contract.

** Two Account Visibility Tiers

   The solution splits accounts into two tiers based on how their ECDH public
   key is published:

*** Public-Key Accounts (Default for Most Participants)

    The account's ECDH public key ~K~ is stored on their ONCHAINID as a
    public claim (ERC-734 key type).  Anyone can look it up:

    #+begin_example
    K = identity.getKey(ECDH_KEY_PURPOSE)  // readable by anyone
    #+end_example

    For these accounts, the BUCK contract can include the public key in any
    receipt event, and any counterparty's wallet can compute the ECDH shared
    secret to encrypt/decrypt the receipt.

    This is the expected default: your ECDH *public key* is public (it
    reveals nothing about your identity), and your *identity data* is
    encrypted behind it.  Anyone can encrypt *to* you, but only you can
    decrypt.

*** Private-Key Accounts (Opt-In for High-Privacy Participants)

    The account does NOT publish an ECDH public key on their ONCHAINID.
    The ~isVerified~ check still passes (the KYC claim exists), but there
    is no public key for counterparties to encrypt to.

    For these accounts, identity exchange must happen explicitly before any
    transfer can produce a proper receipt.  The mechanism: ~approve~.

** The ~approve~ Function as Identity Handshake

   The ERC-20 ~approve~ function is already the moment when an account holder
   explicitly designates a counterparty.  This is the natural point for
   identity exchange:

   #+begin_example
   // Standard ERC-20:
   approve(address spender, uint256 amount)

   // Extended for BUCK:
   approve(address spender, uint256 amount)  // + identity exchange side-effect
   #+end_example

   When Alice calls ~buck.approve(spender, amount)~, the BUCK contract:

   1. Sets the standard ERC-20 allowance
   2. Looks up Alice's ONCHAINID from the IdentityRegistry
   3. Looks up the spender's ONCHAINID from the IdentityRegistry
   4. Reads both parties' ECDH public keys (from ONCHAINID or from stored
      per-counterparty fragments)
   5. Stores a *counterparty identity record* for this pair
   6. Emits an ~IdentityExchange~ event

   #+begin_example
   struct CounterpartyRecord {
       address identity_a;      // ONCHAINID address of party A
       address identity_b;      // ONCHAINID address of party B
       bytes32 pubkey_a;        // ECDH public key (x-coordinate) of A
       bytes32 pubkey_b;        // ECDH public key (x-coordinate) of B
   }

   // Stored per (owner, spender) pair alongside the allowance
   mapping(address => mapping(address => CounterpartyRecord))
       public counterpartyRecords;
   #+end_example

   For *private-key accounts*, the ~approve~ call includes an additional
   parameter: the caller's ECDH public key, encrypted specifically for this
   counterparty.  Since the caller is directly invoking the BUCK contract,
   they can provide this calldata:

   #+begin_example
   function approve(
       address spender,
       uint256 amount,
       bytes32 callerPubKey     // caller's ECDH public key for this pair
   ) public returns (bool) {
       _approve(msg.sender, spender, amount);

       // Look up counterparty ONCHAINID
       IIdentity spenderIdentity = identityRegistry.identity(spender);

       // Read spender's public key (from ONCHAINID or from their prior
       // approve-to-us, if they're also a private-key account)
       bytes32 spenderPubKey = _resolvePublicKey(spender);

       // Store the counterparty record
       counterpartyRecords[msg.sender][spender] = CounterpartyRecord({
           identity_a: address(identityRegistry.identity(msg.sender)),
           identity_b: address(spenderIdentity),
           pubkey_a:   callerPubKey,
           pubkey_b:   spenderPubKey
       });

       emit IdentityExchange(msg.sender, spender, callerPubKey, spenderPubKey);
       return true;
   }
   #+end_example

*** Private-Key Accounts Receiving Incoming Transfers

    For an account with a private ECDH key that wants to *receive* transfers
    from a specific counterparty, the flow uses ~approve~ with amount 0:

    #+begin_example
    // Alice (private-key) wants to receive BUCK from Bob:
    buck.approve(bob, 0, alice_ecdh_pubkey)
    #+end_example

    This does NOT authorize Bob to spend Alice's tokens (amount = 0).  It
    establishes an identity handshake: Alice's public key is stored in
    ~counterpartyRecords[alice][bob]~, allowing Bob's wallet to encrypt
    receipts for Alice when sending her BUCK.

    When Bob later calls ~buck.transfer(alice, amount)~, the BUCK contract
    looks up the counterparty record and includes both public keys in the
    receipt event.

** Receipt Construction: What the Contract Emits

   The BUCK contract emits a *receipt event* containing the trusted plaintext
   fields.  The wallet layer then encrypts this into the final receipt.

   #+begin_example
   event BuckTransferReceipt(
       address indexed from,
       address indexed to,
       uint256 amount,
       address fromIdentity,       // ONCHAINID of sender
       address toIdentity,         // ONCHAINID of receiver
       bytes32 fromPubKey,         // sender's ECDH public key (x-coord)
       bytes32 toPubKey            // receiver's ECDH public key (x-coord)
   );
   #+end_example

   This event is emitted by the BUCK contract during every ~transfer~ and
   ~transferFrom~.  It is *trusted* because the contract itself determined
   all fields:

   - ~from~ / ~to~: from the transfer itself (cannot be forged)
   - ~fromIdentity~ / ~toIdentity~: looked up from IdentityRegistry
   - ~fromPubKey~ / ~toPubKey~: looked up from ONBCHAINIDs (public-key
     accounts) or from stored counterpartyRecords (private-key accounts)

   The wallet layer then:

   1. Reads this event from the transaction receipt
   2. Augments it with off-chain identity data (names, credentials -- from
      Holochain/ONCHAINID private claims)
   3. Encrypts the full receipt using the 3-party ECDH scheme
   4. Stores the encrypted receipt on Holochain (both parties' source chains)
   5. Optionally emits a receipt commitment hash via a companion contract

** Where Public Keys Come From: Resolution Order

   The contract resolves an account's ECDH public key in this order:

   #+begin_example
   function _resolvePublicKey(address account) internal view returns (bytes32) {
       // 1. Check stored counterparty record (private-key account that
       //    previously called approve with their pubkey)
       // ... checked per-pair in the calling context

       // 2. Read from ONCHAINID (public-key account)
       IIdentity id = identityRegistry.identity(account);
       if (address(id) != address(0)) {
           bytes32 pubKey = id.getKey(ECDH_KEY_PURPOSE);
           if (pubKey != bytes32(0)) return pubKey;
       }

       // 3. No public key available -- transfer allowed but no encrypted
       //    receipt can be constructed.  Event emits zero pubkey; wallet
       //    stores unencrypted receipt locally only.
       return bytes32(0);
   }
   #+end_example

** The Complete ~transfer~ and ~transferFrom~ Flow

   #+begin_example
   function transfer(address to, uint256 amount) public override returns (bool) {
       require(identityRegistry.isVerified(msg.sender), "Sender not verified");
       require(identityRegistry.isVerified(to),         "Receiver not verified");
       require(compliance.canTransfer(msg.sender, to, amount), "Compliance");

       _transfer(msg.sender, to, amount);
       compliance.transferred(msg.sender, to, amount);

       // Emit trusted receipt event
       _emitReceipt(msg.sender, to, amount);

       return true;
   }

   function transferFrom(address from, address to, uint256 amount)
       public override returns (bool)
   {
       _spendAllowance(from, msg.sender, amount);
       require(identityRegistry.isVerified(from), "Sender not verified");
       require(identityRegistry.isVerified(to),   "Receiver not verified");
       require(compliance.canTransfer(from, to, amount), "Compliance");

       _transfer(from, to, amount);
       compliance.transferred(from, to, amount);

       // Emit trusted receipt event -- uses counterparty record from approve
       _emitReceipt(from, to, amount);

       return true;
   }

   function _emitReceipt(address from, address to, uint256 amount) internal {
       // Resolve public keys: from counterparty records or ONBCHAINIDs
       bytes32 fromPubKey = counterpartyRecords[from][to].pubkey_a;
       if (fromPubKey == bytes32(0)) fromPubKey = _resolvePublicKey(from);
       bytes32 toPubKey = counterpartyRecords[from][to].pubkey_b;
       if (toPubKey == bytes32(0)) toPubKey = _resolvePublicKey(to);

       emit BuckTransferReceipt(
           from, to, amount,
           address(identityRegistry.identity(from)),
           address(identityRegistry.identity(to)),
           fromPubKey, toPubKey
       );
   }
   #+end_example

   Key point: the ~transferFrom~ path uses the counterparty record that was
   established during the ~approve~ call.  The DeFi router never needs to
   pass receipt data -- it was already stored.


* Cryptographic Receipts: 3-Party ECDH

  The BUCK contract emits trusted plaintext receipt events (previous section).
  The wallet layer encrypts these into 3-party ECDH receipts that only the
  participants can decrypt.

** The Three Keys

   For a transfer from Alice to a DeFi pool operated by Bob:

   | Key               | Held by        | Purpose                                        |
   |-------------------+----------------+------------------------------------------------|
   | ~k_s~ (sender)    | Alice          | Sender's persistent signing/encryption key     |
   | ~k_e~ (ephemeral) | Deterministic  | Derived from hash of trusted receipt event;    |
   |                   |                | reproducible by anyone who knows the event     |
   | ~K_r~ (receiver)  | Bob (deployer) | Pool operator's persistent public key          |

   The ephemeral key ~k_e~ is derived deterministically from the trusted
   receipt event (which is on-chain and immutable):

   #+begin_example
   // The receipt event fields are all on-chain, all trusted:
   receipt_data = abi.encode(
       from, to, amount,
       fromIdentity, toIdentity,
       fromPubKey, toPubKey,
       block.number
   )
   k_e = keccak256(receipt_data)    // deterministic "private key"
   #+end_example

** Encryption Scheme

   The wallet computes the encryption key via ECDH:

   #+begin_example
   // Alice's wallet computes (knows k_s, k_e, K_r):
   shared_1 = ECDH(k_s, K_r)          // sender-receiver shared secret
   shared_2 = ECDH(k_e, K_r)          // ephemeral-receiver shared secret
   encryption_key = keccak256(shared_1 || shared_2)

   // Augment the trusted receipt with off-chain identity data:
   full_receipt = {
       ...receipt_data,                 // from the on-chain event (trusted)
       sender_name: "Alice Johnson",    // from ONCHAINID private claim
       receiver_name: "Bob Smith",      // from ONCHAINID claim (via Holochain)
       purpose: "Uniswap V3 BUCK->USDT swap"
   }

   // Encrypt:
   ciphertext = AES-GCM(encryption_key, encode(full_receipt))
   #+end_example

   The encrypted receipt is stored on Holochain (both parties' source chains).
   A commitment hash can optionally be emitted on-chain:

   #+begin_example
   event ReceiptCommitment(
       bytes32 indexed transferHash,    // hash of the BuckTransferReceipt event
       bytes32 receiptHash              // hash of the encrypted receipt
   );
   #+end_example

** Who Can Decrypt

*** Sender (Alice)

    Alice has ~k_s~ and can recompute ~k_e~ from the on-chain receipt event:

    #+begin_example
    k_e = keccak256(receipt_event_data)   // deterministic from on-chain data
    shared_1 = ECDH(k_s, K_r)            // she has k_s, K_r from event
    shared_2 = ECDH(k_e, K_r)            // she has k_e, K_r from event
    encryption_key = keccak256(shared_1 || shared_2)
    #+end_example

*** Receiver / Pool Operator (Bob)

    Bob has ~k_r~ (private key) and reads the sender's public key and the
    ephemeral public key from the on-chain event:

    #+begin_example
    K_e = G * k_e                         // computable from event data
    shared_1 = ECDH(k_r, K_s)            // K_s = fromPubKey from event
    shared_2 = ECDH(k_r, K_e)            // K_e from event data
    encryption_key = keccak256(shared_1 || shared_2)
    #+end_example

*** Court-Compelled Decryption (Subpoena of Pool Operator)

    A court subpoenas Bob to decrypt a specific transaction receipt:

    #+begin_example
    // Court provides: the transaction hash
    // Bob looks up: the BuckTransferReceipt event from that transaction
    // Bob computes (using his private key k_r):
    shared_1 = ECDH(k_r, K_s)            // K_s from event
    shared_2 = ECDH(k_r, K_e)            // K_e from event
    encryption_key = keccak256(shared_1 || shared_2)
    receipt = AES-GCM-decrypt(encryption_key, ciphertext_from_holochain)
    #+end_example

    Bob reveals only the decrypted receipt.  He never reveals ~k_r~.  He
    cannot decrypt receipts where he is not a party.

*** Regulatory Escrow (Optional)

    A regulatory authority's public key ~K_reg~ adds a third shared secret:

    #+begin_example
    shared_3 = ECDH(k_e, K_reg)
    encryption_key = keccak256(shared_1 || shared_2 || shared_3)
    #+end_example

    The regulator can compute ~shared_3~ (they have ~k_reg~, and ~K_e~ is
    deterministic from the on-chain event).  But they still need ~shared_1~
    or ~shared_2~ -- requiring cooperation from a transaction participant.
    No unilateral surveillance.


* Play-by-Play: BUCK/USDT Swap on Uniswap

  Alice holds 1000 BUCK and wants to swap 500 BUCK for USDT.  Bob has deployed
  a BUCK/USDT Uniswap V3 pool.

** Prerequisites (One-Time Setup)

   #+begin_example
   Alice:
     1. Completed KYC with trusted issuer (off-chain)
     2. Trusted issuer signed claim (topic 1: KYC) on Alice's ONCHAINID
     3. Registry agent called identityRegistry.registerIdentity(
            alice_wallet, alice_onchainid, country_code)
     4. identityRegistry.isVerified(alice_wallet) == true

   Bob (pool deployer):
     1. Completed KYC (off-chain)
     2. Has KYC claim on his ONCHAINID
     3. Is registered in IdentityRegistry
     4. Created BUCK/USDT pool via Uniswap factory
        -> pool deployed at address 0xPOOL
     5. Registered pool in IdentityRegistry:
        identityRegistry.registerIdentity(
            0xPOOL, bob_onchainid, country_code)
     6. identityRegistry.isVerified(0xPOOL) == true
   #+end_example

** The Swap Transaction

*** Step 1: Alice Approves the Router (Identity Handshake)

    Alice calls ~approve~ on the BUCK contract.  This serves double duty:
    standard ERC-20 allowance AND identity exchange.

    #+begin_example
    // Alice approves Uniswap router to spend her BUCK.
    // Her wallet includes her ECDH public key (if private-key account).
    buck.approve(uniswapRouter, 500e18, alice_ecdh_pubkey)
    #+end_example

    The BUCK contract:

    #+begin_example
    1. Sets allowance[alice][uniswapRouter] = 500e18
    2. Looks up alice's ONCHAINID from IdentityRegistry
    3. Looks up uniswapRouter's ONCHAINID from IdentityRegistry
       (router is registered to Uniswap Labs / governance entity)
    4. Reads uniswapRouter's ECDH public key from its ONCHAINID
    5. Stores counterpartyRecords[alice][uniswapRouter] = {
           identity_a: alice_onchainid,
           identity_b: router_onchainid,
           pubkey_a:   alice_ecdh_pubkey,
           pubkey_b:   router_ecdh_pubkey
       }
    6. Emits IdentityExchange(alice, uniswapRouter, pubkeys...)
    #+end_example

    *This is the critical moment.*  Alice is directly calling the BUCK
    contract, so she can provide her public key.  By the time
    ~transferFrom~ is called later (by the router), the identity data is
    already stored.

    Note: the router routes to the pool, so the actual ~transferFrom~
    destination is ~0xPOOL~, not the router.  Alice should also approve
    the pool contract, or the BUCK contract should fall back to resolving
    the pool's public key from its ONCHAINID (public-key accounts don't
    need a prior approve for identity resolution).

*** Step 2: Alice Calls the Router

    #+begin_example
    // Alice initiates the swap (standard Uniswap call -- no BUCK-specific data)
    uniswapRouter.exactInputSingle(
        tokenIn:  BUCK,
        tokenOut: USDT,
        fee:      3000,        // 0.3% fee tier
        recipient: alice,
        amountIn: 500e18,
        amountOutMinimum: 490e6,  // slippage protection
        sqrtPriceLimitX96: 0
    )
    #+end_example

    No identity data passes through this call.  The router is a standard
    Uniswap contract that knows nothing about BUCK identity.

*** Step 3: Router Calls BUCK.transferFrom (Trusted Receipt Emission)

    Inside the router, the swap triggers:

    #+begin_example
    buck.transferFrom(alice, 0xPOOL, 500e18)
    #+end_example

    The BUCK contract executes:

    #+begin_example
    function transferFrom(address from, address to, uint256 amount)
        public override returns (bool)
    {
        // 1. Standard ERC-20 allowance check
        _spendAllowance(from, msg.sender, amount);

        // 2. ERC-3643 identity checks
        require(identityRegistry.isVerified(from), "Sender not verified");
        require(identityRegistry.isVerified(to),   "Receiver not verified");
        require(compliance.canTransfer(from, to, amount), "Compliance");

        // 3. Execute transfer
        _transfer(from, to, amount);

        // 4. Update compliance state
        compliance.transferred(from, to, amount);

        // 5. Emit TRUSTED receipt event (all fields from contract state)
        _emitReceipt(from, to, amount);

        return true;
    }
    #+end_example

    The ~_emitReceipt~ function resolves public keys from the stored
    counterparty records (set during ~approve~) or from the ONBCHAINIDs:

    #+begin_example
    _emitReceipt(alice, 0xPOOL, 500e18) emits:

    BuckTransferReceipt(
        from:         alice,
        to:           0xPOOL,
        amount:       500e18,
        fromIdentity: alice_onchainid,      // from IdentityRegistry
        toIdentity:   bob_onchainid,        // pool registered to Bob
        fromPubKey:   alice_ecdh_pubkey,     // from counterpartyRecord (approve)
        toPubKey:     bob_ecdh_pubkey        // from Bob's ONCHAINID (public)
    )
    #+end_example

    *This event is trusted.*  Every field comes from the BUCK contract's own
    state: the IdentityRegistry, the counterpartyRecords, the transfer
    parameters.  No external caller provided these values for this specific
    transfer -- they were established during the ~approve~ handshake or read
    from on-chain identity contracts.

*** Step 4: Wallet Encrypts the Receipt (Off-Chain)

    Alice's wallet software observes the ~BuckTransferReceipt~ event in the
    transaction receipt and constructs the encrypted receipt:

    #+begin_example
    // 1. Read trusted fields from the on-chain event
    receipt_data = event.{from, to, amount, fromIdentity, toIdentity,
                          fromPubKey, toPubKey, block.number}

    // 2. Derive ephemeral key deterministically from the event
    k_e = keccak256(abi.encode(receipt_data))

    // 3. Compute ECDH shared secrets
    shared_1 = ECDH(alice_private_key, toPubKey)
    shared_2 = ECDH(k_e, toPubKey)
    encryption_key = keccak256(shared_1 || shared_2)

    // 4. Augment with off-chain identity data
    full_receipt = {
        ...receipt_data,
        sender_name:   "Alice Johnson",           // from private KYC claim
        receiver_name: "Bob Smith, DeFi Ops Inc.", // from ONCHAINID (Holochain)
        operation:     "Uniswap V3 swap BUCK -> USDT",
        pool:          "0xPOOL",
        timestamp:     "2026-03-14T15:32:12Z"
    }

    // 5. Encrypt
    ciphertext = AES-GCM(encryption_key, encode(full_receipt))

    // 6. Store on Holochain (both parties' source chains)
    holochain.store(alice_chain, ciphertext)
    holochain.store(bob_chain,   ciphertext)

    // 7. Optionally emit commitment hash on-chain
    receiptCommitter.commit(keccak256(event), keccak256(ciphertext))
    #+end_example

*** Step 5: Pool Executes the Swap

    The Uniswap pool sends USDT to Alice.  Since USDT is not an Alberta Buck
    token, the USDT transfer has no BUCK identity requirements (it follows
    whatever rules USDT imposes, which is typically none).

*** Step 6: Completed State

    #+begin_example
    On-chain (public, trusted):
      - ERC-20 Transfer event: alice -> 0xPOOL, 500 BUCK
      - BuckTransferReceipt event: from/to addresses, ONCHAINID addresses,
        ECDH public keys -- all set by the BUCK contract itself
      - Swap event: BUCK/USDT pool, 500 BUCK in, ~495 USDT out
      - (optional) ReceiptCommitment: hash of encrypted receipt

    Off-chain (Holochain, encrypted):
      - Full encrypted receipt on Alice's and Bob's source chains
      - Contains real names, transaction purpose, full details
      - Decryptable only by Alice, Bob, or regulator+participant

    What anyone can see:
      - Two verified addresses transacted 500 BUCK
      - The pool is operated by a verified identity
      - The public keys used for receipt encryption (but not the receipt)

    What Alice can see:
      - Full decrypted receipt (she has k_s)
      - Bob's identity as pool operator

    What Bob can see:
      - Full decrypted receipt (he has k_r)
      - Alice's identity as the swapper

    What a regulator can see (with escrow key + participant cooperation):
      - Full decrypted receipt (requires cooperation from Alice or Bob)

    What the public CANNOT see:
      - Alice's real name
      - Bob's real name (as pool operator)
      - The purpose of the transaction
      - The off-chain identity data in the encrypted receipt
    #+end_example


* Edge Cases and Design Considerations

** Flash Loans and Atomic Transactions

   A flash loan borrows and repays in a single transaction.  The borrower's
   address must be verified, and the lending contract must be registered.
   The receipt covers the full borrow-repay cycle as a single atomic event.

** Multi-Hop Swaps

   A swap routed through BUCK -> WETH -> USDT involves two BUCK transfers
   (BUCK -> pool1, pool1 -> pool2 if intermediate).  Each BUCK transfer
   generates its own encrypted receipt.  The router contract must also be
   registered if it holds BUCK transiently.

** Contract-to-Contract Transfers

   When one DeFi contract sends BUCK to another (e.g., a yield aggregator
   moving BUCK between pools), both contracts must be registered.  The
   receipt identifies both contracts' deployers as the parties.  This creates
   a clear chain of custody: every BUCK movement has an identified legal
   operator at each end.

** Wrapping and Bridging

   If BUCK is wrapped (WBUCK) for use on another chain, the wrapping
   contract must be registered.  The wrap/unwrap generates receipts.
   On the destination chain, the bridged BUCK may operate under different
   identity rules (or no identity rules if the destination chain doesn't
   enforce ERC-3643).  This is a known limitation -- the Alberta Buck's
   identity guarantees only hold on chains where the IdentityRegistry is
   deployed.

** Gas Costs of Receipt Emission

   Emitting encrypted receipts as Ethereum log events costs gas.  The
   ~encryptedReceipt~ bytes field is the dominant cost.  Estimates:

   | Receipt size | Log cost (~gas)  | At 30 gwei, ETH=$3000 |
   |--------------+------------------+------------------------|
   | 256 bytes    | ~8,000 gas       | ~$0.72                 |
   | 512 bytes    | ~16,000 gas      | ~$1.44                 |
   | 1024 bytes   | ~32,000 gas      | ~$2.88                 |

   For high-frequency DeFi operations, this may be prohibitive.  Options:
   - Emit only a receipt commitment hash on-chain (~200 gas), store full
     encrypted receipt on Holochain
   - Batch receipts into a Merkle tree, commit root periodically
   - Use an L2 for receipt emission (much cheaper gas)

** Privacy of Pool Deployer Identity

   The pool deployer's identity is linked to the pool contract in the
   IdentityRegistry, but this mapping is on-chain and public.  Anyone can
   look up ~identityRegistry.identity(0xPOOL)~ and find Bob's ONCHAINID.

   If Bob's ONCHAINID has only private claims (KYC but not public identity),
   then the public knows "0xPOOL is operated by a verified entity" but not
   WHO that entity is.  Bob's identity is revealed only in encrypted receipts.

   If Bob has a public identity claim (e.g., "DeFi Ops Inc., Alberta"),
   then anyone can see who operates the pool.  This is likely the expected
   case for commercial DeFi operators -- public identity builds trust and
   attracts liquidity.
