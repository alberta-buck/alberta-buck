#+BEGIN_ABSTRACT
A monetary system can promise privacy or it can promise accountability; the
Alberta Buck promises both at once, along a single seam.  /Bulk/ traceability
is defeated -- no chain-analytics firm can reconstruct who paid whom -- while
/bilateral/ disclosure is preserved: the two parties to any payment always
retain the cryptographic means to prove, under compulsion, exactly who the
counterparty was.  The load-bearing half of that promise is the
*non-deniable-receipt invariant*: there must be no path by which someone pays
BUCKs to another and keeps cryptographic deniability of the act.  Concretely, a
recipient who is compelled by a court, and who chooses to comply, must be able
to produce a cryptographically sound receipt naming the payer's /decrypted,
KYC-bound Identity/.

This document shows that BUCK Notes do not yet satisfy that invariant, defines
it formally, and specifies the fix.  The shipped spend path verifies the
/depositor's/ identity (=IdentityRegistry= =.verifySpendCP=), but the mint path
verifies /nothing/ about the /issuer's/: the per-leaf =issuerData= is folded
into the commitment's =idHash= and never checked -- exactly the
"flavor-specific witness verification (Schnorr for A1/B1, ElGamal
re-randomization well-formedness for A2)" that =mint.circom= marked "deferred to
a later revision" and that was never re-introduced.  A dishonest or colluding
issuer can therefore mint a note that pays a recipient while leaving no
recoverable payer Identity.  The remedy is to make the /deferred approve
handshake/ symmetric and fully verified: bind a checked issuer identity at mint
(a Schnorr binding for public issuers; a recipient-hiding re-encryption proof
for the private A2 issuer), and add the =E_depositor_for_issuer= payload to B-note
deposits.  Each leg is stated as a theorem with an executable =py_ecc= check, and
the circom / EVM formal-verification tooling that can mechanize the soundness
arguments is catalogued. ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-notes][Notes]], [[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-proofs][Proofs]])

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Non-Deniable-Receipt Invariant

  The Alberta Buck identity layer is built so that /every/ BUCK transfer leaves
  both endpoints holding decryptable proof of the other's Identity.  For a
  direct =transfer=, the bilateral =approve= handshake establishes this: each
  party re-encrypts their KYC-certified credential under the other's key and
  proves (Chaum-Pedersen) that the re-encryption carries the same Identity point
  \( M \).  BUCK Notes defer and reorganise that handshake through the
  commitment pool, and the invariant must survive the reorganisation.

** Statement

   #+begin_definition
   Let an /Identity/ be the KYC-certified point \( M \in \mathbb{G}_1 \) bound to
   a registered account by an issuer's Pointcheval-Sanders credential and stored
   (ElGamal-encrypted) as \( E_{\text{addr}}[X] = (R_X, C_X) = (r_X G,\ M_X + r_X
   \cdot pk_X) \), with \( pk_X = sk_X G \).  A /payment/ moves value \( v \) from
   a payer \( P \) to a recipient \( D \).
   #+end_definition

   #+begin_definition
   /(Non-deniable receipt.)/  A protocol satisfies the *non-deniable-receipt
   invariant* if for every accepted payment there exists a /receipt/ \( \mathcal{R} \)
   and an efficient public predicate \( \mathsf{RcptVerify} \) such that:
   - *(Completeness.)*  The recipient \( D \) can compute \( \mathcal{R} \) from data
     they hold plus public chain state, and \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \)
     names a registered Identity \( M_P \) and value \( v \).
   - *(Soundness / non-frameability.)*  No PPT adversary can produce
     \( \mathcal{R}' \) with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}') = 1 \) naming an
     Identity \( M_{P'} \) that did not author the payment.
   - *(No deniability.)*  No PPT payer can cause an accepted payment to \( D \) for
     which /no/ accepting receipt naming the payer exists -- even in collusion
     with \( D \).
   #+end_definition

   The third clause is the operative one: it is the collusion-resistant form.  A
   self-interested recipient who simply wants a record is protected by
   completeness alone; the invariant additionally forbids a /willing/ pair from
   constructing an untraceable BUCK payment.

** Why the asymmetry between flavors

   Who can be named, and how, depends on what is known at mint time -- which is
   exactly the axis that defines the note flavors:

   - *B (bearer).*  The depositor is unknown at mint, so the note cannot carry a
     recipient-targeted ciphertext.  The /issuer/ must therefore be a *public*
     Identity: their \( M \) is in the clear and the recipient names them
     directly.  A bearer note from an /encrypted/ issuer (a "B2") is not merely
     undesirable but /impossible/ -- see /Why B2 is impossible/ below -- and the
     mint circuit + =Notes.mint= now enforce its absence.
   - *A (addressed).*  The issuer knows the recipient at mint and has already
     re-encrypted material the eventual depositor can open.  The issuer may stay
     /private/ (A2) by re-encrypting their /own/ Identity under the recipient's
     key (=E_iss-for-rec=), which only the recipient can decrypt, or /public/
     (A1) by attaching it in the clear with a signature.

   In all cases the recipient must end up holding a path to a /verified/ issuer
   Identity.  The next section shows the shipped code does not provide one.

** Why B2 (a private-issuer bearer note) is impossible

   The asymmetry above is not an implementation limitation -- it is a theorem.
   The only party who could re-encrypt the issuer's Identity material so the
   /eventual/ recipient can open it is the issuer, and the issuer does not know
   who that recipient will be.

   #+begin_quote
   *Claim.*  A /meaningful/ private-issuer bearer note (a "B2") cannot exist.

   *Proof.*  A bearer note's redeemer is unknown at mint: it is whoever comes to
   hold the bearer secret \( \rho \).  For that redeemer to name the issuer, the
   note must carry a binding to the issuer's /registered/ Identity that the
   redeemer can decrypt and prove.  The issuer creates this binding at mint --
   when the redeemer is unknown -- so it must be openable under a key whose
   secret the eventual redeemer will hold.  Two cases exhaust the options:

   1. /The key is the redeemer's own registered key./  Impossible: the issuer
      does not know the redeemer and cannot target a key they have never seen.
   2. /The key travels with the note/ -- a note-bound key whose secret is
      derived from \( \rho \).  Then it is openable by *every* holder, because
      holding \( \rho \) is exactly what makes one a holder; the issuer's
      Identity is revealed to the entire, freely-circulating bearer chain.  That
      is no more private than B1 (a public issuer in the clear) -- the issuer
      gains nothing by being "private".

   A binding openable by /no one/ fails decryptability outright.  Hence the only
   binding that is both decryptable-by-the-redeemer and private-from-the-world
   requires a /designated/ recipient -- and a note with a designated recipient
   is an *addressed* (A) note.  "Private issuer" and "bearer" are therefore
   mutually exclusive: B2 collapses into A2.  \( \qquad \blacksquare \)
   #+end_quote

   The contrast with A2 is the whole point.  A2 reveals the issuer to exactly
   /one/ party -- the recipient the issuer chose -- under the issuer's control;
   the re-encryption \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \) is openable only with
   \( sk_{rec} \).  B2 has no chosen party, so any binding it could carry is
   openable by everyone or by no one.  (The earlier near-miss -- publishing
   \( T = r' pk_{rec} \), which leaked \( M_{iss} \) -- was a /soundness/ slip
   inside A2; B2 by contrast is a /structural/ impossibility, independent of any
   binding construction.)

   The protocol *enforces* the theorem rather than assuming it.  The flavor set
   is exactly \( \{A1, A2, B1\} \); there is no B2 codepoint.  The mint circuit
   range-constrains \( \text{flavor} \in \{1,2,3\} \) and projects the per-leaf
   =issuerMode= (A2 \to PRIVATE, \( \{A1,B1\} \to \) PUBLIC), which the Groth16
   proof now /binds/; =Notes.mint= requires a registered public issuer + Schnorr
   for every PUBLIC-mode leaf.  Because a bearer leaf is always PUBLIC-mode, a
   private issuer simply cannot mint a bearer note -- the on-chain image of the
   proof above.  See /The Required Mint SNARK Signal/ and /Implementation
   Status/.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Receipt as a Proof Chain

  For the recipient to prove "I received \( v \) BUCKs issued by Identity \( M_P \)",
  the receipt must chain four links, each independently checkable against public
  chain state:

  #+begin_example
  (a) OPENING     I hold the opening O; cm = Poseidon5(flavor, v, rho, idHash, predicate)
  (b) MINTED      cm appears in the calldata cms[] of mint tx T            [reveals cm]
  (c) ISSUER      from O (+ my key), recover AND authenticate M_P
  (d) PAID        nullifier = Poseidon3(rho, idHash, tag) in a Spent/SpentA
                  event whose pool->recipient Transfer delivered v to me
  #+end_example

  Links (a), (b), (d) are already sound in the shipped contracts.  (b) holds
  because =cms[]= live in the mint transaction's calldata, which is immutable
  chain history; the recipient reveals \( cm \) to anchor it (accepting the
  spend\(\leftrightarrow\)mint linkage, which is the price of complying).  (d)
  holds because the nullifier is a deterministic function of the opening and is
  emitted by =Notes.Spent= / =Notes.SpentA= alongside the ERC-20 =Transfer= from
  the pool.

  *The invariant lives or dies on link (c)* -- and (c) is precisely the step the
  implementation never enforces.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* What the Shipped Implementation Binds

  The spend side verifies the depositor's Identity; the mint side verifies
  nothing about the issuer's.  This asymmetry is the whole bug.

  #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
  | Leg                | Shipped check                                                                 | Guarantee                                  |
  |--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------|
  | Depositor (A-spend)| =IdentityRegistry.verifySpendCP(spender, recipient, E_n, pi)=                  | spender provably /is/ the addressed \(M_{rec}\) |
  | Depositor (B-spend)| none (knowledge of \(\rho\) is the authorization)                             | spender is /some/ registered recipient     |
  | Issuer  (any mint) | none -- =idHash= folds =issuerData= via Poseidon-8, value never checked       | *none*                                     |
  #+LATEX: }

  Concretely, from the source:

  - =circuits/spend_a.circom= constrains ~idHash === Poseidon8(eNoteR.x, eNoteR.y,
    eNoteC.x, eNoteC.y, issuerData[0..3])~, where =issuerData= is a /private
    witness/ holding A1 \( (m_{\text{iss}}, \sigma_R.x, \sigma_R.y, \sigma_s) \)
    or A2 \( (R_{\text{iss}}, C_{\text{iss}}) = E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \).  The
    circuit /hashes it in/ but never verifies the signature or the encryption.
  - =circuits/spend.circom= (the shipped B-spend) leaves =flavor= unconstrained
    and binds /no/ issuer material at all; =idHash= is opaque.
  - =circuits/mint_batch.circom= public inputs are
    ~[oldRoot, newRoot, nextLeafIndex, totalFace, cm]~ with private
    ~[flavor, v, rho, idHash, predicate, siblings]~.  No issuer key, no signature,
    no ElGamal well-formedness.  =mint.circom= says so in as many words:
    /"flavor-specific witness verification (Schnorr for A1/B1, ElGamal
    re-randomization well-formedness for A2) is deferred to a later revision."/
  - =src/Notes.sol= =mint()= records =Minted(issuer = msg.sender, ...)= but ties
    the minting account to the leaves only through transaction authorship, never
    cryptographically to the committed =issuerData=.

* The Gap, Per Flavor

** A2 -- addressed, private issuer

   The opening carries \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} = (R_{\text{iss}}, C_{\text{iss}}) \),
   an ElGamal ciphertext the recipient decrypts with \( sk_{\text{rec}} \) to obtain
   \( M_{\text{iss}} \).  It is bound into =idHash= but *not* proven to re-encrypt the
   issuer's /registered/ credential.  A malicious issuer commits a random
   \( (R_{\text{iss}}, C_{\text{iss}}) \): the note mints and spends cleanly, the
   recipient decrypts to a meaningless point, and link (c) yields no registered
   Identity.  *The payer keeps full deniability.*

** A1 -- addressed, public issuer

   The opening carries \( (m_{\text{iss}}, \sigma) \).  The Schnorr signature
   \( \sigma \) is never verified, and nothing forces \( m_{\text{iss}} \) to
   equal the /minting/ account's registered \( M \).  A malicious issuer can
   omit a valid \( \sigma \) (no receipt), or commit a /victim's/
   \( m_{\text{iss}} \) and a forged \( \sigma \) (frame), and the note still works.

** B1 -- bearer, public issuer

   =spend.circom= binds no issuer Identity whatsoever.  The only issuer signal is
   =msg.sender= in the =Minted= event.  A receipt is therefore constructible
   /only if/ the issuer minted from a *public* Identity -- and the contract
   cannot enforce that, because =cms[]= are opaque and it cannot tell a bearer
   leaf from an addressed one.  A bearer-spendable note minted from an
   /encrypted/ Identity is unnameable.  *The payer keeps full deniability.*

** Summary

   In every flavor the issuer Identity is, at best, /committed/ but never
   /verified/; in B1 it is not even committed.  The depositor half of the
   handshake is checked (=verifySpendCP=); the issuer half is missing.  The fix
   is to add it.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Fix: A Symmetric, Verified Handshake

  Restore the symmetry the "deferred approve handshake" framing always implied:
  the /mint/ is the issuer's half of approve and must be /verified/, exactly as
  the /deposit/ verifies the depositor's half.

** Issuer half, bound at mint

   At mint, the issuer is =msg.sender= -- /not/ anonymous (they authored the tx).
   So the issuer's registered credential \( E_{\text{addr}}[\text{issuer}] \) may be
   read as a public input; only the /recipient/ (for A2) must stay hidden.

   - *Public issuer (A1, B1).*  Require =msg.sender= to be =isPublicIdentity=, and
     verify a Schnorr signature by their registered key \( pk_{\text{iss}} \) over the
     batch commitment \( H_{\text{batch}} = \mathrm{keccak}(\texttt{cms}) \).  Bind
     \( m_{\text{iss}} = M_{\text{iss}} \) (the in-the-clear registered Identity) into
     =idHash=.  Because the registered Identity of a public account is in the clear,
     link (c) reduces to a registry read plus the verified signature.
   - *Private issuer (A2).*  The issuer proves =E_iss-for-rec= re-encrypts their
     /own/ registered \( M_{\text{iss}} \) under the recipient's key
     \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) -- the =verifyApprove= relation with =sender = issuer=,
     =spender = recipient=.  But =verifyApprove= as shipped takes \( pk_{\text{rec}} \)
     as a verifier input and so /names the recipient/, which A2 must not do.  Two
     recipient-hiding realisations:

     1. *In-SNARK (mint circuit).*  Extend =mint_batch.circom= so each A2 leaf
        proves, with \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) and \( M_{\text{iss}} \) as private witnesses:
        \[
        pk_{\text{iss}} = sk_{\text{iss}} G,\quad
        M_{\text{iss}} = C^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}} - sk_{\text{iss}} R^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}},\quad
        E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} = \bigl(r' G,\ M_{\text{iss}} + r'\, pk_{\text{rec}}\bigr),
        \]
        with \( E_{\text{addr}}[\text{issuer}] = (R^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}}, C^{\text{reg}}_{\text{iss}}) \)
        a /public/ input and \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) the same key that re-randomises the
        note ciphertext \( E_{\text{note}} \).  Cost: a handful of in-circuit
        \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) scalar muls per A2 leaf -- the ~10x the original deferral
        feared, but bounded and paid only on A2 mints.
     2. *Recipient-blinded on-chain CP.*  Keep verification on-chain (EIP-196,
        cheap) but blind \( pk_{\text{rec}} \): the issuer proves the
        re-encryption relation against a /Pedersen commitment/ to
        \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) that also appears (identically committed) in
        \( E_{\text{note}} \), so the verifier never learns \( pk_{\text{rec}} \).
        This is a new sigma protocol (Section /Provable Correctness/), avoiding
        in-SNARK \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) arithmetic at the cost of a bespoke proof.

   The in-SNARK route (1) is the recommended default: it reuses the trusted setup
   already in place for the batch mint and keeps the on-chain mint a single
   verifier call.

** Depositor half, at deposit

   - *A1, A2.*  Already shipped: =verifySpendCP= proves the spender is the
     addressed \( M_{\text{rec}} \).  The issuer, who chose \( M_{\text{rec}} \) at
     mint, already holds the symmetric knowledge.
   - *B1.*  Add =E_depositor_for_issuer= to the spend: the depositor re-encrypts
     /their/ registered \( M_{\text{dep}} \) under the public issuer's
     \( pk_{\text{iss}} \), with a =verifyApprove=-style CP proof (=sender =
     depositor=, =spender = issuer=, both public-or-named here so no blinding is
     needed), emitted in the =SpentB= event.  This closes the symmetric direction
     -- the public bearer issuer can identify who cashed their note.

** Enforcing bearer => public issuer

   The contract cannot read =flavor= from an opaque =cm=.  Make the mint
   flavor-aware for the binding: the mint circuit exposes, per leaf, a public
   =issuerMode in {PUBLIC, PRIVATE}= and proves the matching binding
   (Schnorr-bound public \( M \), or in-SNARK re-encryption).  =Notes.mint=
   requires every =PRIVATE= leaf to be an A-flavor (addressed) leaf -- a bearer
   (B) leaf with =issuerMode = PRIVATE= is unsatisfiable -- and requires
   =msg.sender= to be =isPublicIdentity= whenever any =PUBLIC= leaf is present.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* Protocol Changes (Implementation Surface)

** IdentityRegistry

   #+BEGIN_SRC solidity
   // Public-issuer binding: Schnorr by msg.sender's registered key over H_batch.
   function verifyIssuerSchnorr(address issuer, bytes32 hBatch, SchnorrProof calldata s)
       external view returns (bool);

   // Private-issuer binding (A2), recipient-blinded sigma variant (option 2):
   // proves E_iss_for_rec re-encrypts E_addr[issuer]'s M under a *committed* pk_rec
   // that the mint SNARK also binds to E_note, without revealing pk_rec.
   function verifyIssuerCPBlinded(address issuer, ElGamalCT calldata eIssForRec,
                                  bytes32 pkRecCommit, IssuerCPProof calldata pi)
       external view returns (bool);

   // B-note depositor->issuer (mirror of verifyApprove, both parties named):
   function verifyDepositorForIssuer(address depositor, address issuer,
                                     ElGamalCT calldata eDepForIss, CPProof calldata pi)
       external view returns (bool);
   #+END_SRC

   Under the in-SNARK route the A2 binding is discharged inside the mint verifier
   and =verifyIssuerCPBlinded= is unnecessary; the registry then only gains
   =verifyIssuerSchnorr= and =verifyDepositorForIssuer=.

** Notes.sol

   - =mint(...)= gains per-leaf =issuerMode[]= (public input of the mint SNARK)
     and, for public leaves, a =SchnorrProof= over =keccak(cms)=; it requires
     =msg.sender= public when any public leaf is present, and rejects
     =PRIVATE+bearer= leaves.  The A2 re-encryption binding rides inside the mint
     proof (option 1) or is checked per leaf via =verifyIssuerCPBlinded= (option 2).
   - =spend(...)= (B) gains =(E_depositor_for_issuer, CPProof)=, calls
     =verifyDepositorForIssuer(msg.sender, issuer, ...)=, and emits
     =SpentB(nullifier, face, recipient, eDepForIss)=.  The issuer address is
     recovered from the leaf's public-issuer binding revealed at spend.
   - =spendACP(...)= (A) is unchanged on the depositor side; the issuer guarantee
     now comes for free from the mint-time binding.

** Circuits

   - =mint_batch.circom=: add the per-leaf =issuerMode= public signal and, for
     A2 leaves, the re-encryption constraints above (option 1).  For public
     leaves, constrain =idHash='s issuerData prefix to equal the registered
     public \( M \) so the in-clear identity is the one bound.
   - =spend.circom= (B): constrain ~flavor === B1~ and reveal enough of the
     leaf's public-issuer binding to recover the issuer address on-chain.
   - =spend_a.circom=: unchanged (the A2 issuer binding moved to mint).

** Wallet (=alberta_buck.wallet=)

   - Mint: construct the issuer Schnorr proof (public) or the A2 re-encryption
     witness (private), mirroring =notes.id_hash_a1/a2=.
   - Spend (B): construct =E_depositor_for_issuer= and its CP proof.
   - Receipt: implement =RcptVerify= as a standalone verifier (opening + mint-tx
     reference + spend-tx reference + issuer recovery) so a recipient can produce
     and a third party can check the receipt entirely off-chain from chain data.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* Provable Correctness

  We extend the companion /Proofs/ document (Theorems 1-11) with the
  issuer-binding results.  Each is accompanied -- in the proofs document's house
  style -- by an executable =py_ecc= sanity check on BN254.

** Theorems

   #+begin_theorem
   /(Issuer-binding completeness.)/  An honestly generated public-issuer Schnorr
   proof, and an honestly generated A2 re-encryption proof (in-SNARK or blinded),
   verify.
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_theorem
   /(Issuer-binding soundness.)/  If =verifyIssuerSchnorr= accepts, the batch
   commitments were authorised by =issuer='s registered key.  If the A2
   re-encryption proof accepts, \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \) decrypts under
   \( sk_{\text{rec}} \) to the issuer's /registered/ \( M_{\text{iss}} \)
   (the same \( M \) certified by the issuer's PS credential -- /Theorem 6/).
   Reduction: special soundness of the underlying sigma protocol (two accepting
   transcripts sharing commitments extract \( sk_{\text{iss}}, r' \)), as in
   /Theorems 4, 6/; or Groth16 knowledge-soundness for the in-SNARK route.
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_theorem
   /(Receipt completeness.)/  For every note accepted by the patched =mint= and
   redeemed by =spend=/=spendACP=, the recipient can compute \( \mathcal{R} \)
   with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \) naming a registered Identity.
   Proof: links (a),(b),(d) hold as before; (c) now holds by issuer-binding
   soundness (public: registry \( M \) + Schnorr; private: decrypt
   \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \), validity guaranteed by the accepted binding).
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_theorem
   /(Receipt soundness / non-frameability.)/  No PPT adversary produces an
   accepting \( \mathcal{R}' \) naming \( M_{P'} \) that did not author the
   payment.  Reduction: a forged public receipt breaks Schnorr EUF-CMA; a forged
   private receipt breaks re-encryption soundness (/Theorem 7/) or the binding
   hash (collision resistance of Poseidon-8 in =idHash=).
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_theorem
   /(No deniability under collusion.)/  No payer-recipient coalition can produce
   an accepted payment with /no/ accepting receipt: =mint= rejects any leaf
   lacking a valid issuer binding, so every spendable leaf carries one, and the
   binding is recoverable by the holder of the opening.
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_theorem
   /(Recipient privacy preserved.)/  The A2 issuer binding is zero-knowledge in
   \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) and \( M_{\text{iss}} \): the in-SNARK route reveals only
   the Groth16 proof; the blinded route reveals only a hiding Pedersen commitment
   to \( pk_{\text{rec}} \).  No observer learns the recipient.  Reduction: HVZK
   of the sigma protocol (/Theorem 5/) / Groth16 zero-knowledge.
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_corollary
   /(Mutual decryptability.)/  Combining the issuer half (above) with the
   depositor half (=verifySpendCP= for A; =verifyDepositorForIssuer= for B),
   both parties can recover the other's registered Identity, and each can prove
   it under compulsion.  This is the Notes specialisation of the BUCK
   mutual-decryptability invariant (/Proofs/ Theorem 11), now /enforced/ rather
   than /assumed/.
   #+end_corollary

** Executable sanity checks (=py_ecc= on BN254)

   Mirroring the proofs document, each theorem ships a small witnessed check that
   runs in CI alongside the existing =alberta_buck.wallet= vector generation:

   - *Issuer Schnorr*: sign \( H_{\text{batch}} \) under \( sk_{\text{iss}} \), assert
     verify; assert a tampered \( H_{\text{batch}} \) or wrong key fails.
   - *A2 re-encryption*: build \( E_{\text{addr}}[\text{issuer}] \), decrypt to
     \( M_{\text{iss}} \), form \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \) under \( pk_{\text{rec}} \),
     run the prover/verifier; assert the recipient's \( sk_{\text{rec}} \)-decryption
     equals \( M_{\text{iss}} \); assert a random \( E_{\text{iss-for-rec}} \) is
     rejected (soundness witness) and that the transcript hides
     \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) (two runs with distinct \( pk_{\text{rec}} \) are
     indistinguishable to the verifier given the commitment).
   - *Receipt*: assemble \( \mathcal{R} \) from a synthetic mint+spend and assert
     =RcptVerify= = 1; assert a frame attempt (swap \( M_P \)) fails.

   These vectors double as the canonical inputs the Solidity and circom tests
   load via =vm.readFile=, keeping the on-chain verifier, the circuit, and the
   reference math in lock-step (the pattern already used for =verifySpendCP=).

* Formal-Verification Approaches

  "Every step provably correct" spans three artifact classes -- the sigma-protocol
  mathematics, the circom circuits, and the Solidity verifiers -- each with its
  own tractable tooling.

** The cryptographic mathematics

   - *Hand proofs + =py_ecc=*: the existing /Proofs/ methodology -- completeness,
     special-soundness (2-extractability), and HVZK arguments, each pinned by an
     executable BN254 check.  Low-tech, high-confidence, already in the repo.
   - *EasyCrypt*: a proof assistant purpose-built for /game-based/ cryptographic
     proofs; the sigma-protocol soundness and ZK lemmas can be mechanised as
     reductions, giving machine-checked versions of Theorems above.
   - *CryptoVerif* (computational) or *ProVerif* (symbolic): protocol-level
     analysis of the full handshake (mint binding + deposit binding) against the
     non-deniability and recipient-privacy goals stated as queries.

** The circom circuits (soundness is the hard part)

   The danger in arithmetic circuits is /under-constraint/ -- a witness slot that
   the prover can set freely, silently widening soundness (the exact failure mode
   of the unverified =issuerData=).  Tooling that targets this directly:

   - *Picus* (Veridise): automated detection of under-constrained signals; run it
     on the patched =mint_batch.circom= to prove the new issuer-binding signals
     are /determined/ by the public inputs.
   - *Circomspect* (Trail of Bits): static analysis / linting for common circom
     pitfalls (unconstrained signals, =<--= without =<==,= signal-vs-constraint
     confusion).
   - *ecne* (0xPARC): proves R1CS systems compute deterministic functions of
     their inputs -- a soundness witness for the binding constraints.
   - *Coda* / *circom-to-Coq* efforts: full functional-correctness extraction to a
     proof assistant for the security-critical templates (the re-encryption
     gate).
   - Discipline: keep the security-critical constraints (the \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)
     relations) in small, separately-audited sub-templates with explicit
     =Num2Bits= range checks, as the value-conservation bound already does.

** The Solidity verifiers and Notes.sol

   - *Halmos* (a16z): symbolic-execution property tests in Solidity -- assert, for
     all inputs, e.g. "=mint= reverts unless every leaf carries a valid issuer
     binding" and "=spend= emits a recoverable issuer for every accepted note."
   - *Kontrol / KEVM* (Runtime Verification): K-framework formal proofs over EVM
     bytecode for the highest-assurance properties (the binding-or-revert
     invariant, nullifier non-reuse).
   - *Certora Prover*: CVL rules for state invariants (=noteFaceSum= conservation,
     "no accepted spend without a prior valid mint binding").
   - *solc SMTChecker*: lightweight, in-compiler assertions and overflow checks on
     the new code paths.
   - *Foundry*: fuzz + stateful invariant testing as the always-on backstop;
     negative tests below are expressed here first.

** What "provably correct per step" buys

   The chain of guarantees is: =py_ecc=/EasyCrypt prove the /math/; Picus/ecne
   prove the /circuit/ faithfully and exhaustively enforces that math;
   Halmos/Kontrol prove the /contract/ admits no spend whose mint lacked the
   binding.  Composed, no accepted Note payment can exist without a recoverable,
   sound receipt -- the invariant, mechanically checked end-to-end.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* Data Revealed Per Step, and Why Privacy Holds

  The whole construction has to thread one needle: publish enough that each
  party can /name/ the other under compulsion, while publishing so little that no
  /third party/ can name either, or link payments in bulk.  This section walks
  every step of every flavor, tabulates exactly what each participant reveals to
  whom, and argues -- step by step -- that the two privacy requirements survive
  and the mutual-decryptability requirement is met by the data each party is left
  holding.  The reasoning is grounded in a concrete near-miss: the first
  A2-binding draft leaked the private issuer's Identity, and showing /why/ the
  shipped version does not is the clearest way to see the invariant.

** Requirements and adversaries

   #+begin_definition
   Three properties, against three adversaries:
   - *(Bulk-tracing resistance.)*  /Mallory/, a passive observer with the full
     chain history and unbounded compute but no party's secrets, cannot
     reconstruct who paid whom, how much, or link a mint to its spend(s).
   - *(Third-party non-disclosure.)*  Nothing on chain reveals either party's
     KYC-bound Identity point \( M \) to a non-participant -- not even to the
     /counterparty's/ counterparty, and not in aggregate across many payments.
   - *(Mutual decryptability.)*  Each of the two participants, from data they
     hold plus public chain state, can produce a sound receipt naming the other's
     registered \( M \) -- and a /colluding/ pair cannot arrange a payment for
     which no such receipt exists (the non-deniable-receipt invariant).
   #+end_definition

   The first two are /confidentiality/ against outsiders; the third is
   /accountability/ between insiders.  They pull in opposite directions, so every
   datum published is justified below against all three.

** The on-chain footprint of each step

   What actually lands on chain, and what stays off it:

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Step                        | Published on chain                                                                             | Kept off chain (party-held)                                                    |
   |-----------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
   | =register= / =bindContract= | addr, =pk=, =E_addr= (ElGamal ct of \(M\) under own =pk=), =isPublicIdentity=                  | \(m\), \(M\), \(sk\)                                                           |
   | =mint= (any flavor)         | =Minted(issuer, totalFace, startIndex, count, newRoot)=; =cms[]= in calldata (opaque Poseidon) | per-leaf opening \((flavor,v,\rho,idHash,pred)\); \(E_{note}\), issuer payload |
   | =mint= public (A1/B1)       | + =IssuerBound(issuer,newRoot)=; one Schnorr over =keccak(cms)=                                | --                                                                             |
   | =mint= private (A2)         | + =IssuerReencBound(issuer,newRoot,count)=; per-leaf =(Q,U,T_hat,A_1..A_5)=                    | \(pk_{rec}, M_{iss}, r', \beta, \gamma, sk_{iss}\)                             |
   | B-spend                     | =Spent(nf,face,recipient)= or =SpentB(nf,face,recipient,issuer,eDepForIss)=                    | \(\rho, idHash\), opening                                                      |
   | A-spend                     | =SpentA(nf,face,recipient)=; revealed =E_n=; CP-DLEQ =(e,s,T_1,T_2)=                           | \(\rho, idHash, sk_{dep}\)                                                     |
   #+LATEX: }

   Two facts do the heavy lifting throughout.  First, =cms[]= are Poseidon
   commitments: a leaf reveals /nothing/ about \( (flavor, v, \rho, idHash,
   predicate) \) without \( \rho \).  Second, the spend nullifier \( nf =
   \mathrm{Poseidon}_3(\rho, idHash, tag) \) is a PRF output over secret material,
   so it is unlinkable to any =cm= without the opening.

** Per-flavor data flow

*** B1 -- bearer, public issuer

    - *Mint.*  The issuer is a registered =isPublicIdentity= account, so its
      \( M_{iss} \) is attested in the clear off chain.  On chain: opaque =cms[]=,
      =Minted=, and one Schnorr over =keccak(cms)= (=IssuerBound=).  Mallory sees
      "public account \( A \) escrowed =totalFace= into \( N \) opaque leaves and
      signed the batch."  No per-leaf value, recipient, or even count of /live/
      vs padding leaves.
    - *Off-chain delivery.*  The bearer note carries the opening plus
      \( (m_{iss}, \sigma_{iss}) \) -- the issuer's public Identity and a
      signature.  Anyone who holds the note learns the issuer (intended: a
      cashier's cheque names its bank).
    - *Spend.*  The depositor publishes \( nf \), =face=, =recipient=.  Optionally
      (the 8-arg path) =SpentB= additionally carries \( E_{dep\text{-}for\text{-}iss} \), a
      re-encryption of the /depositor's/ registered \( M_{dep} \) under the public
      issuer's \( pk_{iss} \), with a CP proof -- so the issuer can later name who
      cashed the note.
    - *Final holdings.*  Recipient holds \( M_{iss} \) in the clear (names the
      issuer).  Issuer, reading =SpentB=, decrypts \( E_{dep\text{-}for\text{-}iss} \) with
      \( sk_{iss} \) to recover \( M_{dep} \) (names the depositor).  Mutual.

*** A1 -- addressed, public issuer

    - *Mint.*  As B1 (public issuer, Schnorr-bound batch), but the leaf is
      addressed: \( idHash = \mathrm{Poseidon}_8(E_{note}, m_{iss}, \sigma) \) with
      \( E_{note} \) a re-randomisation of the recipient's registered credential.
      Still opaque on chain.
    - *Off-chain delivery.*  The recipient scans candidate notes by
      trial-decrypting \( E_{note} \) with \( sk_{rec} \); a match reveals it is
      theirs.  They learn \( M_{iss} \) from the in-clear payload.
    - *Spend.*  =spendACP= reveals \( E_n \) (the note ciphertext) and a CP-DLEQ
      proving \( sk_{dep} \) decrypts both \( E_n \) and the spender's registered
      \( E_{addr} \) to the same \( M \) -- so =msg.sender= /is/ the addressed
      recipient.  Publishes =SpentA(nf,face,recipient)=.
    - *Final holdings.*  Recipient names the issuer (public \( M_{iss} \) +
      Schnorr); issuer knew the recipient at mint (they chose \( pk_{rec} \)).
      Mutual.

*** A2 -- addressed, private issuer (the hard case)

    Both parties are encrypted-Identity; this is where every published byte is
    scrutinised.

    - *Mint.*  =Notes.mint(..., A2Binding[])=.  For each A2 leaf the issuer
      publishes \( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} = (R_i, C_i) = (r'G,\ M_{iss} + r' pk_{rec}) \)
      and the recipient-blinded binding =(Q, U, T_hat, A_1..A_5, responses)=,
      verified by =verifyIssuerReenc=.  =IssuerReencBound= anchors the count.
    - *Off-chain delivery.*  The issuer sends the opening, \( E_{note} \),
      \( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} \), the binding, and the un-blinding scalar \( \gamma \).
      The recipient scans \( E_{note} \), then decrypts \( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} \) with
      \( sk_{rec} \) to recover \( M_{iss} \).
    - *Spend.*  =spendACP= exactly as A1 (reveals \( E_n \), CP-DLEQ, =SpentA=).
    - *Final holdings.*  Recipient holds \( M_{iss} \) (decrypted) /and/ the
      binding + \( \gamma \), which together prove it is the issuer's /registered/
      Identity (below).  The issuer, having addressed the note, knows
      \( M_{rec} \).  Mutual.

** Why bulk-tracing and third-party privacy hold

   *Mint is opaque; spend is unlinkable to mint.*  At mint Mallory sees opaque
   =cms[]= and a =totalFace=; she cannot read any \( v_i \), recipient, or flavor
   (Poseidon hiding).  At spend she sees a fresh \( nf \), a =face=, and a
   =recipient=; \( nf \) is a PRF over \( (\rho, idHash) \), so inverting it to a
   specific =cm= is pre-image resistance.  No field of a spend points back to a
   mint.  The only residual linkage surfaces are the two that /must/ be public for
   the ERC-20 to pay out -- =face= and =recipient= -- mitigated by fixed
   denominations and relayed payouts, orthogonal to this document.

   *Identities never appear on chain -- the load-bearing check.*  A registered
   \( E_{addr} \) is an ElGamal /ciphertext/ of \( M \); reading it yields nothing
   without \( sk \).  The danger is in the /bindings/, which manipulate \( M \)
   and \( pk_{rec} \) algebraically.  Concretely, the A2 binding must publish
   enough to prove "\( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} \) re-encrypts the issuer's registered
   \( M_{iss} \) under the recipient's \( pk_{rec} \)" without revealing either
   point.  This is exactly where the first draft failed:

   #+begin_example
   Near-miss (rejected draft).  The binding published T = r' * pk_rec.
   But the leaf is C_i = M_iss + r' * pk_rec, so ANY observer computes
       M_iss = C_i - T.
   At mint msg.sender IS the issuer, so this publicly bound the issuer's
   address to its Identity -- collapsing A2 to A1 and breaking BOTH
   third-party non-disclosure and bulk unlinkability (every A2 mint by the
   same issuer now carries the same recoverable M_iss).
   #+end_example

   The shipped binding blinds it: it publishes \( \hat{T} = r' pk_{rec} + \gamma G \)
   for a fresh \( \gamma \).  Now \( C_i - \hat{T} = M_{iss} - \gamma G \), a
   uniformly random offset from \( M_{iss} \) -- so \( \hat{T} \) reveals nothing
   about \( M_{iss} \).  The recipient's key \( pk_{rec} \) is likewise hidden:
   it appears only inside \( Q = pk_{rec} + \beta H \) (perfectly hiding for
   uniform \( \beta \)) and \( U = r' H \), \( \hat{T} \) (uniform).  Soundness is
   undisturbed because \( \gamma \) cancels in the verification relations (L3, L5):
   the proof still forces \( C_i = M_{iss}^{reg} + r' pk_{rec} \).  The
   commitments \( A_1..A_5 \) are zero-knowledge.  So an A2 mint's on-chain
   footprint is, to Mallory, a vector of uniformly-distributed group elements:
   it discloses neither identity nor recipient, and two A2 mints by the same
   issuer to the same recipient are indistinguishable from independent draws.

   *What issuerMode will and will not leak.*  Closing the residual gaps (below)
   requires the mint SNARK to expose, per leaf, a public \( issuerMode \in
   \{PUBLIC, PRIVATE\} \).  That reveals the /flavor class/ of each leaf -- not its
   value, recipient, or identity.  Flavor-class leakage is acceptable: it is the
   same information =isPublicIdentity(msg.sender)= already exposes for the batch,
   and it does not narrow the identity or amount anonymity sets.  The A2 binding
   values stay blinded regardless.

   *CP-DLEQ at spend reveals the spender, not the issuer.*  =spendACP= publishes
   \( E_n \) and a CP-DLEQ.  \( E_n \) is a re-randomised ciphertext (uniform), and
   the DLEQ binds =msg.sender='s /registered/ key -- i.e. it identifies the
   /spender to themselves/ (they are acting), exactly as an EOA sender is the
   =msg.sender= of their own transfer.  It reveals nothing about the issuer:
   the issuer's \( M_{iss} \) is recovered only off chain, by the recipient
   decrypting \( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} \), and disclosed only in a compelled receipt.

** Why mutual decryptability holds -- the final data set

   For each flavor, list the data each party ends holding and the predicate it
   satisfies.  The receipt verifier (=alberta_buck.wallet.receipt= /
   =verify_receipt=) is the public predicate \( \mathsf{RcptVerify} \).

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Flavor | Recipient names issuer via                                                  | Issuer names depositor via                          |
   |--------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
   | B1     | in-clear \(M_{iss}\) + batch Schnorr (=verifyIssuerSchnorr=)                 | =SpentB=: decrypt \(E_{dep\text{-}for\text{-}iss}\) with \(sk_{iss}\) |
   | A1     | in-clear \(M_{iss}\) + batch Schnorr                                         | chose \(pk_{rec}\) at mint (symmetric knowledge)    |
   | A2     | decrypt \(E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec}\) -> \(M_{iss}\); binding + \(\gamma\) prove it is the *registered* \(M_{iss}\) | chose \(pk_{rec}\) at mint |
   #+LATEX: }

   The A2 row is the subtle one, and the *coupling* is what closes it.  The
   recipient holds three facts: (i) the binding proves \( C_i = M_{iss}^{reg} +
   r' pk_{rec} \) with \( M_{iss}^{reg} \) the issuer's registered Identity (and,
   via \( \gamma \), \( M_{iss}^{reg} = C_i - \hat{T} + \gamma G \), a value the
   receipt verifier recomputes); (ii) their verifiable decryption proves
   \( M_{named} = C_i - sk_{rec} R_i = C_i - r' pk_{rec} \); (iii) both equal the
   named point \( M \).  Subtracting, \( r' pk_{rec} = r' pk_{rec} \) forces
   \( pk_{rec} = \) the key in \( Q \) -- the /E_note <-> Q coupling/ -- so the
   recovered \( M \) is provably (a) the issuer's registered Identity and (b)
   addressed to /this/ recipient.  No new proof was needed: the coupling falls
   out of composing the mint binding with the recipient's own decryption.  This
   is why the A2 receipt now reads =VALID= rather than =UNVERIFIED ISSUER=.

** The colluding pair, and why enforcement must be at mint

   Completeness (an honest recipient /can/ produce a receipt) is met off chain.
   The third clause -- no deniability even when /payer and payee collude/ --
   needs the binding to be /unavoidable/: there must be no way to create a
   spendable leaf lacking it.  Off-chain verification cannot give this (a
   colluding recipient simply accepts a binding-less note), so the check must be
   enforced on chain at mint.  =verifyIssuerReenc= is called from =Notes.mint=,
   but it verifies the bindings the issuer /supplies/.  Phase 2c's leaf-tie now
   (a) ties each \( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} \) to a specific committed leaf and (b) requires
   /every/ A2 leaf to carry one -- the SNARK exposes per-leaf =eIss=, which
   =Notes.mint= passes as the proof's public input.  This upgrades the A2
   guarantee from "sound for an honest issuer" to "no leaf unbound, no binding
   floating."  It does *not* reach full collusion-resistance: the binding does
   not force =eIss='s key to be the recipient's, so a colluding pair can still
   encrypt =eIss= under a throwaway key (/The A2 recipient-key coupling gap/,
   above).  Closing that needs the in-SNARK =E_note= \(\leftrightarrow\) =eIss=
   coupling, the recommended next phase.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Required Mint SNARK Signal

  Three of the residual gaps (bearer-from-non-public, the B-spend issuer tie, and
  A2 leaf-binding + completeness) converge on one circuit change: the batch-mint
  circuit must expose, per leaf, a verified public =issuerMode in {PUBLIC,
  PRIVATE}= and the leaf's issuer payload, so =Notes.mint= can gate on them.  The
  =issuerMode= half is now *shipped* (see below and /Implementation Status/); the
  per-leaf issuer-payload exposure is the remaining increment on the same
  foundation.

** What remains, and the decisions that shaped it

   With =issuerMode= shipped, two items from the original convergence list
   remain.  This session's design pass *changed how both are done*, under one
   governing constraint: on-chain EVM cost is paramount, while off-chain wallet
   compute is effectively free (the wallet has full chain history, Holochain
   bulk-data access, and ample CPU; expensive checks before-mint and on-receipt-
   before-deposit are fine).

   - *Per-leaf =issuerMode=* (public output) -- *SHIPPED* (=b14e0f0=, =584bb52=,
     =68e5b55=).  =mint_batch.circom= range-constrains =flavor \in {A1,A2,B1}= and
     emits =issuerMode[i]= as the deterministic projection (A2 \to PRIVATE,
     \( \{A1,B1\} \to \) PUBLIC); the regenerated per-N Groth16 verifiers /bind/
     it (public-signal vector =[issuerMode[0..N), oldRoot, newRoot,
     nextLeafIndex, totalFace, cm[0..N)]=, arity =2N+4=).  =Notes.mint= gates:
     every =PUBLIC= leaf needs =msg.sender= =isPublicIdentity= + a Schnorr over
     =keccak(cms)=; every =PRIVATE= leaf needs an A2 binding.  Because a bearer
     leaf always projects to =PUBLIC= and there is no B2 codepoint, a
     bearer-from-non-public mint is unprovable -- the on-chain enforcement of
     /Why B2 is impossible/.

   - *Public-leaf identity binding -- decided: OFF-CHAIN, no circuit/contract
     change.*  The original plan constrained the leaf's committed \( m_{iss} \)
     to equal the minter's registered \( M \) in-circuit.  Dropped: a public
     issuer is already named soundly by =msg.sender= + the Schnorr batch binding,
     and the wallet finds the mint tx from full history (search =Notes.mint=
     calldata for the =cm=) \to =msg.sender= \to registry \( M \).  The committed
     \( m_{iss} \) is redundant for naming, so the receipt names =msg.sender='s
     \( M \) and ignores the committed scalar; there is nothing to enforce on
     chain.  Bonus: *not* exposing \( m_{iss} \) denies a bulk observer a shared
     scalar by which to group one public issuer's notes.

   - *A2 leaf <-> binding tie -- decided: RAW exposure via a DEDICATED A2 mint
     circuit (SHIPPED, Phase 2c).*  Expose each A2 leaf's
     \( E_{iss\text{-}for\text{-}rec} = (R_i, C_i) \) as four public outputs and have
     =Notes.mint= match them against the =eIss= in each binding.  /How the match
     is realised/ (refined in implementation): rather than a contract-side
     field-compare, =Notes.mint= passes the bindings' =eIss= /as/ the A2 proof's
     public input, so the Groth16 accept itself enforces "committed leaf's =eIss=
     == binding's =eIss=" -- the equality is a SNARK constraint, not EVM code.  The
     only contract-side =eIss= check is the canonical =< FIELD_R= bound (the
     verifier rejects public inputs \( \ge \) the scalar field anyway).  Two
     sub-decisions stand:
     1. *Raw, not hash-bound.*  A SNARK-friendly digest (Poseidon) is
        EVM-hostile: matching =H(binding.eIss) == tag[i]= would force the
        contract to recompute Poseidon /on chain/ (~tens of K gas/leaf, plus a
        hashing dependency =Notes= does not have today).  Raw =eIss= + a field
        compare is cheaper and simpler, and =eIss= is already on chain in the
        binding calldata, so raw exposure leaks nothing new.  (A SNARK-friendly
        hash is cheap in-circuit but dear on-chain; an EVM-friendly hash
        (keccak) is the reverse -- there is no hash cheap on both sides, so for a
        value both must agree on, expose it raw.)
     2. *A dedicated circuit, not the shared one.*  A Groth16 circuit has fixed
        public-input arity, so adding =eIss= to =mint_batch= would make /every/
        public/bearer batch pay for =4N= all-zero inputs.  Instead
        =circuits/mint_batch_a2.circom= is a separate family used only by the
        private-mode mint: it constrains =flavor == A2=, opens =idHash= to
        =Poseidon8(eNote, eIss)= (the V2 A2 layout), and exposes =eIss=
        (layout =[eIss[0..N)(4 each), oldRoot, newRoot, nextLeafIndex, totalFace,
        cm[0..N)]=, arity =5N+4=).  Public/bearer batches keep using =mint_batch=
        unchanged and pay nothing for the A2 machinery.

** Why this preserves privacy

   In =mint_batch_a2=, =eNote= (the note ciphertext the A-spend later reveals)
   stays a PRIVATE witness, so mint<->spend remain unlinkable; only =eIss= --
   already public in the A2 binding -- is exposed.  =issuerMode= leaks only
   flavor-class.  Amounts and identities stay hidden; value-conservation and the
   Merkle relations are unchanged.

** What the A2 tie buys, and what it does not

   The count-only check =Notes.mint= shipped before Phase 2c verifies one binding
   /per private leaf/ by *count*, so a willing issuer+recipient pair can submit
   \( N \) copies of one binding while committing \( N \) different =eIss= -- only
   the matching leaf is bound, the rest carry committed =eIss= with no verified
   binding at all.  The leaf-tie closes exactly this: the bindings' =eIss= /are/
   the A2 circuit's public inputs, so a Groth16 accept proves each committed leaf
   carries its own verified binding -- no leaf unbound, no binding floating to a
   different leaf.  It /must/ be on-chain -- an off-chain receipt check is
   skippable by a colluding recipient.

   *What it does NOT buy: the recipient-key coupling.*  The leaf-tie binds each
   committed leaf to a verified re-encryption of the issuer's /registered/
   Identity -- but =verifyIssuerReenc= binds =eIss= to the key committed in the
   proof's \( Q \), which the *issuer* chooses (it is /not/ proven to be the
   addressed recipient's registered key; see the verifier's own docstring and
   the /coupling/ note in =issuer_reenc=).  A colluding issuer+recipient can
   therefore still defeat naming: address =E_note= to the recipient's /real/ key
   (so the note is spendable) while encrypting =eIss= under a /throwaway/ key.
   Every leaf then carries a valid binding (the leaf-tie and =verifyIssuerReenc=
   both pass), the recipient spends, yet under compulsion their \( sk_{rec} \)
   decrypts =eIss= to a meaningless point -- no receipt names the issuer.  This
   is a genuine residual hole in the invariant's third (collusion) clause, *not*
   closed by Phase 2c.  See /The A2 recipient-key coupling gap/.

** The A2 recipient-key coupling gap

   The leaf-tie and the count check both concern /which committed leaf a binding
   answers to/.  The remaining gap is orthogonal: /whether the key =eIss= is
   encrypted under is the addressed recipient's/.  Nothing on chain forces it.

   #+begin_example
   Attack (collusion, survives Phase 2c).  Issuer I and recipient D collude.
     - E_note := re-randomisation of D's REGISTERED credential  (D can spend)
     - eIss   := ElGamal(M_I^reg, pk_bogus)  for a key D does NOT hold
     - idHash := Poseidon8(E_note, eIss);  cm := Poseidon5(A2, v, rho, idHash, 0)
   mint_batch_a2 proves flavor=A2, idHash opening, exposes eIss; Notes.mint
   field-ties eIss and verifyIssuerReenc accepts (eIss DOES re-encrypt M_I^reg,
   just under pk_bogus -- the issuer need not know pk_bogus's discrete log).
   D spends via spendACP (E_note under D's real key).  Compelled, D decrypts
   eIss with sk_D -> garbage.  No receipt names I.  Untraceable A2 payment.
   #+end_example

   The structural cause: =E_note= and =eIss= are /independent/ ciphertexts, both
   folded only into =idHash=; the recipient's key enters the system at *spend*
   (the =spendACP= CP-DLEQ ties =E_note= to the spender's registered key) but
   =eIss='s key is never tied to =E_note='s.  The honest-recipient /coupling/ in
   the receipt verifier (=verify_receipt=, note-a2) recovers \( M_{iss} \) and
   proves \( pk_{rec} = Q\text{'s key} \) -- but that is /completeness/ (an honest
   D /can/ name I); it does nothing against a D who simply declines, which is the
   collusion clause.

   *The fix is the in-SNARK =E_note= \(\leftrightarrow\) =eIss= key-coupling.*
   =E_note= cannot be exposed on chain (it is revealed at spend, so exposing it
   at mint would link mint to spend), so the coupling must live where =E_note= is
   already a private witness: inside =mint_batch_a2=.  Add to each A2 leaf a
   witness \( (sk_{iss}, pk_{rec}, r', r_n) \) with the issuer's registered
   \( (R^{reg}_{iss}, C^{reg}_{iss}) \) a public input (the issuer is
   =msg.sender=, so their credential is public), and constrain
   \[
     pk_{iss} = sk_{iss}G,\quad M_{iss}=C^{reg}_{iss}-sk_{iss}R^{reg}_{iss},\quad
     E_{iss}=(r'G,\ M_{iss}+r'pk_{rec}),\quad
     E_{note}=(r_nG,\ M_{rec}+r_n pk_{rec}),
   \]
   the /same/ \( pk_{rec} \) in both.

   *Correction (found while scoping the phase): the shared-key constraint above is
   NOT sufficient by itself -- it is vacuous.*  \( M_{rec} \) (and, with it, the
   recipient credential) is a /free/ witness, so for a bogus note (\( eIss \) under
   \( pk_{bogus} \), \( E_{note} \) really under the recipient's \( pk_D \)) the
   issuer witnesses \( pk_{rec} = pk_{bogus} \) and \( M_{rec} = M_D + r_n(pk_D -
   pk_{bogus}) \): both equations hold, the circuit accepts, the note still spends.
   The free recipient-side witness absorbs the constraint.  A /sound/ coupling must
   *pin \( pk_{rec} \) to a real registered key*, which forces an in-circuit
   *registry set-membership*:

   1. witness the recipient's registered \( (pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D]) \) + a Merkle
      path proving the leaf \( \mathrm{Poseidon}(pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D]) \) is in
      the IdentityRegistry accumulator (root a public input) -- so the credential
      is /real/, not invented;
   2. constrain \( E_{note} = \mathrm{rerand}(E_{\text{addr}}[D]) \) under
      \( pk_D \) (so \( E_{note} \) is genuinely under that registered key -- and
      hence spendable only by D, which =verifySpendCP= re-confirms at spend);
   3. constrain \( eIss = (r'G,\ M_{iss} + r' pk_D) \) under the /same/ \( pk_D \).

   Now the spender is provably the holder of the key \( eIss \) is encrypted under,
   so a compelled \( sk_D \) /must/ recover \( M_{iss} \).  The membership proof
   reveals only that the recipient is /some/ registered account (the leaf is a
   private witness; only the root is public), so A2 recipient privacy survives.

   *This is materially heavier than "a handful of G1 muls".*  The identities live
   on BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) -- the proof system's own curve -- so every point op
   (the rerandomisation, the two encryptions, \( sk_{iss}R_{iss} \)) is /non-native/
   \( \mathbb{F}_p \) arithmetic emulated in an \( \mathbb{F}_r \) circuit
   (circom-pairing / circom-ecdsa style), realistically \( \sim\!10^6 \) R1CS *per
   A2 leaf*, plus the new registry accumulator.  This is the recommended next phase
   for A2 -- /not/ shipped; Phase 2c deliberately stops at the leaf-tie (cheap, and
   a strict prerequisite: the coupling needs the per-leaf =eIss= exposure it adds).
   It cannot be moved on-chain: =E_note= is never on chain at mint (exposing it
   links mint to spend), so the one venue where =E_note=, =eIss=, and \( pk_{rec} \)
   coexist privately is inside =mint_batch_a2=.  See /Scope: the A2 coupling phase/.

*** Can it be closed now?  The non-native-curve obstacle

    The cost line above ("a handful of \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) scalar-muls") understates
    the real obstacle, which is /which curve those muls are on/.  The identities
    live on *BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)* -- the same curve the Groth16 proof system
    is built over.  A circom circuit's native field is BN254's /scalar/ field
    \( r \); BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) coordinates live in the /base/ field
    \( p \neq r \).  So every \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) operation in the coupling
    (\( sk_{iss}R_{iss} \), \( r'pk_{rec} \), \( r_n pk_{rec} \), the point adds)
    is /non-native/ \( \mathbb{F}_p \) arithmetic emulated inside an
    \( \mathbb{F}_r \) circuit -- limb decomposition + =mulmod= with range checks,
    the circom-pairing / circom-ecdsa style.  circomlib's EC gadgets are
    *BabyJubjub* (whose base field /is/ \( r \), hence native and cheap), not
    BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \); there is no drop-in.  Each emulated \( \mathbb{F}_p \)
    multiply is thousands of \( \mathbb{F}_r \) constraints, so the coupling is
    realistically \( 10^5\!-\!10^6 \) R1CS /per A2 leaf/ -- pushing =mint_batch_a2=
    from ~170K total to millions, a larger ptau (\( 2^{22+} \)), and minute-to-hour
    prove times.  This is exactly the native-\( \mathbb{G}_1 \)-in-circom cost the
    spend side already declined; it is a *dedicated multi-week phase*, not a now-fix.

    The escapes all hit a wall:
    - /Move identities to BabyJubjub/ (native in-circuit EC): a ground-up redesign
      of the identity layer -- the PS credential (a pairing), ElGamal, the
      registration NIZK, and the on-chain CP-DLEQ / Schnorr / re-enc verifiers all
      depend on BN254 + the EIP-196/197 precompiles, which BabyJubjub lacks.
    - /On-chain sigma coupling/ (cheap via EIP-196, like =verifyIssuerReenc=):
      needs =E_note= as an input, but =E_note= is the spend ciphertext -- putting it
      (or a deterministic commitment to it) on chain at mint re-links mint to spend,
      the precise unlinkability we protect.
    - /Spend-side enforcement/ (prove at =spendACP= that \( sk_{rec} \) decrypts
      =eIss= to the issuer's registered \( M \)): same non-native \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)
      in the /spend/ circuit, and it needs \( \gamma \) (disclosed only in the
      compelled receipt) to know the target point -- revealing it at spend would
      de-anonymise the issuer publicly.

*** The backstop: the gap degrades to KYC-traceability, not untraceability

    The colluding A2 pair does /not/ obtain untraceable money.  Both parties act
    under on-chain addresses -- the issuer is =msg.sender= at mint (and the funds
    pulled trace to them), the recipient is =msg.sender= at =spendACP= -- and each
    address is a /registered/ Identity whose KYC issuer (the trusted PS-credential
    signer) can de-anonymise it under subpoena.  So the bogus-=eIss= attack costs
    the pair only the *bilateral, self-service receipt* (the recipient can no longer
    /unilaterally/ name the payer); it does /not/ defeat *KYC traceability* (a court
    compelling the registry's trusted issuer still names both).  The receipt
    invariant is the stronger, third-party-free guarantee; the gap means A2
    /collusion/ falls back to the weaker subpoena-the-KYC-issuer path that every
    registered Identity is subject to anyway.  Whether to spend a multi-week
    non-native-EC phase to restore the stronger guarantee for the A2-collusion
    corner, or to accept the KYC backstop there, is a threat-model decision -- it is
    *not* a silent hole: =verify_receipt= stamps such a note =UNVERIFIED ISSUER=.

*** Scope: the A2 coupling phase

    The sound construction (set-membership + the three constraint groups above) is
    a *major cryptographic-engineering phase*, not an increment.  Workstreams, in
    dependency order:

    1. *Registry accumulator.*  =IdentityRegistry= gains an incremental Poseidon
       Merkle tree of registered entries (leaf =Poseidon(addr, pk, E_addr)=),
       maintained on =register= / =bindContract=, with an on-chain root the mint
       circuit consumes.  Decision: on-chain Poseidon (gas on each registration --
       rare, tolerable) vs an off-chain-computed root checked by a small SNARK.
       Mirrors the Notes pool's tree but over the identity set.
    2. *Non-native BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) gadgets.*  Integrate a circom bigint /
       EC library (circom-pairing / circom-ecdsa lineage) for \( \mathbb{F}_p \)
       arithmetic + variable-base scalar-mul + point-add over BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)
       inside the \( \mathbb{F}_r \) circuit.  This is the dominant cost and the
       dominant *audit* surface (under-constraint in emulated EC is the classic
       footgun -- Picus/ecne the sub-templates).  Handle the \( \bmod r \) vs
       \( \bmod p \) coordinate canonicalisation (the same care =eIss < FIELD_R=
       already takes, now load-bearing).
    3. *Circuit =mint_batch_a2_coupled=.*  Extend =mint_batch_a2= per leaf with the
       Merkle-membership + rerandomisation + dual-encryption constraints; new
       public inputs (=registryRoot=, the issuer's registered \( E^{reg} \)).  The
       =eIss= leaf-tie and Merkle insertion stay.
    4. *Ceremony + sizing.*  At \( \sim\!10^6 \) R1CS/leaf the practical batch sizes
       *shrink* (N=1,2,4; N=16/32 would need =pot25+=, a multi-day, hundreds-of-GB
       ceremony).  Expect a separate small-N pin set and a fresh, larger ptau.
    5. *Verifier + Notes wiring.*  New per-N =MintBatchA2CoupledN*Verifier= +
       adapter (arity grows by the new public inputs); =Notes= reads the registry
       root and routes the A2 path to the coupled verifier.
    6. *Wallet prover + tests.*  The prover supplies the recipient credential +
       Merkle path + rerand/encryption witnesses (real ciphertexts now, not
       arbitrary words); a *collusion regression* (=eIss= under a non-recipient key
       now *reverts at mint*) and a *privacy* test (two mints to distinct recipients
       indistinguishable).  =verify_receipt= drops the =UNVERIFIED ISSUER= path for
       coupled mints.

    *Cost / risk envelope.*  Dominant: non-native EC -- \( \sim\!10^6 \) R1CS/leaf,
    minute-scale proves, big ceremony, heavy audit.  Plus a new registry-accumulator
    subsystem.  Realistic effort: a /design spike/ (accumulator shape; pick/port the
    EC library; pin the exact relation) -> an /N=1 proof-of-concept/ (witness-check
    the collusion + privacy properties end to end) before committing to the per-N
    ceremony + wiring + audit.  A backend with native non-native support (Halo2 /
    PLONK lookups, or a 2-cycle like BN254/Grumpkin for native in-circuit EC) would
    cut the EC cost dramatically but is itself a proving-stack migration.

    *Where the cost lands -- and a substrate that absorbs it.*  The
    \( \sim\!10^6 \) R1CS is a /prover/ cost; the on-chain Groth16
    /verification/ is constant (~250K gas) whatever the circuit size, so EVM
    cost was never the obstacle.  Since the wallet proves off chain, a
    free-compute substrate -- /Holochain/, which already holds the wallet's
    bulk data -- absorbs it: the agent hosts the registry tree (only the
    /root/ need be on-chain), generates the non-native-EC proof at leisure,
    and the EVM does one constant-gas verify.  Per-leaf proving with
    recursive aggregation (one verify for many leaves) sidesteps the
    hundreds-of-GB batch ceremony and restores the larger \( N \) the line
    above gives up.  The envelope thus shifts: the /prover-feasibility/
    objection (minute-scale proves, giant ceremony) largely dissolves on
    such a substrate, leaving the proving-stack choice (thread 2) and -- the
    true dominant residual -- the *audit*, which free compute does nothing
    for.

    *Recommendation.*  Weigh this against the KYC backstop (above) before starting:
    it restores /bilateral, self-service/ non-deniability for the A2-collusion
    corner only, at the price of a major phase and shrunken A2 batch sizes.  If
    greenlit, gate it on the spike + PoC; do not begin the ceremony/wiring/audit
    until the N=1 PoC confirms the relation is sound and the costs are as modelled.

*** The identity-targeted alternative: name the identity, not the account

    The account-targeted coupling above is hard because it fights the grain of
    the system.  A direct EOA transfer /deduces/ the identities from the two
    accounts in play -- the account is primary, \( M \) is derived.  A Note is
    the opposite: it /names/ the identities and treats the accounts as
    incidental envelopes, required only to /match/ the expected \( M \).  The
    account-targeted construction re-imports the EOA model (pin a specific
    registered /account/ \( pk_D \)) and pays for it in non-native account-leaf
    membership.  Targeting the /identity/ directly is the lighter problem, and
    it is the one that fits the spirit of Notes.

    *The shift.*  Encrypt the issuer payload under the recipient's identity
    /point/ rather than an account key:
    \[
      eIss \;=\; (R_e,\ C_e) \;=\; \bigl(r\,G,\ M_I + r\,M_{\text{rec}}\bigr),
      \qquad M_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{rec}} G,
    \]
    where \( m_{\text{rec}} = H(\text{id}_{\text{rec}}) \) is the identity scalar
    that /every/ Fountain account of the recipient shares.  The decryption
    secret is therefore \( m_{\text{rec}} \), not any account key: the issuer
    needs only \( M_{\text{rec}} \) (which the recipient revealed off chain), and
    /any/ account the depositor later uses can open \( eIss \).  This is exactly
    "the spender knows the recipient's \( M \), not which account will deposit."

    *Anti-framing stays at mint (shipped).*  =verifyIssuerReenc= already forces
    \( eIss \) to re-encrypt =msg.sender='s /own/ registered \( M_I \) under the
    committed point, so the decrypted issuer is always the true minter, never a
    substituted victim.

    *The coupling moves to spend, as a registry-identity membership.*  Maintain
    a registry accumulator of registered identity /points/ \( M \) with public
    root \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \).  The depositor's spend circuit proves, with
    private witnesses \( (m_{\text{rec}}, M_I, \pi) \) and the committed
    \( eIss = (R_e, C_e) \):
    \[
    \begin{aligned}
      &\textbf{(N1)} & M_{\text{rec}} &= m_{\text{rec}}\,G
        &&\text{— I hold the addressed identity's scalar;}\\
      &\textbf{(N2)} & idHash &= \mathrm{Poseidon}(M_{\text{rec}},\, eIss,\, \ldots)
        &&\text{— } M_{\text{rec}} \text{ is \emph{this} note's target;}\\
      &\textbf{(N3)} & M_I &= C_e - m_{\text{rec}}\,R_e
        &&\text{— decrypt the issuer's identity;}\\
      &\textbf{(N4)} & 1 &= \mathsf{MerkleVerify}(\mathsf{rt_{id}},\, \pi,\, M_I)
        &&\text{— the issuer is a registered identity.}
    \end{aligned}
    \]
    The receiving account is separately tied to \( M_{\text{rec}} \) by the
    existing on-chain =verifySpendCP= (so the KYC-identifiable payout vehicle
    matches the named identity).

    *Why it closes the gap.*  (N3)+(N4) are /mandatory/ to spend.  A colluding
    issuer who keys \( eIss \) to anything but \( M_{\text{rec}} \) makes (N3)
    decrypt to a non-member, (N4) fails, and the *spend reverts* -- there is no
    spendable-but-unnameable note.  (N1)+(N2) stop the depositor cheating by
    feeding a convenient \( m_{\text{rec}} \): it must be the scalar of /this
    note's committed target/.  Everything is in zero knowledge, so \( M_I \) and
    \( M_{\text{rec}} \) never appear on chain -- this /overcomes the earlier
    spend-side objection/ (/Can it be closed now?/), which assumed \( M_I \) had
    to be revealed; a membership proof of the decrypted point reveals nothing.

    *The receipt is unilaterally recipient-produceable.*  The depositor holds the
    one secret \( m_{\text{rec}} \) that names /both/ parties: their own identity
    \( M_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{rec}} G \) trivially, and the issuer's
    \( M_I = C_e - m_{\text{rec}} R_e \) by decryption.  So even "in a pinch" --
    with no issuer participation at spend -- the recipient can produce a
    plaintext receipt naming both, third-party-checkable against
    \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) and the mint =verifyIssuerReenc=.  This is the
    /recipient-burden/ posture made cryptographically complete rather than merely
    economic: the always-KYC-identifiable party holds the entire bilateral
    receipt, and the mandatory (N3)+(N4) make producing it a /precondition of
    payment/.

    *Why it is the easier problem.*  The expensive account-set-membership over
    full account leaves \( \mathrm{Poseidon}(pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D]) \) is
    replaced by a /native/ Poseidon-Merkle membership of a single point \( M_I \);
    the non-native EC collapses to two scalar-muls (\( m_{\text{rec}} G \) and
    \( m_{\text{rec}} R_e \)) against the account-targeted construction's five-odd
    (a rerandomisation plus two full encryptions plus \( sk_I R_I \)).  A further
    split is open: prove (N1),(N3) as an /on-chain blinded sigma/ (EIP-196, the
    =verifyIssuerReenc= shape, values Pedersen-committed and hidden) and leave
    /only/ the native membership (N4) to a small SNARK -- removing non-native EC
    from the circuit entirely.  Residuals are honest: the depositor's
    own-identity tie (fold into =verifySpendCP= vs. an in-circuit check), the
    identity accumulator's root maintenance (Holochain holds the tree; only the
    root is on-chain), and a full cost + audit.  It is a *promising variant to
    PoC*, not a shipped result -- but it is materially lighter than the
    account-targeted phase and matches what a Note /is/.

** Cost and migration

   The added constraints are a handful of equality checks plus the =issuerMode=
   range/flavor gate per leaf -- cheap relative to the dual Merkle walk already
   dominating the circuit (measured: +6 R1CS/leaf for the =issuerMode= gate).
   Any circuit change re-runs the per-N trusted setup and redeploys the per-N
   verifiers, rotated in via =Notes.setMintVerifier= behind a governance cutover
   root (the migration story in /Phasing/).  The =issuerMode= regen was carried
   out this way (reusing the existing ptau, regenerating
   =MintBatchN{1,2,4,8,16,32}Groth16Verifier=).  The A2 =eIss= leaf-tie does not
   touch =mint_batch= at all -- it ships as the *separate* =mint_batch_a2= family
   (its own per-N verifiers + ceremony), so the existing public/bearer verifiers
   stay byte-for-byte as deployed.  The public-leaf \( M \) tie needs no circuit
   (off-chain, above).  The relation the tie enforces is already shipped as the
   off-chain =verifyIssuerReenc= binding; =mint_batch_a2= only *exposes* =eIss=
   so =Notes.mint= can field-match it.

* The EOA Transfer Mirror: Anonymity Is the Cost

  A standard private->private EOA =transfer= achieves the /same/
  mutual-decryptability invariant with *no SNARK* and *free* collusion-resistance.
  Splitting that transfer into =Notes.mint= -> =Notes.spend= through an A2 note is
  what makes it expensive.  Mapping the cryptographic steps of the two flows
  isolates exactly /why/, exactly /what it buys/, and -- decisively -- /where the
  cost comes from/.  The conclusion reframes the A2 coupling phase (/The A2
  recipient-key coupling gap/, /Scope/) from "necessary work" to "an optional
  premium", and sets the shipping priority: *public-issuer notes (A1/B1) now, A2
  reserved as a future option.*

** The A2 Note flow (Issuer I -> Recipient D)

   Both parties are encrypted-Identity; the flow /is/ anonymous (mint and spend are
   unlinkable).  The issuer is present and named at mint; the recipient is present
   and named at spend; they are never co-present.

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Step          | Cryptographic step                                                                                                                    | Who is named                                                            | Guarantee                                                       |
   |---------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
   | A1 =register= | identical to E1                                                                                                                       | --                                                                      | identity \(\leftrightarrow\) KYC                                |
   | A2 =mint=     | =issuer_reenc=: \(eIss=\mathrm{Enc}(M_I,pk_D)\) + CP \(eIss\equiv E_{\text{addr}}[I]\) -- but \(pk_D\) *blinded* (\(Q=pk_D+\beta H\)) | *issuer only* (reads \(E_{\text{addr}}[I], pk_I\); \(pk_D\) /not/ read) | sound on the /issuer/ side, but bound to an issuer-*chosen* key |
   | A3 =spend=    | =verifySpendCP=: \(sk_D\) decrypts \(E_n\) & \(E_{\text{addr}}[D]\) to the same \(M_D\)                                               | *recipient only* (reads \(E_{\text{addr}}[D], pk_D\))                   | the spender provably /is/ D                                     |
   | A4 receipt    | =verifiable_decrypt= \(eIss\) -> \(M_I\) + the \(\gamma\) coupling                                                                    | --                                                                      | names I /iff/ \(eIss\) was under D's real key                   |
   #+LATEX: }

** The mapping

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | EOA step                                | A2 step                                                  | Same relation?     | The difference                                                   |
   |-----------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+--------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------|
   | E2 Alice->Bob approve (=verifyApprove=) | A2 mint =issuer_reenc=                                   | *yes*              | \(pk_D\) *blinded* vs \(pk_B\) *named* -- /the whole gap/        |
   | E2 Bob->Alice approve (sound, named)    | A2: nothing sound -- D's address revealed at spend + KYC | *no*               | A2's issuer-names-recipient is address+KYC, not a crypto receipt |
   | E3 transfer (Alice<->Bob *linked*)      | A2 mint->spend (*unlinked*)                              | *no*               | A2 adds bulk-surveillance resistance -- /the source of all cost/ |
   | E4 =verifiable_decrypt=                 | A4 =verifiable_decrypt= + \(\gamma\) coupling            | *yes* (same prim.) | A2 adds the blinding-undo                                        |
   #+LATEX: }

   The first row is the whole story: =issuer_reenc= /is/ =verifyApprove= with the
   recipient's key blinded.  The CP relation, =verifiable_decrypt=, and the receipt
   are identical; the one change -- blinding \(pk_D\) -- is the entire gap.

** The structural diagnosis: the two anchors are never co-present

   =_verifyApprove= is cheap /and/ collusion-resistant because it reads *both*
   parties' registered records in one call (=_E_addr[sender]=, =_pk[sender]=,
   =_pk[spender]=) -- both anchors co-present, both named.  In A2 the anchors are
   /split in time by anonymity/:

   - the *issuer's* anchor \(E_{\text{addr}}[I]\) exists only at *mint* (I = =msg.sender=);
   - the *recipient's* anchor \(pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D]\) exists only at *spend* (D = =msg.sender=).

   No on-chain check can read both at once the way =verifyApprove= does, so
   =issuer_reenc= /blinds/ \(pk_D\) to bridge them -- and blinding \(pk_D\) is
   exactly what drops the "under the recipient's real key" guarantee.  The
   set-membership SNARK (/Scope/) is the *only* venue that re-unites the anchors
   (\(E_{\text{addr}}[I]\) a public input at mint, \(pk_D\) a membership-proven
   witness).  /That separation, and the cost of bridging it, is the whole reason A2
   is hard./

** The finding: the cost is the anonymity

   EOA's mutual decryptability needs no SNARK and is collusion-resistant /for free/
   precisely because the transfer is *not anonymous* -- both parties are named at
   one moment.  A2's entire extra apparatus (the mint SNARK, the \(pk_D\) blinding,
   the coupling gap, and the set-membership phase that would close it) is the price
   of *mint<->spend unlinkability*.  There is no free lunch: cheap collusion-resistant
   mutual decryptability /requires/ naming both parties; anonymity /forbids/ it.
   Three corollaries:

   1. *The cost is the anonymity* (above) -- the irreducible tension.
   2. *A2 is already asymmetric.*  The recipient-names-issuer direction is the
      /only/ one A2 holds to "collusion-resistant, on-chain, self-provable"; the
      issuer-names-recipient direction is merely "D's registered address is on
      chain at spend + KYC" -- the /same/ KYC backstop, not collusion-resistant.
      The coupling phase would upgrade one direction to a bar the other does not
      meet.
   3. *B-notes already show the cheap path.*  =verifyDepositorForIssuer= is
      /literally/ =verifyApprove=, cheap and collusion-resistant for B-notes
      *because the issuer is public (named)*.  So A2's hardness is specifically
      /issuer anonymity/.  The system already offers the cheap, fully-sound option:
      mint as a *public issuer (A1/B1)* -> plain =verifyApprove= / Schnorr, already
      shipped.

** The recipient bears the burden -- the load-bearing safeguard for A2

   This is what makes A2 shippable as a future option /without/ the coupling, and
   it sharpens (and lightly corrects) the operator intuition that "depositing an A2
   note exposes the recipient."

   An /honest/ recipient verifies the note *at delivery*, off chain, before
   accepting it: decrypt \(eIss\) with \(sk_D\), and check it yields the issuer's
   *registered* Identity (run the \(\gamma\) coupling).  A bogus note (\(eIss\)
   under a throwaway key, or \(\gamma\) withheld) *fails this check*, so an honest
   recipient *rejects the payment* -- they will not accept value they cannot later
   receipt.  Therefore a recipient who /accepted and deposited/ a note that later
   proves un-nameable (=UNVERIFIED ISSUER= under compulsion) was either *negligent*
   (skipped the delivery check) or *colluding*.  Either way, *the suspicion falls
   on the recipient, not on the (anonymous) issuer.*  The recipient is always
   KYC-identifiable -- their /own/ registered address is =msg.sender= at spend --
   so under audit they must produce a valid issuer-naming receipt or stand as the
   holder of funds from an unaccountable source.

   /Correction to the framing:/ the collusion cannot *falsify* an issuer identity
   (frame an innocent third party).  /Receipt soundness / non-frameability/
   (Theorem above) prevents binding \(eIss\) over a victim's credential -- the
   binding is always over the /actual minter's/ registered record, so the only
   reachable outcomes are "names the true minter" (=VALID=) or "names no one"
   (=UNVERIFIED ISSUER=).  Collusion can *deny / obscure* the issuer (render it
   un-nameable); it cannot *substitute* a false one.  And the flag is not automatic
   on chain -- the spend reveals no \(eIss\) -- it surfaces /at audit or
   compulsion/, when the recipient is asked to account for the deposit.

   The consequence is a clean economic posture: A2 does not give cryptographic
   /bilateral/ non-deniability under collusion, but (a) the recipient is always
   KYC-identifiable; (b) the recipient is /incentivised/ to verify at delivery (the
   honest recipient is fully protected); and (c) accepting an un-nameable A2 note
   is a *self-inflicted AML red flag on the colluding recipient's own KYC'd
   account*.  The collusion risk is /borne by, and self-limiting to, the recipient/
   -- which is why the on-chain coupling can be deferred without leaving an
   unbounded hole.

** Decision: A1/B1 now, A2 reserved

   - *Ship public-issuer notes (A1, B1) as the primary product.*  They already
     carry the full EOA-grade guarantee -- sound, collusion-resistant, on-chain,
     bilateral -- via =verifyIssuerSchnorr= / =verifyDepositorForIssuer= (= the
     =verifyApprove= family).  No SNARK coupling, nothing reserved.
   - *Reserve A2 (private-issuer) notes as a future option*, gated by the
     recipient-burden posture above: an A2 deposit a recipient cannot receipt
     marks /the recipient/.  Wallets MUST verify \(eIss\) at delivery and refuse a
     note that does not resolve to a registered issuer, and SHOULD surface, on the
     A2 receive screen, that accepting an unverifiable A2 note transfers the
     accountability to the recipient.
   - *The set-membership coupling phase (/Scope/) is the premium* that would
     restore EOA-grade /bilateral/ non-deniability to A2-under-collusion -- to be
     undertaken only if a deployment specifically needs no-trusted-third-party
     traceability for private-issuer notes, and only after the design spike + N=1
     PoC.

** Directions for future research

   The open problem, stated cleanly by the mirror: /can collusion-resistant
   mutual decryptability be achieved when one party is anonymous at the binding
   step (the recipient at A2 mint), without in-circuit set-membership?/  The
   structural diagnosis suggests the anchors' time-separation forces ZK
   set-membership, but this is not proven.  Concrete threads:

   1. *Lower bound / impossibility.*  Formalise "the two anchors are never
      co-present on chain" and ask whether /any/ on-chain-only protocol (no SNARK
      over the registry) can bind \(eIss\) to the recipient's registered key while
      hiding which recipient.  A clean impossibility would settle that the SNARK is
      necessary; a loophole would be a cheap fix.
   2. *Proving-stack choice for the set-membership route.*  Non-native BN254
      \(\mathbb{G}_1\) in circom/Groth16 is \(\sim\!10^6\) R1CS/leaf.  A backend
      with native non-native support -- Halo2/PLONK with lookups, or a 2-cycle
      (BN254/Grumpkin) giving /native/ in-circuit EC on the partner curve -- could
      cut this by an order of magnitude.  Evaluate vs the Groth16 toolchain already
      in the repo.  Orthogonally to the /circuit/ cost, the /prover/ cost is
      off-loadable: the proof is generated off chain on the wallet's Holochain
      agent (which already holds the registry tree and has ample compute) and the
      EVM verifies it in constant gas -- so the stack choice and the substrate
      compose, a cheaper stack shrinking the circuit and a free substrate
      absorbing whatever remains.
   3. *Registry accumulator design.*  On-chain incremental Poseidon tree
      (gas on each registration) vs an off-chain-maintained root proven correct by
      a small SNARK; interaction with the existing mapping-based registry.
   4. *Recipient co-participation, and where the binding lives.*  The
      account-targeted coupling (/Scope/) does /not/ actually need the recipient
      online: the issuer witnesses the recipient's /public/ registry record
      \((pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D],\) Merkle path\()\) -- it is addressing the note
      to that account -- and the in-SNARK membership needs no \(sk_D\).  So the
      issuer mints to an /offline/ recipient.  Two richer variants deserve a
      rigorous treatment:
      - */Interactive A2./*  If both parties are co-present off chain (Holochain),
        they run the full bilateral =approve= inside the wallet and prove it at
        mint -- the EOA transfer's /free/ collusion-resistant mutual
        decryptability -- while still depositing later, unlinkably.  This buys
        EOA-grade bilateral non-deniability at the cost of an online recipient
        (fine for self-wormholes and present counterparties).
      - */Identity-targeted, spend-side membership./*  Address the note to the
        recipient's /identity point/ \(M_{\text{rec}}\) rather than a specific
        account, encrypting \(eIss\) under \(M_{\text{rec}}\) (decryptable by the
        identity scalar \(m_{\text{rec}}\) that /every/ Fountain account of the
        recipient shares) -- so the issuer needs only \(M_{\text{rec}}\), never an
        account.  Enforce the coupling at /spend/: the depositor's circuit
        /mandatorily/ proves "\(eIss\), decrypted under the identity \(m\) I
        authenticate as, yields \(M_I\), and \(M_I \in\) the registry-identity
        tree" -- in ZK, so \(M_I\) stays hidden.  A bogus \(eIss\) decrypts to a
        non-member and the spend /reverts/; a colluding recipient cannot both
        spend and decline to name.  This /overcomes the earlier spend-side
        objection/ (/Can it be closed now?/): that objection assumed \(M_I\) had
        to be /revealed/ at spend, but a ZK membership proof of the decrypted
        point reveals nothing.  It needs one non-native scalar-mul (the
        decryption) plus a /native/ Poseidon-Merkle membership against a tree the
        Holochain agent already holds -- plausibly cheaper than the mint-side
        account-targeted coupling, and the recipient's \(m_{\text{rec}}\) is
        present at spend by construction.  Anti-/framing/ still rides on the
        mint-side =verifyIssuerReenc= (it forces \(eIss\) over the issuer's /own/
        registered \(M_I\), so the spend-decrypted member is the true minter,
        never a substituted victim).  Open: a full circuit cost + audit, and
        whether the depositor's own identity proof folds into the existing
        =verifySpendCP= or needs an in-circuit credential check.  Full treatment
        with the spend relation in display math: /The identity-targeted
        alternative: name the identity, not the account/.
   5. *Mechanism design as an alternative to crypto.*  Formalise the
      "recipient bears the burden" safeguard: under what audit probability and
      penalty does the recipient-burden deter A2 collusion as effectively as
      cryptographic prevention?  If the economic argument is tight, it /obviates/
      the coupling phase -- the most leverage for the least engineering.
   6. *Auditor backstop stronger than subpoena.*  Threshold/selective escrow of
      \(\gamma\) (or of \(M_I\)) to a designated regulator key, so A2 collusion is
      detectable by an auditor without public disclosure -- a middle point between
      "bilateral, no third party" and "subpoena the KYC issuer."

   - =Notes.mint= PRIVATE-mode overload now routes to =a2MintVerifier= (new slot +
     =setA2MintVerifier=), passing the bindings' =eIss= as the proof's public
     input: a Groth16 accept /is/ the leaf-tie (the circuit exposes the committed
     leaf's =eIss=, so equality with what =Notes= passes is a SNARK constraint,
     not a contract-side compare -- no on-chain Poseidon, no field loop beyond a
     canonical =eIss < FIELD_R= bound).  Per-leaf =verifyIssuerReenc= still runs;
     =a2Bindings.length == cms.length=.
   - =prove_mint_batch_a2.js= + =gen_mint_fixtures_a2.sh= (a =--eiss= pin so a
     fixture leaf can carry the canonical =issuer_reenc= binding).
   - Tests: =test/MintVerifierA2.t.sol= (cross-artifact parity; tampered =eIss= /
     =cm= fail the pairing check) and =test/NotesA2Tie.t.sol= (the leaf-tie mints
     against the /real/ verifier; the *duplicate-binding* and *floating-binding*
     attacks revert =bad mint proof=).  Forge 420 passed, 1 skipped.

   *What Phase 2c does NOT close:* the recipient-key coupling (/The A2
   recipient-key coupling gap/).  The leaf-tie makes every committed leaf carry a
   verified binding; it does not force that binding's key to be the recipient's.
   Full A2 collusion-resistance is the in-SNARK =E_note=\(\leftrightarrow\)=eIss=
   coupling -- the recommended next phase.

** Decisions / deviations from the spec text above

   - *=verifyDepositorForIssuer= reuses the =verifyApprove= relation verbatim* --
     no new sigma protocol, no new wallet signer, no new vector.  The existing
     =approve= (Alice->Bob) vector *is* a valid depositor(Alice)->issuer(Bob)
     proof.  FS binds (depositor, issuer, chainid); the specific note is tied in
     only by =SpentB= emitting (nullifier, eDepForIss) atomically -- the
     nullifier is NOT in the CP transcript.
   - *=Notes.spend= binds the =recipient= (the beneficiary), not =msg.sender=.*
   - *=mint= is gated-only* -- the two binding overloads (PUBLIC Schnorr, PRIVATE
     A2 leaf-tie) are the only mint paths; the legacy unbound 6-/7-arg overloads
     were removed.  =spend= keeps the optional 5-arg (legacy) / 8-arg (bound B)
     forms: the depositor->issuer binding is a hardening, not load-bearing for the
     receipt (a B1 issuer is already named at mint).
   - The shipped event is =SpentB(nullifier, face, recipient, issuer, eDepForIss)=
     (issuer included).  The depositor *names* =issuer= as a parameter; it is
     *not* recovered from the leaf, because the B-spend circuit does not reveal it.

** Closed since Phase 1

   - *A2 re-encryption binding* -- the central Phase 2 item.  Shipped via the
     blinded-CP route (=verifyIssuerReenc=), /not/ in-SNARK.  Both the relation's
     soundness and its issuer/recipient privacy are done and tested.
   - *=RcptVerify= for A2* -- the A2 receipt now verifies =VALID= (the coupling
     via =verify_receipt=, =b5e8b79=); the =UNVERIFIED ISSUER= banner remains only
     for a note whose issuer supplied /no/ binding.

** Residual gaps (NOT done)

   1. *A2 recipient-key coupling* (the private-issuer collusion corner) -- =eIss=
      is bound to /a/ key the issuer chose, not provably the recipient's, so a
      colluding issuer+recipient can leave the issuer un-nameable while the note
      stays spendable (/The A2 recipient-key coupling gap/).  The leaf-tie (Phase
      2c, *DONE*) and bearer-from-non-public (Phase 2b, *DONE*) are closed; this
      one is *reserved, not on the critical path* (/The EOA Transfer Mirror/): it
      affects only /private-issuer/ notes under /collusion/, the fix is the
      set-membership + non-native-\( \mathbb{G}_1 \) premium, and in the interim
      the *recipient-burden* posture bounds it (an un-nameable A2 deposit marks the
      recipient, who is KYC-identifiable and was obliged to verify at delivery).
      Public-issuer notes (A1/B1) have no such gap.
   2. *Issuer-in-the-B-spend circuit* -- today the B-depositor /names/ the issuer
      at =SpentB= (sound for an honest depositor); revealing the issuer from the
      leaf in the B-spend circuit would remove the honesty assumption.  Lower
      priority -- a B1 issuer is public and already named by =msg.sender= at mint,
      so the receipt does not depend on this; it only hardens the =SpentB= event.
   3. *Formal verification* (Picus / Halmos / EasyCrypt) -- not started.

   /Closed by gated-only minting:/ the "enforcement is opt-in" gap (the unbound
   6-/7-arg =mint= overloads that let a non-public issuer append A1/B1/A2 leaves
   with no binding) is *gone* -- those overloads, and the =bindingRequired=
   cutover flag that would have disabled them, were removed.  =Notes.mint= now has
   exactly two paths (PUBLIC Schnorr, PRIVATE A2 leaf-tie), both requiring a
   registered issuer; "bearer => public issuer" and "every A2 leaf bound" hold by
   construction, not by the issuer's overload choice.

* Remaining Work and Approaches

  Phase 2b (issuerMode) and Phase 2c (the A2 =eIss= leaf-tie) are *shipped*.  The
  governing decision (/The EOA Transfer Mirror/) is: *public-issuer notes (A1/B1)
  are the shipping priority -- they already carry the full EOA-grade guarantee --
  and A2 (private-issuer) notes are reserved as a future option under the
  recipient-burden posture.*  In that light:

  1. *Ship A1/B1 (priority).*  Nothing cryptographic is missing: =verifyIssuerSchnorr=
     (public-issuer batch binding) and =verifyDepositorForIssuer= (= =verifyApprove=)
     give sound, collusion-resistant, bilateral mutual decryptability for public
     issuers.  Remaining work here is product, not crypto: wallet mint/receive/
     receipt UX, indexer, and the end-to-end A1/B1 receipt path.
  2. *A2 wallet posture (if A2 is offered at all).*  The recipient MUST verify
     =eIss= at delivery (decrypt with \( sk_D \), run the \(\gamma\) coupling) and
     *refuse* a note that does not resolve to a registered issuer; the receive
     screen SHOULD warn that accepting an unverifiable A2 note transfers
     accountability to the recipient (/The recipient bears the burden/).  This is
     the safeguard that lets A2 ship without the coupling.
  3. *A2 recipient-key coupling (reserved premium).*  Restores EOA-grade /bilateral/
     non-deniability to A2-under-collusion; needed only if a deployment requires
     no-trusted-third-party traceability for private-issuer notes.  The sound
     construction (set-membership + non-native \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)), its cost, the
     research threads, and the spike+PoC gate are in /The A2 recipient-key coupling
     gap/ and /Scope/.  *Not on the critical path.*
  4. *Enforce the eIss leaf-tie is reflected in the A2 receipt.*  =verify_receipt=
     (note-a2) can now assert the spent leaf's =eIss= was leaf-tied at mint (the
     =UNVERIFIED ISSUER= path tightens to "issuer supplied no binding for /this
     leaf/").  Pure off-chain wallet work.

  *Off-chain (record for the wallet/receipt spec):* the public-leaf issuer is
  named from =msg.sender= + the Schnorr batch binding, with the wallet locating
  the mint tx by scanning =Notes.mint= calldata for the =cm= (full chain
  history).  The committed =m_issuer= is not used for naming.

  *Not in scope / deferred:* the B-spend issuer-in-circuit (residual gap 3),
  formal verification (residual gap 4), and a production trusted-setup ceremony
  (the dev ptau + per-circuit contributions use fixed dev entropy -- see
  README.org "SNARK Circuits and Trusted Setup").

* Open Questions

  1. *In-SNARK vs blinded-CP for A2.*  /Decided: blinded-CP/ (=verifyIssuerReenc=,
     =5a9d0af= / =1a03c5b= / =d000e40=).  It keeps the mint SNARK out of native
     G1 arithmetic (the ~+250K R1CS/leaf the spend side already declined), costs
     ~111K gas on chain, and reuses the audited =verifyApprove= shape; the bespoke
     part is the \( U,\hat T,Q \) linearisation, written up and tested.  The
     remaining circuit work (=issuerMode= + leaf-tie) is enforcement plumbing, not
     the binding relation.
  2. *Batch vs per-leaf issuer signature.*  A single Schnorr over
     =keccak(cms)= is cheapest but binds the /whole batch/ to one public issuer;
     mixed-issuer batches need per-leaf signatures or per-issuer sub-batches.
  3. *Receipt non-repudiation scope.*  =RcptVerify= proves the issuer /authored/
     the note; tying that to a real-world legal identity still rests on the KYC
     binding of \( M \) (the PS credential).  This document assumes that binding;
     it does not re-derive it.
  4. *Predicate-bearing notes.*  The =predicate= field is out of scope here;
     a programmable predicate that itself moves value would need its own
     receipt analysis.
