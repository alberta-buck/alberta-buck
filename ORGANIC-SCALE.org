#+TITLE: The organic environment at civilization scale: where the simulation is, what the viewer must show, and the effort to get there
#+AUTHOR: Perry Kundert (design owner) with Claude
#+DATE: 2026-09-11

* The question

The design owner's thesis (2026-09-11): introducing advanced monetary
operations into a thin, incomplete organic equilibrium environment will
not provide a good foundation for judging their effectiveness.  The
organic environment that is wanted: a civilization-scale growth path
from a few million BUCK of assets (tens of households, farmsteads,
businesses) through hundreds of billions (thousands to hundreds of
thousands of them), populated by the full spectrum of roles --

- ISSUING: wealth -> BUCK (mortgages, operating lines, pledged assets);
- SAVING, short to mid term: BUCK held; BUCK into the BuckBasket; BUCK
  earned, exchanged for wealth, then pledged as a standing BuckCredit
  facility to avoid demurrage;
- RETIRING: issued or earned BUCK -> USDC to pay down external debt,
  then retired opportunistically as BUCK is available at a discount --

and a viewer that shows every collected metric and every agent's state
and actions against the simulation clock, for Python or JavaScript
worlds alike, so that agents' decisions (when, and why, given the
global state) and the system's response to their aggregate can be
seen unfolding.  This document takes stock against that goal and
estimates the work.  It is a plan, not a result: nothing here is
measured yet beyond what the ledger already records.

Whole categories absent from the Python world: an INCOME economy
(wages paid in BUCK, expenses, goods priced in BUCK, merchants who
set prices), EXTERNAL DEBT as a stock (USD mortgages and lines that
BUCK earnings pay down), INSURERS as agents (D8: who vets the latent
credit), INTERMEDIARIES (the credit unions of the mission statement,
running BuckCredit pools and arbs for their members), a DEMOGRAPHY
(household formation, exit, wealth and income distributions), and
the GROWTH PATH itself as a first-class object (the ladder from a
village to a nation).  The JS side holds the household doctrine
(=farmer=, =basket-investor=, =mortgage-retiree=, =MonthlyIncome=) as
exemplars proven by ledger audit, each "fitting on one screen" -- the
right seeds, not yet populations.

** The scale

The matrix arms are the deepest organic worlds banked: about 285
identities (30 honest debtors, 120 + 30 + 60 depositors, savers,
borrowers, the cast), BUCK supply ~64M, basket NAV ~14M, pools seeded
at $10M, five years at four ticks a day.  One such arm is 9-10.5 hours
on datasim (32-core Xeon) and ~3 hours on the Mac: about 15 ms per
agent-tick on pyrevm, the fixed cost of one on-chain act.  That fixes
the compute wall for agent-per-household simulation at fine cadence:

| households as agents | 5 y at 4 ticks/day, one core | 5 y at day_step 10, 1 tick (the equilibrium campaign's coarse mode) |
|----------------------+------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 300 (today)          | ~9 h                         | ~14 min                                                             |
| 3,000                | ~90 h                        | ~2.3 h                                                              |
| 30,000               | ~900 h (five weeks)          | ~23 h                                                               |
| 300,000              | impossible                   | ~10 days                                                            |

So "hundreds of thousands of households" is not an agent count; it is
a COHORT count times a cohort size.  A cohort agent stands for N
households drawn from a distribution (income, assets, debt, horizon),
acts with fractional flows (the cohort's aggregate issue / save /
retire each tick) and carries the heterogeneity as sampled sub-books,
so that 300 cohorts of 1,000 households run in today's compute.  The
existing =growth-*.toml= experiments (steady 25%/yr, S-curve, decline,
population churn, =realistic=) and the =endog= arrival hazards are
the germ of the demography; they scale populations by count, not by
cohort, and stop at tens of agents.

** The telemetry and the viewer, today

- Aggregate metrics: every frame carries ~210 keys -- the common-mode
  state (=basketVal=, =buckK=, =supply=, =treasuryBuck=, the pools'
  =poolBal= / =spotBuck= / =spotUsdc= / =refUsd=), the controller's
  terms (=pid_*=), and per-CLASS counters by prefix (=bcd_= debtors,
  =dm*= depositors, =saver_*=, =fat_*=, =bia_=, =bpi_=, =dba_= / =dbb_=,
  =exc_*=, =raid_*=, =crb_=, =ut_=, =fac_=, =sd_=, =sh_=, =mk_= / =mo_=
  the desk, =fk_= the fence, =growth_*=, =regime_*=, =mx= the markout
  ledger).  This is what the matplotlib panes (=plot_equilibrium= and
  friends) draw.
- Per-agent state: TELEMETRY.md schema v1 -- =meta.telemetry= is the
  roster (id "Class#idx", class, index, stride, resolved knobs) and
  =frame["ag"][id]= is a small state record for agents that implement
  =telemetry()= (holdings and P&L).  Implemented by the excursion
  family, the saver and two more in =equilibrium_agents.py=, the
  undertakings, the facility and the seeder: 7 of 29 classes.  The
  depositor, debtor and borrower populations -- the organic core --
  report only their class aggregates.
- Actions: NONE in the vector.  Agents print human lines to stdout
  ("[directmintagent-108] redeem failed ..."); the markout ledger
  attributes SWAPS to an actor tag through the pool probe; the JS
  agents keep a =this.ledger= array and a journal of tagged sends.  A
  "why did this agent act" question cannot be answered from a vector
  today.
- Live source: the sim server (=alberta_buck.sim.server=) streams the
  SAME frame objects over =ws://.../s/<sid>/frames= with =/control= and
  =/rpc= channels, one world per client session -- a browser viewer of
  the frame schema is already fed live.  The JS sims (=eqsim=,
  =farmsim=, =mini-sim=, =join-sim=) write their OWN summary JSON, SVG
  charts and JSONL journals, not the frame schema.
- The consistency question, answered: within Python the per-class
  aggregates are consistent by convention (prefix + counter) and the
  per-agent records are consistent where they exist (schema v1); the
  JS agents do not emit the Python schema.  One contract for both is
  the first deliverable below.

* The viewer

** What it must do (the design owner's specification, restated)

A time slider across the top drives a "current" simulation time.  The
LEFT column stacks the simulation metric panes, the matplotlib panes
as live graphs, every collected series available.  The RIGHT column
holds one TAB per agent class present in the run, populated
dynamically from the roster; inside a tab, one pane per agent with
graph pens for that agent's state fields and a log of its actions
showing the three nearest the current time.  Sources: a Python vector
(complete, or a checkpointed partial one), a live Python world (the
server's frames channel), or a JavaScript simulation emitting frames
incrementally.  The purpose is to see WHEN and WHY each agent decides,
against the global state it sees, and how the system's response to
the aggregate unfolds.

** Design

1. *Telemetry schema v2* (Python first).  Keep v1's roster and state
   record; add per frame, per agent, =acts=: the list of actions since
   the previous frame, each ={"t": tick, "kind": "...", "args": {...},
   "why": {...}, "ok": bool, "err": "..."}=.  The recording point
   already exists: the loop's pool probe attributes a send to
   =actor_tag(a)=; a thin recorder on =chain.send= under the acting
   agent's tag captures EVERY send (not only swaps), and the agent's
   decision variables (the bvib it read, the gate it passed) go in
   =why= from the same place the agent already prints them.  Every
   class implements =telemetry()= (a base-class default from its
   counters), so the organic core appears per agent.  Frame size
   grows with actions; the vectors stay tens of MB.
2. *The JS adapter*.  The JS doctrine's =this.ledger= and tagged
   sends map one-to-one onto =acts=; a small emitter writes the frame
   schema (=meta.telemetry= + frames) incrementally (JSONL, one frame
   per line, or the websocket channel), so the viewer never knows
   which language produced the world.
3. *The viewer itself*: one HTML file plus one script, no server
   required, in =alberta_buck/sim/viewer/=; served by the sim server
   too.  Plotting with uPlot (vendored through =package.json= as a dev
   dependency -- house rule 11), which draws 1,800 frames x 200 series
   without effort.  Left: panes auto-discovered from frame keys and
   grouped by prefix, with a saved layout for the canonical panes.
   Right: tabs from the roster's classes, per-agent panes with pens
   from the =ag= fields, the action log window bound to the slider.
   Sources: drag-and-drop or URL for a vector (partial vectors read
   because frames are checkpointed every 25 days), =ws://= for a live
   world, JSONL for a JS world.  Export: the current view as PNG/SVG
   for the documents of record.
4. *Where it pays first*: the D7 grid's attribution panel (R14c: WHY K
   moved -- the price loop's and the position loop's terms beside the
   facilities' books) is exactly this viewer's left column; the
   organic program's questions (why did the debtors stop issuing?) are
   its right column.

* The organic program

** What "organic" must mean at each rung

A SCALE LADDER, each rung a world that must hold equilibrium under its
own organic load before any monetary operation is judged on it:

| rung | assets | households (as cohorts x size)       | pools (BUCK/USDC, TOKEN/BUCK) | what the rung tests                                                        |
|------+--------+--------------------------------------+-------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1    | $3M    | 30 x 1 (agents ARE the households)   | $0.3M                         | the village: few actors, lumpy flows; does K even have a signal?           |
| 2    | $30M   | 300 x 1                              | $3M                           | today's matrix, made organic (income, external debt, businesses)           |
| 3    | $300M  | 300 x 10                             | $30M                          | first cohorts; the fast facilities' caps vs depth                          |
| 4    | $3B    | 300 x 100                            | $300M                         | the town: pools deep, K's authority vs demand growth                       |
| 5    | $30B   | 300 x 1,000                          | $3B                           | the region: does the funding gate throttle a growing economy?              |
| 6    | $300B  | 300 x 10,000                         | $30B                          | the nation: the desk and undertakings as fractions of a large economy      |

Each rung runs the organic gates first (the matrix's: bv tail within 1%,
K interior, channels alive, plus the new ones: income serviced, debt
paid down, savings life-cycles completing) and only then the
catalogue's disruptions and the D7 designs.  The rungs differ in what
breaks, which is the point: a desk cap that is 10% of NAV is decisive
at rung 2 and invisible at rung 6.

** The agents to build (Python populations from the JS seeds)

1. *The income channel*: a =HouseholdAgent= with a wage in BUCK (a LABR
   share of the basket as the wage index), expenses, a savings rate,
   and the three destinations of surplus (hold BUCK; deposit into the
   basket; buy insured wealth and pledge it as a STANDING undrawn
   BuckCredit facility -- the demurrage-avoiding saver the design
   owner names).  This is the missing saver.
2. *The business*: =FarmsteadAgent= / =BusinessAgent=, the Python port
   of =farmer.js=: pledged operating assets, an operating line drawn
   seasonally and repaid from lumpy revenue, a mortgage, payroll to the
   household population (closing the income loop).
3. *The external-debt retiree*: the Python port of =mortgage-retiree.js=
   with =MonthlyIncome=: a USD (USDC) mortgage as a stock; BUCK earned
   or issued is swapped to USDC to pay it down, and the BUCK credit is
   retired opportunistically at a discount.
4. *The intermediary*: a =CreditUnionAgent= holding members' BuckCredit
   pools and running the member-facing arbs (the mission statement).
5. *The insurer*: vets and prices the latent credit (D8), the counter-
   party of every facility; a rate, a capacity, a default model.
6. *Demography*: a =Demography= driver that forms and retires households
   and businesses by hazards, samples their incomes and assets from
   distributions that scale with the rung, and grows the population
   along the ladder -- the =growth-*= experiments generalized.
7. *Cohorts*: the aggregation layer (one agent, N households) with
   fractional flows and sampled sub-books; a cohort must reproduce the
   sum of N single agents within a stated tolerance at rung 2 before it
   is trusted at rung 3+ (the L3-style gate of this program).

* The verdict on the thesis, and what to run now

The thesis is right for the QUANTITATIVE conclusions the D7 grid was
going to draw -- gains, capacities, weights, carry costs, attacker
P&L, depositor fairness all depend on how deep and how organic the
world is -- and those conclusions should wait for rung 2 made organic
and for at least one deeper rung.  It is not right for the STRUCTURAL
question the grid's first cycle asks: whether the two-loop K is
identical to Direct with zero gains, whether S and V habituate at all,
whether a book-loading attacker can move K -- those hold or fail in a
thin world as in a deep one, and the mechanics are cheap to establish
now.  So: run cycle 1 of the controller-alternatives plan as a
STRUCTURAL check on the current environment (it also exercises the
viewer's left column), build telemetry v2 and the viewer immediately
(they pay for both programs), start the organic agents in parallel,
and re-judge the catalogue and the D7 designs rung by rung as the
ladder comes up.  The gate to record: "no monetary operation is judged
at a rung whose organic gates have not passed."

* Decisions for the design owner

1. The ladder's rungs (the table above) and the definition of the
   organic gates at each rung.
2. Cohort granularity and the equivalence tolerance (a cohort of 10 vs
   10 agents: within what error on issuance, savings and retirement?).
3. The minimal income economy: wages indexed to LABR paid in BUCK; do
   goods get BUCK prices (merchants), or is expenditure a flow only?
4. The viewer stack and home: uPlot vendored through =package.json=,
   =alberta_buck/sim/viewer/=, served by the sim server -- or a
   different choice.
5. Whether the JS platform becomes the second source now (the emitter
   in wave 4) or after the Python populations exist.
6. Whether cycle 1 of the D7 grid runs on the current environment as
   the structural check (recommended) or waits for rung 2.
