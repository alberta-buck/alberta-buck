#+TITLE: BuckCredit accounting -- transferability, the credit limit, and balanceOf
#+AUTHOR: Perry Kundert
#+date: 2026-08-15
#+category: Alberta Buck
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

Record of a settled design question and the work that came out of it.  The
correctness fixes are implemented on this branch; what remains open is
listed in section 6.  Once the master design document catches up, this
file is retired.

* The model this is built to

  BuckCredit exists so that insured assets can back BUCK issuance.  It
  follows that a holder's spendable balance is *not* a static ledger
  entry.  It moves when:

  - the holder acquires or activates more credit,
  - an insurer reappraises an asset,
  - an asset depreciates naturally on its schedule,
  - BUCK_K moves under the whole system.

  =balanceOf= has to reflect all four.  That is the feature, not a leak in
  the abstraction, and it decides everything below:

  1. *A BUCK_CREDIT must not be transferable while it is backing BUCK.*
  2. *No caching unless it is unavoidable.*  A stale credit limit is a
     wrong balance.  Correctness and simplicity beat cost here, and any
     cached copy of a quantity whose inputs live in two other contracts is
     a wrong answer waiting to happen.
  3. *An account holding BUCK_CREDIT may cost more to transfer out of.*
     That is the right place for the cost to land.  An account with a
     plain positive balance, and any Carrying account, pays nothing for
     machinery it does not use.
  4. *Going underwater is a supported state, not a failure.*  A holder
     whose collateral has fallen below their draw must accumulate BUCK and
     repay before they can release the credit -- the same order as any
     loan.  If they do nothing at all, that is fine too: the insurance is
     paid up in perpetuity and stays in force, and Jubilee relief accruing
     on the coverage shrinks what closing the position costs, year on
     year, without the holder lifting a finger.

* What was wrong, and what was done

** D1 A drawn position could walk away from its collateral   [FIXED]

   =creditLimit(a)= sums the activated value of the credits =a= owns, while
   the BUCK drawn against it stays as a negative balance on the account
   that drew it.  Nothing stopped a holder from transferring the credit
   away.  Plain =transferFrom=, no receiver hook, nothing exotic:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   alice draws her full 1,000 of headroom  -> signedRaw(alice) = -1,000
   alice transfers the credit to dave      -> creditLimit(alice) =      0
                                              creditLimit(dave)  =  1,000
   dave draws it too                       -> bob holds          =  2,000 BUCK

   2,000 BUCK issued against 1,000 of activated coverage.
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Fixed in =BuckCredit._update=: a credit whose =activatedValue= is
   non-zero cannot change owner.  Minting (=from == address(0)=) is
   exempt, so =createCredit= still works, and the credit becomes
   transferable again the moment a burn takes its coverage back to zero.

   =activatedValue= is the predicate rather than Buck's =mintsBacked=
   because it is *local*: BuckCredit enforces the property without
   consulting Buck, so it does not depend on another contract being
   correct or even reachable.  D3 is what makes the two agree.

   Regression: =test/BuckCreditBacking.t.sol= and
   =test/BuckCreditReappraisal.t.sol= (=test_position_closesAndReleasesTheCredit=
   walks the whole cycle -- buy, locked, burn, free again).

** D2 The credit-limit cache was dead, and unsound if revived   [REMOVED]

   =_refreshCreditLimit= had no callers, so =creditLimitBlock= was only
   ever written to zero and =creditLimit()= always recomputed.  The cache
   never populated.

   Reviving it was never an option, because the cached quantity was
   =totalCurrentValue * currentBuckK=, and BUCK_K moves whenever any mint
   or burn advances the PID -- from any account, with nothing to announce
   it.  Two transactions in one block were enough to read a stale limit.
   =onCreditMutation= covered NFT mutations; it could not cover BUCK_K,
   and no hook can cover natural depreciation either, which is continuous.

   Removed entirely: =creditLimitCache=, =creditLimitBlock=,
   =_refreshCreditLimit=, =_invalidateCreditCache=, =Buck.onCreditMutation=,
   and BuckCredit's four =IBuckHook= call sites and the interface itself.
   =creditLimit()= is now the live computation and nothing else.

   Three things fell out of it:

   - *The call graph between the two contracts is now one-way.*  BuckCredit
     never calls Buck.  That in turn means the reentrancy guard added
     alongside this work has no exemptions to make -- =onCreditMutation=
     was the only reentrant path, and it is gone.
   - *=setBuck= is now purely an authorisation record*, not a callback
     wiring.  It still gates =activateFromBuck= / =deactivateFromBuck=.
   - Every NFT mint, transfer, activation and reappraisal stopped paying
     for a cross-contract call whose only effect was to zero a slot
     nothing read.

   The cost of liveness, measured on this branch:

   | =creditLimit= for ...                      |    gas |
   |--------------------------------------------+--------|
   | an account with no credits                 |  6,327 |
   | an account with 10 credits, NONE schedule  | 27,603 |
   | one credit, 40 years into declining balance | 11,925 |

   This is exactly the distribution constraint (3) asks for: the holder of
   ten insured assets pays for ten insured assets on every outbound
   transfer, and nobody else pays for them at all.

** D3 A downward reappraisal stranded the position it shrank   [FIXED]

   =updateCredit= clamped =activatedValue= down to a reduced =faceValue=
   while =mintsBacked=, which lives in Buck, was left alone.
   =_allocateBurn= sizes the unwind from =mintsBacked= and
   =deactivateFromBuck= measures it against =activatedValue=, so once they
   diverged the holder could not close the position:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   after mint            face 2,000   activated 1,000   mintsBacked 1,000
   reappraise to 500     face   500   activated   500   mintsBacked 1,000

   burn(800)   -> revert "BuckCredit: deactivate > active"
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Burning down in chunks did not recover it -- the coverage ran out
   before the backing did, and the residue could never be unwound, which
   also disqualified the credit from ever backing BUCK again
   (=_allocateMint= skips any token whose =faceValue <= mintsBacked=):

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   burned down by          : 400.000000
   residual activatedValue :  55.555528
   residual mintsBacked    : 555.555528     <- permanently stuck
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Fixed at the root: an insurer may reappraise freely down to the
   coverage the holder has already bought, and no further.  Activated
   coverage is a completed purchase -- its pool principal was paid up
   front and funds its premium in perpetuity -- so writing it down is
   revoking a policy, not revaluing an asset.  The insurer keeps every
   other lever: =faceValue= down to the activated line, the depreciation
   schedule, the floor, the premium rate.  An asset that has genuinely
   lost value is what the claim path is for; ordinary decline is what the
   schedule is for, and that still moves the holder's credit limit
   without touching the coverage itself.

   Two consequences worth naming:

   - The clamp branch inside =updateCredit=, and its =_covSeconds=
     rescaling, are gone with it.
   - =_allocateBurn= and =_allocateBurnView= now take
     =min(mintsBacked, activatedValue)= anyway.  With the rule above they
     cannot differ; the clamp is there so that whatever future edit puts
     them out of step costs the system a rounding rather than costing a
     holder their exit.

** D4 balanceOf includes credit headroom   [INTENDED -- not a defect]

   For a non-Carrying account =balanceOf= returns held BUCK plus unused
   credit headroom, so an account holding no BUCK at all reports 5,000 if
   it has 5,000 of activated coverage behind it.

   This was raised as an ERC-20 integration hazard, and the honest reading
   is that it is one -- but it is also the entire point.  BUCK backed by
   an insured asset *is* spendable, and a holder acquiring, reappraising
   or depreciating an asset must see their balance move.  An earlier draft
   of this document proposed splitting =balanceOf= (held only) from a new
   =spendableOf= (held + headroom); that is *rejected*.  It would have made
   =balanceOf= cheap and static, which is precisely the wrong property.

   What follows from keeping it:

   - Integrators must be told, prominently, that a BUCK balance is a live
     function of the holder's insured assets and of BUCK_K.  This belongs
     in the integration notes, not in a contract change.
   - Nothing in =src/= is affected: every in-tree =buck.balanceOf(...)=
     consumer reads a *pool* balance (=RebalanceDirectorBase=,
     =BuckBasketUniswapV3= x3, =BuckBasket=), and pools are Carrying, so
     they take the =return raw= branch and never touch the credit path.

** D5 A holder at their limit cannot burn   [INTENDED]

   Releasing coverage costs more limit than the refund repays -- burning
   =A= releases =A/(1-eff)= of coverage and refunds only the difference --
   so at =used == limit= every burn fails the post-burn solvency check.
   That is the intended order of operations: repay the credit, then
   release the coverage, exactly as with any loan.

   And a holder who does neither is not stuck in any harmful sense.  The
   policy is paid up and stays in force, and the Jubilee relief accruing
   on the coverage reduces what closing the position costs:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   coverage in force at t0      : 1,000.000000
   coverage in force at t+10yr  : 1,000.000000    <- unchanged, still insured
   redeemCost at t0             : 1,000.000000
   redeemCost at t+10yr         :   800.000001    <- ~2%/yr forgiven
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Regression: =test_fullyDrawnHolder_repaysThenCloses= and
   =test_openPosition_decaysInFavourOfTheHolder=.

** D6 A depreciated credit under-delivered, and the deposit leaked   [FIXED]

   Two faults, and the second only surfaced because the first was being
   fixed.

*** The draw

    =_allocateMint= sized =take= against the /undepreciated/ =faceValue=
    and grossed it up only for the premium inversion, while =creditLimit=
    reads the /depreciated/ =currentValue=.  The holder came up short by
    exactly the depreciation applied to the cover taken.  A 300,000
    structure three years into 200bp/yr delivered 47,000 on a =mint(50,000)=.

    The allocator now inverts in *present insured value* and grosses the
    face units back up:

    #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
    V         = ceil(amount * BP / denom)          present value that settles `amount`
    principal = V - amount                         = V * effRate / BP
    take      = ceil(V * faceValue / depreciatedFace)
    #+END_EXAMPLE

    Two things follow from charging premium on =V= rather than on the face
    slice.  It is what the holder should pay -- you pay for the cover you
    have, not the cover the asset used to be worth.  And it is the only
    formulation that does not fall over: charging on the face slice gives
    ~take = amount * BP / (rho * BP - effRate)~, unsatisfiable once
    ~rho * BP <= effRate~, so a 200bp credit would become unmintable at any
    price below 20 % of face.  With the premium on =V=, =denom= is
    independent of =rho=, the cost per BUCK delivered stays
    ~amount * effRate / denom~ however old the asset is -- which is also
    why cheapest-first by =premiumRate= remains the right selector -- and
    ~rho == 1~ reduces the gross-up to the identity, so nothing changes for
    a non-depreciating credit.

    =CreditSlice= gains =depreciatedFace=, and =BuckCredit= a public
    =depreciatedFaceValue(tokenId)=.

*** The refund

    The first cut ran the same inversion on the burn side, which quietly
    made the pool deposit only *partly* refundable: cover bought when an
    asset was worth 100,000 and released when it was worth 50,000 returned
    1,250 of a 2,500 deposit.  Caught in review, and wrong.

    The deposit is returnable.  Its yield funds the premium in perpetuity
    -- that is what makes a policy a one-time purchase rather than a
    recurring expense -- so what the holder pays for cover is the
    /opportunity cost/ of the deposit, not the deposit.  Re-pricing it on
    exit charges them twice for one decline: the pool has already been
    compensated for holding an over-sized deposit against shrinking cover,
    because it earned its assumed ROI on the whole deposit while owing
    premium only on what was still insured.

    So releasing does not re-price.  It hands back the deposit the cover is
    carrying, pro rata on the face units let go:

    #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
    capV   = mintsBacked * rho
    netCap = capV - mintsPrincipal
    units  = ceil(amount * mintsBacked / netCap)
    refund = mintsPrincipal * units / mintsBacked
    #+END_EXAMPLE

    That needs =mintsPrincipal[tokenId]=, a new mapping, because the
    deposit is the sum over past draws of ~V_i * effRate_i / BP~ and both
    the appraisal and the rate may have moved between them --
    ~mintsPrincipal / mintsBacked~ is a weighted average with no closed
    form.  It is appended after =demurragePayerRequest= so slots 0-7 are
    untouched.

    Two properties fall out, and both are worth having.
    ~capV <= mintsPrincipal~ means the position has no spendable left in it
    -- the holder is underwater and must repay first, the same rule the
    post-burn solvency check enforces, reached from the other direction.
    And with no loose BUCK held, ~netCap == creditLimit - used ==
    balanceOf~: *burning exactly their spendable closes the position and
    squares the deposit*, which the earlier version could not do at all --
    it reverted.

    Regressions: =test_mintDeliversInFullAgainstADepreciatedCredit=,
    =test_premiumIsChargedOnPresentInsuredValue=,
    =test_premiumCostPerBuckIsIndependentOfDepreciation=,
    =test_depositReturnsInFullAfterDepreciation=,
    =test_mintBurnRoundTripIsExactOnADepreciatedCredit=,
    =test_burn_returnsTheWholeDepositAcrossPartialReleases=, with
    =test_nonDepreciatingCreditIsUnchanged= as the control.

** D7 Credits could be pushed onto anybody   [FIXED]

   =createCredit= minted an ERC-721 to an address that had never consented,
   and ERC-721 =_mint= asks the receiver nothing.  A fresh credit grants no
   headroom -- =activatedValue= is zero -- but it costs its holder gas
   forever after: =totalCurrentValue= walks =tokenOfOwnerByIndex= over every
   token they own, and since the limit is read live that walk runs on every
   outbound transfer from a non-Carrying account.  An attacker could raise a
   chosen address's transfer cost without bound for the price of the mints.

   Fixed with a per-pair opt-in: =setCreditIssuer(insurer, accepted)=, and
   =createCredit= reverts with =BuckCredit: insurer not accepted by client=
   unless it is set.  Revocable, and it governs only new issuance, since
   credits already held may be backing BUCK.

   The rule is uniform -- naming yourself the insurer does not exempt you.
   There is no reading of "I am my own insurer" that needs a carve-out, and
   declining to open one keeps the invariant statable in a line and avoids a
   hole shaped exactly like D8.

   The gate is an internal =virtual= so =BuckCreditHarness= can stand it down
   for fixtures whose holders never send a transaction of their own; it is
   exercised against the production contract in =BuckCreditIssuance.t.sol=.

   One consequence worth naming: a contract that holds credits now needs a
   way to call =setCreditIssuer=.  The sim's SimLP and agent proxies already
   have an =exec= passthrough, so they route the opt-in through it.

** Minor

   - =storedLimit= -- the Phase-1a "highest-ever credit limit" ratchet, a
     cached derived value that nothing had written in a long time but that
     was still exported as a public getter answering zero.  Removed; its
     slot is now occupied by =demurragePayer=, so =mintsBacked= (4) and
     =_receiptFragments= (5) keep the indices several test fixtures reach
     by hand via =vm.store=.
   - =_selectCheapest= still makes two external calls per NFT
     (=tokenOfOwnerByIndex= + =creditInfo=) when =batchCreditInfo= exists
     precisely to avoid that.  A =tokensOfOwner(address)= bulk view on
     BuckCredit would collapse the whole selector to two calls.  Open.

* The shape underneath it

  There were two ledgers for one quantity:

  | ledger           | lives in   | written by                                              | read by                         |
  |------------------+------------+---------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------|
  | =activatedValue= | BuckCredit | =_activate=, =deactivateFromBuck=, (was: =updateCredit= clamp) | =currentValue= -> =creditLimit= |
  | =mintsBacked=    | Buck       | =_allocateMint=, =_allocateBurn=                        | the mint / burn allocators      |

  They were equal only by convention.  Every defect above was a
  consequence of their being separately mutable: D1 let the token carry
  one of them away, D3 moved one and not the other, and D2 kept a third,
  derived copy invalidated against only some of its inputs.

  After this branch the convention is an enforced invariant -- the only
  writer of =activatedValue= is the activation path Buck drives, and
  reappraisal may no longer undercut it -- and there are no derived copies
  of it at all.

* Options considered and not taken

  Kept for the record, because each is the obvious thing to reach for.

** Caching the credit limit safely

   It *can* be done, in one shape and no other: cache =totalCurrentValue=
   alone, keyed on =block.number= and invalidated by an NFT-mutation hook,
   and multiply by a live =currentBuckK()= on every read.  That is sound --
   =totalCurrentValue= depends on the holder's credit set, each token's
   parameters and =block.timestamp=, all of which either route through a
   mutation or are constant within a block.

   Not taken.  It reinstates the hook and the invalidation surface to save
   gas that constraint (3) says we are content to spend, and it leaves the
   next reader unable to tell by inspection whether a given balance is
   fresh.

** Blocking transfer on Buck's =mintsBacked= instead

   Authoritative with respect to the money ledger, but it costs a
   STATICCALL on every credit movement and inverts the dependency:
   BuckCredit would need Buck live and correct in order to move a token.
   With D3 fixed the two predicates coincide, so the local one wins.

** Allowing transfer with a post-transfer solvency check

   =used <= creditLimit(sender)= after the move would let a holder with
   several credits sell an undrawn one.  More permissive, but it needs the
   Buck call anyway and lets a credit carrying =mintsBacked > 0= change
   hands, leaving premium bookkeeping attached to a token whose new owner
   did not pay it.  A possible later relaxation, not a starting point.

** Clamping both ledgers together on reappraisal

   =updateCredit= could call into Buck to clamp =mintsBacked= alongside
   =activatedValue=.  It keeps them equal but does not answer the real
   question: the pool principal for the clamped-away coverage has already
   been paid, and the holder's drawn balance does not shrink with the
   limit.  Somebody has to absorb the difference.  Refusing the clamp
   settles it without inventing a settlement rule.

** Splitting =balanceOf= from =spendableOf=

   Rejected -- see D4.  It optimises for the wrong property.

* Still open

** D8 Anyone may be an insurer, so anyone may mint BUCK for free

   Found while scoping D7, and it is the more serious of the two by a wide
   margin.  =createCredit= vets nobody.  A credit's =faceValue= and
   =premiumRate= are whatever its issuer declares, and a zero-premium credit
   costs nothing to draw on -- ~poolPrincipal == 0~, so the counter-cyclical
   funding gate is inapplicable by design.  So one verified account can write
   itself a policy on an asset it never names and mint against it:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   mallory issues herself a zero-premium credit, faceValue 1,000,000,000
   mint(500,000,000)  ->  principal paid  0
                          spendable       500,000,000 BUCK
                          transferred on  totalSupply 0 -> 500,000,000
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Nothing downstream catches it: =totalCurrentValue= sums whatever credits
   an account holds without asking who issued them, and the identity layer
   only establishes that the *minter* is a registered person, not that the
   *insurer* is good for anything.

   D7's recipient opt-in does not close this and cannot -- the attacker is
   both insurer and client, so she accepts herself.  Only vetting the insurer
   closes it.

   The intended shape is deliberately *not* a governance allowlist: issuance
   stays open to any comer who can demonstrate they are good for the claims
   -- on-chain solvency (a buffer held, and provably available to the
   parametric payout trigger) plus a rating.  That system is unbuilt.  Until
   it exists, BUCK's supply is bounded only by the honesty of everyone
   holding a registered identity, and this is the blocker before mainnet.

** Smaller

  1. Integration notes for =balanceOf= -- *done*: see "Integrating with
     BUCK" in =alberta-buck-ethereum.org=.
  2. =tokensOfOwner= bulk view, to collapse =_selectCheapest= from 2N
     external calls to 2.
  3. =BuckCreditHarness.forceActivate= reaches states production cannot:
     it activates without moving Buck's =mintsBacked=.  It is now only
     used by fixtures that never burn, but it would be safer for it to
     move both.
  4. Custom errors in place of the 42 revert strings in =Buck.sol= would
     return about 2.2 KB of bytecode (measured: 18,657 -> 16,389 with the
     reasons stripped entirely, so ~2.0 KB net of the 4-byte selectors).
     Margin is 5,919 B today, so this is headroom for later growth rather
     than a present need; it would convert 32 =vm.expectRevert= string
     assertions across the suite.

* Invariants now enforced, and worth asserting directly

  1. =mintsBacked[tid] == activatedValue(tid)= for every token, at every
     observation point.  Held by construction on both sides now; deserves
     an explicit invariant test.
  2. =activatedValue(tid) > 0= implies the token cannot change owner.
     Asserted in =BuckCreditLimit.t.sol= and =BuckCreditBacking.t.sol=.
  3. =creditLimit(h)= is a pure function of live BuckCredit state and
     BUCK_K, with no path dependence.  There is no incremental accounting
     left for it to disagree with.
  4. BuckCredit makes no call into Buck.
