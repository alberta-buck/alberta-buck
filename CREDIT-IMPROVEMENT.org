#+TITLE: BuckCredit accounting -- transferability, the credit limit, and balanceOf
#+AUTHOR: Perry Kundert
#+date: 2026-08-15
#+category: Alberta Buck
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

Working proposal.  Nothing here is implemented; the defects are all
reproducible from =test/CreditProbe.t.sol= and =test/BuckReviewProbe.t.sol=
on this branch.  Once settled, this folds back into
=alberta-buck-ethereum.org= and this file is retired.

* Constraints this proposal is written to

  1. A BUCK_CREDIT must not be transferable while it is drawn.
  2. =balanceOf= for a credit account must be *correct* first and cheap
     second.  Caching is welcome only if it is provably safe.
  3. Transferring out of an account backed by BUCK_CREDIT is expected to
     cost more than transferring from a plain positive balance.  That is
     not a deal-breaker; a wrong answer is.

  Read together, (2) and (3) point somewhere slightly further than
  "fix the cache": the cheapest correct =balanceOf= is one that does not
  consult the credit machinery at all.  See section 4.

* What is broken today

** D1 A drawn position can walk away from its collateral

   =creditLimit(a)= is derived from =totalCurrentValue(a)=, which sums
   =activatedValue= over the NFTs =a= currently owns.  Nothing stops a
   holder who has already drawn against that coverage from transferring
   the NFT away.  Plain =transferFrom=, no receiver hook, nothing exotic.
   A negative raw balance is only ever checked at the moment it is
   created, so there is no later solvency test to catch it.

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   alice draws her full 1,000 of headroom  -> signedRaw(alice) = -1,000
   alice transfers the NFT to dave         -> creditLimit(alice) =      0
                                              creditLimit(dave)  =  1,000
   dave draws it too                       -> bob holds          =  2,000 BUCK

   2,000 BUCK issued against 1,000 of activated coverage.
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Severity: this is the one that mints unbacked BUCK.  Everything else
   in this document is correctness or cost.

** D2 The per-block credit-limit cache is dead, and unsound if revived

   =_refreshCreditLimit= has no callers.  =creditLimitBlock= is therefore
   only ever written by =_invalidateCreditCache=, which sets it to zero,
   and =block.number= is never zero -- so =creditLimit()= always takes the
   recompute branch and the cache never populates.  Every non-Carrying
   transfer pays a full NFT scan through =balanceOf(from)=:

   | measurement                                    |    gas |
   |------------------------------------------------+--------|
   | =creditLimit=, 10 NFTs, NONE depreciation      | 25,446 |
   | =creditLimit=, 1 NFT, 40yr declining balance   | 12,235 |

   The important part is not that it is dead.  It is that *wiring it up
   as written would be incorrect*.  The cached product is
   =totalCurrentValue * currentBuckK=, and =currentBuckK= moves whenever
   any mint or burn advances the PID via =IBuckK.compute()= -- which fires
   no cache invalidation at all.  Two transactions in one block are
   enough:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   creditLimit before K change: 1,000.000000
   creditLimit after  K change:   500.000000     <- same block
   cache block stamp          : 0                <- cache is dead, so this
                                                    read was live and right
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   =onCreditMutation= covers NFT mint / burn / transfer / activate /
   updateCredit.  It does not and cannot cover BUCK_K.

** D3 A downward reappraisal strands the position it shrinks

   =updateCredit= clamps =activatedValue= down to the new =faceValue=, but
   =mintsBacked= lives in Buck and is not clamped with it.  =_allocateBurn=
   sizes the unwind off =mintsBacked=; =deactivateFromBuck= checks that
   unwind against =activatedValue=.  Once they diverge the holder cannot
   close the position:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   after mint            face 2,000   activated 1,000   mintsBacked 1,000
   reappraise to 500     face   500   activated   500   mintsBacked 1,000

   burn(800)   -> revert "BuckCredit: deactivate > active"
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   Burning down in chunks does not recover it.  The coverage runs out
   before the backing does, and what is left can never be unwound:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   burned down by          : 400.000000
   residual activatedValue :  55.555528
   residual mintsBacked    : 555.555528     <- permanently stuck
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   The residue also permanently disqualifies the NFT from future minting,
   because =_allocateMint= skips any token whose =faceValue <= mintsBacked=.
   The asset can recover its whole value and still be unusable as credit.

   If the holder had already *spent* the draw when the reappraisal lands,
   they are underwater immediately and the designed exit is closed:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   after spend        signedRaw -1,000   creditLimit 1,000
   after reappraisal  signedRaw -1,000   creditLimit   500   balanceOf 0
   burn(100)  -> revert "BUCK: post-burn credit used exceeds limit"
   #+END_EXAMPLE

** D4 balanceOf reports credit headroom as spendable ERC-20 balance

   For a non-Carrying account =balanceOf= returns held BUCK *plus* unused
   credit headroom.  An account holding nothing reports 5,000 BUCK if it
   has 5,000 of activated coverage.  This is deliberate and documented as
   "what alice can spend right now", and as a user-facing semantic it is
   the right one -- but it is the number every ERC-20 integration reads,
   and it moves when a PID controller moves BUCK_K.

   Worth noting for the impact assessment in section 4: *nothing in
   =src/= actually depends on this today*.  Every in-tree
   =buck.balanceOf(...)= consumer reads a *pool* balance
   (=RebalanceDirectorBase=, =BuckBasketUniswapV3= x3, =BuckBasket=), and
   pools are Carrying, so they take the =return raw= branch and never
   touch the credit path.

** D5 A fully drawn holder cannot burn at all (by design -- but see D3)

   The post-burn check requires =used <= creditLimit= *after* the unwind.
   Burning =A= releases =U = A/(1-eff)= of coverage and refunds only
   =U - A=, so the limit always falls faster than =used= does.  At
   =used == limit= -- the normal end state of mint-then-spend -- every burn
   fails:

   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   fully drawn: signedRaw -988.888888   creditLimit 988.888888
   burn(1)  -> revert "BUCK: post-burn credit used exceeds limit"
   #+END_EXAMPLE

   The contract says so explicitly ("if you owe X ... repay first"), so
   this is a design property, not a defect: you repay the credit, then
   release the coverage.  It is listed here because it is what makes D3
   bite so hard.  A holder pushed underwater by someone else's action --
   a reappraisal, or simply depreciation of the insured asset -- has no
   path back through =burn=, only through acquiring BUCK elsewhere.
   Whether that is acceptable for *involuntary* undercollateralisation is
   a policy question this proposal raises but does not answer.

** Minor

   - =storedLimit= is declared =public= and never read or written.  It
     generates a getter that always returns zero, which indexers and
     integrators will reasonably take to mean something.
   - =_selectCheapest= makes two external calls per NFT
     (=tokenOfOwnerByIndex= + =creditInfo=) when =batchCreditInfo= exists
     precisely to avoid that.  A =tokensOfOwner(address)= bulk view on
     BuckCredit would collapse the whole selector to two calls.

* The shape underneath all of it

  There are two ledgers for the same quantity:

  | ledger           | lives in   | written by                              | read by                     |
  |------------------+------------+-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------|
  | =activatedValue= | BuckCredit | =_activate=, =deactivateFromBuck=, =updateCredit= clamp | =currentValue= -> =creditLimit= |
  | =mintsBacked=    | Buck       | =_allocateMint=, =_allocateBurn=        | the mint / burn allocators  |

  In production they are equal by construction -- there is no public
  =activate()=, so activation only ever happens inside =_allocateMint=.
  The contract comments say as much.  Every defect above is a consequence
  of the two ledgers being *separately mutable*:

  - D1: the limit follows =activatedValue=, which follows the NFT, while
    the drawn balance stays with the account.
  - D3: =updateCredit= moves one ledger and not the other.
  - D2: the cache is a third copy of a derived quantity, invalidated
    against only one of its inputs.

  The through-line for the proposal is therefore: *make the equality an
  enforced invariant rather than a convention, and stop keeping derived
  copies of it.*

* Options

** T -- Transferability of a drawn credit

*** T1  Block =_update= while =activatedValue > 0=   [recommended]

    #+BEGIN_SRC solidity
    function _update(address to, uint256 tokenId, address auth)
        internal override returns (address from)
    {
        from = super._update(to, tokenId, auth);
        require(from == address(0) || credits[tokenId].activatedValue.isZero(),
                "BuckCredit: coverage activated");
        ...
    }
    #+END_SRC

    Local to BuckCredit; one cold SLOAD (~2,100) on an NFT transfer, and
    zero on the mint path once =from == address(0)= short-circuits.  The
    =from == address(0)= arm is required so =createCredit= still works.

    Its real virtue is not the gas.  It makes the safety property
    *self-enforcing inside BuckCredit*, with no dependency on Buck being
    correct and no cross-contract call to get wrong.  Combined with R2
    below (which restores =mintsBacked == activatedValue= as an
    invariant), "activated" and "drawn" are the same predicate, so the
    local check is exactly the right one.

    Consequence to accept deliberately: an insured asset cannot be sold
    with a live policy attached.  Burn down to zero, then transfer.  That
    is consistent with the stated design -- "closure only ever by the
    holder's burn" -- and it is what constraint (1) asks for.

*** T2  Block on =IBuck(buck).mintsBacked(tokenId) != 0=

    Authoritative with respect to the money ledger, and strictly stricter
    than T1 in exactly the case where the two diverge.  Costs a
    STATICCALL (~2,600) on every NFT movement and inverts the dependency:
    BuckCredit would need Buck to be live and correct in order to
    transfer a token.  Worth keeping in reserve if R2 is rejected and the
    ledgers are allowed to diverge.

*** T3  Allow the transfer, require sender post-transfer solvency

    =used <= creditLimit(sender)= after the move.  More permissive: a
    holder with several NFTs could sell an undrawn one.  But it needs the
    Buck call anyway, and it lets an NFT carrying =mintsBacked > 0= change
    hands, leaving premium bookkeeping attached to a token whose new
    owner did not pay it.  Not recommended as the primary rule; a
    reasonable *later* relaxation once T1 is in place and the invariant is
    enforced.

*** T4  Move the liability with the NFT   [rejected]

    Transferring the drawn position along with the collateral needs a
    signed balance to move between two accounts with no consent model on
    the receiving side.  Not worth the machinery.

** L -- The credit limit and its cache

*** L1  Delete the cache   [recommended]

    Remove =creditLimitCache=, =creditLimitBlock=, =_refreshCreditLimit=,
    =_invalidateCreditCache=, =onCreditMutation=, and BuckCredit's three
    =IBuckHook= call sites plus its =buck= / =setBuck= wiring.
    =creditLimit()= becomes a pure function of live state.

    Cost on the transfer path: *zero*.  The cache is already dead, so this
    is deletion of code that never runs.  It is a net saving elsewhere --
    every NFT mint / transfer / activate / reappraisal currently pays for
    a cross-contract call whose only effect is to zero a slot nothing
    reads.

    Two bonuses worth having:

    - It removes the only BuckCredit -> Buck call, which is the only
      reason =onCreditMutation= has to be exempt from the new reentrancy
      guard.  After L1 the guard has no exemptions at all, and BuckCredit
      no longer calls into Buck for any reason.
    - It removes an entire class of future bug: a derived value cached
      against an incomplete set of invalidation triggers.

    Storage: slots for the two mappings must stay occupied to preserve
    the layout the =vm.store= consumers depend on.  Replace them with an
    explicitly named private gap rather than leaving public getters that
    return stale zeroes.

*** L2  Wire the cache as written   [rejected]

    Unsound, per D2: no invalidation on BUCK_K.

*** L3  Cache the NFT scan only, not the product   [safe, if caching is wanted later]

    The unsoundness in L2 comes entirely from caching the *product*.
    Cache =totalCurrentValue= alone, keyed on =block.number= and
    invalidated by =onCreditMutation=, and multiply by a live
    =currentBuckK()= on every read:

    #+BEGIN_SRC solidity
    function creditLimit(address a) public view returns (uint256) {
        uint256 cv = creditValueBlock[a] == uint64(block.number)
            ? creditValueCache[a]
            : buckCredit.totalCurrentValue(a);
        return cv == 0 ? 0 : cv * buckK.currentBuckK() / BUCKK_SCALE;
    }
    #+END_SRC

    This is sound.  =totalCurrentValue= depends on the holder's NFT set,
    each token's parameters, and =block.timestamp=; the set and the
    parameters all route through =_update= / =_activate= /
    =deactivateFromBuck= / =updateCredit=, every one of which fires the
    hook, and the timestamp is constant within a block.  It also keeps
    the expensive part (the scan) while paying for the cheap part (one
    staticcall) every time.

    Recommendation: do *not* ship this now.  Constraint (2) says
    correctness and simplicity beat cost, and L1 is strictly simpler.
    Keep L3 on file as the answer to "can the credit scan be cached
    safely?", which is yes, in this exact shape and no other.

** R -- Reappraisal

*** R2  Forbid reappraisal below activated coverage   [recommended]

    #+BEGIN_SRC solidity
    require(newFaceValue >= c.activatedValue.asUint(),
            "BuckCredit: face below activated coverage");
    #+END_SRC

    One line, and it deletes D3 at the root rather than papering over its
    consequences.  It also matches the economics the design already
    states: activated coverage is a *purchased policy*, paid for upfront
    with pool principal that funds its premium in perpetuity.  An insurer
    unilaterally writing it down is a breach of that contract, not an
    appraisal.  The insurer keeps every other lever -- =faceValue= down to
    the activated line, the depreciation schedule, the premium rate --
    and an asset that has genuinely lost value is what the *claim* path
    is for.

    With R2 the whole =_covSeconds= rescaling branch inside =updateCredit=
    becomes dead and can go too, along with its rounding.

*** R1  Clamp both ledgers together via a new hook

    =updateCredit= calls back into Buck to clamp =mintsBacked= alongside
    =activatedValue=.  Keeps the ledgers equal, but it does not answer the
    real question: the pool principal for the clamped-away coverage has
    already been paid, and the holder's drawn balance does not shrink
    with the limit.  Somebody has to absorb the difference, and making
    that "the holder, silently, by being pushed underwater" is what
    happens today.  If reappraisal-below-activated is ever genuinely
    needed, this needs a settlement rule -- most likely a pro-rata pool
    refund mirroring the burn path -- and that is a larger design than
    R2.

*** R3  Size the burn unwind off =min(mintsBacked, activatedValue)=

    A band-aid: it lets the burn proceed but leaves =mintsBacked= residue
    that still disqualifies the token from future minting.  Worth adding
    anyway as a defensive clamp *alongside* R2, so that any divergence
    from a source not yet thought of degrades into "burnable" rather than
    "stuck".

** B -- What balanceOf means

*** B2  =balanceOf= = held only; add =spendableOf=   [recommended]

    #+BEGIN_SRC solidity
    /// ERC-20 balance: BUCK this account actually holds, net of demurrage.
    function balanceOf(address a) public view returns (uint256);

    /// What this account can move right now: held + unused NFT-backed
    /// headroom.  The transfer path checks this, not balanceOf.
    function spendableOf(address a) public view returns (uint256);
    #+END_SRC

    This is the change that best serves constraint (2), and it does so by
    *removing* work rather than adding it.  =balanceOf= becomes a pure
    function of the account's own packed slot: one SLOAD, one demurrage
    computation, no NFT scan, no BUCK_K read, no external calls at all.
    There is nothing left in it to cache, and nothing left in it to get
    stale.  The expensive live scan moves to =spendableOf=, which is
    consulted in exactly one place -- the solvency check inside
    =_nonCarryingTransfer= -- where it has to be live anyway.  That is
    constraint (3) landing exactly where it was aimed: the cost sits on
    the credit draw, not on every read of the token.

    It also retires D4.  No integration can be told an account holds BUCK
    that nothing put there.

    Blast radius, measured rather than assumed: no consumer in =src/=
    changes behaviour, because every in-tree =balanceOf= call site reads a
    Carrying pool (see D4).  What changes is what wallets and third-party
    contracts see for a *user* account with credit.

    Two things to state plainly rather than discover later:

    - A wallet showing =balanceOf= would stop showing available credit.
      That is arguably correct -- headroom is a facility, not a balance --
      and =spendableOf= is the number for a wallet to display.
    - =transfer= would succeed for amounts above =balanceOf=.  This does
      not violate ERC-20 (the standard constrains when a transfer must
      revert, not the converse), but an integration that does its own
      =require(balanceOf(x) >= amt)= pre-check would block a legitimate
      credit draw.  Under-reporting is the safe direction to be wrong in;
      over-reporting, which is today's behaviour, is not.

*** B1  Keep =held + unusedCredit=, document loudly

    Zero code, zero risk, and the hazard stays.  The honest version of
    this option is that it is only tenable if BUCK is never composed with
    contracts that were not written for it -- which is the opposite of
    the stated goal.

*** B3  Also remove the implicit credit draw from =transfer=

    Require an explicit =mint= to draw; make =transfer= check held balance
    only.  The cleanest ERC-20 story, and it makes every draw pass
    through the allocator that does the =mintsBacked= bookkeeping.  It is
    also the largest behavioural change in this document and it makes
    ordinary spending a two-step for credit users.  Not proposed now;
    noted because B2 is a strict prerequisite for it, so B2 keeps the
    door open.

* Proposed change set

  In dependency order.  Each step is independently shippable and
  independently testable.

  | # | change                                              | risk   | note                                        |
  |---+-----------------------------------------------------+--------+---------------------------------------------|
  | 1 | R2: =newFaceValue >= activatedValue=                | low    | one require; kills D3 at the root           |
  | 2 | R3: defensive =min()= clamp in =_allocateBurn=      | low    | belt and braces for step 1                  |
  | 3 | T1: block =_update= on activated coverage           | low    | closes D1, the only unbacked-BUCK defect    |
  | 4 | L1: delete the credit-limit cache and the hook      | medium | pure deletion; removes the guard exemption  |
  | 5 | B2: split =balanceOf= / =spendableOf=               | medium | retires D4; makes =balanceOf= uncacheable-by-construction |
  | 6 | retire =storedLimit=; add =tokensOfOwner= bulk view | low    | hygiene and allocator cost                  |

  Steps 1-3 are the correctness fixes and should go together.  Steps 4-5
  are the simplification constraint (2) asks for, and they are much
  easier to review once 1-3 have pinned the invariant.

** Tests that change

  - =BuckCreditLimit.t.sol::test_cache_invalidates_on_NFT_transfer= and
    the sibling cache tests: deleted with step 4.  The NFT-transfer test
    also force-activates before transferring, so it needs step 3 anyway.
  - =BuckCreditHarness.forceActivate= breaks =mintsBacked ==
    activatedValue= by design.  With steps 1 and 3 in place it can push
    an NFT into a state production cannot reach.  Either make it
    increment Buck's =mintsBacked= too, or restrict its use to fixtures
    that never transfer or burn.
  - =test/BuckReviewProbe.t.sol= and =test/CreditProbe.t.sol= are
    characterization probes: they assert today's behaviour, defects
    included.  Invert them into regression tests as each step lands, and
    delete the files when the list is empty.

** Invariants worth asserting once the change set is in

  1. =mintsBacked[tid] == activatedValue(tid)= for every token, at every
     observation point.  Currently a comment; should be an invariant test.
  2. =activatedValue(tid) > 0= implies the token cannot change owner.
  3. =sum over holders of creditLimit(h)= is a pure function of live NFT
     state and BUCK_K, with no path-dependence -- i.e. re-deriving it from
     scratch always agrees with any incremental accounting.
  4. =balanceOf(a)= makes no external call.
