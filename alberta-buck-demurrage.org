#+begin_abstract
Held and issued-but-unspent BUCK pay demurrage on the BUCK remaining in the
account. Each
BuckCredit position earns Jubilee relief on the BUCK it actually issued,
proportional to issuance and elapsed time. The total gross demurrage basis is
all outstanding BUCK, including BUCK held by Jubilee. The fund receives the
charges and may invest its assets; it pays the relief when issuance is
retired. Gross demurrage includes charges paid by Jubilee to itself, so
cumulative gross demurrage and BUCK actually held by the fund are distinct.
#+end_abstract

* Status

This is the intended monetary design, clarified through 2026-10-29. The executable
[[file:alberta_buck/sim/demurrage_reference.py][Python reference model]] and its tests exercise the mathematics below. The
current Solidity contract and Rust mirror still use a linear charge on a
holder's gross raw balance and advance Jubilee on issuance; they do not yet
implement the model in this document. Their existing tests establish the old
behavior, not conformance with the new one. The detailed implementation audit
is [[file:doc/review/net-demurrage-analysis.org][Net-balance demurrage: audit and redesign]]. The proposed division of
responsibility among Buck, each credit NFT, Jubilee and each insurer's pool
is in [[file:doc/review/credit-facility-accounting-design.org][Buck, credit facilities, Jubilee, and insurer pools]].

Earlier versions of this document described linear demurrage on the holder's
raw balance and contained older storage sketches. Those formulas are
superseded; the previous text remains available in the Git history.

* Issuance, holdings, and relief

A BuckCredit authorizes credit; BUCK actually issued into existence form the
demurrage base and earn relief. When a facility issues BUCK into an account's
issued-but-unspent balance, they are already issued and pay demurrage even
before they are transferred. Merely potential capacity that has not been
issued is not BUCK. While issuance remains outstanding, the corresponding
net BUCK are in issued-but-unspent balances, ordinary accounts, pool or other
service accounts, or Jubilee. The underlying asset's insured coverage and a
user's not-yet-issued capacity are different quantities from outstanding
issued BUCK.

Let =I(t)= be total outstanding BUCK issuance, =r= the nominal annual rate,
and =P_j(t)= the outstanding issuance attributable to credit position =j=.
As issuance changes, the system checkpoints the old base and starts
integrating the new base. Over an interval,

#+begin_example
BUCK-years issued       = integral(I(t) dt)
gross demurrage         = r * integral(I(t) dt)
position j BUCK-years   = integral(P_j(t) dt)
position j target relief = r * integral(P_j(t) dt)
#+end_example

BUCK-years are the integral of outstanding issued BUCK, including issued but
unspent BUCK, not the age of a potential credit limit. When an issuance position is reduced, its
target relief is computed from its BUCK-years. Jubilee first redeems a
proportion of its investment positions and computes the actual relief from
its available full-value BUCK; the account repays the remaining principal.
Actual relief can be lower or higher than the target, but is bounded by the
principal being reduced and by the available net BUCK. In the reference, a partial
reduction takes a pro-rata share of that position's accumulated BUCK-years.
Separate positions retain separate issuance histories. For issuance held for
50 years at a nominal 2% per year, the target reaches the entire position; the
actual payment depends on the fund and cannot exceed the position.
Gross issuance-seconds continue accumulating for system demurrage after the
target reaches that cap. Claimable position seconds are capped before new
principal is added, so excess age beyond 50 years cannot immediately relieve
newly issued BUCK. The reference keeps one average age within each NFT; after
new issuance, the combined principal accrues toward its combined cap.

An account's received/held BUCK, issued-but-unspent BUCK, and the outstanding
issuance of each NFT it owns are not interchangeable. Its ERC-20 =balanceOf=
reports the full spendable balance: held plus issued-but-unspent net BUCK for
an ordinary account. Separate views can expose each amount. Spending issued
BUCK does not issue it again, and receiving BUCK does not automatically retire
an NFT. Relief reduces a liability only when it
is paid. The target and the actual relief computed after liquidation must
each be visible; a target shortfall is not an additional guaranteed claim.

* Demurrage on remaining balances

Demurrage already transferred to Jubilee no longer belongs to the former
holder and is not charged again to that holder. Held and issued-but-unspent
BUCK follow the same net-balance rule. For an idle account with a
remaining balance =H= and no other flows, the Python reference uses a nominal
continuous rate:

#+begin_example
  dH/dt = -r H
  H(t)  = H(0) * exp(-r t)
#+end_example

The reference uses continuous compounding because the contract has a
per-second nominal rate and a result must not depend on the frequency of
permissionless settlement. If a future implementation instead chooses an
effective annual rate or another schedule, that convention and its rounding
must be specified and used consistently in contracts, models, and tools.

New incoming BUCK join the remaining balance at their arrival time. Outgoing
BUCK leave the balance at their outgoing time. Subsequent charges apply to the
resulting net holdings, never to fees already transferred out. For example, an
account with 0.5 BUCK remaining incurs approximately 0.009901 BUCK of
continuous demurrage over the next year at 2%; it does not pay 2% of a much
larger historical raw balance.

The fund's BUCK also experience demurrage. If the fund holds BUCK directly, its
demurrage is transferred to the fund itself: it counts in total gross
demurrage, but the self-transfer has no net effect on fund assets. A fund
position in a basket or desk requires separate accounting for the underlying
BUCK, beneficial ownership, realizable asset value, and its returns. It cannot
assume that a mark or a cumulative demurrage figure is immediately spendable
BUCK.

* The 100 BUCK, 50-year example

One credit issues 100 BUCK into an ordinary account's unspent balance. Its
ordinary held balance starts at zero and =balanceOf= reports the full spendable
unspent amount. Nothing is transferred, invested, retired, or added for 50
years. At a nominal continuous 2% rate,
the Python reference produces, ignoring rounding:

| Years | Unspent / balanceOf | Jubilee BUCK | Gross demurrage | Jubilee self-demurrage | Target relief |
|-------+---------------------+--------------+-----------------+------------------------+--------------|
|     0 |     100.000000 |     0.000000 |        0.000000 |               0.000000 |     0.000000 |
|     1 |      98.019867 |     1.980133 |        2.000000 |               0.019867 |     2.000000 |
|    25 |      60.653066 |    39.346934 |       50.000000 |              10.653066 |    50.000000 |
|    49 |      37.531110 |    62.468890 |       98.000000 |              35.531110 |    98.000000 |
|    50 |      36.787944 |    63.212056 |      100.000000 |              36.787944 |   100.000000 |

The 100 BUCK gross demurrage is the sum of 63.212056 transferred from the
unspent balance and 36.787944 paid by Jubilee to itself. The latter is counted
as a charge, but does not create an additional 36.787944 BUCK in the fund.
Unspent balance plus fund still hold 100 BUCK, matching outstanding issuance.
Crediting Jubilee with all 100 *in addition* to leaving 36.787944 unspent would
create 36.787944 BUCK without a source.

The target relief at year 50 is 100, but the fund directly holds only
63.212056 in this no-return, no-transfers example. It needs another
36.787944 BUCK to pay the full target. Acquiring that amount from the owner,
for example by selling other assets or realizing returns, would leave 100 in
the fund; a 100 BUCK Jubilee payment can then extinguish the credit. The
external consideration and market price are not represented by the closed
BUCK ledger. Under the available-relief rule the actual relief is computed
from the fund's net liquid BUCK after any proportional redemption; the
borrower pays the remainder. No separate fixed 100 BUCK payout is assumed.

* Fund investments and liquidity

Jubilee may put its assets to work in the BUCK-maximizing BuckBasket and,
when available, desk implementations that seek other assets such as USDC.
A useful accounting implementation must distinguish:

- cumulative gross demurrage, including Jubilee's self-payments;
- liquid BUCK owned by the fund;
- deployed positions, their acquisition cost, current mark and realizable
  proceeds after liquidity, price impact and transaction costs;
- other assets and the BUCK they can actually acquire;
- each credit position's accrued and payable relief; and
- the cash and positions reserved for anticipated relief payments.

For the fund's cash ledger, separate cumulative 256-bit counters can record
full-value demurrage received, relief paid, BUCK invested, and net BUCK
withdrawn. With no other flows, liquid net BUCK equals received demurrage plus
investment withdrawals, less relief payments and investments. Other inflows
and outflows are recorded separately. Demurrage paid by the fund to itself
cannot be added to net incoming cash a second time. A running average cost
basis on the pooled investment additionally identifies gains or losses only as
the corresponding shares are withdrawn. The net of either pair of cumulative
counters can be negative after gains; unsigned totals must not be subtracted
without signed or otherwise guarded arithmetic.

When a BuckCredit position is reduced, the fund first redeems a proportional
slice of its positions, then computes relief from the net BUCK in the fund.
Even a redemption returning zero BUCK leaves a well-defined available relief,
possibly zero. The borrower supplies the difference; if the borrower lacks
that amount, the reduction cannot complete. A modeled investment return must
have a source, such as a transfer from a counterparty, a realized external
asset sale, or new, separately recorded issuance. It must not appear solely
because the fund paid demurrage to itself. The reference includes an idealized
BUCK-only strategy that pays demurrage into fund cash and liquidates at its
current mark, plus returns transferred explicitly from a counterparty. The
strategy's mark falls as its BUCK pay fees, while the same fee increases fund
cash: counting the mark decrease alone as a loss of total fund value would be
misleading. The reference does not model actual basket prices, collateral
values, transaction costs, delay, or market depth.

The original available-relief experiment scales the target by post-redemption
net cash divided by a 10% reserve target, capped at net cash and principal.
That signal can mistake uninvested cash or redemption proceeds for returns.
In a two-facility idle example it lets the first reducer receive the entire
fund, leaving the second unable to close. It is retained only as a comparison,
not a safe contract rule.

A proportional rule, now the reference default, allocates the fraction =f= of pre-sale cash plus
the actual proceeds from selling fraction =f= of the position, capped by the
principal. Its default =f= is the selected facility's baseline target divided
by all outstanding capped targets. It computes relief from realized funds and
does not mistake a cash reserve for investment ROI; with two identical idle
facilities it lets each receive its half. The 10% reserve can remain an
investment/liquidity target without being the payout multiplier. Neither rule
has yet been validated against actual basket liquidity, costs, or adversarial
ordering. See the [[file:doc/review/credit-facility-accounting-design.org][facility design]] for the concrete comparison.

The simulator permits the fraction to be supplied explicitly. A fixed-2%
quote and retirement are retained only as comparison diagnostics.
The command-line reference defaults to the requested target relief's share of
total outstanding target relief and can also compare the share of total
issued BUCK. Credits of different ages can make these two fractions very
different. The reference sums its positions to obtain this benchmark; an
efficient contract implementation of the same aggregate remains to be designed.

* Account state and carrying services

The present contract uses one signed raw account balance and a timestamped
=BUCK-seconds= accumulator. Its positive side represents gross held BUCK and
currently uses the accumulator for the holder's accrued fee; its negative side
represents actual credit drawn and uses the accumulator to integrate the
outstanding lien for relief. Zero must have zero accumulated seconds. A sign
change must settle the old interpretation before recording the new one. This
is a description of the current code, not the proposed model: the current
account sign cannot represent held BUCK, issued-but-unspent BUCK and multiple
independent NFT liabilities at once. The proposed design stores the BUCK and
credit-facility responsibilities separately and attributes each NFT's relief
to that NFT. The signed view of an ordinary positive holding must never turn
negative merely because its accumulated fee exceeds its raw amount.

The existing Carrying mode lets service contracts, including AMM pools,
report nominal raw balances to preserve ERC-20 reserve accounting, while
passing a proportional accumulated fee with a transfer. These nominal amounts
are not necessarily the same as unencumbered economic value. The corrected
model must preserve a clear distinction between nominal pool balances,
remaining value, transferred fee attribution, and the fund's actual assets.
For Jubilee's own Carrying balance, a relief payment must deliver its full
net value. If the fund has 100 nominal BUCK with 20 BUCK of carried fee,
its net balance is 80. To deliver 40 net BUCK under proportional continuous
fee allocation, it sends 50 nominal BUCK carrying 10 of fee; 50 nominal and
10 of fee remain, or 40 net. The fund's already-credited self-demurrage must
not be credited again on payout. The actual on-chain rounding must follow the
chosen accumulator representation; the Python integer example is not yet a
derivation for Solidity's buck-seconds rounding.
The current sign encoding and Carrying rules must be replaced or adapted; they
do not justify charging an already transferred fee again.

Each upstream insurer has its own Carrying premium pool. An initial deposit
of 10 full-value BUCK is refunded as 10 nominal BUCK, with that pool's average
carried fee. This differs from Jubilee relief, which must deliver full net
value. A pool can invest and redeem its positions before paying; an insurer's
deposit and refund must not be mixed with another insurer's pool. A renewal
can settle an NFT's actual relief, update insurance terms, and optionally add
issuance atomically without first releasing its old coverage.

Delegated demurrage also needs an explicit net-balance treatment: its payer
must have enough actual remaining value, and the outcome must remain
conserved when a payer is depleted or multiple sponsees settle in different
orders. See the implementation audit for the existing lazy behavior.

The reference is an event-driven, floating-point monetary model, not an EVM or
fixed-point implementation. Its tests cover 1 to 1,000-year idle holdings,
changes to outstanding issuance, proportional reductions, separate NFT
positions, full spendable balances, insurer-pool refunds and atomic renewals,
small net balances, settlement-frequency independence, fund self-demurrage,
strategy returns and liquidation, zero proceeds, grossed-up Carrying relief,
conservation, and insufficient debtor funds.
Before contract deployment, implement
and cross-check fixed-point precision and rounding, supply and claim
conservation, settlement and retirement transitions, pool and delegated-fee
semantics, fund liquidity, and migration of any existing account history. The
five-year economy and sandboxes must subsequently use the implemented rules;
existing runs use the old contract and do not validate this design.
