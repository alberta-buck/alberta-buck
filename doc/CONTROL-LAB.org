* Start with three prices

This is a sequence of small, reproducible experiments on the actual Solidity
contracts in pyrevm. Each arm lasts 120 days, with three TOKENs, one external
feed driver, and at most one actor of each enabled type. The aim is to see
what each mechanism does before trying to judge the complete system.

The initial feed resembles three-phase voltage, with a positive DC offset
because a TOKEN price cannot be negative:

\[
 p_i(t) = p_{i,0}\left[1 + 0.12\sin\left(2\pi t/30 + 2\pi i/3\right)\right],
 \qquad i=0,1,2.
\]

The base prices are all 100 USDC, the period is 30 days, and the phases differ
by 120 degrees. The sum of the three sine components is zero. With exactly
equal reference quantities, the external basket index would stay at one.
The contract's integer weights are 3333/3333/3334 bp: their actual reference
has a small ripple of at most 0.12 bp and a mean of one. Plots and agents
use the contract's actual quantities. This is a useful *differential-mode
control*, with a stated precision bound rather than a fabricated monetary
disturbance.

The =mixed= profile adds two common triangular pulses, a lasting ramp in
TOKEN C, and independent seeded Gaussian white noise with a standard deviation
of 0.2% of each token's baseline mean per observation. This noise remains
active even when the separate random-walk selector is zero. Use =three-phase=
with random walk zero for the clean baseline. The =shock= profile adds a
30% step to TOKEN A. These are selectable inputs, not separate economic
policies. The same input sequence is used in every arm of a profile.

* Bootstrap from known history

Every monitored arm starts with 1280 daily baseline observations, from
day -1280 through day -1. The calculation begins from the known mean prices
(100 USDC for each TOKEN here), then processes the past three-phase signal
to initialize all seven EMA levels. This supplies both a long anchor near
the known mean and the appropriate shorter trends at the experiment's
starting phase. It does not treat the first live price as equilibrium.

The selected triangles, ramps, steps and noise are excluded from this
history. All profiles share the same historical input for the same baseline
feed. Nothing from day zero or later is used. =bootstrap.history_days=
controls the history length, with a minimum of 1280 days in this lab.

Only the resulting historical filter state is imported into the contract;
the experiment's credit books and agents are not aged through those years.
The JSON retains the complete historical prices, means, integer filter
levels and source digest. Governance accepts the import before the first
live publication. The digest identifies the calculation; it does not prove
that historical prices were authentic. Imported history grants neither
current price freshness nor observed-day credit toward desk persistence.

Rejected samples and observation gaps preserve these levels. On recovery,
the monitor applies at most one daily update and clears velocity evidence
when continuity is missing. It never resets its anchor to a disturbed spot
price. The filter plots place the known historical mean at zero so the
three phases can be compared directly.

* Read the two price panes separately

The first pane puts everything in USD: external target prices are dashed,
actual TOKEN/USDC pool prices are dotted, and TOKEN/BUCK prices multiplied by
BUCK/USDC are solid. All are divided by the corresponding base price.
This pane shows whether the routes agree about the TOKEN's external value.

Starting with the monitor arm, the second pane shows the three native
TOKEN/BUCK price indices. This is where the monetary response is easiest to
see. The difference between the first two panes is the changing value of
BUCK in USDC; it is not automatically an arbitrage opportunity.

The subsequent panes add the reference basket cost, sensor excursions,
K and its P/I contributions, the desk's account and limits, private flow,
and finally basket allocation. The price and common-mode axis ranges are
fixed within each profile so a quiet stage cannot look dramatic merely
because its plot was rescaled.

The basket reference has fixed quantities. An increase in its USD cost
therefore does not imply that BUCK should still cost one USDC. The private
agents compare BUCK/USDC with that changing external basket value.

* The small world and its clock

All arms seed 1 million units of quote-side active liquidity in each pool
at the known mean prices. Before observation begins, funded bootstrap swaps
put both TOKEN/USDC and TOKEN/BUCK at the same unperturbed day-zero phase:
100, approximately 110.39, and approximately 89.61. BUCK/USDC starts at one.
The pools then age through the TWAP window. Thus the first experiment step
does not manufacture an arb opportunity by suddenly moving only one venue
from three identical prices. Bootstrap swaps and their fees are excluded
from experiment flow totals but retained in the initial balance snapshot.

The passive basket receives TOKEN quantities worth 1 million USD at the
known means; the separate desk receives 400,000 USD of each TOKEN on the
same basis. Bootstrap liquidity belongs to an external LP;
it is not created by the basket response being tested. Unused actors can be
deployed and funded without being scheduled. This preserves the same
initial pools and contract addresses across the controlled branches.

The private buyer starts with 1 million USDC. The issuer has 1 million BUCK
of fixed-face activated credit, whose available amount depends on K. They
each have a desired flow of 5,000 native units per day at fair value, with
a 25,000-unit daily cap, divided by ticks per day. Buyer units are USDC spent;
issuer units are BUCK sold. Let =premium = BUCK/USDC / external_basket - 1=.
The buyer's adverse deviation is =premium= and the seller's is =-premium=:

\[
 R_{day}=\min(R_{max},R_{par}\exp(-adverse/b)),\qquad b=0.01.
\]

Thus a buyer facing a 1% premium still buys at about 37% of its normal rate;
a seller facing a 1% discount still sells at about 37%. A favourable price
increases the rate up to the cap. Each swap is also limited by actual
remaining cash or spendable credit. The configuration exposes
=rate_at_par_per_day=, =backoff_bp= and =max_per_day= for each agent.
These agents create understandable accumulation/divestment pressure without
modelling a mortgage, investment return or alternating debt lifecycle.
The buyer holds its purchases. The issuer only issues and sells; automatic
repayment, refinancing, insolvency and liquidation are outside this small
actor's model. Buying BUCK to hold it is not an accounting burn.

There are four market steps per day. At each step:

1. Advance the clock and move the external TOKEN/USDC pools to the new feed.
2. Run the bounded arbitrage actor, then the enabled private actors, then
   another bounded arbitrage pass. Buyer/issuer order alternates by step.
3. Probe the actual sensor immediately. A failed read is recorded and
   published as unavailable; a successful probe does not preempt the day's
   scheduled observation with the old TWAP.
4. Advance another 601 seconds, allowing the 600-second TWAP to cover the
   resulting market. Turn the policy wheel: K, desk, optional basket work,
   and the shared sensor, with their real scheduling/dependency checks.
5. Run a bounded arbitrage pass and record the pool prices, publications,
   controller state, accounts, flows and gas in JSON.

The JSON includes a starting sample and the 120-day endpoint: 481 frames.
Time labels identify the market step; the final snapshot is taken 601
seconds later. A just-completed desk trade has immediate spot impact but
does not instantly rewrite the historical TWAP. Its feedback into K is
therefore visible on subsequent steps.

K's integration horizon is explicitly one market step (six hours here),
identical across arms. This is a deliberate laboratory configuration; it
does not inherit the earlier simulation's one-hour catch-up clamp. The
monitor itself retains its 30-minute eligibility and one-hour freshness
settings. A six-hour calling schedule leaves it stale between calls; the
wheel must satisfy its observation dependency before using it.

Gas is recorded from EVM receipts, but transactions have no economic gas
price in this backend. The external driver has ample fixture funding and
represents an imposed outside market. Its capital is not a profitability
claim about the system under test.

* Account for monetary flow even in the unperturbed price experiment

The synthetic three-phase feed has no imposed common monetary shock. That
does not make the freely trading market flow-neutral. The arb is configured
to retain profit in BUCK; it has no spender or reinvestment rule in this
lab. V3 fees also accumulate outside active liquidity until collected.
Both can make BUCK dear and lower bvib while the external basket is steady.
The new balance pane shows arb custody, changes in pool BUCK outside active
depth, and the decrease in active BUCK reserves. At fixed liquidity the
pool-minus-virtual change is uncollected BUCK fees; changing LP positions
also changes the finite-range virtual offset.

In the original =5236410= K-only run, arb custody reached 16,781.89 BUCK and
uncollected BUCK fees increased by 4,925.85 over 120 days. Actual pool BUCK
fell by exactly the arb custody increase. This accounted for the apparent
unexplained contraction. K moved from 0.75 to 0.78385 in response to raw
bvib averaging 0.99507. Its integral did not cause that price path: the
fixed-K linked arm had the same prices.

Two startup defects were separate from this continuing flow: all venues
now start at the correct phase, and reference quantities now match their
rounded declared weights. Previously the quantity rescaling plus the new
leg's rounding remainder made the reference cost 1.0001 at the supplied
mean prices. The correction uses the original anchor prices, never current
spot prices. The old and new manifests preserve this distinction.

V3's oracle still quotes integer ticks. The replay's raw bvib can therefore
differ from its exact spot reference by less than about one basis point;
K integrates that small measured error. This quantization is recorded and
bounded by regression tests. No normalization of the plotted bvib or
resetting of K is used to hide it. Profit spending, fee collection and
reinvestment are explicit future model variants, not implicit money flows.

* Rejected valuations can still be arbitrage opportunities

An excessive spot/TWAP difference rejects a price as a trusted valuation
input. It does not suppress arbitrage. The separate arbitrage executor
reads current pool prices, attempts a bounded cycle, and requires positive
realized profit after pool fees within the same atomic transaction. A
displaced TOKEN/BUCK or TOKEN/USDC pool can therefore be traded immediately,
even while the shared valuation sensor is unavailable. The regression tests
exercise both cases with real swaps and verify positive BUCK receipts.

The sensor currently validates TOKEN/BUCK pools; TOKEN/USDC disturbances
reach it through market transmission, while the arbitrage executor reads
both venues directly. The lab records completed cycles and their BUCK
profit. These receipts exclude gas costs and do not model competition for
the same opportunity.

The desk takes a directional position instead of closing a profitable
cycle. It therefore keeps its live valuation, depth and capital checks.
Whether a large price move is a fleeting dislocation or a lasting repricing
matters to that position. Future work can add an explicit dislocation signal
or scheduling priority without pretending a rejected price is reliable
monetary-policy evidence.

The campaign configuration is [[file:control-lab-campaign.toml][control-lab-campaign.toml]]:

| Selector | Values | Meaning |
|----------+--------+---------|
| Profile | three-phase, mixed, shock | Existing baseline; pulses/ramp/noise; temporary external repricing |
| Random walk | 0, 0.5, 1 | Amplification of the same seeded independent walk for each TOKEN |
| Buyer flow | low, medium, high | 2,500 / 5,000 / 10,000 USDC per day at fair value |
| Seller flow | low, medium, high | 2,500 / 5,000 / 10,000 BUCK per day at fair value |

For each token, the walk evolves as =W[n] = W[n-1] + sigma * sqrt(dt) * Z[n]=,
with =W[0] = 0=, independent standard-normal innovations and =dt= in days.
The feed is multiplied by =exp(amplification * W[n])=. At full strength,
=sigma = 0.01=: daily log-price volatility is 1%. This is a driftless walk in
log space; exponentiation gives price levels a small positive expectation
drift. It is not a mean-reverting disturbance. The seed and innovations are
shared across all stages, profiles and amplifications. Bootstrap history
excludes the walk, and amplification zero exactly retains the original feed.
Mixed-profile white noise remains a separate, non-cumulative perturbation.

The distinction matters when interpreting parity. Both kinds of randomness
use signed Gaussian draws; no positive-only noise is subtracted. White noise
is a fresh displacement at each observation. A walk accumulates its draws,
so a negative displacement persists until later increments reverse it.
Zero expected log drift does not require any particular path, its time
average, or the three-token basket to stay at its initial level. Independent
walks also break the cancellation of the three unperturbed sine waves.

For the saved seed =41404=, full-strength walk multipliers at day 120 are
1.007334, 0.868639 and 1.000700 for TOKENs A, B and C respectively. TOKEN B's
13.14% decline relative to its clean path dominates the basket's endpoint.
The saved three-phase runs give these means over all 481 observations:

| Walk | External reference basket, USD | Replay + K bvib | All actors + desk bvib |
|------+--------------------------------+-----------------+-----------------------|
|    0 |                       1.000000 |        0.999947 |              0.999637 |
|  0.5 |                       0.978617 |        0.978568 |              0.999408 |
|    1 |                       0.958608 |        0.958560 |              0.999113 |

The last column uses medium buyer and seller flows. In replay, BUCK/USDC
is held at one, so bvib inherits the falling reference dollar value. With
both private flows and the desk available, BUCK's dollar price follows that
reference much more closely and bvib remains near one. The external basket's
dollar value and its cost in BUCK are different quantities.

For a future experiment asking specifically for random fluctuations around
an unchanged baseline, use white noise or a mean-reverting process as an
explicit alternative. Keep the walk as a persistent-displacement test.
Subtracting this complete run's average would use future observations and
artificially remove the monetary disturbance being tested. Multiple seeds
are needed to distinguish a systematic response from this particular path.

These quantities retain the same exponential price backoff, 25,000-unit
daily caps and finite initial cash/credit as the original lab. K gains and
desk policy remain fixed in this first campaign. A high desired rate can
exhaust an actor's resources during the run; the private-flow pane makes
that visible. No funding is silently replenished.

** Executed trades on the price panes

The triangle convention follows the project's earlier
[[file:../alberta_buck/sim/plot_rebalancing.py][rebalancing simulation plots]]: an upward triangle means an executed TOKEN
buy and a downward triangle means an executed TOKEN sell. Desk buys/sells
are green/red, basket buys/sells are teal/orange, and arbitrage buys/sells
are purple/pink. A grey diamond means the basket paired existing TOKEN
with BUCK into liquidity; that operation does not include a TOKEN swap.
Sensor votes and portfolio valuation updates are not trade markers.

Markers appear on both the USD and native-BUCK token price panes. Each
marker combines fills in the same observation, token, actor and direction,
at their volume-weighted execution price. The USD pane converts that price
using the observation's closing BUCK/USDC quote; it is not an additional
USD trade. Arbitrage markers show the TOKEN/BUCK leg. The opposite
TOKEN/USDC leg appears in the execution log. The six-hour observation
resolution does not imply simultaneous transactions: the log retains
=after-feed=, =after-agents=, =policy= and =after-policy= phases and transaction
ordering. Raw JSON also retains log indices and exact integer trade amounts.

Click a legend item to hide dense arbitrage markers while inspecting the
desk or basket. Hover a marker for the token, fill count and quantities;
click it to select the observation and open the execution log. The chart's
arrow-key cursor also selects the log's day. Run totals distinguish zero
operations from old datasets that did not record executions.

The records come from successful transaction receipts: =MonetaryOperation=,
=Responded=, =Cycled= and each constituent pool's =Swap=. Buy/sell directions
come from actual signed pool amounts, not the proposed operation or sensor
signal. In particular, the desk supplies BUCK by buying TOKEN, and absorbs
BUCK by selling TOKEN. LP pairing and valuation-only response events are
recorded separately from swaps.

The initial mixed, zero-walk, medium-flow runs illustrate the distinction:
the desk-only arm executes 11 monetary operations containing 12 TOKEN buys
and 21 TOKEN sells. The basket arm executes 12 TOKEN buys, 10 TOKEN sells,
99 LP pairings and 3,046 triangle-arbitrage cycles; its desk executes zero
operations. The two private flows keep the desk mostly inside its 3% entry
band. These are measured executions, not missing graph activity. Receipt
instrumentation leaves the original price paths and account balances intact.

The instrumented campaign completed in 29.5 minutes with ten workers.
All 351 runs match baseline =7554cc4= frame-for-frame after excluding the
new event records. The receipt audit accounts for 987,604 arbitrage cycles,
11 desk operations and 2,133 basket TOKEN swaps across the complete selector
grid. All 24 Python checks and browser checks for all three profiles pass.

*** Clean three-phase case: the recurring harvest is missing

With zero walk and medium private flows, the basket makes six TOKEN buys
and six TOKEN sells in 120 days. All twelve swaps occur by day 20.25:

| TOKEN | Actual buy days | Actual sell days |
|-------+-----------------+------------------|
| A | none | 15, 15.5 |
| B | 20, 20.25 | 5, 5.25, 5.5, 5.75 |
| C | 10, 10.25, 10.5, 10.75 | none |

There are also 92 LP pairings, 3,197 arb cycles and zero desk operations.
These swaps are concentrated in the initial deployment/allocation transient;
the later repeating signal does not produce repeating active rebalancing.
Passive LP inventory changes and fees are separate from this active policy.

TOKEN A illustrates why. It peaks at days 7.5, 37.5, 67.5 and 97.5, and
troughs at 22.5, 52.5, 82.5 and 112.5. The daily four-of-six EMA quorum

After day 30, the largest ungated absolute target gaps observed are only
1.10, 1.28 and 1.63 percentage points for A/B/C, respectively: all are below
the 2 pp trading band. Larger gaps occur while the trend gate forbids the
corresponding trade. The 30%-of-declared-weight leash is about 10 pp wide,
while observed deviations from declared weights are at most 3.2 pp, so
it does not override that gate. Credit constraints need not be invoked
to explain this lack of allocation-driven trades.

This behavior follows the present policy rules, but it does not demonstrate
the intended repeated reversion-to-the-mean harvest. A clean periodic
acceptance experiment should compare recurring sell-high/buy-low behavior
after deployment settles, with fills, fees and inventory returns against
a matched passive LP and holding benchmark. An exact trade at each price
extremum is not a requirement for a causal controller. Waiting until the
excursion has mostly disappeared is nevertheless too late for this signal.
The filter/gate/band combination needs a measured comparison before tuning;
the baseline remains unchanged for that comparison.

appear at day 26, then recur across later cycles. Every token now has both
buy and sell executions. The band-only comparison retains leaned targets;
it removes only the trend veto, rather than changing to fixed targets.

This supports separating an actionable reversal gate from long-window
consensus. The fast candidate trades substantially less than the ungated
one while capturing recurring opportunities. The equity numbers are marks
including inventory/LP, with swap costs reflected in balances and gas
excluded; they are not realized or liquidation returns. The policies also
change credit use and private flows. Ending basket liens are approximately
712,228 / 158,474 / 401,473 BUCK respectively, and both alternatives nearly
exhaust the private issuer's spendable BUCK. Their larger bvib errors and K
changes therefore need joint analysis with issuance and deleveraging before
claiming superior whole-system equilibrium. No default gate is silently
selected from this one clean signal.

** Original Org / static-figure workflow

Edit [[file:control-lab.toml][control-lab.toml]]. Perturbations select TOKEN indices =[0,1,2]=,
and support =sine=, =triangle=, =ramp=, =step= and =noise=. Amplitudes are
fractions of the base price and add to the three-phase signal. A ramp holds
its final offset; a triangle returns to zero; a step ends at its duration.
Noise is Gaussian with the specified standard deviation. =independent=true=
gives each selected TOKEN its own draw; otherwise they share a common draw.
The seed, profile and complete configuration are retained with the results.
Nonpositive prices are rejected rather than silently clipped.

* What the baseline runs establish

Historical initialization removes the arbitrary phase bias. In the pure
three-phase monitor, the initial long anchors are approximately
-0.094%, +0.045% and +0.049% from the known centered means, in log units.
The previous first-observation initialization placed them at +0.36%,
+10.25% and -10.61%. Over the corrected run, all three excursion ranges
are close to -12.34% through +11.63% in log units, instead of assigning
TOKEN B a predominantly negative excursion and TOKEN C a positive one.

This correction does not remove the EMA quorum's lag. TOKEN A's first
four-of-six turn confirmation is at day 15, after its day-7.5 price peak.
The remaining delay is a filter/policy question, now distinguishable from
the initialization artifact. No timing optimization is included in this
baseline.

The controlled =linked= and =k= arms have *identical pool-price histories*
in both default profiles, even though K changes. This demonstrates the
missing actuator: changing credit capacity alone does not place a trade.

The unperturbed =monitor-k= calibration averages bvib 0.99994666, with a
mean oracle-minus-spot difference of -0.5332 bp. Its terminal K is 0.75035783
from an initial 0.75. The reference is now normalized; the remaining small
integral movement is the measured V3 tick bias. With arb enabled and no
private response, terminal K is 0.78245 because profit custody and LP fees
keep removing BUCK from active trading depth.

The intent-driven private agents produce opposite monetary pressures in
the pure three-phase case:

| Private actors | Mean bvib | Terminal K | USDC spent buying | BUCK sold |
|----------------+-----------+------------+-------------------+-----------|
| Buyer only | 0.97235 | 0.93854 | 60,584 | 0 |
| Issuer only | 1.02354 | 0.58964 | 0 | 79,445 |
| Both | 0.99964 | 0.75245 | 682,181 | 699,409 |

The buyer accumulates even after BUCK becomes dear, at a reduced rate; the
issuer keeps selling after BUCK becomes cheap. K responds with the intended
sign. Both actors together transact much more while keeping prices closer
to the reference. This demonstrates the apparatus's response to specified
flows, not a financial model of either actor's motivation.

In the mixed profile, the desk-only monetary arm executes eleven operations.
With both private agents, the desk executes none in this run. Private
responses keep the error below the desk's actionable region sufficiently
that adding it has no effect on the recorded price path. That outcome is
visible evidence about this parameterization, not a missing plotted trace.

The measured mean absolute parity errors for the mixed profile are about
1.14% with linked markets and K alone, 1.15% with the desk, 3.50% with only
the buyer, 2.78% with only the issuer, and 0.23% with both private actors.
Adding basket response gives 0.29%. The mixed buyer-only arm reaches K's
0.95 rail: it has no issuer to use the enlarged capacity. These are diagnostic comparisons on one
synthetic feed and seed, not an equilibrium-superiority or ROI acceptance
test. There are no depositor entry/exit populations in this lab.
