3. *Depositors own the gains.*
4. *Payouts are in TOKEN.*

* 2. What it replaces, and why

In the present basket, a receipt records a BUCK principal R, which is the
BUCK minted to pair its TOKEN.  Its claim is V = R x 2B/O, where B is the
depositors' BUCK side and O the total principal.  A redemption burns R and
pays min(V/2, V - R) in TOKEN; any BUCK surplus goes to the treasury.

| TOKEN moves (in BUCK) | holding it | depositor now             |
|-----------------------+------------+---------------------------|
| +21%                  | +21%       | +10% (treasury +10% of R) |
| -19%                  | -19%       | -20%                      |

The treasury takes the BUCK half's gains, and the depositor covers the BUCK
half's shortfalls.  The accounting underneath also has faults the equity
design removes by construction:

- *BUCK deposits count double.*  A BUCK deposit's principal is its whole
  value, where a TOKEN deposit's is half of its value.  A BUCK deposit and
  redemption pays TOKEN out of the other depositors' positions.
- *Entry is at face, not at value.*  A new deposit shares the pool's
  history at once: +5% after a 5% move.
- *Value is read from pool balances and at the spot.*  Balances include
  anyone's tokens, and the spot can be pushed by whoever is trading, within
  a 5% guard.

* 3. The machine on one page

** 3.1 State

| state         | what it is                                                                                  |
|---------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| wallet        | =idle[i]= TOKEN_i and =idleBuck=, placed by no one yet; counted, never read from a balance |
| positions     | one full-range position per pool; its liquidity =L[i]= is the basket's own count           |
| =debt=        | ONE pooled number: the BUCK the basket has minted and not burned                            |
| =pending=     | credit that deposits brought, not yet minted                                                |
| =S=           | shares outstanding                                                                          |
| =treasury=    | the basket's own shares (the 25% cut is paid in them)                                       |
| receipts      | per ERC-721: (=shares=, =basis=), where the basis is the BUCK value brought, less what has left |

** 3.2 Value (in BUCK, at each pool's TWAP P_i)

#+begin_example
  gross  G  = idleBuck + sum_i [ (idle_i + owedTok_i) P_i + owedBuck_i + 2 L_i sqrt(P_i) ]
  equity Q  = G - debt
  price  pi = Q / S
  headroom  = K Q - debt              (K of the day)
#+end_example

=2 L sqrt(P)= is the full-range position's fair value.  It is the value of
the reserves the position holds at the TWAP, so it cannot be moved by
pushing the spot.

** 3.3 Two verbs, four components

- *deposit(asset, amount)* books equity: it mints shares at the price, adds
  K x value to =pending=, and leaves the asset in the wallet.  Nothing is
  swapped and nothing is minted (section 4).
- *redeem(id, fraction)* takes the receipt's share of everything, pro rata.
  It burns its share of the debt first, gives the treasury its cut of the
  gain in shares, and pays TOKEN (section 5).
- *The wheel's components* do all the placing (section 7):
  - Sync: fees go to the wallet.
  - Deploy: idle TOKEN is paired with BUCK and added as liquidity.
  - Fund: idle BUCK and credit go to the neediest pool.
  - Rebalance: overweight liquidity goes back to the wallet.

** 3.4 The invariants (checked after every step of the prototype's scenario)

1. =minted - burned == debt=: every BUCK the basket minted is either
   outstanding in =debt= or burned.
2. =sum(receipt.shares) + treasury == S=.
3. =0 <= pending <= K x gross=.
4. At every mint, =debt + amount <= K x equity=.  This is checked only at
   the mint: after a price fall the debt may exceed K x equity, and that is
   no margin call (section 6).

* 4. Deposit

1. *Valuation, conservative both ways.*  The deposit is valued at the LOWER
   of its pool's spot and TWAP, and the basket it joins at the HIGHER of
   each pool's.  A lagging TWAP or a pushed spot then gives the entrant no
   edge in either direction (=test_a_lagging_twap_gives_the_entrant_no_edge=,
   TOKEN and BUCK, spot up and down).  A guard (5%) only bounds a
   manipulated pool; it no longer refuses ordinary lag.
2. *The deposit pays for its own deployment.*  Shares are minted on its
   value less the pool fee on the swap the wheel will make to pair it:
   - (1+K)/2 of a BUCK deposit, at the dearest pool's fee, because the Fund
     pool is not yet chosen;
   - (1-K)/2 of a TOKEN deposit, at its own pool's fee.

   At 0.3% and K = 0.75 that is 0.26% for BUCK and 0.04% for TOKEN.  Price
   impact is not charged: it grows with the deposit's size against the
   pools' depth, and stays shared.  A per-block deposit cap is the lever if
   it ever matters (=test_a_deposit_pays_for_its_own_deployment=).
3. *Shares* = net value x S / Q_high, or net value for the first deposit.
4. *Credit*: =pending += K x value=, with K the K of the day.
5. *The asset lands in the wallet*, and that is all.  TOKEN and BUCK
   deposits are identical: equity E, credit KE, and after the wheel places
   them a position of (1+K)E
   (=test_a_buck_deposit_is_the_same_equity_as_a_token_deposit=).

* 5. Redemption

In this order:

1. *The cut.*  gain = shares x pi - basis.  The treasury takes
   lambda x max(gain, 0) / pi shares (lambda = 25%), so the rest of the
   receipt's shares, =out=, leave.
2. *Withdraw the fraction f = out / S of everything*: the wallet's idle
   TOKEN and BUCK, every position's liquidity, and every owed fee.  This
   is pro rata, so it is fair by construction and needs no valuation.
3. *Burn f x debt first.*  If the BUCK withdrawn exceeds it, the residue
   is swapped into TOKEN, pro rata to the TOKEN being paid.  If it falls
   short, TOKEN is sold to cover the shortfall.  If even that cannot cover
   it, the redemption *refuses* (=Underwater=) rather than burn less than
   its share.
4. *Drop f x pending*: the exit takes its share of the credit not yet
   drawn.  Otherwise orphaned credit would accrue to the holders who stay.
5. *Pay TOKEN*: the withdrawn mix, one transfer per constituent.  A
   single-TOKEN payout is a router's job, at the redeemer's cost.

The exit's own swaps (residue or shortfall) are its own costs, because they
come out of its payout.  A redemption straight after a deposit takes back
at most what it brought (=test_a_round_trip_takes_back_only_its_own=), and
an exit leaves the others' price whole
(=test_a_pro_rata_exit_leaves_the_others_price_whole=).

- *The treasury* leaves by the same door with no cut (=redeemTreasury=,
  governance only).  Its shares carry their share of the debt like anyone's
  (=test_the_treasury_leaves_by_the_same_door=).
- *The stress fee* (CARRY-CONVEXITY D2) carries over as an exit fee.  The
  shares retire, but their fee fraction of the assets, and of the debt,
  stays with the holders who remain.  It is off by default; its condition
  (a duress exit while BUCK is dear) is the present shell's
  (=test_an_exit_fee_stays_with_the_holders_who_stay=).

* 6. Credit and K

- *Credit follows deposits.*  A deposit of E brings KE of =pending= at the
  K of its day.  The wheel mints it only as it places the deposit (Deploy
  and Fund), and never past =headroom = K x Q - debt= at the K of the day
  it mints.
- *Earnings compound unlevered.*  Fees and the wheel's captures are equity
  with no credit.  Rebalancing and exit residue never mint
  (=test_fees_are_equity_and_compound_unlevered=, =test_residue_never_mints=).
  So leverage drifts below K as the basket earns, and above it after a
  fall.
- *A K cut is no margin call.*  Nothing is called back; it only shrinks
  headroom.  New money gets only the room the smaller offer leaves, and the
  pooled debt rises back to K x Q (=test_a_K_cut_is_no_margin_call=).
- *A K rise re-offers the credit deposits brought.*  Pending credit mints
  again as headroom opens, and never beyond what the deposits brought.
- *The debt is pooled.*  Every share carries the same debt ratio, so
  deposits made at different K share one pool fairly: the difference
  between their K lives in the pending credit they brought, until it is
  drawn.
- *Supply integrity.*  Each exit burns its full share of the debt before
  anything is paid (=test_the_debt_is_whole_and_burned_before_any_payout=).
  Bad debt needs a TOKEN collapse below (K/(1+K))^2 of the entry price
  (18% at K = 0.75); before that, the redemption refuses
  (=test_an_underwater_redemption_refuses_rather_than_steals=).

* 7. The wheel's components

Each component is a wheel kind: slots, a =due= view and a =run=.  None of
them is a user's verb.  Every component works only on more than a *grain*
(1e-4 of the gross), so dust neither triggers work nor holds the basket
unsettled.

| component | slots | due when                                                         | run                                                                                                                 |
|-----------+-------+------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Sync      | N     | pool i's owed fees > grain (on chain: once per epoch)            | collect them into the wallet                                                                                        |
| Deploy    | N     | idle TOKEN_i > grain                                             | pair it with the wallet's BUCK, then with minted pending credit; sell part of the TOKEN only if both fall short; add liquidity to pool i |
| Fund      | 1     | no TOKEN waiting, and idle BUCK + mintable credit > grain        | take the neediest pool j (the largest target x (positions + available) - position); mint just enough; swap half of the amount into TOKEN_j (for Deploy) |
| Rebalance | N     | the basket is *settled* and pool i's weight > target + band      | unwind min(excess, step x position) of pool i to the wallet; sell its TOKEN for BUCK (for Fund)                     |

*Settled* means nothing worth placing waits in the wallet or as mintable
credit.  While Deploy and Fund are placing new money the weights are in
flux, and a Rebalance then would fight them.

How work flows:

| event          | the wheel's path                                        |
|----------------+---------------------------------------------------------|
| TOKEN deposit  | Deploy(i)                                               |
| BUCK deposit   | Fund -> Deploy(j), pool by pool, up to the targets      |
| fees           | Sync(i) -> Deploy(i) (-> Fund, for their BUCK)          |
| a price move   | Rebalance(i) -> Fund -> Deploy(j)                       |
| an arb capture | the wallet (TOKEN or BUCK) -> Deploy / Fund             |
| an exit        | none: redeem draws pro rata from the wallet and positions |

Deploy's swap belongs to the deposit, which paid the entry charge for it.
Rebalance's trades belong to the basket, like all rebalancing: a TOKEN
deposit that overweights its pool leaves Rebalance a job, and one that
underweights it saves Rebalance a job.  Fund sizes each step to the
neediest pool's gap, so a BUCK deposit is never dumped into one pool and
then unwound (=test_funding_fills_every_pool_to_target_without_churn=).

The targets and the band are the simple policy: equal weights and 2%.  The
director (=IRebalanceDirector=) can supply the policy instead.  Its
=redeemHint= and =effortOf= choose Rebalance's pool and size, its
=depositHint= chooses Fund's pool, and its deviation-times-MA timing
(=BASKET-REBALANCE.md=) gates Rebalance's =due=.

* 8. The split

lambda = 25% of the gain above the cost basis, at exit, in shares moved to
the treasury, and nothing on a loss.  Per unit of equity E at K = 0.75,
with the TOKEN moving by p (in BUCK) over the receipt's life and a harvest
h (fees, rebalancing, the wheel's captures) on the position:

- depositor equity = (1+K) E (sqrt p + h) - K E
- payout = equity - 0.25 x max(equity - E, 0)

| scenario              | holding | today (treasury) | equity | *payout* (basket) |
|-----------------------+---------+------------------+--------+-------------------|
| p = 1.00, h = +3%     |   100.0 | 103.0 (+3.0)     |  105.2 | 103.9 (+1.31)     |
| p = 1.21 (TOKEN +21%) |   121.0 | 110.0 (+10.0)    |  117.5 | 113.1 (+4.38)     |
| p = 0.81 (TOKEN -19%) |    81.0 |  80.0 (0)        |   82.5 | 82.5 (0)          |
| p = 0.81, h = +3%     |    81.0 |  86.0 (0)        |   87.8 | 87.8 (0)          |

The p rows with h = 0 are =test_the_design_payoffs=, and the arithmetic of
the cut is =test_the_basket_takes_a_quarter_of_the_gain_and_none_of_a_loss=.

The "fair" alternative, K/(1+K) = 43% of gains and losses, leaves the
depositor an unlevered LP and puts the basket in every loss.  It would
need a first-loss reserve, and BUCK it cannot burn beyond that.  It is not
built.  The credit's leverage on the harvest, (1+K)h, is the depositor's
reward.  The basket's is a quarter of every gain, on a levered harvest,
and it never carries a loss: its minted BUCK is burned first.

* 9. What the prototype taught

Each of these changed the design; each has its test.

1. *Pooled debt, not per-receipt debt.*  A single number, fair because
   every share carries the same debt ratio.  The K of each deposit's day
   lives in the pending credit it brought.
2. *Credit comes from deposits, not from value.*  Fees arrive in both
   tokens and would otherwise earn no credit, or would re-lever without a
   deposit.
3. *Exits take their share of pending credit.*  Without it, credit
   orphaned by early exits accrues to those who stay.
4. *The cap binds at the mint, not as an invariant.*  debt <= K x Q fails
   the moment prices fall, which is no margin call.
5. *Conservative entry.*  At the TWAP alone, a TOKEN deposit while the spot
   is below a lagging TWAP bought shares cheap.
6. *The entry charge.*  Without it, a BUCK deposit's deployment swaps cost
   the other holders about 0.26%.
7. *Fund sized to the gap.*  All-to-the-neediest dumped a BUCK deposit into
   one pool, and Rebalance then unwound two thirds of it.
8. *Rebalance only when settled.*  Otherwise it fights Fund while new money
   is still being placed.
9. *The grain.*  With a dust threshold, Sync collected crumbs every block,
   the wallet was never settled, and rebalancing starved: weights drifted
   to 0.28-0.38 against a 2% band.
10. *Explicit books.*  The wallet and the liquidity are the basket's own
    counters.  The desk's balances, stray transfers and liquidity others
    mint to the basket's position key are never counted.  Pool reads are
    for prices only.
11. *Price impact stays shared.*  It is quadratic in size over depth.
    Charging it would take per-deposit attribution in a pooled wallet,
    which is not worth it.

* 10. Building it in Solidity

** 10.1 Where it goes

- *A new shell, =BuckBasketEquity=*, on the shared =BuckBasketStorage=,
  replaces =BuckBasketProRata= as the base of =BuckBasketOps= (the desk).
  It keeps the constituents, the wiring, the receipt NFT, the director
  hook, the venue-facet dispatch and the desk.  It drops the allocator
  (=_allocateSellHigh=, =_allocateSingleToken=), =_bonusSlice= and
  =stressBonusPrincipal=, =treasuryLiquidity= and =treasuryBuckPending=
  with =sweepTreasury=, and =rebalanceStep= (now the Rebalance component).
  =creditDepositors= and =creditTreasury= become =creditWallet(token, amount)=.
- *The venue facet* (=BuckBasketUniswapV3=) supplies the pool primitives,
  built on the =wip/basket-accounting= pieces (=_positionBuck= is the
  position's fair value at the TWAP, =_virtualReserves= the swap sizing,
  and =withdrawLiquidity= returns principal only):

  | primitive                               | does                                                           |
  |-----------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
  | =positionMint(i, tok, buck)=            | add full-range L; return (L, tokUsed, buckUsed)                |
  | =positionBurn(i, L)=                    | remove L; collect exactly the principal burned                 |
  | =positionSync(i)=                       | burn(0) then collect everything owed; return (tok, buck)       |
  | =swapExactIn(i, sellBuck, amountIn, minOut)= | one swap in pool i                                        |
  | =sqrtPrices(i)=                         | (spot, TWAP) sqrtP: the valuation's only pool read             |

- *The components live in the basket*, as a second facet (=EquityWheelFacet=)
  if the shell's size demands it.  They move the basket's assets and need
  its V3 callbacks.  The wheel gets one thin kind, =EquityKind=
  (=src/wheel/UpkeepKinds.sol=), with 3N+1 slots.  Its =_due= calls
  =basket.wheelDue(c, i)= and its =_run= calls =basket.wheelStep(c, i)=
  (=onlyWheel=).  =BasketWheel= adds it to its bases and loses =SweepKind=.
  =ArbKind._distribute= calls =creditWallet=.
- *Size*: =BuckBasketOps= is 21.4 KB today, and the allocator it drops is
  the bulk of ProRata's 16.2 KB.  Measure after step 2 (section 10.9); the
  facet split is the fallback.
- *The Fence* (=BuckBasketFence=) stays on ProRata.  It is a separate
  experiment, and not this design's.

** 10.3 Entry points

- =deposit(address asset, uint256 amount, uint256 minShares) returns (uint256 id)=
  with =asset == buck= for BUCK.  It runs =_syncAll()=, then applies the
  guard, values the deposit conservatively, applies the charge, mints
  shares, adds =pendingCredit=, adds to the wallet and mints the receipt.
- =redeem(uint256 id, uint256 bp, uint256[] minOut) returns (uint256[] paid)=.
  It runs =_syncAll()=, then takes the cut, runs =_exit=, and checks
  =minOut= per TOKEN.
- =redeemTreasury(uint256 bp, address to)=, =onlyGov=.
- Views: =gross()=, =equity()=, =sharePrice()=, =valueOf(id)=, =headroom()=,
  =weights()=, =wheelDue(c, i)=.
- =wheelStep(uint8 c, uint256 i) onlyWheel returns (uint256 work)= and
  =creditWallet(address token, uint256 amount) onlyWheel=.
- Governance: =setEquityParams(...)= and =setWheel=.

*Sync on touch.*  deposit and redeem first collect every pool's owed fees
into the wallet (=positionSync=).  The price then includes them exactly,
with no fee-growth math on chain, and a redemption's pro-rata share of the
wallet IS its share of the fees.  The Sync component compounds between
touches on a timer.  The views may add the uncollected fees
(feeGrowthInside) for the UI; that is optional.

*Donations.*  Liquidity minted to the basket's position key by others is
not in =liquidityOf= and is never counted.  Seed the first deposit from
governance (dead shares) against share-price inflation.

** 10.4 The primitives' rules

- =_mint(amount)=: =amount = min(amount, pendingCredit, headroom)=, then
  =buck.mintFromBasket(this, amount)=, =debt +==, =idleBuck +==.
  =require(debt <= K x equity)= after.
- =_burn(amount)=: =buck.burnFromBasket=, =debt -==.
- Every swap is =swapExactIn= with a =minOut= from the TWAP less a
  tolerance.  Deploy and Fund sizes are small against depth, so a failed
  bound makes a =run= return 0 and the wheel moves on.

** 10.5 Invariants (Foundry invariant tests; a handler for deposit, redeem, wheel ticks and price moves)

1. =debt= equals the equity path's mints less its burns (the handler's
   ghost counters; the desk's issuance is =monetaryOutstanding=, apart).
2. =sum(holdings.shares) + treasuryShares == totalShares=.
3. =idleToken[i] + monetaryTokenHeld[i] <= token_i.balanceOf(basket)= and
   =idleBuck + monetaryBuckHeld <= buck.balanceOf(basket)=.

* 12. The harvest, and the BUCK basis (proposed, 2026-09-29)

The design owner asked three things: why the prototype shows no yield,
even in strictly reverting markets; whether the multi-EMA rebalancing
strategy is still being built; and whether paying and holding BUCK would
make the arbitrage and the reweighting simpler, cheaper and able to work
together.  The answers are measured by =alberta_buck/sim/basket_harvest.py=
over three synthetic worlds, each TOKEN independent with daily volatility
of about 1.5%:

- *fast*: an OU log price with a 10-day half-life, where the reversion
  lives in the noise;
- *cycle*: slow damped cycles (a year's period, 15% swings) with momentum,
  plus fast noise, where the reversion lives in the swings;
- *walk*: a random walk, where nothing reverts (the control).

Every basket starts from the same placed deposit (K = 0.75, 90% of each
pool its own).  An outside arbitrageur re-pins every pool to the edge of
its no-arbitrage band (the pool fee plus a 0.1% outside cost) each step,
after the wheel's tick.  The results are 8 seeds over 4 years, with 6-hour
steps and a 0.3% pool fee unless stated.  "Over holding" compares each
basket with its own starting holdings never traded.  "cmix" is those
holdings' value weights rebalanced every step at the outside price, at no
cost: all the rebalancing premium there is.

** 12.1 Why the prototype showed no reversion yield

*A full-range position is a constant-mix rebalancer that trades at stale
prices, and whoever re-pins the pool is paid its rebalancing.*  The pool
sells TOKEN as the price rises and buys as it falls, which is the reversion
harvest, but it does so at its own price.  The arbitrageur who moves it to
the outside price keeps the difference, which is the LP's loss-versus-
rebalancing (LVR), about sigma^2/8 of the position per unit of time.  In a
reverting market that is almost exactly the rebalancing premium: the
harvest goes to the arbitrageur, and the pool keeps only the fee on the
arbitrageur's volume.

For one pool (OU, 1.5% a day, a 20-day half-life), the premium is 1.0% a
year of the position:

| outside arbitrage | pool fee | pool keeps | the basket's own arbitrage keeps (best band) |
|-------------------+----------+------------+----------------------------------------------|
| once a day        | 0.3%     | 0.22%      | 0.90%                            |
| once a day        | 1%       | 0.49%      | 0.90%                            |
| every hour        | 0.3%     | 0.52%      | 0.75%                            |
| every hour        | 1%       | 0.79%      | 0.83%                            |

The finer the steps against the fee, the more the fee recovers by itself.
The coarser, the more only the basket's own arbitrage can.

The prototype's scenario (section 9.1) is the coarse case at its worst:
- one 2% jump a step, re-pinned exactly to the price by the outside
  arbitrage, against a 0.3% fee;
- no arbitrage of the basket's own;
- rebalancing through its own pools.  Every trade moved its own price for
  the outside arbitrageur to re-pin.
- Over short holdings, entry and exit costs, the 25% cut, and a comparator
  (holding the deposited TOKEN) dominated by relative price noise swamp the
  rest.

*A pool cannot harvest slow swings at all.*  A constant-mix rebalancer earns
the variance of short-horizon moves, the quadratic variation.  A smooth
cycle has almost none of it, however large its amplitude.  Swings are
harvested only by trades timed across pools: selling a leg near its top,
buying another near its bottom.  That is the director's work (12.3).

** 12.3 The multi-EMA director: what it is for

*Status.*  The Solidity =PairsRebalanceDirector= is built and is the sim's
default director in every pro-rata and ops run, driven by the
=DirectorKeeperAgent=.  It is a ladder of 7 EMAs per leg, from 5 to 320
days, with a quorum of 4 votes on a confirmed turn (=BASKET-REBALANCE.md=
2c).  The equity prototype did not use it: its Rebalance had fixed targets
and a 2% band.  The =TurnDirector= in =reserve_basket.py= ports the ladder
per leg (each TOKEN's log price against the basket's mean) and adds what
the experiment says it needs.

What the experiment says:

1. *The turn gate alone adds little on LP positions*: about 0 to +0.5% a
   year.  A pool's weight moves by only half its price move, and the pool
   already sells into every run within itself.
2. *The value is in leaning against the excursion.*  Each target is scaled
   by exp(-tilt x excursion), where the excursion is the leg's price against
   its anchor, and then renormalized.  The lean is worth +2.8% a year over
   the arbitrage alone in the fast world.
3. *The gate is the regime knob.*  Over the arbitrage alone, at tilt 1
   and 2, a year:

   | quorum (of 6 votes) | cycles     | fast reversion | walk        |
   |---------------------+------------+----------------+-------------|
   | 0 (ungated)         | -0.1, +0.3 | +2.7, +4.5     | 0.0, -0.3   |
   | 2                   | +1.2, +2.2 | +1.0, +1.6     | -0.1, -0.4  |
   | 4                   | +1.9, +3.7 | +0.2, +0.5     | -0.1, -0.4  |
   | 6                   | +0.8, +1.7 | 0.0, 0.0       | -0.2, -0.3  |

   In momentum cycles the gate is what makes the lean pay, and quorum 4
   does best.  In memoryless reversion the gate costs, by trading late.  In
   a walk every setting is within noise of zero, slightly negative, with
   more variance.  This is =BASKET-REBALANCE.md='s frontier again: gated
   for trends, ungated for reversion.
4. *The longer the anchor, the better the lean.*  In the cycle world at
   quorum 4, the anchor as an EMA of:
   - 320 days gains +1.1% and +1.7% (tilt 1 and 2);
   - 1280 days, +1.6% and +3.0%;
   - 5000 days (all but fixed), +1.9% and +3.7%.

   A real anchor is a governed reference for relative prices, or a
   multi-year EMA.  Its risk is a permanent repricing, which the leash
   bounds.  (The first run's clock error made its "320-day" anchor an
   80-day one, which lost against the year-long cycle.)
5. *The leash keeps the mandate; the lean only times it.*  A weight more
   than 30% off its DECLARED target trades regardless of the gate.  Measured
   against the leaning target, the leash tripped in the middle of a run
   (a test caught it).
6. *Proceeds park in the reserve.*  Between selling a leg that has turned
   down and buying one that has turned up, the BUCK waits in the reserve,
   up to 3x its target.  Past that, Fund places it anyway.  New credit is
   placed by itself, and the parked BUCK stays parked
   (=test_the_turn_director_buys_no_falling_knife=).  The gate needs this
   parking; without a reserve, Rebalance's proceeds went straight to Fund.

"Let the run run" is the gate: never trim a leg still running up, never buy
one still running down.  An LP cannot ride a run fully, because its pool
sells into it.  Riding one would mean holding unpooled TOKEN, a momentum
sleeve.  This design does not propose one.

** 12.4 The BUCK basis

- *Deposits*: TOKEN or BUCK, valued in BUCK, as now (section 4).
- *Exits pay BUCK.*
  - The exit's value is taken at the exiter's marks: each pool at the lower
    of spot and TWAP, the mirror of entry.  It pays that value less the
    same charge a BUCK deposit pays, (1+K)/2 of the dearest pool fee.
  - Its share of the debt is burned, and both come out of the reserve.
    *No pool is touched.*
  - An exit the reserve cannot cover goes pro rata: its fraction of
    everything, the TOKEN sold for BUCK, its own conversion borne.
- *The reserve* is the wallet's BUCK.  Its target is the larger of
  =reserve= x gross (fixed) and a flow-sized amount.  The flow-sized amount
  puts z standard deviations of the daily net flow, x (1+K), at the band's
  floor, which is where the wheel starts refilling (sized at the target,
  the floor would hold only half of it).  The reserve floats within +-50%
  of its target, so deposits and exits NET inside the band without a
  trade.
- *The components*, in slot order:
  - Arb: the outside arbitrageur's trade, made first.
  - Daily: the director's sample, and the day's flow folded into the
    reserve's sizing.
  - Sync.
  - Deploy: pairs idle TOKEN with the reserve's spare BUCK, then with
    minted credit, then by selling TOKEN.
  - Fund: buys the director's pick, or places the overflow, or places new
    credit alone.
  - Trim: refills the reserve at its floor, or trims the director's pick.
- *Routing.*  Every trade takes the better of its pool (counting the part
  of the fee that comes back to the basket) and the outside market.
- *The arbitrage takes no narrower band than an outsider's.*  On its own
  share of a pool the basket trades with itself.  So at the outside price,
  a "capture" inside the outsiders' band gains only the outside LP's share
  of the gap, and pays the outside cost on all of it.  That is a
  rebalancing bet, not an arbitrage
  (=test_inside_the_band_a_trade_is_not_arbitrage=).  Measured, the narrow
  band gains nothing over the outsider's edge (within 0.02-0.16% a year,
  either sign).  This corrects the premise of =ArbKind='s planned v2 ("the
  band only the basket can take ... recouped by the fee its own pool
  earns"): what the basket gains is what the outsiders would have taken,
  and the float lets it take that first.

Flows, in the fast world over a year: 2 arrivals a day (lognormal sizes
about 10k, 30% in BUCK), 60-day mean holds, about 600 exits, and a first
holder of 1M who stays:

| basket                   | pool ops per exit | exits paid by the reserve | reserve (of gross) | the stayer's return a year |
|--------------------------+-------------------+---------------------------+--------------------+----------------------------|
| TOKEN payout (prototype) |               9.0 | -                         | -                  | -0.86%                     |
| TOKEN payout, routed     |               7.4 | -                         | -                  | +2.35%                     |
| BUCK, 5% reserve         |              0.07 | 99.0%                     | 3.8%               | +3.18%                     |
| BUCK, flow-sized (z = 2) |              0.09 | 98.7%                     | 3.6%               | +3.06%                     |

With no flows, the same stayer earns +1.4-1.5% in every basket.
- *The flows cost the TOKEN-payout prototype's stayers about 2.3% a year.*
  The prototype's trades go through its own pools:
  - a BUCK deposit's deployment (about (1+K)/2 of the deposit);
  - an exit's conversion of its residue.

  A basket trading in its own pool pays its price impact twice: once as
  impact, and again when the outside arbitrageur re-pins the pool.
  Routed, the same flows earn the stayer +0.9% a year: the charges exceed
  the routed costs.
- *The reserve earns the stayer +1.4-1.8% a year from the flows*: flows
  net, the exit charge compensates the stayers, and an exit touches no
  pool.  The flow-sized reserve covers 98.7% of exits, holding 3.6% of
  the gross, about what the fixed 5% target averaged here.  It follows the
  flows rather than the basket's size.  The rest are the unusually large
  retirements, and they go pro rata.

Why the BUCK basis is simpler:

| | TOKEN payout | BUCK basis |
|-+--------------+------------|
| an exit | N burns, N collects, residue swaps, N transfers | one transfer and one burn |
| the payout | a bundle of N TOKENs | BUCK |
| deposits against exits | none net: every flow is placed or withdrawn | net inside the reserve's band |
| the arbitrage | a flash-callback cycle (=ArbKind= v1) | the same: it needs no float (13.1) |
| the director's proceeds | straight to Fund | parked until a buy is due |
| where captures land | a TOKEN or BUCK in the wallet, redeployed | BUCK in the reserve |

What it costs:
- *Cash drag.*  2-6% of the gross earns nothing.  In the table the reserve
  basket still matches or beats the TOKEN one, because routing and netting
  pay for it.
- *An exit charge*, the mirror of the entry charge.
- *A run.*  Once the reserve is spent, exits go pro rata and bear their own
  costs, so the first exits pay only the flat charge.  That advantage is
  bounded by the charge, and the stress fee (5, 11.5) is the lever.  A big
  exit may prefer the pro-rata path when its routed conversion costs less
  than the charge.  It still pays its own way: the stayers' price stays
  whole (=test_an_exit_beyond_the_reserve_goes_pro_rata_and_bears_its_conversion=).

** 12.5 What else the experiments taught

1. *Don't trade in your own pool when you can route.*  See 12.4.  It
   applies to the TOKEN design too.
2. *The pool fee is a moat.*  At 1% the outside arbitrage takes half as
   much, and the fee pays for it.  The wheel's arbitrage matters most where
   prices jump against the fee (coarse steps).
3. *Judge a basket against its own holdings and against cmix*, not against
   the TOKEN a depositor brought.
4. *The prototype's first deposit, placed through thin pools, moved them
   several-fold* before arbitrage pinned them back.  This is the price
   impact section 4 leaves shared.  With routing it is one outside trade.

* 13. The reserve as unminted credit (2026-09-29)

The design owner's proposal: do not mint and hold the whole K x deposit.
Keep part of the credit limit unminted as the reserve instead, and mint it
when the arbitrage, a reweighting or an exit needs it.  It costs no
demurrage, is instantly available up to K x the deposits, and moves with
K.  Each claim is a proportion of the whole, and each exit burns its
proportion of the debt of the moment.

Prototyped as =CreditBasket= (=reserve_basket.py=, 9 tests), with a stress
run in =basket_harvest.run_stress=.

** 13.1 What it changes

- *One credit limit*: K of the moment x min(the open receipts' cost basis,
  equity).
  - It replaces each deposit's pending credit (K on its day), and the
    bookkeeping that went with it: pending dropped pro rata at exits, and
    re-offered when K rises.
  - Deposits bring credit and earnings do not, because the limit is on the
    basis.  After a fall, nothing mints past K x equity.
- *Valuation does not change.*  Unminted credit is not an asset, so the
  share price never includes it, and neither entry nor exit needs K.
  Every share carries the same debt ratio, so a move in K treats every
  holder alike.  An incoming depositor is valued exactly as before.
- *Liquidity is the BUCK held plus the unminted headroom*, and the wheel
  keeps it at the flow-sized target.  Normally the basket holds no BUCK:
  every BUCK it receives repays debt, and every BUCK it needs, it mints.
- *Exits*:
  - An exit is paid from the BUCK held, or else minted, if the limit
    after the exit allows it.
  - Its share of the debt, plus the mint, is *owed*.  Trim sells positions
    and burns until the owed is repaid, which completes the exit's pro rata.
  - Beyond the limit, the exit goes pro rata at once.
- *The director's parking is headroom*, and a buy ahead of a sale mints.
- *The arbitrage needs no float*, so the reserve is not its float after
  all.  The outsider's trade funds itself through the V3 callback, as
  =ArbKind= v1 already does.
- *Demurrage is not the reason* (the owner's point).  At 2% a year on a
  reserve of about 3-4% of the gross, it costs under 0.1% a year.

** 13.2 The catch: a K cut takes the headroom

A cut from 0.75 to 0.60 removes 15% of the basis from the limit.  With it
goes the headroom that exits would mint from.  Three answers, one per
=mode=:

- *burn*: BUCK always repays debt.  After a cut there is no liquidity, and
  exits go pro rata until K recovers.
- *deleverage*: after a cut, Trim sells and burns until the headroom is
  back.  The basket follows K down, which is in effect a margin call,
  however orderly.
- *hold*: after a cut, Trim sells into BUCK and HOLDS it, without burning
  it.  Liquidity is kept, the leverage is untouched, and nothing is called
  back.  Normally no BUCK is held.

** 13.3 Ruled (2026-09-29)

The design owner's answers to 13.5's first four questions:

1. *Mint the full K on deposit.*  Each deposit mints K of its day x its
   value at once, into the wallet, for the wheel to place.  When K rises,
   the extra room, up to the new K x the deposits, is minted only when BUCK
   is required: for exits and the arbitrage.  No margin calls: a K cut does
   not touch BUCK already issued, and only lowers what new deposits bring.
2. *The limit is K x equity*: earnings raise the room available on demand.
3. *An exit's debt share is burned after the exit*, by the wheel (=owed=).
4. *The exit charge mirrors the entry charge.*

In the prototype this is =CreditBasket(issue=True, base="equity",
mode="hold")=:
- Liquidity is the BUCK held plus the headroom (K x equity - debt), kept
  at the flow-sized target.  Normally that means holding the BUCK, since
  deposits mint their full K.  After a K rise, or as earnings grow, the
  headroom takes over and the held BUCK beyond what liquidity needs is
  placed.
- Fund places only BUCK held beyond what liquidity needs.  Nothing is
  minted just to be placed.
- There is no pending credit.

** 13.4 The ruled design, measured

- *Liquidity*: 99.6% of exits paid at once in normal flows, 97.8-98.0%
  through the stress.
- *Leverage in a cut*: during a cut new deposits still mint K of their day,
  even while the pool's debt is above K x equity.  So the pool
  de-levers only as prices recover.
  - Peak debt/equity is 0.92 in the fall, against 0.80 for the minted
    reserve, whose deposits' credit waits for the pool's headroom.
  - It is 0.77 with no fall.
  - It is far from bad debt: that would need a further fall of about three
    quarters.  But it is the one place the rule departs from the pooled
    limit (13.5, question 11).
- *Returns* are within noise of the minted reserve (-9.42% against -9.33%
  in the fall, +2.32% against +2.27% without it).

3. [ruled] An exit's debt share: burned after, by the wheel.
4. [ruled] The exit charge: the mirror of the entry charge.

** 13.6 The design as ruled

This is the design to implement.  Its prototype is =ruled_basket()= and
=ruled_wheel_tasks()= in =reserve_basket.py=, and the whole machine is
=test_the_ruled_design_keeps_its_books=.  Sections 3-9 still hold where
this does not say otherwise: shares, the cost basis, conservative marks,

*State.*

| state                        | what it is                                                                                 |
|------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| S, receipts, treasury shares | as 3.1: a receipt is (shares, basis)                                                       |
| debt                         | the BUCK minted and not burned                                                             |
| idleBuck                     | BUCK held: liquidity that headroom cannot supply, deposits' BUCK and credit awaiting Fund, sale proceeds |
| idleToken[i]                 | TOKEN awaiting Deploy                                                                      |
| liquidity[i]                 | the basket's own position counts                                                           |
| owed                         | exits' debt shares and mints, not yet repaid                                               |
| flowMs, dayFlow              | the EMA (30 days) of the daily net flow squared; today's net flow                          |
| the director's ladder        | per leg: EMAs of 5-160 days and the 1280-day anchor, and their last steps                  |

*Limit and liquidity* (K of the moment):

#+begin_example
  headroom  = K x equity - debt
  liquidity = idleBuck + max(headroom, 0)
  target    = max(1% of gross, 2 sqrt(flowMs) (1+K) / (1 - 0.5))   floor = target / 2
  keep      = max(target - max(headroom, 0), 0)                    the BUCK held for liquidity
#+end_example

*Verbs.*

- *deposit(asset, amount)*: value v at the conservative marks, less the
  charge, buys shares.  The basket mints K x v at once, which is the
  deposit's own credit, whatever the pool's leverage.  The asset and the
  BUCK land in the wallet, and dayFlow += v.
- *redeem(id, fraction)*:
  1. The cut: 25% of the gain over basis, as treasury shares.
  2. The exit's value is taken at the exiter's marks, less the charge.
  3. It is paid from idleBuck, then minted from the headroom left after
     the exit.  owed += f x debt + the mint.
  4. If neither covers it, the exit goes pro rata: f of everything, the
     TOKEN sold by the better route, f x debt burned, the rest paid in BUCK.
  5. dayFlow -= the payment, and BUCK at rest repays owed.
- *redeemTreasury*: the same door, no cut, governance only.

*The wheel's components*, in slot order:

| component | slots | due when                                                                                          | run                                                                                                            |
|-----------+-------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Arb       | N     | pool i beyond 0.5% of the outside price                                                           | the outside arbitrageur's trade, first (the V3 callback funds it); the profit to idleBuck                     |
| Daily     | 1     | a new day                                                                                         | fold dayFlow into flowMs; the director's sample                                                                |
| Sync      | N     | pool i's owed fees > grain                                                                        | collect into the wallet                                                                                        |
| Deploy    | N     | idle TOKEN_i > grain                                                                              | pair with the BUCK held beyond keep; sell part of the TOKEN if short; add liquidity                             |
| Fund      | 1     | BUCK held beyond keep > grain, and the director picks a pool or the excess passes 3x the target   | buy the pick's (else the neediest's) TOKEN with half, by the better route; Deploy pairs it                     |
| Trim      | 1     | settled, and owed > grain, or liquidity under its floor, or the director names a pool             | unwind at most 5% of a position; sell its TOKEN by the better route; the proceeds repay owed, the rest is held |

*The director.*
- Targets are the declared ones x exp(-excursion), renormalized.  The
  excursion is the leg's log price against the basket's mean, measured
  against its 1280-day EMA.
- A leg has TURNED when at least 4 of its 6 shorter EMAs are moving back.
  The director never trims a leg still running up, nor buys one still
  running down.
- The leash is 30% of the declared target, and parking goes up to 3x the
  liquidity target.

*Invariants.*
1. minted - burned == debt.
2. The receipts' shares + the treasury's == S.
3. A deposit mints exactly K x its value.
4. Every other mint (exits, the arbitrage) is at most K x equity - debt.
5. owed >= 0 and idleBuck >= 0.
6. No payout exceeds its fraction's value at the exiter's marks.

*Parameters.*
- Constants: lambda 25%; the charge, (1+K)/2 x the dearest pool fee, both
  ways.
- Governed:
  - liquidity: floor 1% of gross, z = 2, band 50%, flow EMA 30 days,
    parking 3x;
  - the arbitrage band 0.5%;
  - Trim's step 5%;
  - the grain 1e-4;
  - the director: windows 5-160 and 1280 days, quorum 4, tilt 1, band 1%,
    leash 30%;
  - the stress-fee hook, off.

Where the build departed from 13.7, and why:

1. *The arbitrage stays in the wheel* (=ArbKind=).  It needs no float -- the
   V3 callback funds it -- and it already credits the basket through
   =creditDepositors= / =creditTreasury=, which the equity shell answers by
   landing the captures in the wallet.  "In the basket" is where its profit
   goes, not where its code runs.  So =BasketWheel= keeps its kinds and adds
   =EquityKind=; the components are 2N + 3 slots, not 3N + 3.
2. *No better-route swap yet.*  The basket trades in its own pools, and the
   wheel's arbitrage re-pins them and keeps what an outsider would have
   taken, which prices the basket's own trades at about the outside route's
   cost.  A route through TOKEN/USDC and BUCK/USDC is a later refinement.
3. *A wheel swap is capped at 1% of the pool's depth* (=swapCapBp=).  The
   prototype's outside market had unlimited depth; on chain, a gap-sized
   swap into a thin pool moved it several-fold.  A large deposit is placed
   over many ticks, the arbitrage re-pinning the pool between them.  "No
   deposit cap" stands: what is bounded is the wheel's pace.
4. *The liquidity target is capped at 20% of the gross* (=ceilBp=).  A young
   basket's first deposits are its whole size, and the flow sizing read them
   as one enormous flow and kept everything as liquidity.
5. *One snapshot per step.*  Each component reads every pool's marks once
   and computes gross, equity, headroom, target and keep from it; a
   due-check had cost a million gas re-reading them.
6. *The desk is a mixin* rather than =BuckBasketOps= rebased, so the
   pro-rata desk and its tests stand as the sim's baseline and the equity
   desk is a second concrete contract.
7. *Sim compatibility*: the shell keeps =depositToken= and the two-argument
   =redeem= (0 bp redeems all, the pro-rata convention), and emits the
   pro-rata shells' =Deposited= and =Redeemed= events.  The grain's floor is
   1e3 raw units, tiny at 6 decimals (the sim's BUCK) and at 18.

*Identity.*  BUCK transfers are identity-gated.  With BUCK payouts an
exiter must be able to receive BUCK, where TOKEN payouts let a depositor
never touch BUCK.  A private account receives BUCK from a public contract
only after an identity-bound =Buck.approve= of it: it re-encrypts its
registered identity under the contract's key and proves, in a
Chaum-Pedersen proof, that the two ciphertexts carry the same identity.
So an equity-basket depositor makes that approve once, from its wallet,
before its first exit.  The sim does exactly that
(=identity.identity_approve=).  The identity cache keeps the identity
secret key for the agent classes that approve (=KEEP_IDENTITY_SECRET=), and
their entries regenerate once.

What the sim taught:

1. *A young basket's pools are thin, and a capped step assumes a market.*
   The sim's TOKEN/BUCK pools start empty; before tick 0 no arbitrageur
   trades.
   - Run to rest there, the wheel's capped sales went into its own pools
     with nobody re-pinning them, and the basket opened at half its value.
   - So before tick 0 the wheel makes only the pools' first pairings, which
     need no market.  The rest is placed while the arbitrageurs trade.
   - The arbitrage's triangles are set after those pairings: an arbitrage
     against half-built pools only moves the marks.
2. *Deploy pairs pro rata.*  Stepped pool by pool, the first pool paired
   its TOKEN with all the usable BUCK and left the last pool to be placed
   only by selling into itself.  That pool stayed at 0.3% weight with its
   TOKEN idle, and its idle TOKEN blocked Fund and Trim for the whole basket.
   Deploy now shares the usable BUCK in proportion to the TOKEN waiting in
   every pool.
3. *The arbitrage keeps the basket's pools on the market.*  In the
   "rebalancing" world BUCK fell 14% against USDC in eight days, on ProRata
   and on the equity basket alike.  Without the triangles, the basket's
   pools implied BUCK at 0.94-1.05 while its market was 0.86, and exits
   lost about 10% in USD.  With them, the two agree within about 2%, and a
   TOKEN depositor's four-day round trip returned 97-99% of its USD.
4. *BUCK payouts give a saver BUCK's market price at exit.*  This is the
   economic consequence of ruling 9.  A depositor's USD outcome is its
   basket value in BUCK times BUCK/USD at the exit.  TOKEN payouts left
   that exposure with the TOKEN, which BUCK weakness does not touch.  The
   arbitrage removes only the gap between the basket's pools and the
   market, not a fall in BUCK itself.


** 13.10 Where the basket trades TOKEN, and the fallback exit in kind (2026-09-29; a ruling asked)

The owner's question: buying TOKEN to place a BUCK deposit drives TOKEN up,
and dissolving positions to pay BUCK drives it down.  A payout of whatever
mix a dissolved position returns moves no market and costs only ERC-20
transfers.  With a stock of BUCK and BUCK credit sized to the flow, where is
the basket's hand forced, and is the damage only at bootstrap?

*Where the basket trades, and why:*

1. *An exit does not trade.*  It is paid from the BUCK held, then from
   minted headroom (K x equity - debt): one BUCK transfer and perhaps a
   mint.  No position is touched.  In the prototype's flows experiment
   that came to 0.03-0.09 pool operations per exit.
2. *The net outflow is sold later, paced.*  An exit's debt share, plus
   anything minted to pay it, becomes =owed=.  BUCK from deposits, fees and
   arbitrage captures repays it with no trade.  Only what inflows do not
   cover reaches Trim, which burns a slice of a position and sells its
   TOKEN.  Trim takes the director's pick or the most overweight pool, at
   most 2 x =swapCapBp= (2% of that pool's depth) a step, with the
   arbitrage re-pinning between steps.  This is inherent to BUCK payouts:
   value that leaves as BUCK comes out of TOKEN, unless inflows net it.
   It is the trade the owner wants: selling the overweight, likely dear,
   TOKEN.
3. *A deposit trades, and a levered basket's inflow buys TOKEN.*  A
   deposit of value v is placed with its K v of minted BUCK as full-range
   positions, which are 50/50 by value.
   - A BUCK deposit buys (1 + K)/2 x v of TOKEN: 87.5% at K = 0.75.
   - A TOKEN deposit sells (1 - K)/2 x v: 12.5%.
   - Deploy pairs a TOKEN deposit with the BUCK waiting in the wallet first,
     pro rata, so the two kinds of deposit net.  They cancel only when TOKEN
     deposits are (1 + K)/(1 - K) times the BUCK deposits: 7 to 1 at 0.75.
   - Fund aims the purchase at the director's pick or the largest
     underweight, which is again the trade the owner wants.
4. *The pro-rata fallback exit trades, at once.*  When the BUCK held and
   the headroom cannot pay an exit (a large exit, or one after a K cut),
   =_exitProRata= burns the exit's fraction of every position, then sells
   all of the TOKEN through =monetaryLeg= to pay BUCK.  This is the one
   place the basket's hand is forced: an unpaced sale of N TOKENs, as large
   as the exit.  (=BuckBasketEquity.sol=, =_exitProRata=.)

*The bootstrap.*  The large moves seen in the sim were bootstrap's (13.9
lesson 1):
- A wheel run to rest before tick 0 sold into its own empty pools with
  nobody re-pinning them.  It now only makes the first pairings there.
- The day-0 move of about +50% in every TOKEN/BUCK pool was the wheel's
  arbitrage, and it was correct.  On the first tick the scenario's outside
  market jumped: BUCK/USDC fell to 0.814 and TOKEN/USDC rose to 37.3.  That
  implies about 45.8 BUCK per TOKEN, where the young pools still held the
  price they were seeded at.  The arbitrage re-pinned them in one trade,
  large in percentage only because they were thin.  They agreed with the
  market at the next tick.
- After that, the largest single move each actor made in that run was
  about 1.7% (the anonymous arbitrageurs) and 0.8% (the TOKEN accumulators).

*Proposed (asked; not built): the fallback exit pays in kind.*  The exit's
fraction f of every position is burned and paid as it comes out: each
TOKEN, plus the BUCK left after its debt share is burned, plus f of the
wallet (idle BUCK and idle TOKEN).  That is N position burns and N + 1
transfers with no swaps, cheaper than the swaps, and it leaves the TOKEN
markets alone.
- *It always covers its debt share while the basket is at or under its
  limit.*  A full-range position is 50/50 by value at the current price, so
  its BUCK side is half the positions' value.  At the limit D = K x E, so
  D / G = K / (1 + K) < 1/2 for any K < 1: the BUCK side covers the debt
  share with BUCK to spare.
- *Past a leverage of 1* (D > E, reached only by a K cut plus a fall in the
  basket's BUCK value), the BUCK side falls short of the debt share by some
  s.  The exit then burns the BUCK it has, keeps TOKEN worth s at the
  conservative mark in the wallet, and adds s to =owed=.  Trim sells that
  TOKEN later, paced.  The exiter bears s; the remaining holders are
  unchanged at the marks.
- *Normal exits stay one BUCK payout* (ruling 9).  Only the fallback's
  payout asset changes.  An in-kind option for any exit, at the exiter's
  choice, would also be possible, but it is not proposed.
- *To build*:
  - =_exitProRata= in =BuckBasketEquity.sol=: drop the =monetaryLeg= sale;
    transfer each TOKEN and the BUCK residue; retain the shortfall's TOKEN
    as above.  =Redeemed= reports the BUCK paid, and a new event, or the
    existing =WalletCredited= pattern, reports each TOKEN paid.
  - The prototype's =CreditBasket= fallback, and its test
    (=test_an_exit_beyond_the_reserve_goes_pro_rata_and_bears_its_conversion=),
