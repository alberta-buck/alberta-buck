#+AUTHOR: Perry Kundert (design owner) with Claude

* 0. Status

A design, not yet built.  It replaces the pro-rata basket's principal /
burn-obligation accounting (=BuckBasketProRata=, inherited by the ops and
Fence shells) with shares of equity.  The rulings in section 1 are the
design owner's (2026-09-28); the choices in section 7 are still open.

* 1. The rulings

1. *Every deposit is equity, valued in BUCK* -- a TOKEN deposit and a BUCK
   deposit alike.
2. *Each deposit is matched by K worth of BUCK credit* that the basket
   mints to pair with it, whatever was deposited (K = =buckK= at the
   deposit: the same credit-per-unit-of-real-value BuckCredit grants
   against insured assets).
3. *Depositors own the gains.*
4. *Payouts are in TOKENs.*
5. *The basket's share:* "fair" is K's share of the returns and losses;
   splitting the excess returns with the depositor may be the better
   incentive for depositors and the better return for the basket.  Both
   are designed below (section 4).

* 2. Why: what the present accounting does

A receipt records a BUCK principal R (for a TOKEN deposit worth R the
basket mints R to pair with it); its claim is V = R x 2B/O (the
depositors' BUCK side B, total principal O); a redemption burns R and
pays min(V/2, V - R), in TOKEN only, any BUCK surplus to the treasury.

| TOKEN moves (in BUCK)  | holding it | depositor now     |
|------------------------+------------+-------------------|
| +21%                   | +21%       | +10% (treasury +10% of R) |
| -19%                   | -19%       | -20%              |

The treasury takes the BUCK half's gains; the depositor covers the BUCK
half's shortfalls.  And the accounting underneath has four faults the
equity design removes by construction (measured on
=wip/basket-accounting=, and in =doc/CONVERGENCE.org= 5.1):

- *BUCK deposits count double.*  Their principal is their whole value,
  where a TOKEN deposit's is half of its; a BUCK deposit and redemption
  pays the depositor TOKEN out of the other depositors' positions and burns
  her BUCK (4,000 BUCK in: a claim of 7,999; the TOKEN depositor -4,000
  once arbitrage restores the price).  Alone in a pool, she is paid
  nothing.
- *Entry is at face, not at value.*  A new deposit shares the pool's
  history at once (+5% after a 5% move).
- *Value is read from pool balances and at spot*: balances hold
  anyone's tokens; spot is the transactor's to push, within a 5% guard.

* 3. The model

** 3.1 The units

- *Shares of the GROSS position.*  Receipts hold shares of everything the
  basket's depositor positions are worth -- every pool's liquidity at a
  TWAP price, their accrued fees, any loose TOKEN or BUCK awaiting
  re-investment.  Gross value per share: G/S.
- *Each receipt carries its own debt* d: the BUCK the basket minted for it
  (d = K0 x E at its deposit, K0 the K of that day).  Debt is per receipt,
  not pooled, so deposits made at different K share one share pool fairly.
- *Each receipt carries its cost basis* E: its equity at deposit, in BUCK.
- A receipt's EQUITY is s x G/S - d.  Depositors own it.

** 3.2 A deposit

Of TOKEN_i worth E, or of BUCK E (valued at the pool's TWAP, the spot
guarded against it):

1. The basket mints m = K x E BUCK -- its credit -- and records d = m.
2. It LPs the deposit and the credit, (1+K)E, full range, balanced: a
   TOKEN deposit sells (1-K)E/2 of its TOKEN for BUCK first; a BUCK deposit
   (into the director's hint pool) buys (1+K)E/2 of TOKEN.
3. Shares are minted on the GROSS value actually added, measured at the
   TWAP after the LP: s = dG x S/G.  The swap's fee and impact are the
   depositor's, not the other shareholders'.  Entry is at value: a new
   deposit neither gains nor loses by the pool's history.

TOKEN and BUCK deposits are treated identically: equity E, debt K x E,
position (1+K)E.  At K < 1 the equity is (1+K)/2 exposed to the TOKEN at
inception (87.5% at K = 0.75); the BUCK part is the deposit's own.

** 3.3 A redemption

1. Withdraw the receipt's share s/S of every depositor position,
   principal AND its share of the accrued fees -- pro rata, so fair by
   construction with no valuation needed.  (Sell-high draws -- more from
   overweight pools -- remain available, valued at the TWAP.)
2. Burn the receipt's debt d from the BUCK withdrawn; cover a shortfall by
   selling TOKEN, convert an excess BUCK into TOKEN.  The debt is always
   repaid first.
3. Apply the split (section 4) to the gain = equity at exit - E.
4. Pay the rest in TOKEN: the depositor's chosen constituent, or the
   withdrawn mix, converted through the basket's own pools at a
   TWAP-guarded price.

** 3.4 K

The credit is fixed at the deposit.  A later K cut withdraws the OFFER
(new deposits get less credit), never a receipt's credit: no margin call
(the K-cut doctrine).  A later K rise could let a receipt top up its
credit -- the basket minting more BUCK into its pools against existing
equity, a monetary expansion by the basket -- which is left to the
monetary rules, not this design.

K now governs the basket's issuance the way it governs BuckCredit's: BUCK
created per unit of real collateral.  Per deposit the basket mints K
instead of 1, so a basket at K = 0.75 has ~12% less depth per unit of
equity than today's 1:1 pairing.

** 3.5 Fees, the wheel, the stress fee, the treasury

- *Fees* are counted in G as they accrue (the positions' tokensOwed, from
  the pools' fee growth -- a view) and leave with each exit's share; the
  wheel compounds them into liquidity periodically (a routine kind), with
  no debt: fees are equity.
- *The wheel's credits* are equity with no debt -- simpler than today's
  bonus principal.  The fee-band enhancement (counting the fee a cycle pays
  into the basket's own pool) falls out: that fee is G.
- *The stress fee* is a share of an exit's payout left in G, for the
  remaining shareholders.
- *The treasury* holds the basket's share of returns (section 4) in its
  own position (liquidity with no shares) -- the basket's own capital.
- *The monetary desk* is untouched: its book is plain balances outside the
  depositor positions.

** 3.6 Supply integrity

A receipt's debt is burned before anything is paid, so every BUCK the
basket minted is burned when its deposit leaves.  Only if a position falls
below its debt -- (1+K) sqrt(p) < K, a TOKEN collapse to (K/(1+K))^2 of
its entry price (18% at K = 0.75, 24% at 0.95) -- is there bad debt, which
the treasury's capital absorbs first.

* 4. The split: two designs

Per unit of equity E, K = 0.75, the TOKEN moving by p in BUCK over a
receipt's life and a harvest h (fees, rebalancing, the wheel's captures)
on the position.  "Lender" is the equity before any split: the position
(1+K)E(sqrt p + h) less the debt KE.

| scenario             | holding | today (treasury) | lender | *fair* (basket) | *shared, lambda 25%* (basket) |
|----------------------+---------+------------------+--------+-----------------+-------------------------------|
| p = 1.00, h = +3%    |   100.0 | 103.0 (+3.0)     |  105.2 | 103.0 (+2.25)   | 103.9 (+1.31)                 |
| p = 1.21 (TOKEN +21%)|   121.0 | 110.0 (+10.0)    |  117.5 | 110.0 (+7.50)   | 113.1 (+4.38)                 |
| p = 0.81 (TOKEN -19%)|    81.0 |  80.0 (0)        |   82.5 | 90.0 (-7.50)    |  82.5 (0)                     |
| p = 0.81, h = +3%    |    81.0 |  86.0 (0)        |   87.8 | 93.0 (-5.25)    |  87.8 (0)                     |

** 4.1 Fair: K's share of returns and losses

The basket put in K for the depositor's 1, so it takes K/(1+K) of every
gain and pays K/(1+K) of every loss (43% at K = 0.75): the depositor ends
with E(sqrt p + h) -- exactly an unlevered LP of their own deposit.

- For: symmetric; the depositor's downside is halved against today's.
- Against: the basket carries half the downside of every position -- it
  needs a first-loss reserve (its accumulated gain share), and beyond the
  reserve a loss share means BUCK it cannot burn (net issuance).  The
  depositor gets nothing for the credit: their return is what LPing alone
  would earn.

** 4.2 Shared excess: depositors own the levered equity, the basket takes a share of the gain

The depositor owns the equity, gains and losses (the "lender" column); at
exit the basket takes lambda of the GAIN above the receipt's cost basis,
nothing on a loss (a per-receipt high-water mark: fees are taken only at
exit, so the basis is the mark).  Taken in TOKEN, into the treasury.

- For: the depositor gets the credit's leverage on the harvest -- (1+K)h,
  5.25% on a 3% harvest before the basket's share -- and more of the
  upside than "fair" whenever lambda < K/(1+K); the basket earns a share of
  a levered harvest across the whole basket and never carries a
  depositor's loss (its minted BUCK is repaid first, section 3.6); it is
  the standard, legible performance fee.
- Against: the depositor carries the levered downside (-17.5% where the
  TOKEN fell 19%: less than holding, because the equity is 87.5% TOKEN).
- lambda = K/(1+K) gives the depositor exactly "fair"'s upside with the
  full downside -- so the incentive is lambda BELOW it: 20-30%.

** 4.3 The benchmark ("excess" over what)

Section 4.2 measures the gain against the cost basis (in BUCK).  Two
alternatives: a hurdle (the basket takes its share only of the gain above
a rate -- BUCK's demurrage, say, so a depositor always nets at least what
holding BUCK costs); or holding the TOKEN (the basket's share only of what
it earned beyond the TOKEN's own move -- the purest "excess", but at K < 1
the equity is not fully TOKEN-exposed, so part of that difference is
direction, not harvest).  The cost basis is the simplest and is
recommended; a hurdle is a one-parameter refinement.

* 5. Recommendation

*Shared excess (4.2), lambda = 25%, against the cost basis, taken at
exit, in TOKEN.*  It keeps the basket out of the depositors' losses (no
reserve to size, no BUCK that cannot be burned), pays the depositor the
credit's leverage on the harvest, and pays the basket a quarter of every
depositor's gain -- on a (1+K)-levered harvest across the whole basket.
Fair (4.1) remains a governance switch if the basket should share losses.

* 6. Building it

1. *Contracts.*  The pro-rata shell's deposit / redeem / claim accounting
   becomes shares, per-receipt debt and basis, and a TWAP-valued G (with
   accrued fees from the pools' fee growth).  The Fence has ~1 KB of
   headroom, so the accounting core lives where the venue logic does (a
   facet behind the shell's fallback), as the wheel's credits already do.
   The receipt's =deposits(id)= becomes (shares, debt, basis, token, time).
2. *Tests -- the properties, one each:* a deposit neither gains nor loses
   by the pool's history (entry neutrality); an immediate redemption
   returns the deposit less its own costs (TOKEN and BUCK alike); a
   redemption takes exactly its pro-rata share; the debt is burned in full before any payout (supply
   integrity); the split's arithmetic at lambda and in fair mode; K cut
   after a deposit changes nothing of it.
3. *The simulation.*  The direct-mint agents' bookkeeping (they read
   =deposits=), eqmetrics' depositor readings, the server's savings block
   and the Savings tab's receipt value (a receipt's equity replaces
   min(V/2, V - R)).
4. *Then rerun* the savings experiments -- E2-E4 read depositor P&L
   through the present accounting, BUCK deposits and all.

* 7. Open

1. lambda, and whether it is fixed or governed.
2. The benchmark: cost basis (recommended) or a hurdle.
3. Redemption draws: pro rata (fair by construction) or sell-high
   (helps rebalance; needs TWAP valuation).
4. The payout TOKEN: the depositor's choice, or the withdrawn mix.
5. K top-ups when K rises: here, or left to the monetary rules.
