* The idea

The desk (=EquityDesk=) needs a reserve to act before it has ever issued: Q1
sells reserve TOKEN for cheap BUCK, and Q3 buys the reserve back when BUCK
recovers.  Today that reserve is the founding grant, held as plain TOKEN in the
desk's contract.  The proposal:

1. The desk puts its founding holdings into the BuckBasket -- it deposits them
   and holds the receipt's shares -- so the reserve earns the basket's harvest
   instead of sitting idle.
2. It pledges those shares: its marked credit is marked at a net value that
   includes the receipt, refreshed by a wheel step, so it can issue (Q4)
   against them.
3. Since a deposit makes the basket issue BUCK (it draws =K x= the deposit to
   pair it in its pools) and an exit retires it (its lien share is repaid from
   the positions' BUCK), perhaps depositing and exiting can themselves be part
   of the desk's issue and retire strategy.

* What the desk has to do (the quadrants, and where a reserve enters)

| BUCK is             | quadrant | the desk                                   | supply    | needs            |
|---------------------+----------+--------------------------------------------+-----------+------------------|
| cheap, reverting    | Q1       | sells reserve TOKEN for BUCK, holds it     | unchanged | a TOKEN reserve  |
| dear, reverting     | Q3       | sells held BUCK back for TOKEN             | unchanged | held BUCK (Q1's) |
| dear, persistent    | Q4       | draws its credit, sells the BUCK for TOKEN | rises     | credit (its mark)|
| cheap, persistent   | Q2       | buys BUCK; while drawn it repays the lien  | falls     | a lien to repay  |

Q1 and Q3 are the "foreign reserve" round trip and work from day one on the
grant.  Q4 then Q2 -- issue dear, retire cheap -- works once the desk has
issued.  The one unfinished case is BUCK cheap and persisting before any issue:
the BUCK bought is held, supporting the price as well as retirement would while
held, but staying in supply and paying the holding fee (=_deskRetire= is a
no-op; Buck has no way to destroy held BUCK).

* Assessment

** As the reserve's home: promising

- *It earns.*  The reserve collects the basket's harvest instead of idling.
- *The correlation is the right way round.*  When BUCK is cheap a basket costs
  more BUCK, so the desk's shares buy more BUCK exactly when it must buy.
- *In-kind exits deliver what Q1 spends.*  An exit beyond the basket's
  liquidity is paid in kind -- a fraction of every position and of the idle
  TOKEN, by transfer -- which is the TOKEN Q1 sells for BUCK.
- *Pledging needs no new machinery.*  The desk's marked credit is already
  re-marked at its net value before every operation; the receipt joins
  =netValue()= at the basket's LOW share price (after the 25% cut and the exit
  charge it would pay to leave).
- *No re-hypothecation spiral.*  The mark is NET of the desk's lien: drawing
  against shares, buying TOKEN and depositing it adds assets and lien alike, so
  net value -- and the room under =K x= it -- does not grow.  (A mark at gross
  would spiral to =K/(1-K)=, 3x at K = 0.75.)
- *The desk's trades cannot inflate its own collateral.*  Its swaps move the
  pools that value its shares, but the LOW mark takes the lower of spot and
  TWAP, so a push raises nothing.

** As the issue/retire lever itself: weak, and wrong-way under stress

A deposit's BUCK and an exit's retirement happen INSIDE the pools, as liquidity
added or removed at the current price.  They change supply and pool depth, not
BUCK's price -- and the price is the desk's job.

- *An exit during an inflation attack* thins the pools just when depth is what
  resists the attacker.
- *A TOKEN deposit while BUCK is dear* buys a little BUCK as the wheel places it
  (the =(1-K)/2= swap), pushing the price the wrong way; a BUCK deposit sells
  BUCK ((1+K)/2), the right way, but the basket then draws =K x= it again.
- *An exit paid in BUCK* hands the desk BUCK the basket drew for it: no price
  effect until the basket's Trim sells TOKEN to repay, at most 1% of a pool's
  depth per step.  It makes the basket do the desk's buying, slowly.

So the swaps stay the lever; the basket is where the reserve waits.

** Costs and rule changes

- Every round trip pays the treasury's 25% of any gain over cost basis, and
  the entry and exit charges.  A ruling: is the desk's receipt exempt from
  the cut (it is the system's own capital), or does it pay like anyone?
- D2 ("nothing the desk holds or owes enters the basket's books") becomes "the
  desk is an ordinary depositor": its receipt is in the basket's books by
  design, its lien and held BUCK are not.  The invariant suite
  (=test/basket/EquityDeskInvariant.t.sol=) needs a desk-deposit handler, and
  its ghosts must tell a legitimate deposit from a leak.
- A large desk exit may be paid in kind; Q1 wants TOKEN anyway, but the
  desk's accounting of =monetaryTokenHeld= must take TOKEN arriving from an
  exit.
- The founding grant enters the basket as equity, so the depositors' share
  price is unaffected, but the basket's size (and so the desk's bounds, which
  are sized in the basket's equity) grows by the grant.

** Where it might also help: the standing book

A desk holding shares could open a /standing book/ cheaply: draw part of its
line at founding against the shares and sell it for TOKEN.  Then Q2 always has
a lien to retire against, and Q1 has twice the ammunition.  The costs are a
one-time supply increase at launch and a desk long commodities against a BUCK
lien (its room shrinks if commodities fall; no margin call).

* An experiment to decide it

On the E2 shock arms (dump and squeeze at x1 and x4, the grinds, the
book-loading attack), three desks beside the equity basket:

1. today's: the grant as plain TOKEN;
2. the grant deposited as shares, pledged; Q1 funded by in-kind exits;
3. as 2, plus a standing book of a fraction of the line.

Read: the peak and mean deviation of BUCK from its basket; days =K= railed;
the desk's P&L AND its inventory (never P&L alone); the depositors' return
(does the desk's in-and-out cost the other depositors?); the treasury's cut
paid by the desk; how often Q2 found no lien.

* Open questions

1. The cut: exempt the desk's receipt, or not?
2. How much of the reserve stays as plain TOKEN, for Q1 without an exit?
3. Does the desk's exit compete with depositors' exits for the basket's
   liquidity in the same stress, and should it queue behind them?
4. Should the standing book be sized in K (a fraction of the line) or in the
   basket's equity (like the bounds)?
