#+TITLE: Review: a minimal wheel-driven basket and desk, with a path to joint control
#+DATE: 2026-10-02
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

* Recommendation and scope

Build the simple system first: a shared, quality-aware price reader and EMA
service; the proposed paced basket response; a separately capitalized desk
that intervenes outside a meaningful parity band; the existing direct K
controller; and atomic consistency arbitrage. Make the accounting, sampling,
execution bounds and wheel liveness correct before adding predictors.

The user's clarified objective is the design criterion: harvest relative
reversion and rebalancing returns, while the desk leaves enough persistent
error for K's integral to move, without spending the desk into exhaustion.
The longer-term objective is coordinated estimation and control of all three
trading mechanisms and the slow credit lever.

This review reads branch =doc/basket-machine= at =16c3ded=, including the
working-copy =OPTIMAL-CONTROL-BIG-PICTURE.org= and relevant portions of
=BASKET-MACHINE=, =K-AND-OPS=, =alberta-buck-ethereum-wheel.org=,
=alberta-buck-ethereum-basket.org= and =alberta-buck-ethereum.org=.
Recommendations below are proposals, not changes to the existing rulings.
The initial review made no production changes and did not rerun the reported
historical economy experiments. The subsequently authorized implementation,
tests and new comparison runs are recorded separately in
[[file:BASKET-MACHINE-IMPLEMENTATION.org][BASKET-MACHINE-IMPLEMENTATION.org]].

* What the big-picture note gets right

The monetary reference is the declared basket quantity valued in BUCK,
=bvib = basketValueInBuck()=, with parity at 1. It is neither depositor equity
nor an average of constituent log prices. The direct controller's error is
=e = 1 - bvib=. Positive error raises K; negative error lowers K.

There are three distinct sources of trading opportunity: inconsistency
between venues, relative commodity movements, and BUCK's deviation from
basket parity. Keeping separate books and policies for these is a useful
starting point. The basket's equity belongs to depositors; the monetary desk
has its own capital and credit. A shared observation service need not own
funds or choose any trades.

The note is appropriately explicit that the shared signal, replacement
Respond step, and broader persistence/stock-transfer mechanism are not all
implemented. It also correctly refuses to promote the failed position-to-K
feedback experiments into a recommended controller.

Several interpretations need tightening:

- Separate objectives do not decouple the plant. Basket, desk, arb and
  external traders alter the same pools. Desk trades change the basket's
  market value without touching its accounting records. Arb profits credited
  as idle TOKEN also affect weights. Stability is a property of their joint
  dynamics, delays and constraints.
- Small error does not return K to =buckK0=. At zero error the existing
  integral normally retains its learned adjustment. This memory is desirable.
- K changes available credit, not outstanding debt by decree. A cut imposes
  no margin call. New issuance, repayment, demurrage, Jubilee relief, and
  basket deleveraging must be distinguished in the supply response.
- A NAV-sized trade is not a price change. Equal BUCK notional across pools
  is not equal price impact when active liquidity differs. The desk's
  =_swapAcross= comment overstates its common-mode purity.
- Conditional integration limits windup; it does not prove economic
  controllability, adequate borrower response, or joint stability. Even the
  note's claim that only opposite error can release a rail is too strong:
  changes in the proportional/derivative contributions can change saturation.
- Commodity reversion is a hypothesis about a horizon and regime. Physical
  production constraints do not establish a stationary token price, a fixed
  mean, or recovery before the desk's budget expires. Permanent repricing and
  token/issuer failure belong in the validation set.

* Implementation findings that precede tuning

** Confirmed: Q2 can change the book without retiring held BUCK

[[file:../src/basket/EquityDesk.sol::function _deskRetire][EquityDesk._deskRetire]]
is an empty override. That is appropriate for BUCK that already repaid the
desk's lien on receipt. However,
[[file:../src/basket/MonetaryDesk.sol::if (outright && room && monetaryBuckHeld > 0)][MonetaryDesk._absorb]]
calls it on previously held inventory, decreases =monetaryBuckHeld=, and
decreases =monetaryOutstanding= as if a new retirement occurred.

A temporary Foundry probe capitalized a desk, executed Q1 into a positive
BUCK balance, then executed Q2. The held book decreased and outstanding
became negative, while the actual signed balance and =totalSupply= were
unchanged. This is a reproduced accounting/semantic mismatch, not a claim
of a demonstrated profitable exploit.

For the minimal equity desk, derive held BUCK and lien from the actual
account. A purchase into debt repays debt immediately; a purchase beyond
that holds BUCK. Persistence alone cannot turn already-held BUCK into a
permanent burn. Q3 spending after repayment can draw credit again. Reconcile
the operation classification with actual balance changes, relief and
demurrage. Do not import the legacy pro-rata mint/burn semantics.

** Confirmed: position and outright caps are entry tests, not hard final bounds

=_absorb= checks whether existing held inventory is at its cap; =_supply=
checks existing outstanding issuance. The next action is not consistently
clipped to the remaining room. Temporary probes setting each cap to 1 bp of
basket equity and requesting a 50 bp operation reproduced one-trade
overshoots, including with the existing pool-leg cap enabled.

Clip the authorized amount against every remaining budget before execution
and enforce conservative postconditions afterward. Define whether a cap
uses opening NAV, a committed valuation, or a changing NAV. Repricing can
make an existing position exceed a percentage cap without a new trade; the
required invariant is then no further increase, not a forced liquidation.

** Confirmed: a failed Ops attempt consumes the wheel's epoch

[[file:../src/wheel/UpkeepKinds.sol::abstract contract OpsKind][OpsKind._run]]
sets =opsEpochSeen= before the external call and catches its revert. A probe
made that call fail, made advice available, and advanced a block within the
same epoch. The wheel still reported no pending operation and did not retry.

The assignment inside =MonetaryDesk.monetaryOperation= itself rolls back on
=NoAdvice=; the wrapper's assignment does not. This distinction should be
explicit in the big-picture note. Key completion to a successfully consumed
advice version; apply bounded retry/backoff for unavailable advice or a
temporary venue failure. A completed valid HOLD can be memoized separately.

** Source-confirmed: oracle failures can become apparently valid spot readings

[[file:../src/basket/BuckBasketUniswapV3.sol::function _readPoolPrice][The venue's price reader]],
its =marks= function, and
[[file:../src/basket/EquityTurnDirector.sol::function _tick][EquityTurnDirector._tick]]
fall back to =slot0= when history cannot be consulted. In =marks=, this can
make LOW, HIGH and TWAP identical during precisely the failure that removed
the independent comparison. The direct K controller can therefore consume
spot under a TWAP-labelled interface.

Publish =valid=, interval, timestamp and history coverage explicitly. Missing
history should prevent new risk based on that reading. Bootstrap should be
an explicit limited mode. Do not silently omit a failed constituent and
renormalize the basket, since that changes the monetary reference. Preserve
repayment and appropriately specified in-kind exits independently.

** Source-confirmed: advice freshness and persistence are underspecified

=monetaryEffort()= returns only effort and an outright bit.
=monetaryOperation()= checks the director's current epoch, not the age of
the observation that produced that effort. =_foldBasket= may retain an old
ladder after a failed read. =monOverEpochs= increments once on a processed
epoch, without requiring adjacency or the same sign of the deviation.

Attach an observation/advice version and validity interval. Count persistence
only from valid observations of the same side. A gap or side reversal should
reset or explicitly degrade the evidence. Elapsed time is not evidence that
an unobserved price stayed past the leash.

** Source-confirmed: the harvest response still has the idle-TOKEN veto

[[file:../src/basket/BuckBasketEquityWheel.sol::function _fundPlan][Fund]] and
[[file:../src/basket/BuckBasketEquityWheel.sol::function _trimPlan][Trim]]
return no plan while any idle TOKEN exceeds the grain. Trim also defers to
Fund. This confirms =BASKET-MACHINE='s diagnosis: a constant deposit stream
can disable the intended rebalancing, including a leashed leg.

Implement the unified response described there: count idle TOKEN in weights,
use it as sell inventory, and let deposits supply underweight positions.
Re-snapshot and re-mark after a sale before a subsequent credit-funded buy.
Risk-reducing liquidity/underwater actions must not depend on a harvest vote.

** Source-confirmed: execution limits need to be expressed in executable liquidity

[[file:../src/basket/MonetaryDesk.sol::function _swapAcross][The desk]] sizes
using pool ERC-20 balances and their ratio. In V3 these are not the active
virtual reserves or marginal exchange rate. The basket's =Marks.depth= and
=CycleMath.reserves= already use active liquidity and sqrt price.

The shared =monetaryLeg= swap helpers use the extreme sqrt-price limits and
have no caller-specific =minOut=. Small notional relative to token balances
does not establish small price impact. Add explicit execution price bounds,
minimum output and live validation. Even active virtual-reserve sizing is
only local between liquidity changes; tick crossings must be bounded.

At constant liquidity and zero fees, selling BUCK equal to 1% of its virtual
reserve raises the TOKEN price in BUCK by =(1.01)^2 - 1=, about 201 bp.
Thus a 1% depth cap is not intrinsically small relative to a 100 bp
monetary band. Repeated allowed steps can compound it further.

** Source-confirmed: wheel scheduling is only partially wired for this economy

The equity deployment in [[file:../alberta_buck/sim/deploy.py::# --- The equity basket's director and work wheel][deploy.py]]
wires basket Equity and Arb work. The equity desk is driven by
=MonetaryKeeperAgent= rather than its own configured Ops slot. Some other
configuration paths wire Compute/Director kinds, but that is not automatic
for every equity deployment.

Choose one explicit deployment topology. A single wheel may schedule both
accounts; separate wheels are also possible. Account isolation does not
require separate schedulers. Sampling dependencies must be checked at the
consumer, since round-robin order alone cannot ensure Sample precedes Desk
when a tick begins halfway through the slot table.

* Minimal implementable architecture

#+begin_example
pool observations + actual account state
                  |
          validated price snapshot
                  |
          +-------+------------------+
          |                          |
  differential EMA ladder       raw bvib + desk filters
          |                          |
     basket Respond            bounded desk / direct K
          |                          |
          +-------- pools -----------+
                      ^
             arb + external traders

WorkWheel schedules bounded steps. Each consumer checks its own prerequisites.
#+end_example

Start with a small bounded set of assets and features. Use one validated
price reader, with a fast raw-bvib path appropriate to K's cadence and a
daily ladder path for the basket. Do not make a 30-minute K cycle depend on
an unlabeled daily cached price. For a small explicitly capped asset set,
one atomic daily sample is simpler than a resumable general analytics engine.

Publish per-leg normalized prices, raw bvib, differential and common-mode
ladders, observation time, coverage and validity. Preserve the different
window vectors: the equity anchor is 1280 days; the existing common ladder
ends at 320. Keep transaction-time account marks and live execution checks
outside stale analytical caches.

For larger jobs, collect against one fixed historical cutoff, compute into
a staging version, and commit only a complete required snapshot. V3's
=observe= can query past intervals while retained history covers them.
Expire a job if that history disappears. Optional spectral features can
publish independently with their own age, rather than delaying critical
price or safety observations. See [[https://developers.uniswap.org/docs/protocols/v3/concepts/price-oracles][Uniswap's oracle documentation]].

Basket Respond should execute one bounded sell, buy, matched pair, or HOLD.
Keep the current understandable excursion/turn rule initially; compare it
against a plain declared-weight no-trade band. A harvest gate may postpone a
discretionary mean-reversion trade, but must not defeat a hard mandate or
risk-reducing action. There is no unconditional positive-ROI promise: retain
trends, structural breaks, fees, gas, demurrage and external arb competition
in the comparison.

* An outer desk band that leaves K room to work

Let =b=bvib=, =z=b-1=, and let positive =u= mean selling/issuing BUCK.
The desk absorbs BUCK for positive z and supplies it for negative z.
K continues to use =e=-z=, independently of whether the desk is active.

Use separate entry and release distances:

#+begin_example
0 < b_release < b_enter

WAIT:   enter ABSORB if z >= b_enter; enter SUPPLY if z <= -b_enter
ABSORB: buy bounded BUCK while z > b_release and budgets permit
SUPPLY: sell bounded BUCK while z < -b_release and budgets permit
HOLD:   no valid advice, insufficient edge, exhausted budget, or unsafe venue
#+end_example

The thresholds may be asymmetric. Require a timely validated reading to
agree with the filtered direction, so an old EMA cannot authorize a trade
after the market has reversed. Time spent outside a wider threshold may
permit a bounded credit draw or other explicitly defined persistent action;
it must not manufacture a retirement that the account did not perform.

Size effort from excess beyond the release boundary, not the full distance
to parity. Conceptually:

#+begin_example
u_requested = -sign(z) * k_d * max(abs(z) - b_release, 0)
u_allowed   = clip by remaining inventory, capital, credit, rate,
              execution-price and liquidity budgets
#+end_example

The gain's units must be explicit: BUCK per unit deviation, or a defined
NAV fraction per unit deviation. A live pool-impact bound should prevent an
operation from intentionally trading through the release band. Current
TWAP will not immediately reveal the trade's full effect, so the check
needs executable spot/liquidity geometry as well as the slower observation.

An illustrative testing configuration is entry at 3% and release at 2%; this
is a hypothesis to test, not a deployment recommendation. Compare several
bands with identical exogenous flows and capital. Do not widen a band merely
to force K to move if the actual economy is already at parity without the
desk accumulating risk.

With the current stored =Ki=643004= and daily elapsed seconds, the unsaturated
integral contribution changes as follows over 90 days of constant error:

| bvib | e | Change in K from the integral |
|------+----+-------------------------------|
| 1.01 | -1% | approximately -0.05 |
| 1.02 | -2% | approximately -0.10 |
| 1.03 | -3% | approximately -0.15 |
| 0.99 | +1% | approximately +0.05 |
| 0.98 | +2% | approximately +0.10 |

These are arithmetic consequences of the controller, not predictions of
market recovery. K starts at 0.75 with an upper rail of 0.95 in the default
experiment; equal opposite errors therefore have unequal remaining rail
authority. The proportional contribution at 1% error is only about 0.001.
The integral consumes the raw error; it does not require a larger Kp.

Select desk capital and pacing together with the residual band. A budget of
10% of NAV spent at 40 bp of NAV each day lasts 25 days, before costs and
valuation changes. A 90-day integral timescale does not make that budget
sufficient. Measure the actual borrowing/repayment response and its lag in
both directions, including an unresponsive population.

The desk needs an explicit reserved-capital floor and an intervention budget
that cannot reset merely because a timer elapsed or a book entry changed.
Net inventory alone misses repeated buy-high/sell-low losses. Track turnover,
actual spending and drawdown using a specified valuation basis. At exhaustion
the desk must stop increasing exposure and allow a wider price departure;
it cannot promise both indefinite parity and finite reserves against
unbounded external selling. Market losses can still reduce capital even
while the desk is inactive.

* The EMA quorum, Fourier buckets and phase

The connection is real, but the current ladder is not a DFT. With regular
daily sampling, each rung implements

#+begin_example
m_w[n] = alpha_w x[n] + (1-alpha_w) m_w[n-1]
alpha_w = 2/(w+1)
H_w(z) = alpha_w / (1 - (1-alpha_w) z^(-1))
#+end_example

This is a one-pole low-pass filter. Every rung passes slow movement; each
has different attenuation and phase delay. The six shorter rungs are thus
correlated measurements, not independent frequency buckets or independent
statistical votes. The equity director votes when the sign of its rung's
daily step opposes the current excursion from the anchor. It discards
amplitude and almost all phase information. The pairs director additionally
smooths velocity, so its delay differs from the equity director's.

The spans are not oscillation periods. Calculating the half-power frequency
from the transfer function gives:

| EMA span, daily samples | Half-power oscillation period, days |
|------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| 5 | 15.3 |
| 10 | 31.2 |
| 20 | 62.7 |
| 40 | 125.6 |
| 80 | 251.3 |
| 160 | 502.6 |
| 1280 | 4021.2 |

The 1280-day rung is intentionally an anchor, not a well-resolved
1280-day spectral channel. For slowly varying signals, the 20-day rung's
group delay approaches 9.5 days. Frequency-dependent delay matters to
monetary action even if a backtest finds the same filter profitable.

Adjacent differences =m_5-m_10=, =m_10-m_20=, and so on suppress the shared
slow component and form broad overlapping bands. They are inexpensive
derived features of the existing state. Geometrically spaced bands have a
closer relationship to logarithmic multiresolution analysis than to a single
uniform DFT grid; this particular ladder is not an orthogonal wavelet transform.

Explicit complex channels would expose amplitude and phase at chosen
frequencies. One option mixes the signal with a fixed sine/cosine oscillator
and smooths both components; another uses damped complex resonators. These
give recursive, exponentially weighted spectral measurements without a full
FFT. A sliding DFT instead maintains a specified finite window, usually
requiring an outgoing sample and a ring buffer.

For a clean narrowband component, phase can distinguish a peak, a trough and
the two directions between them. In mixed, drifting market signals, phase
must be qualified by amplitude, coherence, bandwidth, filter delay and age.
A level shift also produces spectral energy. No transform alone establishes
that a past cycle will continue. Keep a separate level/trend component: a
bank that removes DC would discard the persistent monetary error K needs.

The exact Fourier/filter-bank duality is described in Julius O. Smith's
[[https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/sasp/Dual_Views_Short_Time.html][STFT filter-bank interpretation]],
and the EMA transfer function in his
[[https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/filters/Elementary_Filter_Sections.html][one-pole filter treatment]].
Lindeberg's [[https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02648][time-causal, recursive scale-space research]]
is relevant to efficient multiscale derivatives and delay; it studies
cascaded filters and is not a proof of this parallel quorum's trading rule.

[[file:review/control-filter-bank.svg][Frequency and phase illustration]] is
generated by [[file:review/control-filter-bank.py][control-filter-bank.py]].
It includes a warmed-up synthetic 80-day sinusoid to show what the quorum
actually gates. This is a regular-sampling floating-point calculation, not
a contract simulation or evidence of economic return.

* What the wheel makes feasible, and what it does not

| Calculation | Plausible bounded implementation | Main qualification |
|-------------+----------------------------------+--------------------|
| EMAs, variances, residuals | O(number of features) per accepted sample | Correct elapsed time and quality metadata |
| EMA differences | Subtract already stored rungs | Overlapping bands, no explicit quadrature |
| Selected Fourier channels | Fixed number of complex recurrences per sample | Sampling, leakage, drift and fixed-point stability |
| Small Kalman observer | Two-to-four-state blocks; scalar sequential measurements | Identified model, uncertainty, adversarial outliers |
| Full-window DFT/FFT | Frozen ring-buffer version, resumable stages | More state writes and completion latency |
| Small neural model | Fixed quantized weights, bounded inference | Must outperform simple baselines under regime changes |

Amortization distributes cost; it does not remove total gas, persistent
storage writes, data availability or completion latency. A wheel scans a
bounded number of slots, but a slot can still contain an O(N) sample or a
much more expensive calculation. Give each task an explicit worst-case work
bound, deadline and failure behavior. Admission of a large asset/feature set
must not silently destroy those bounds.

Neither user transactions nor keeper rewards guarantee a maximum response
time. Voluntary calls may disappear during stress. Safety checks belong at
every operation that can increase exposure; expensive analysis should not
be a prerequisite for a basic stop. A clock adapter, timestamp semantics,
sequencer availability and transaction ordering must be validated per chain.

The current EMA gap catch-up substitutes today's reading across missed days.
It is algebraically exact for that assumed held input, not a recovery of
the actual missing path. This distinction is especially important for phase
and persistence. Query retained historical observations where available;
otherwise label the gap, increase uncertainty, or re-prime according to a
specified recovery policy. Setting =dTMax= bounds a K jump after silence but
does not reconstruct the missing integral. The default experiment leaves
that cap unset.

The reserve reward is per working tick, not per completed analysis. Splitting
identical work into many rewarded calls changes payout. Future analytics
need payment tied to unique progress or completion and a total job budget,
so publishing unnecessary tiny stages cannot consume the upkeep reserve.

ANN inference is computationally possible; a large language model currently
has no identified advantage for these numerical control decisions. A useful
learned model would predict a narrowly specified quantity, such as short-term
impact or borrower uptake, with immutable versioned weights and bounded
authority. Proof of correct inference would not prove the prediction correct.

* Stablecoin channels and circuit breakers

Use three or more observation/execution channels around one basket reference
and one monetary authority for a fungible BUCK. Three independent K
controllers writing policy for that same BUCK would create another coupling
problem. Fully separate currencies are a different architecture and would
need conversion and liability rules.

Normalize decimals and quotation direction. Observe stablecoin cross-rates,
multiple TOKEN/stablecoin routes, BUCK/stablecoin routes, depth, age and
where appropriate an external redemption/USD reference. Bound each source's
influence; liquidity weights alone can reward an adversary for temporarily
supplying liquidity. Group venues by issuer, collateral, bridge and oracle
dependencies rather than treating every pool as an independent vote.

There is a fundamental observability limitation:

#+begin_example
(TOKEN priced in S_j) / (BUCK priced in S_j) = TOKEN priced in BUCK
#+end_example

A consistent depeg of S_j can cancel out of that ratio. Every triangular
cycle can remain consistent while the stablecoin fails. Cycle residuals
detect inconsistent prices, not the truth of a dollar peg. Cross-stablecoin
prices can reveal relative disagreement, but cannot determine an absolute
common depeg without an additional trusted anchor or redemption evidence.

DAI and USDS are one dependency family for this purpose: Sky's
[[https://github.com/sky-ecosystem/usds/blob/master/README.md][contracts provide a 1:1 converter]].
Sky's [[https://github.com/sky-ecosystem/dss-lite-psm][USDC LitePSM]] also
illustrates a dependency connecting nominally separate channels. This does
not quantify today's collateral exposure; it shows why a naive majority
of ticker symbols is not a fault-tolerance argument.

A segment state machine can be =HEALTHY -> SUSPECT -> ISOLATED -> RECOVERY=.
Use a fast disagreement/invalidity test and hard flow limits to stop new
exposure, followed by persistent evidence and hysteresis for isolation and
slow recovery. A slower TWAP or statistical alarm can corroborate the event;
it cannot protect against an arbitrarily large first transaction by itself.
No finite detector lag can guarantee zero loss under arbitrary shocks.

Enforcement must cover every protocol-controlled path: accepting stablecoin
collateral/deposits, issuing BUCK, subsidizing arb, routing treasury trades,
valuing backing, transferring capital between segments, and providing or
replenishing LP. Segment exposure and gross outflow limits must be enforced
where assets leave or credit is granted, not just in a user interface.
Do not lend the same backing to several segment accounts without a global
exposure constraint.

An existing Uniswap V3 pool cannot be paused by disabling this wheel or
router. Direct third-party swaps continue. The protocol can stop its own
support and withdraw its own liquidity, but that withdrawal requires a
transaction and cannot reverse a completed drain. If pre-swap rejection is
required, the interface needs an enforceable adapter, controlled venue or
appropriate hook. Even then other external venues remain outside its remit.

For the short-term system, include a segment enable/disable guard and
capital limits now; introduce cross-channel diagnosis once multiple channels
exist. Recovery must preserve the original basket reference and use explicit
valuation/re-prime rules, rather than quietly redefining parity. Across L1
and L2, cross-chain observations also need authenticated delivery and their
own freshness limits; they are not a synchronous shared sensor.

* Longer-term alternatives grounded in related work

** A. Bounded hybrid rules with a shared observer (recommended first)

The proposed outer-band desk, direct PI controller, basket no-trade bands,
and atomically profitable arb form a hybrid feedback system: continuous
prices/accounts plus discrete modes and transaction-triggered actions.
It is economical to audit and implement. It needs explicit budgets and
scenario validation rather than an unsupported claim of optimality.

[[https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/moor.15.4.676][Davis and Norman (1990)]]
establish no-trade regions under proportional transaction costs in their
investment model. That supports the policy shape, not these particular
weights, EMA thresholds or guaranteed ROI. EVM gas includes fixed costs;
minimum trade sizes and hysteresis may be preferable to repeated tiny
trades exactly to the boundary.

[[https://docs.reflexer.finance/risk/pid-failure-modes-and-responses][Reflexer's documented PID failure modes]]
are a close monetary precedent: liquidity, manipulated observations and
ineffective market incentives matter alongside gains. RAI's redemption-rate
actuator differs from BUCK's credit factor, so gains and response times do
not transfer directly.

** B. Small robust observer plus constrained action selection

Estimate a small state: basket parity error, persistent net flow, temporary
inventory imbalance and realized credit uptake. Include actual protocol
actions as known inputs, so a desk's own purchase is not automatically
interpreted as exogenous demand. Unknown external flows remain disturbances;
an observer cannot perfectly separate causes from price alone.

Evaluate a small fixed menu of allowable actions and sizes, or use an
explicit precomputed MPC policy. Penalize parity error, inventory risk,
turnover and credit changes, with hard capital and execution constraints.
Freeze or fall back when estimates are unreliable. Neither approach needs
a generic on-chain nonlinear solver.

[[https://sites.engineering.ucsb.edu/~jbraw/mpc/][Rawlings, Mayne and Diehl's MPC text]]
provides the framework for constrained and robust predictive control.
[[https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06408][Ames et al.'s control-barrier-function work]]
motivates separating safety constraints from performance optimization.
Applying either to BUCK requires a fitted model, explicit disturbance bounds
and allowance for inter-transaction delay; the cited guarantees do not
automatically cover strategic market participants.

** C. Off-chain optimization with on-chain bounded execution

Allow competitive solvers to propose routes or short-horizon actions against
a committed observation version. On-chain logic checks freshness, available
capital, price bounds, segment status and minimum realized outcome, then
executes atomically. The wheel maintains observations and settlement.

[[https://stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/cfmm_routing.html][Angeris et al. (2022)]]
formulate multi-CFMM routing and arbitrage as convex optimization when fixed
execution costs are ignored; including them introduces mixed-integer choices.
This is directly relevant to joint route selection. Feasibility or a
computation proof does not certify fair observations, economic optimality
under omitted costs, or future stability. Always retain a simple fallback.

** Research common to all three

[[https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06046][Milionis et al., Loss-Versus-Rebalancing]]
explains why LP fees and recovered arbitrage must be assessed together with
adverse selection. A profitable arb cycle in one token does not establish
positive combined basket/desk return. Avoid counting the same internal
transfer as two independent sources of profit.

[[https://people.kth.se/~kallej/papers/cdc12_tutorial_tabuada.pdf][Tabuada's event/self-triggered control tutorial]]
connects scheduling and control design. A wheel is an execution mechanism;
a controller still needs acceptable bounds on observation/action delay.

[[https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sastry/pubs/PDFs%20of%20Pubs2000-2005/Publications%20of%20Grad%20Students%2020042005/Sinopoli/SinopoliKalmanFiltering2004.pdf][Sinopoli et al. (2004)]]
analyze Kalman estimation with intermittent observations. Their results make
observation availability part of estimation performance. They concern
specified stochastic models, not arbitrary adversarial oracle manipulation;
BUCK would additionally need robust residual tests and isolation.

The general formulation is
=x_next = f(x, protocol_actions, external_actions, elapsed_time)= with
=observations = h(x) + measurement_error=. External agents may respond to
the published policy. A realistic long-term target is bounded loss and
recovery within a specified disturbance/market regime, with safe degradation
outside it; no finite-capital design can force equilibrium against arbitrary
unbounded inputs.

* Implementation order and acceptance criteria

1. Reconcile equity-desk inventory, debt repayment and persistent-operation
   semantics; enforce remaining-room caps and execution prices. Keep basket
   and desk accounts separate.
2. Add validated, timestamped price/advice interfaces; remove silent spot
   fallback for risk-bearing decisions; make persistence and failure retry
   correct. Wire the intended wheel topology explicitly.
3. Implement the unified basket response and outer-band desk. Preserve
   direct K, integral memory, rails and a deliberate missed-sample policy.
4. Run closed-loop comparisons with identical shocks: static K; K alone;
   K plus basket/arb; K plus basket/arb/desk. Sweep desk bands and budgets
   together, and identify credit response separately for increases and cuts.
5. Add multiple stablecoin channels and enforced segment limits. Then test
   EMA differences, selected spectral channels and a small observer in
   diagnostic mode before they receive execution authority.

Required checks include constant deposits not starving rebalancing; actual
holdings reconciling with desk books across all operation sequences; no
new overshoot of action budgets; stale/partial observations authorizing no
new exposure; no price-invalid trade after a delayed sample; and bounded
gas with the largest admitted configuration.

Economic tests need transient shocks, sustained flow, permanent repricing,
no borrower response, K at both rails, liquidity withdrawal, a depeg and
correlated depegs, missing oracle history, keeper outages, and an attacker
placing trades before and after every predictable action. Randomize caller
order and tick budgets; a simulator that always services every due task
before users trade can hide the relevant failure.

Measure integrated and tail parity error, K trajectory and rail time, actual
issuance/repayment, desk capital and exhaustion time, basket return relative
to hold and a plain-band baseline, net arb recovery, costs, signal age and
worst-case gas. Value strategy return independently of the pool movement
the strategy itself caused. Profitability and monetary stabilization are
separate outcomes.
