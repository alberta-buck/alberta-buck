The phrase "credit disk" does not appear in this branch. The credit

* What the control problem actually is

The parity the controller is built to hold is one basket equal to one
BUCK. In the direct embodiment the setpoint is the constant 1.0 BUCK and
the process variable is =basketValueInBuck()= (=src/BuckKControllerDirect.sol=).
Error is =1.0 - basketValue=. Basket above par means BUCK is cheap
(inflation in the controller's words); K is decreased. Basket below par
means BUCK is dear; K is increased.

=basketValueInBuck= is the declared basket amounts times TWAP prices in
the TOKEN/BUCK pools, centred near =1e18= at par
(=doc/BASKET-MACHINE.org=, contract boundary; =IBuckBasketRef=). It is
not =equity()=. Marking the monetary signal off equity would fold the
lien into cheap/dear. A move in TOKEN prices moves this gauge even when
nobody trades BUCK. That is the disturbance: the target "1 basket" is a
moving bundle of token values, while the setpoint in BUCK stays 1.

K is the credit factor, not a price. =BuckCredit='s limit is K times
marked current value. =EQUILIBRIUM.md= states the same relation as
=creditLimit = collateralValue * K / 1e18=, with the sim default
=k0 = 0.75= and rails =(0, 0.95)= (=alberta_buck/sim/experiment.py=
=DEFAULTS["deploy"]=). A rise offers more credit. A cut withdraws the
offer. Nothing is margin-called (=doc/K-AND-OPS.org= F4, corrected
2026-09-26; =test_a_K_cut_is_no_margin_call= in
=alberta_buck/test/test_equity_basket.py=). Supply answers only if
borrowers take the offer, and the arrival hazard in the sim is an
assumed function of K and bvib, not a measured economy (F4, F10).

The direct PID (=BuckKControllerDirect.compute=) is integral-dominant on
raw bvib. Defaults (=experiment.derive_gains=):

#+begin_example
Kp_real = kp_frac * dk_rail / e_max          = 0.02 * 0.5 / 0.10 = 0.1
Ki_real = dk_rail / (e_max * tau_I * 86400)  = 0.5 / (0.10 * 90 * 86400)
Kd      = 0
#+end_example

Stored gains are =real * 1e12=. =derive_gains()= on this tree returns
=(100000000000, 643004, 0)=. Output is

#+begin_example
buckK = clamp(buckK0 + Kp*err + Ki*I + Kd*dErr/dt, buckKMin, buckKMax)
#+end_example

with =err= in ppm and =I= in ppm·seconds. At a rail the integrator may
only step back toward the band. =doc/BASKET-MACHINE.org= ("The residual
bvib K's integral needs") states the consequence of this Ki: held for one
integral time, a constant fractional error =e= moves K by about =5 e=.
A 1% error is about 0.05 on K. The proportional term on the same error
is about =0.1 e=.

So the problem is not "drive bvib to 1 with K." K is a slow stock lever
on credit capacity. Fast price-holding has to be done by someone else,
or K integrates whatever residual those actors leave, including a
residual that TOKEN-price moves keep shifting. If the fast actors remove
the error, K's integral sees almost nothing (F3, F9: a defended world
hides the controller's own law). =BASKET-MACHINE= raises that as an open
question, not a ruling: the copied desk band may be too tight for the
integral to wind, and the equity calm twins on record did the opposite
(tail bvib about 1.7--2% rich in BUCK, K on the 0.95 rail).

* How BASKET-MACHINE sits against the other optimal-control ideas

** Agreements

- One signal, two modes. The wheel paper ("One filter bank, two modes")
  splits differential =c_i - mean(c)= from common =log(basket in BUCK)=.
  =BASKET-MACHINE= keeps them on two window vectors that are not aliased.
  Index 2 is 20 on both only by coincidence. The common mode stays
  =log(basketValueInBuck)= against par, which is K's process variable and
  the desk's signal. It is not the mean of the legs. =CONVERGENCE= 5.1
  is cited in the director for a +1,000 to +1,700 bp drift of an
  equal-weighted mean over two years.
- K is not moved into the signal, the basket response, or the desk.
  =BASKET-MACHINE= non-goals match =K-AND-OPS= decision 10: the price loop
  reads raw bvib.
- The basket response is a relative-value trade (weights), which
  =CONVERGENCE= §4 calls harvest leg 2. The desk and the undertakings are
  leg 3 (bvib vs par). The wheel's cycles are leg 1 (venue inconsistency).
  =BASKET-MACHINE= does not fold =ArbKind= into the response, and it does
  not hand the basket a quadrant.
- Turn tests stay different. Basket: velocity quorum, 4 of 6, ruled
  2026-09-30. Desk: duration past the leash, because a velocity test left
  Q2 and Q4 inert. =BASKET-MACHINE= refuses to collapse those into one
  boolean.
- Idle TOKEN must not switch the basket's response off. That is a local
  fix to Fund/Trim starvation. It is not a new bvib law. Section 14 of
  =doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= is the evidence the design cites: deposits with
  Fund and Trim never due.
- The 2026-10-01 refinements line up with =K-AND-OPS= on the residual.
  While the desk holds price, K's error is whatever is left inside or at
  the band. One lever zeros one integral (F3, proved as a conserved
  direction in =kdesign.py= and =test_joint_plant_has_a_conserved_direction=).

** Gaps

- =BASKET-MACHINE= copies the deployed monetary band onto the new signal
  and the adapter: deadband =100e9= (100 bp), leash =200e9=,
  =persistEpochs= 30, =measIdx= 2, =kappa1e9 = 250_000_000=, cap 40 bp
  per epoch. It says 40 bp a day is fast beside a 90-day integral. In
  =PairsRebalanceDirector= that cap is *bp of NAV*, and =monetaryEffort=
  is =min(cap, kappa * |cm| / 1e9 / 1e9)= with sign. At |cm| = 100e9 the
  formula yields 25 bp of NAV; at 200e9 it yields 50, then the cap binds
  at 40. A NAV-sized trade is not a bvib increment. The note compares
  them as if they shared units. This tree does not contain the price
  impact that would convert one into the other.
- The persistence seam (level 2) is not in the basket-machine design.
  The adapter still publishes =outright= from =persistEpochs=, and the
  desk's quadrant branches stay. That is the existing outright bit, not
  the K-AND-OPS rule that K carries only a converted stock.
- The estimator in =K-AND-OPS= §5.4 (persistent vs transient flow, then
  =K = K0 + clip(-F_p / g) + Kp e + Ki Int(e)=) is not in
  =BASKET-MACHINE= and is not a contract. =g= in F4 (~50k BUCK/day per
  unit K, and the other step-test figures) is marked thin-cast and not
  to be re-quoted until re-identified. This note does not simulate that
  law.
- LQR position gains, lambda 24, and gamma are retired or demoted
  (=K-AND-OPS= 5.3). =BASKET-MACHINE= does not revive them. It also does
  not use them. The optimal-control content it does use is the no-trade
  band: trade to the boundary, not to the point target, citing Davis and
  Norman (1990). Bertram (2010) thresholds stay open, as in the wheel
  paper.
- The equity desk has no wheel. =deploy.py= deploys one =BasketWheel=
  and does not call =setOps= on =EquityDesk=. Sampling depends on
  =MonetaryKeeperAgent=. A day the wheel is idle is sampled only if that
  keeper runs. =observe='s catch-up is closed form, which is why the
  design refuses a third wheel.
- =creditDepositors= still does =idleToken[i] += amount=. After Deploy
  is deleted, that TOKEN is response inventory and may be sold. Arb's
  code is unchanged; the pool the TOKEN ends in is not.

** Conflicts, or tensions that are not yet rulings

- Tight desk band vs K's integral. Pro-rata E3, as quoted in
  =BASKET-MACHINE=: bvib mean 1.0002--1.0004, inside the 100 bp deadband,
  no advice for 731 days, so the integral barely moves. Equity twins at
  =conv-e3-eq=: tail bvib 0.9805--0.9826, K at 0.950 for 93--98% of that
  tail. Same K, two gauges. The design does not say which world the new
  signal is for. Widening the band is explicitly "a later ruling."
- Weight trades move the TOKEN/BUCK pools that define bvib.
  =CONVERGENCE= E3's question ("do the harvest legs add?") is exactly
  this coupling. =BASKET-MACHINE= says the response is not sized to move
  bvib. It can still move bvib. The wheel then re-pins the same pools.
  Nothing in the proposed step limits that side effect.
- =K-AND-OPS= says K must never see a signal the fast actuators can
  suppress, at a gain. =BASKET-MACHINE= leaves K on raw bvib, which is
  correct under decision 10, and then asks whether the desk should leave
  a larger residual on purpose so the integral can see it. Those are the
  same seam, stated from opposite ends. Neither document builds the
  discrete escalation that was supposed to resolve it.
- A second credit on the same LP is refused as =K / (1 - K)= (three times
  at K = 0.75) if the mark is gross. =DESK-LP-RESERVE.org= is discussion,
  not built. =BASKET-MACHINE= does not adopt the desk depositing its
  grant into the basket.
- Cycle 1's position-coupled designs are not a candidate implementation
  of "better optimal control." At the derived knee the x4 dump paid the
  attacker $3.14M where design A paid $0 (=K-AND-OPS= F2). Any continuous
  high-gain path from an adversary-visible inventory into K is treated
  there as an attack surface.

* A proposed big-picture architecture

This is a reading of the documents that already agree, not a new
controller. Each actuator gets one job. They coordinate by what they are
allowed to see and by time scale, not by sharing a gain.

** Setpoint, sensor, disturbance

- Setpoint: bvib = 1. One declared basket bundle priced at one BUCK.
- Sensor for K and for the desk: =basketValueInBuck()=, TWAP, not spot,
  not =equity()=, not a reserve ratio (F9: reserve-ratio pricing made a
  concentrated position look like a price).
- Sensor for the basket response: per-leg differential of TWAP ticks
  versus the legs' mean, plus the basket's own weights including idle at
  TWAP (=responseWeightsBp= in the proposal). Not bvib.
- Sensor for arb: the three-pool cycle's direct profit. Not a daily
  ladder.
- Disturbance: TOKEN prices (and flow that trades the TOKEN/BUCK pools).
  Both move bvib. A constituent added to the basket jumps bvib;
  =reprime()= exists so that jump is not one PID spike. The proposal
  does not change =reprime=.
- Disturbance the controller must not treat as a gain: anyone's inventory.
  Fills, desk books, and loaded positions reach K only through the
  residual they leave in raw bvib, or through a discrete outright act
  that is still the desk's, not a proportional term.

** Who actuates what

| Actuator | Moves | Does not move | Cadence |
|----------+-------+---------------+---------|
| Private arbs and savers | TOKEN/BUCK and BUCK/USDC toward outside venues, inside their own edge | K | blocks to a day |
| =ArbKind= | the same gaps, when the cycle pays, profit to depositors/treasury | K, weights | a wheel slot, when due |
| Basket response (proposed) | one leg's weight toward the leash or the band edge, paced by =stepBp= and =swapCap= | K; not a bvib target | one wheel slot |
| Desk quadrants | the desk's own TOKEN and BUCK, and its own lien, by =monetaryEffort= in bp of NAV | the basket's =_equityS= (invariant D2); K's gains | keeper, once per epoch, after =observe= |
| Undertakings / ladder / facility (Python) | inventory at their own edge | K, if decision 10 holds | ticks to days |
| K | every credit limit | no price directly; a cut moves price only if someone sells into the premium | =ComputeKind=, =dT= (sim default 1800 s) |

** So they do not fight

1. Relative value and parity stay on different sensors. The response
   may trade a rich leg for a cheap leg while bvib is anywhere. That
   trade is allowed to change bvib as a side effect; it is not allowed
   a second objective of pushing bvib to 1. Giving it that objective
   would duplicate the desk inside the basket's TOKEN, which
   =BASKET-MACHINE= refuses.
2. The desk does not read =mayTrim=, =mayFund=, or per-leg =turned=.
   The basket does not read =monetaryEffort=. The shared contract
   publishes both views and decides neither trade. That is the whole
   of the =BASKET-MACHINE= contract boundary.
3. K does not read the desk's book and the desk does not read K's
   gains. The only coupling is the gauge. If the desk pins bvib inside
   the deadband, K integrates a small error and mostly stays at =buckK0=.
   If the desk is silent (equity twins; =NoAdvice= when effort is 0 or
   the director was not poked), K integrates the raw gauge and will
   rail on a residual of a couple of percent. That is not a fight
   between two laws. It is one law looking at a gauge the other law
   did not move.
4. Anti-windup is the rail protocol, not a second optimizer. Once K is
   at 0 or 0.95, further error in the same direction does not wind I.
   A later release is then only the opposite error. =K-AND-OPS= §5.4's
   "release when the pipe equals the remaining flow" is not implemented;
   do not describe the rail as that landing.
5. The wheel is a scheduler. Kinds that are all due compete for
   =maxWork=. Compute is the first slot, which helps, but a cursor
   left in the Arb range with =maxWork= smaller than the due set
   defers =compute= until a later tick. See the sim. Coordination here
   is "the keeper's =max_work= covers the due set often enough that
   =dT= is actually met," not a new kind.
6. Do not add a proportional gain from desk inventory, wheel profit, or
   idle TOKEN into K. Cycle 1 is the measurement that this coupling
   rails the lever and can pay a book-loader.

What this picture does *not* include, because the branch does not have
it: an on-chain Kalman split, a feedforward =F_p / g=, a joint LQR, a
second credit NFT on basket LP, the desk's reserve inside the basket,
and a route comparison of TOKEN/BUCK versus TOKEN/USDC/BUCK inside the
response. The last is an explicit open note in the 2026-10-01
refinements.

* Work-wheel / EVM state machines that match the code

The understandable implementation is the one the contracts already are:
a slot table, one transition per slot, no hidden continuous controller.

** Machine 1 --- =BuckKControllerDirect= (exists)

State: =buckK0=, =buckK=, =P=, =I=, =D=, =lastUpdate=, =lastBasketCost=,
=lastBuckPrice=. Input: =basket.basketValueInBuck()=. Transition:
=compute()= if =block.timestamp - lastUpdate >= dT=, else return the
cached K. The integral step is =err * dt= (=_integralStep=). Shadow may
override that step; Direct does not. =reprime()= is only the basket,
after =addBasketToken=. =setBuckK0= and =retune= re-derive I so the
output does not step (bumpless). That is the whole law. A work-wheel
implementation is =ComputeKind=: one slot, try/catch, return 1 if
=compute= did not revert.

** Machine 2 --- the wheel cursor (exists)

State: =cursor=, =idleClock=, =scannedIdle=, =reserve=. Transition:
=tick=. From the cursor, examine at most =maxScan= slots, run at most
=maxWork= that are due, pay =kappaBp= of the reserve only if work > 0.
Idle block: one storage read until =rearm=. Kinds compose by C3 order
in =BasketWheel=. Equity today is not one slot: =EquityKind= is
=2N+3=. The proposal cuts Deploy, Fund, and Trim down to one Respond,
and appends Sample after Arb so triangle indexes do not move.

** Machine 3 --- the signal (proposed, not in the bytecode)

State: per-leg deviation, seven EMAs, seven velocities; a separate
common-mode ladder; =lastDay=; persistence counter. Transition:
=observe()=, idempotent per calendar day, catch-up
=e' = x + (e - x) q^n= with the gap capped at 3650. No balances. Callers
named in the design: =SampleKind=, =MonetaryKeeperAgent= immediately
before =monetaryOperation=, Daily, and anyone. Publishing views is not
a trade.

** Machine 4 --- the basket response (proposed)

One slot. Read =running=, =votes=, =excursion=, and
=responseWeightsBp=. Apply leash and band locally. One sell leg and one
buy leg, or one side, or a hold that may still mark. The step is
specified as balance deltas in =BASKET-MACHINE= §5 (idle sold before
LP is burned, fresh =_snap= before each post-trade mark). This note
does not re-derive those formulas and does not simulate them: they
move weights, and the doc says they are not a bvib controller. A sim
that turned =sellCap= into a bvib change would be inventing impact.

** Machine 5 --- the desk (exists; the adapter does not)

=MonetaryDesk.monetaryOperation= already requires an epoch and an
effort. The proposal only re-points =monetaryDirector= at an adapter
whose =epochNow= is the signal's day and whose =monetaryEffort= is
built from =monetaryReading= with the adapter's kappa and cap. Leaving
=opsDirector= on the pairs director while =monetaryDirector= is the
adapter is called out as a two-clock bug. The equity desk is not given
=setOps= by this design.

** What would make a "better" strategy still look like a wheel

If a later ruling adopts the =K-AND-OPS= seam, the on-chain shape that
stays understandable is still a slot, not a solver:

- a level-2 slot that increments a dwell counter when the desk's book
  is past a threshold for a day, and that calls the existing outright
  path (Q2 / Q4) once, as a logged state change;
- K's slot unchanged, still raw bvib, still one integrator;
- no new gain. =K-AND-OPS= allows two further uses of position only as
  experiments: a one-sided rate limit that can only slow K, and a
  saturation flag with gamma left at 0. Neither is to be scheduled as
  a proportional term.

Explicit MPC is dismissed there for this plant: a box-constrained
scalar with anti-windup is already the piecewise-affine law. The
branch's own LQR answer was that the useful term was feedforward of
the disturbance, and that the position feedback gains railed the
lever. Feedforward needs an identified =g= this tree says not to quote.

** Limitations of the new run

- No price impact, no AMM, no borrower uptake, no =g=. A railed K here
  does not move the next day's bvib. In the real system it would, with
  the sign in =BuckKControllerDirect= and with a lag F4 says is a ramp,
  not a same-day price fix (full-authority price shift was reported as
  0.2--0.4% of bvib, thin-cast).
- Constant-error rows match "about =5 e= per integral time" only as an
  average rate. Day 90 at 3 bp is −0.00153 from 0.75, in the
  neighbourhood of the "about 0.0015 over a season" sentence for a
  2--4 bp residual. The year row is larger because the integral keeps
  going. The sentence was about one season, not 365 days.
- The 185 bp row is a constant, not the equity twin's path. It shows
  that a residual of that size, if it does not go away, puts this Ki
  on the 0.95 rail in about half a year from 0.75. It does not show
  that those cells failed for that reason. The doc already says K was
  on the rail for most of the tail.
- Desk effort in bp of NAV was not turned into a bvib path. Doing so
  would invent depth and impact.
- =dt = 86400= is one sample a day. For =Kd = 0= and a piecewise-constant
  bvib it matches finer sampling of the same held value, because
  =I += err * dt= is linear and anti-windup compares the same raw output.
  It does not match a bvib that moves inside the day. F4 says the daily
  price error is noise (std 0.70% open-loop in that writeup) and that a
  derivative term would amplify it. This run sets =Kd = 0=, which is the
  default.

** Allocation distance and graded turn evidence (2026-10-05)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: graded-turn-response
:END:

The clarified objective is to let a reliable outward run continue, then
rebalance a fraction of the remaining allocation error as evidence of an
inward turn strengthens. An overweight TOKEN rising relative to the basket
is allowed to run within its risk limits; a subsequent downward turn makes
selling appropriate. An underweight TOKEN falling is the mirror case:
accumulate as upward-turn evidence develops. This is a proposed policy to
test, not a claim that reversals or rebalancing profits can be guaranteed.

The subsequent clarification makes /cessation of departure/ the start of
participation: release quenching while outward motion slows, before requiring
a sign reversal. The [[file:PAIR-OBSERVER.org][pairwise multi-venue observer experiment]] explored
this rule, proportional counter-token allocation and historical cycle scaling
alongside the existing EMAs. Its measured uncertainty failures remain explicit.

The [[file:BLOCK-PAIR-OBSERVER.org][current per-block EVM specification]] shelves historical commodity-cycle
analysis. It proposes a two-state tracker with route disagreement and measured
prediction-error diagnostics, a complete per-block publication, and separate
basket/arb/desk execution conditions. Full-block service requires an ordered
coordinator and an included caller transaction, not merely a larger round-robin
wheel budget.

The observation phase is implemented as =BlockPairSignals= and displayed in
[[file:control-lab/block-pairs/index.html][the EVM pair-observation lab]]. Matched observer-off worlds retain identical
economic results. The first campaign exposed missing deterministic lag in
the rate allowance. Deriving its response timescale raised clean-cycle
reference coverage from 57.45% to 99.17%, without changing the estimated rate.
See the [[file:control-lab/block-pairs/results.org][observer measurements]].

The subsequent [[file:control-lab/pair-basket/results.org][graded basket comparison]] implements paired transfers with
proportional recipients, atomic execution and own-impact uncertainty. It
fixed a bookkeeping clock that discarded unspent trade time and a gate
that blocked independent LP placement. However, clean-cycle activity still
nearly stops after the first month, and noisy-flat allocation error worsens.
Higher terminal equity marks are not evidence of repeatable harvesting.
The [[file:BLOCK-PAIR-OBSERVER.org][implementation checklist]] therefore keeps the basket experiment under
review before standalone cash legs and the separate arb/desk phases.

The present implementation does not express this objective fully:

- =BasketSignals.votes()= counts signs of six EMA increments opposing the
  long-anchor excursion. Magnitude and uncertainty are discarded, and the
  direction is relative to the anchor rather than the actual allocation gap.
- =BuckBasketEquityResponse._plan()= caps a band-edge allocation gap by a
  per-position step and depth. Its =votes / 6= size adjustment applies only
  after =turned()=. This is neither a continuous evidence score nor a
  uniformly increasing participation rule. In the experimental fast-gate
  adapter, a permitted trade can receive the full step before four votes,
  then a two-thirds step when the fourth vote appears.
- The fast-gate experiment demonstrates that the old veto misses available
  opportunities. It does not establish the desired graded replacement.

*** Separate the book, the observer and the execution rule

For a candidate implementation, let =g = currentWeight - targetWeight=,
=D = bookValue * max(abs(g) - band, 0)=, and =C= be an inward-turn evidence
score between zero and one. Sell for positive =g=, buy for negative =g=.
A simple bounded participation law is:

#+begin_example
requested notional = D * (1 - exp(-lambda * C * elapsedDays))
executed notional  <= min(requested notional, inventory/cash/credit/depth limits)
#+end_example

Here =lambda= sets the maximum response rate. For small intervals this is
approximately =D * lambda * C * elapsedDays=: more imbalance or stronger
evidence increases the opportunity taken, holding other inputs fixed.
The response shrinks as either market motion or our trades close the gap.
An economic minimum size prevents tiny fee-dominated fills. Leash and
liquidity/deleveraging actions remain separately reasoned overrides.

Service frequency must not manufacture extra participation. Consume each
elapsed-time allowance once, recompute from the remaining book, and bound
catch-up after gaps. Repeated wheel calls on the same observation cannot
each spend a fresh full-size opportunity. The no-trade band, rate and
execution limits must be identical when comparing observers.

Keep the declared allocation and any excursion-dependent target lean
visible separately. The current lean itself increases the imbalance when
a TOKEN appreciates; changing it while changing the observer would confound
the comparison. Neither a target update nor our own inventory adjustment is
evidence of a market turn. Begin with relative-price motion as the observer
input and calculate allocation pressure separately from the current book.
For a direct estimate of price-driven weight motion, revalue fixed positions
with the target held fixed; include the LP valuation response. Pool prices
can still contain our own execution impact, so retain intervention markers
and compare outside-venue evidence where available.

*** Two small observers worth comparing

An EMA version can retain continuous, signed slopes rather than votes.
Orient each slope toward closing the actual gap, express it per unit time,
and normalize by a causal noise scale appropriate to its horizon, with a
nonzero floor. A strong short-horizon reversal can then contribute heavily;
weaker sustained agreement across short and medium horizons can also build
the score. Long horizons describe the preceding run rather than vetoing its
end until they reverse too. The filters share observations: agreement is
not independent confirmation, and the score must not be labelled a calibrated
probability. Cap contributions from neighboring horizons and validate false
starts on held-out paths. Deceleration while still moving outward provides
weaker evidence than an observed inward slope; the latest response proposal
releases a bounded amount of participation during that deceleration, then
increases its rate once closure begins.

A two-state Kalman alternative estimates relative-price level and velocity,
and their covariance. A local linear model advances level by velocity times
elapsed time, allows velocity to change, and observes a noisy level. This is
a standard state-space construction; see the
[[https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/notebooks/generated/statespace_local_linear_trend.html][local linear trend model and implementation]]. For the proposal here, orient
estimated velocity toward closing =g= and compare it with its estimated
standard error and a noise floor. Map evidence beyond a minimum meaningful
inward speed continuously onto =C=. The resulting score describes the
model's evidence for present motion, not the probability of profitable
execution or of reaching the allocation target.

Kalman filtering estimates state and error covariance under a specified
model; see [[https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~./motionplanning/papers/sbp_papers/k/Kalman1960.pdf][Kalman's original paper]]. It does not remove the responsiveness
versus noise trade-off. With a scalar random-walk state, direct observations
and a converged constant gain =a=, its update is simply
=level = (1-a)*level + a*observation=: an EMA. The useful additional structure
here is velocity and uncertainty, not the name of the filter. Misspecified
process or observation noise can make its confidence misleading.

Use historical initialization of state and covariance, causal noise
estimates, uncertainty floors and elapsed-time-aware propagation. Do not
count overlapping TWAP observations as independent new evidence. Large
innovations can indicate a real jump as well as bad data: flag them for
diagnosis rather than automatically rejecting every exploitable displacement.
Atomic arb remains independent of a directional turn estimate. Only filtered
estimates available at execution time may drive actions; retrospective
smoothing is a separate diagnostic. These distinctions are developed in
[[https://users.aalto.fi/~ssarkka/pub/cup_book_online_20131111.pdf][Sarkka's Bayesian Filtering and Smoothing]].

A two-state, scalar-observation filter has bounded work per TOKEN: two state
values, three independent covariance entries, and scalar innovation division.
It is a plausible EVM candidate, subject to fixed-point/covariance tests and
gas measurement. The wheel can service constituents incrementally and publish
coherent dated observations. Acceleration and switching-regime models are
later comparisons if measured errors justify their extra assumptions.

The related trading-control literature also separates signal estimation
from how quickly to trade. [[https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/DynamicTrading.pdf][Garleanu and Pedersen (2013)]] derive partial
adjustment with signals of different persistence and transaction costs.
That supports studying graded adjustment and signal horizons, but their
objective and market model do not establish optimality for our LP, credit,
desk and endogenous BUCK-price system.
