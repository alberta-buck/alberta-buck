#+DATE: 2026-10-11

The machine is described in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE.org][the design as built]] and measured in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org][the results]]. This is the
ordered list of work that takes it from a lab subject to the replacement for the older basket,
desk and signal contracts. Each numbered step is meant to be one reviewable change. Check a box
when its step is merged, and delete this file when the list is empty.

Some items were carried here from the older working documents when those were reviewed on
2026-10-10. Each names its source, so the detail can be found while the source still exists.

* 0. Two rules agreed on 2026-10-10

** Pay the callers, and measure everything net of it

Gas is modelled by paying for it. From the basket's side an operation is free so long as the
slice it gives the wheel covers the callers and still leaves a gain. Agreed: the wheel's reserve
pays in BUCKs, a fixed share of what it holds to each turn that does work; the basket fixes
what it spends, and the gas price sets how many turns that buys.

- [ ] Give the basket a way to fund the reserve. There is none on-chain today: =WorkWheel.fund=
  takes BUCKs from whoever calls it, and only simulated agents do. Agreed on 2026-10-10: the
  upkeep facet's Daily step pays in 10% of the day's fees collected and arbitrage credited, up
  to the reserve's cap. The 10% is a starting value, not a measurement. The basket's quarter of
  a depositor's gain is not part of it: by ruling that leaves only when governance redeems the
  treasury's shares (=BASKET-EQUITY.org= 13.5, item 9).
- [ ] Say where the excess goes when the reserve is at its cap. The wheel's design returned it
  to the depositors (=BASKET-WHEEL.org= 8.3); here that means it stays in the basket.
- [ ] Switch the reserve on in the lab, and give the lab a keeper that calls only when the pay
  covers twice its gas at a stated gas price and ETH price. A turn that does no work pays
  nothing, so a real caller simulates first and sends only a working turn; the keeper should do
  the same. Report every result net of the funding.
- [ ] Let deposits and exits turn the wheel. Inside another transaction a turn whose work is
  done costs a storage read; alone it costs the 21,000-gas base as well. The shell's deposit
  and redeem should advance the wheel by one bounded turn (=BASKET-WHEEL.org= 5 and 8.2), which
  needs the idle scan below to be cheap first.
- [ ] Record gas per wheel and per kind in each frame. =doc/review/wheel-gas.py= does it from
  outside for now.
- [ ] Make the idle turn cheap, and due only when there is work. Most of an idle turn's 900,000
  gas is re-reading pools and asking the policy about every ordered pair, and nearly every turn
  runs because the credit's mark is a grain stale. Cache within the response, skip the policy
  when no share is urgent, and let a stale mark wait for work that needs it.
- [ ] Tie the smallest trade to what a turn is paid, in place of the fixed 100 ppm grain. The
  steady release trades on every turn a gap is open, and each constituent now trades on its
  own, so a turn can hold several small swaps. The allowance already accumulates while a
  constituent waits, so a floor on the piece costs no pace.

** Urgency in place of the allocation band

Built: see [[file:BASKET-MACHINE.org][the design]] for the rule and [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org][the results]] for what it earns.

- [X] Trial it from outside the contracts. The trial script went when the rule was built.
- [X] Build it: counts in the policy, gaps reported at each response, a view that carries the
  counts to the present. A transfer no longer restarts a count.
- [X] Patient arms in the sizing campaign, and an urgency pane on the lab page.
- [ ] Decide between urgency and a plain line again. With the lean and the open gap, a
  patience of 1, 2 or 4 gives the same answer on cycles and nearly the same elsewhere. A
  2-point line with the lean matches it on cycles, earns four fifths as much on commodity-like
  prices with under two fifths of the transfers, and loses three fifths as much on the one-way
  move. Judge on gain per transfer net of what the callers are paid, on more than one path.
- [ ] Decide whether growth should follow the gap or its square. The harvest from closing a gap
  goes as its square; the built rule uses the gap.
- [ ] The count advances only when the basket responds. In the lab that is nearly every turn.
  Where responses are rare, an unobserved stretch earns at most a day, so the count lags.
  Decide whether the Daily step should report gaps too.
- [ ] Offer patience against the 2-point band as a selector in the interactive lab. It doubles
  the runs of every stage with the basket active.

* 3. Move the simulators onto it

The older contracts stay in the next release beside the new ones. They go when these runs show
the machine is clearly better in every regime.

- [ ] Python: the general simulator (=loop.py=, =agents.py=, =snapshot.py=, =scenario.py=,
  =basket_wheel_agent.py=, =director_agent.py=, =server.py=) reads the basket through about thirty
  functions. Most already exist on the equity shell (=basketValueInBuck=, =constituents=,
  =deposits=, =depositToken=, =redeem=). List what the pro-rata, fence and ops paths use that the
  shell lacks (=sweepTreasury=, =treasuryBuckPending=, the fence calls, the director hints) and
  decide for each: provide, replace or drop.
- [ ] JS: =eqworld= deploys =BuckBasketProRata= with =BuckWithBasketHooks=. Port it. The third
  sandbox planned in =DEMO-SANDBOX.org= is not started and should start on the machine.
- [ ] The savings sandbox. Its page talks only to the shell, so saving and redeeming need
  nothing. What is done: the server runs a world on the machine when its experiment asks; the
  frames carry the leaned targets, each response by what it did, the credit and the desk
  policy's side and days; the page draws them, knows the strict venue's refusal, and offers
  the upkeep in its world settings; the world is on its five commodities. What is left:
  - Five years each way, old machinery and new, on real prices and on their reverting twins
    (=doc/review/savings-old-new.py=). Write the outcome into the results.
  - Make the machine the hosted world's default once it holds up.
  - The page's screenshot. Its browser test passes on both upkeeps
    (=make nix-venv-sandbox-savings-test=, and with =SAVINGS_TEST_BASKET=equity-machine=).
  - The published contracts list (=scripts/contracts-dist.py=) carries only =BasketDesk= of the
    machine's contracts, so a checkout without a Foundry build cannot serve it.
- [ ] Define the comparison before running it: the regimes, the measures and what "clearly
  better" means for each. The earlier programs already name most of it.
  - /Rebalancing regimes./ Reverting, trending, the equilibrium casts, the commodity profile.
  - /Disruptions, from the catalogue/ (=CONVERGENCE.org= 3): a squeeze and a dump at one and
    four times size, a slow grind each way, book-loading, a sensor fault, liquidity leaving.
  - /Disruptions never yet run/ (=REBALANCING-EQ.org=, recommendations 3, 4 and 7): a commodity
    spike in the middle of a run, a mass exit of depositors, a shift in the rate cycle, an
    oversupply flood that drives K to its lower rail, a population that turns over.
  - /Measures./ Depositor value at reference, parity error, drawdown, turnover, gas. For the
    desk, its inventory beside its profit, never profit alone (=K-AND-OPS.org= F9).
- [ ] Hold fixed what is not being compared. In the debtor matrix nothing about the controller
  changed between every arm failing and every arm passing: what changed was how agents execute
  (at once or paced), how deep the exit route is, and who the population is
  (=REBALANCING-EQ.org=, finding 1). Old and new must share all three.
- [ ] Vary one axis at a time from a baseline, not a full grid (the star method, =WAVE3.org=).
  Run more than one seed before believing any arm: three of five seeds failed the equilibrium
  campaign's plain baseline.
- [ ] Keep cells repeatable. A new agent at count zero must leave every existing cell identical
  byte for byte; the order of a cell's settings is part of its identity; no simulator code is
  edited while a grid runs. These were the earlier program's rules and the comparison needs
  them.
- [ ] Judge the desk's numbers in a populated market. The earlier program's verdict
  (=ORGANIC-SCALE.org=): a thin cast can settle whether a rule engages at all or whether an
  attacker can move K, but not gains, capacities, carrying costs or an attacker's profit, which
  depend on how deep and how populated the world is. Its gate was that no monetary operation
  is judged in a world whose own population has not passed. It also found that holders with
  standing BUCKs are what makes retirement possible: arms whose savers held none showed
  issuance only (=REBALANCING-EQ.org=, finding 4). The lab's one buyer and one seller, both
  spent by day 120, is thinner than the cast that verdict was about.
- [ ] Run old against new on identical inputs and write the outcome into the results.

* 4. Decisions the evidence is waiting on

The first few are argued in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org][the results]]. The rest come from the older working documents.

- [X] Wallet-sourced transfers left half their value as BUCKs inside the recipient's position,
  and turned the basket's spare TOKEN into BUCKs. Resolved by the net mark: every TOKEN is
  paired on arrival with BUCKs the basket issues, there is no spare TOKEN, and a sale and a
  purchase are separate acts on the line.
- [ ] The pools rebalance first, and badly. A liquidity position sells a TOKEN as its price
  rises and buys as it falls, at every price on the way. In the shock run the basket's pools
  shed 282 of 2,807 TOKEN on the way up, at a mean of 102 BUCKs against a peak of 110, before
  the policy had sold anything, and twice what its transfers then sold. A 30% jump showed as
  34.1% of the book where a wallet would have shown 36.6%. Decide how much of the book belongs
  in the pools. Owning the arbitrage would recover some of what they give up.
- [ ] Whether to count owned arbitrage at all before per-block cadence and a competing
  arbitrageur are in the lab. Today it measures the feed's step. The wheel's own analysis
  (=BASKET-WHEEL.org= 4) bounds what is real: a gap between two and three pool fees is worth
  taking only to the basket, whose fee on its own pool comes back to it. Above three fees a
  searcher takes the whole gap and the basket keeps its fees. The earlier cast's arbitrageur
  also wanted a 60 bp margin that real searchers compete away. Measure owned arbitrage inside
  that band, against an arbitrageur at a competitive margin.
- [ ] Lead the tracker. In the shock run the first sale now comes a day after the jump, where
  it came 1.75 days after before the deep market and the lean. The day that is left is the
  tracker's: it will not call a turn until it has seen the rate fall for one response time, and
  until then the policy reads the TOKEN as still rising.
- [ ] The lean is built; what is left is to judge it. At a tilt of one half the gain on cycles
  rises from 41,472 to 70,345 and on commodity-like prices from 19,782 to 27,204, and the loss
  on the one-way move grows from 5,174 to 11,004. The older design ran a tilt of one against a
  multi-year average (=BASKET-EQUITY.org= 13.5, item 5).
  - The tilt. One half undoes the pools' damping and no more. Judge it on more than one path
    and seed, against a wallet holder who rebalances, and on five years of real prices.
  - Who keeps the reference true. Decided 2026-10-11: fixed when the basket is made, changed
    by governance if at all, and in time adjusted automatically over many years. A reference
    that follows price slowly is not needed for bounded commodities: over the five years to
    2025-09 the simulator's five stayed between 0.67 and 1.44 times their own basket, and a
    tilt of one half reached the 15% cap only for gold, on 9% of days. Gold ends that window
    at 1.39 and still rising, with the basket at the cap. That is a hard case to test against,
    not a fault to tune away.
  - Where it lives. The facet works the targets out from the shell's settings. The policy still
    knows nothing of them, and its direction guard keeps its own reference (step 1).
- [ ] What holds the price inside the band. The earlier architecture had four layers
  (=CONVERGENCE.org= 3): the wheel for agreement between venues; undertakings, a stress fee and
  a facility that held the price inside the band from tick to tick; a persistence test that
  turned a lasting position into a change of supply; and K for the stock of credit over
  months. This machine has the wheel's arbitrage, a desk that acts only beyond 3%, and K.
  Inside 3% the expectation (2026-10-11) is that profit-seeking agents hold parity, trusting K
  to restore it. The basket now acts as one of them on its own book: its size leans against
  parity, five times the error as a share of equity. The undertakings, the ladder, the facility
  and the seeder exist as simulator agents, not contracts. Measure what the lean and those
  agents hold between them before adding an inner layer.
- [ ] One refused sample clears the desk's persistence. The desk draws credit only after 30
  consecutive valid days on one side, and =OuterBandDeskPolicy= clears its side and its count
  whenever the reference is not fresh. Sampling is permissionless, and a reading is refused
  when any pool's spot is 5% from its TWAP. So one push of one pool, once a month, at the
  moment a sample is due, may be enough to stop the desk ever issuing. The earlier program met
  the same guard, noted that it trips on exactly the days an attack is running, and ruled that
  a value that cannot be read is held at its last good reading and flagged stale
  (=K-AND-OPS.org= F8). Cost the attack, then decide between holding, a tolerance of missed
  days, and a count that decays.
- [ ] How long is persistent. Thirty days is assumed. The earlier program left the horizon open
  and said the book's own dwell should set it (=K-AND-OPS.org=, decision 27). It also proposed
  an estimator that would make the test continuous: a two-state filter that splits each day's
  net flow into a lasting part, which is K's business, and a passing part, which is the desk's.
- [ ] Deposits and exits. A deposit is paired on the next turn and an exit is paid in BUCKs
  from the line, then sold down over the turns that follow. Measured on the savings world;
  the lab still has neither. What is left is pairing inside the deposit itself, so that
  nothing waits even a turn (see "let deposits and exits turn the wheel", step 0).
- [ ] The net rule, as built, and what is still to settle. K bounds what the basket has let
  out: lien - BUCKs held <= K x equity. In normal running it has issued at most half of that;
  the rest is for exits. A cut in K forces no sale. The most TOKEN the basket can hold stays
  (1 + K) x equity; counting gross assets as collateral, the leverage loop of lending
  protocols, would allow 1/(1 - K) and is not taken.
  The trial that proved it, on the savings world's first year, one seed (the fork that became
  the facet, before the leash was added to it):
  | | Older | Machine before | Net trial |
  |--------+--------+--------+--------|
  | TOKEN waiting, day 30 / 90 / 364, real prices | 6.6% / 0 / 0 | 45.8% / 33.2% / 12.7% | 0.1% / 0 / 0 |
  | Share price at day 364, real prices | 1.0389 | 1.0362 | 1.0523 |
  | Share price at day 364, reverting prices | 1.0484 | 1.0745 | 1.0691 |
  | Pool fees, real prices | 28,080 | 22,482 | 35,720 |
  | Basket's cost in BUCK off 1, mean, real / reverting | 0.65% / 0.60% | 0.62% / 0.59% | 0.61% / 0.53% |
  | Gas, billions, real prices | 2.27 | 2.49 | 2.77 |
  It had issued between -3.5% and +16.4% of equity, never near its ceiling of 37%, and could
  spend at least 57% of equity at every day's end. Read the share prices with care: on real
  prices the lead over the older upkeep is made in the first month, when the older upkeep
  sells half of what waits while the pools settle, and the two grow alike after it; and the
  trial's lien is twice the size, so it accrues more relief than the demurrage it has yet
  borne (see step 1).
  Still to settle:
  - The parity gain (5) and the ceiling (half). Both are first values.
  - The policy's ordered pairs. A constituent is worked against BUCKs alone, so one reading
    for each against the rest would replace n x (n - 1) questions a turn.
  - Whether the rate should count only the others that agree. It is the average over all of
    them, so one closing pair among four moves a constituent at a quarter of the pace.
  - One seed and one year. The five-year runs are the next evidence.

- [X] What held deployment back before the net rule, measured the same day. TOKEN waiting, as
  a share of equity, real prices: 45.8% at day 30 and 12.7% at day 364; with a reserve of 4% of
  the gross and no flow term, 42.1% and 16.1%; with a trial that sold half of what waited, as
  the older upkeep does, 10.8% and none. Placing was one constituent a turn under swap caps,
  and the liquidity target held a fifth of the gross for three months after a deposit. Selling
  to place cost 2.4% of share price in a year on reverting prices.
- [ ] Place deposits toward the targets. A deposit lands as its own TOKEN in its own pool, so
  the depositors' mix pushes the weights (=BASKET-EQUITY.org= 14.2, items 4 and 5). Two cures
  were proposed and neither is built: place a deposit's value by the targets, selling nothing
  already held; or price the mix into the entry charge, so a deposit that moves the weights
  toward target pays less.
- [ ] The desk's reserve as basket shares. An idea of 2026-09-30, not built; its note is
  removed and this is its record. The desk would deposit its founding TOKEN in the basket, hold
  the receipt, and count it in the net value its credit is marked at.
  - /For./ The reserve earns instead of idling. A basket costs more BUCKs when the BUCK is
    cheap, so the shares buy more exactly when the desk must buy. A large exit is paid in kind,
    which is the TOKEN the desk sells. The mark is net of the lien, so pledging cannot spiral.
  - /Against./ Depositing and exiting change supply and depth inside the pools, not the price,
    so they are no substitute for the desk's swaps, and an exit thins the pools during an
    attack. Every round trip pays the basket's quarter of the gain and both charges.
  - /To decide it./ Three desks on the shock arms: the grant as plain TOKEN; the grant as
    pledged shares; the same with part of the credit drawn at founding. Read the deviation of
    the BUCK from its basket, days K spent at a rail, the desk's profit and inventory, the
    depositors' return, and how often the desk had no lien to retire.
  - /Open./ Is the desk's receipt exempt from the quarter? How much stays as plain TOKEN? Does
    the desk's exit queue behind depositors' in the same stress? Is a standing draw sized in K
    or in the basket's equity?
- [ ] Rebalancing loses to holding in a lasting trend. On the 2020-25 history, in which bitcoin
  rose ninefold, the older directors lost 1% to 4% a year against holding (=CONVERGENCE.org= 4).
  Bitcoin was most of that, and it is out of the basket. The lab tests a lasting move only with
  the two-step profile. Its historical profile removes each TOKEN's trend by construction, and
  its commodity profile gives all three the same one. The five-year savings world on its five
  commodities, old machinery against new, is the real test (step 3).
- [ ] Take bitcoin out of the record. The papers (=alberta-buck-ethereum.org=, =-basket=,
  =-wheel=) and the working documents (=REBALANCING-EQ.org=, =CONVERGENCE.org=,
  =CARRY-CONVEXITY.org=, =BASKET-EQUITY.org=) still describe a six-TOKEN basket with a bitcoin
  satellite, and their five-year results were measured on it. Say in each what a constituent
  must be, and rerun on five whatever is still claimed.
