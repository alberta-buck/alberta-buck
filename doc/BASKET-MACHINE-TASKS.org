* 0. Two rules settled on 2026-10-10

These came out of the results and go in before the consolidation, because both change what the
lab measures.

** Pay the callers, and measure everything net of it

Gas is modelled by paying for it. From the basket's side an operation is free so long as the
slice it gives the wheel covers the callers and still leaves a gain.

- [ ] Record gas per wheel and per kind in each frame. =doc/review/wheel-gas.py= does it from
  outside for now: upkeep is about 4.8 million gas a day at four turns a day, a turn that
  trades nothing costs 900,000, and a transfer adds 900,000.
- [ ] Switch the wheel's reserve on in the lab. Fund it with a slice of the basket's income, set
  the share paid per working turn, and give the lab a keeper that calls only when the pay
  covers twice its gas at a stated gas price and ETH price. Report every result net of the
  funding.
- [ ] Decide the slice. It should be a fixed share of income with a cap, as the wheel paper has
  it, so the basket's cost is known and the reserve is a gas budget and not a second yield.
- [ ] Do not pay "the last turn in the block". Pay each turn that does work, as the wheel does
  now. Paying only the last one makes callers wait for each other. One call can already do all
  the work that is due.
- [ ] Make the idle turn cheap. Most of its 900,000 gas is re-reading pools and asking the
  policy about every ordered pair. Cache within the response, and skip the policy when no share
  is urgent.
- [ ] Tie the smallest transfer to what a turn is paid, in place of the fixed 100 ppm grain.

** Replace the allocation band with urgency that builds

Today a constituent may be rebalanced only when its share is more than 2 points off target. On
commodity-like prices that is 4% of the time. The replacement, per constituent:

#+begin_example
urgency  <-  max(0, urgency + (|gap| - slack) x days elapsed)      reset when the gap changes side
may be rebalanced when urgency >= patience
#+end_example

=gap= is in points of the book. =patience= is in point-days and is the one knob: with a slack of
a quarter point and a patience of two point-days, a gap of 2.25 points qualifies in a day, 1.25
in two, 0.75 in four, and a quarter point never does. This is a CUSUM, the cumulative-sum test
used to catch a small shift that persists; the same thing is called integrate-and-fire in
electronics and send-on-area in event-triggered control, against the send-on-delta of a fixed
band. It decides only whether a gap may be worked. The pair policy still decides when, and the
gap still sets how much.

- [ ] Keep the accumulators in the policy, which already holds per-TOKEN state, so the shell's
  storage is untouched. The upkeep facet reports its gaps once per response.
- [ ] Trial it as arms of the sizing campaign at patience 1, 2 and 4 point-days against the 2-
  and 1-point bands. Judge on gain per transfer, not gain alone.
- [ ] Offer patience as a selector in the interactive lab. This replaces the idea of a 1-point
  against 2-point selector.

* 4. Decisions the evidence is waiting on

Each is argued in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org][the results]].

- [ ] Wallet-sourced transfers leave half their value as BUCK inside the recipient's position.
  At the lab's sizing the three wallets are empty in about 90 days and the basket is 13% net
  BUCK. Where no one supplies that BUCK, K runs to its rail. Either accept a fully paired
  book as the resting state, or make a wallet-sourced sale buy into the wallet.
- [ ] Measure the gap on the TOKEN held, not on weights that liquidity mutes, so the urgency
  rule above does not depend on how much of the book is paired.
- [ ] Whether to count owned arbitrage at all before per-block cadence and a competing
  arbitrageur are in the lab. Today it measures the feed's step.
- [ ] Lead the tracker: the release starts about 1.7 days after a turn, the filter's own lag.
- [ ] A target lean past the declared weight, once timing is settled. It is zero today.
