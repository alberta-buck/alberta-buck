* 2. The answer, in one page

K and the monetary operations are two controllers with two different
jobs, and the branch's central mistake was to wire the second one's
inventory into the first one's gains.

- *The monetary operations* (the desk's quadrants, the undertakings and
  their ladder, the facility population, the seeder) hold the PRICE
  inside the band, now, by taking positions.  Their process variable is
  bvib against their own edge; their output is a trade; their timescale
  is ticks to days; their cost is carry, and their failure mode is
  inventory that will not go away.
- *K* holds the STOCK of credit-backed BUCK at the level at which a
  basket bundle prices at par over months.  Its process variable is the
  PERSISTENT component of net demand at par; its output is a
  credit-limit multiplier; its timescale is the 90-day integral time and
  the borrowers' own uptake lag; its failure mode is saturation.
- *The seam between them is PERSISTENCE, not magnitude.*  A position that
  outlives the fast actuators' willingness to hold it is escalated,
  discretely and auditably, to a permanent supply change (the desk's Q2
  burn / Q4 acknowledgment), and only then does K carry it.  K does not
  read the books continuously at a gain.

The branch built the continuous coupling (D4, then D7's second PID loop
on the aggregate position) and then measured it on a 66-cell grid.  The
grid says: the price-only controller passes every arm; every
position-coupled design pins K at its ceiling somewhere; and at the
gain the offline derivation recommended, an attacker who loads the
book ends the x4 dump $3.14M ahead where it ends flat under every other
design.  Coupling K tightly to the operations book hands an adversary a
lever on the money supply.

Optimal control and estimation are still needed, but not where the
branch put them.  The LQR's own answer was that the optimal law is
mostly FEEDFORWARD from the disturbance flow; its feedback gains on the
position rail the lever.  So the estimator's job is the SPLIT -- how much
of today's flow is persistent (K's business) and how much is transient
(the desk's business) -- which is a two-state Kalman filter on the fused
net flow, and is exactly the persistence test made continuous.  Every
estimated input an adversary can manufacture must reach K only through a
rate limit and a persistence test, never through a proportional gain.

Read this as the design fact it is: *any continuous, high-gain path from
an adversary-visible inventory into the money supply is an attack
surface.*  The book-loading attack of prediction 10 succeeds -- not
through the market, but through the controller.

** F3.  One lever can zero one integral (STRUCTURAL, algebraic)

WP-16, =alberta_buck/sim/kdesign.py=, test
=test_joint_plant_has_a_conserved_direction=.  For the reduced plant --
price error self-regulating, aggregate position integrating, one lever,
both integrals costed -- the combination =k_s (a Int(e) + e) + k_e s= is
unchanged by any input (z A = z, z B = 0).  The joint LQR has no finite
solution; the Riccati iteration diverges and its gains drift with the
horizon.

The economic content: the price integral and the facilities' inventory
are two GAUGES ON ONE QUANTITY, the cumulative net demand for BUCK at
par.  K moves supply; the band decides which gauge the flow lands in.
While a facility holds price, the flow is in inventory and the price
gauge reads nothing; when the facility is drained or absent, the same
flow is in price and the inventory gauge is pinned at its cap.  K never
has two independent errors to serve.

Consequence, kept: K carries ONE integrator on ONE signal.  If both
integrators exist, their contents can shift between them without moving
K (a closed-loop eigenvalue at exactly 1, eigenvector (0, Kqi, 0, -Ki)),
bounded in the contract only by the rails -- and the pathological case is
two integrators pinned at opposite rails with K interior and the
anti-windup fighting a saturation that is not real.

** F4.  K is a weak, slow, asymmetric lever (THIN-CAST: re-identify before quoting)

WP-16's step tests: ten 1-year cells, gains zeroed at day 120, K stepped
at day 150; plus three loaded-book (grind) confirmation cells.

| quantity                                | measured                                      |
|-----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------|
| supply flow per unit K                  | ~50k BUCK/day as a RAMP (R2 0.81); 56k loaded |
| at full rail authority (dk_rail 0.5)    | ~25k BUCK/day                                 |
| a 60-day $8-12M grind demands           | 130-200k BUCK/day                             |
| draining a full $15M book at the rail   | ~600 days                                     |
| price shift from full authority         | 0.2-0.4% of bvib                              |
| daily price error with the loop open    | std 0.70%, lag-1 autocorrelation -0.53        |
| a cut to K 0.55                         | -6.3M BUCK in 5 days (the SAVERS sold; see 3) |
| cuts vs rises, BUCK/day per unit K      | 55k vs 44k at depth 10; 44k vs 75k at depth 40 |

Three design consequences, all of which survive re-identification even
though the numbers will move:

1. *The daily price error is noise at the day scale.*  The cast restores
   price within the day and overshoots.  A larger proportional gain moves
   K by that noise; a derivative term amplifies it.  This closes the
   "faster K" direction: K cannot be made responsive by tuning.
2. *K acts through voluntary borrowing.*  Its channel is the borrowers'
   arrival hazard and their K-scaled limits, so a step in K yields a
   supply ramp that compounds late.  A K move lasting a day moves supply
   by a few thousand BUCK.
3. *The lever is asymmetric -- but neither side compels.*  A rise OFFERS
   credit nobody has to take.  A cut WITHDRAWS the offer: it lowers every
   credit limit, possibly below the BUCK an account has already issued,
   and nothing is called -- there is no borrowing to call.  An account
   under water simply cannot issue more; one with credit left can issue
   less.  (Corrected 2026-09-26, design owner.  The -6.3M "at once" that
   read as a margin call was the SaverAgent population: the cut made BUCK
   dear -- spot 1.057 -> 1.088 on the step day -- and savers sell at a
   premium, 6.4M of =saver_hold= in days 150-160 of =d10-m20= against
   =d10-ctrl=, supply contracting as that BUCK found the accounts drawn
   below zero.  A price response of the cast, not a mechanism of the
   protocol.)  So a cut acts on the flow of NEW issuance, like a rise,
   and on the stock only through the price it moves; the two sides
   differ in size (whoever is near their limit, whoever sells into a
   premium), not in kind.  No single linear gain describes both.

*Why these are thin-cast numbers.*  The flow per unit K is set, in
today's sim, by one line: a pending borrower's arrival clock advances at
=(K / 0.75) x (1 + 5 (1 - bvib))=, capped at 3x
(=equilibrium_agents.py=, the =endog= hazard: =arr_k_ref=, =arr_gain=,
=arr_max=).  That is an assumed elasticity, not an economy.  The
within-day price reversion is likewise the excursion arbs' thresholds and
their effectively unlimited capital.  Re-identify both on the merged tree
before any number here is quoted again -- and see F10, because master's
insurance-pool change moves the borrower's economics directly.

** F5.  The fast facilities' books are transient, not integrating (STRUCTURAL, with a thin-cast caveat)

WP-16 finding 2.  On a calm arm at an engaged band the undertakings' desk
chases the daily noise: it absorbs and unwinds at par within days (|q| >
1000 BUCK on 12-20% of days, max 50k, 38 trades in a control cell), and a
K step leaves the book unchanged.  The only K-dependence is a STANDING
book of +126k BUCK per unit K -- proportional to the flow,
self-regulating, 0.8% of the reserve.

So the regime D7's position loop was designed for -- "the facility absorbs
the flow while it holds price, so the position integrates" -- is not the
regime a calm world is in.  Feeding a transient book to an integrator is
what produces F1's permanent bias.  The position signal only integrates
during an excursion, which is exactly when the lever cannot answer it.

** F6.  Habituation is not the binding constraint; saturation is (STRUCTURAL)

Cycle 1's habituation columns (prediction 9): where an excursion existed,
the aggregate position returned to within 10% of zero in 18-42 days on
the dumps, 103-104 days on the cohort step, 147 days on the book-load,
with 0-2 sign changes -- comfortably inside the 190-day window the
derivation's tau_s implies.  The books do come back.  What does not come
back is K, once it is at the rail.  Any future gate on the position loop
should test the RAIL and the bias, not the return time.

** F8.  An unreadable NAV is a sensor fault, not saturation (STRUCTURAL; decision 9, closed)

=BuckBasketOps.capacity()= sizes the desk's caps against NAV read through
=_navBuckSafe()=, which swallows reverts to 0.  NAV is unreadable when the
desk is disabled, when there is no depositor liquidity beyond the treasury
(=NoValue()=), or when spot is off TWAP by more than =defaultMaxDeviationBp=
(500 bp) inside =twapWindow= (600 s) -- the =Slippage()= guard, which trips
on exactly the days an attack is running.  Cold pools bypass the guard
entirely, and K's own present-value read never runs it.

Policy of record: hold the LAST GOOD cap with a staleness flag; position is
always readable (it depends on nothing external); a disabled stabilizer is
excluded from the aggregate.  Never "pinned", never "idle".  Cycle 1's
guard-trip arm confirms the consequence: under the fill-unit aggregation
the guard trip produces no K kick and no rail (V and Vk pass that arm),
while the as-built shadow designs rail through it.

** F9.  Two measurement lessons that changed results, not just plumbing (STRUCTURAL)

1. *Gauge the price from the pool's own quote, never from balances*
   (decision 8, merged).  Reserve-ratio pricing made a concentrated
   liquidity position read as a price move; the artefact's real channel was
   the excursion arbs' ex-ante gate, which is why the fix had to touch every
   quote primitive (13 sites) and not the three named readers.  A seeder-on
   cell's K gap of 0.025 went from artefact to economics.  Lesson: any
   agent's DECISION gate is a gauge, and a gauge that disagrees with the
   venue is a finding waiting to be mis-attributed.
2. *A defended world hides the controller's own law* (sensor damping).  The
   monthly fit of K's law has R2 0.90 in an undefended world and 0.5-0.7
   in a defended one, because the facilities remove the error K is
   integrating.  This is not a defect; it is the mid-ranging structure
   working, and it is why K's inputs must be chosen for what they measure
   about PERSISTENCE rather than for how well they predict the next month.

** 5.2 The seam: persistence, not magnitude

The escalation path is the one the pre-2008 Federal Reserve desk used and
the one =alberta-buck-operations.org= already specifies: repeated
temporary operations in one direction are the signal for an outright
one.  Concretely:

1. Level 1 (the actuators) takes a position to hold price.  Its own local
   skew unwinds its own book; the aggregate is nobody's business but its
   own while it is transient.
2. Level 2 asks one question: *is this flow persistent?*  Today that is a
   dwell test (a standing book for N days); under section 5.4 it is an
   estimator.  When the answer is yes, the position is CONVERTED to a
   permanent supply change -- the desk's Q2 burn of absorbed BUCK, or its
   Q4 acknowledgment of issued BUCK as permanent supply -- a discrete,
   auditable act with a governance trail.
3. Level 3 (K) carries the converted stock, and only that.  The book
   leaves level 1 when it is escalated, so there is no double count and
   nothing for an adversary to inflate: to move K, an attacker must now
   sustain a flow long enough to pass the persistence test, which is the
   cost the ladder and the fee are designed to charge.

Cycle 1 is the measurement that chooses the discrete seam over the
continuous one.  D4's shadow term and D7's position loop are the
continuous coupling; at every gain tested they either do nothing (lambda
below the knee) or rail the lever and pay the attacker (at and above it).

*What remains of the position signal in K.*  Not a proportional gain.  Two
admissible uses, both to be tested rather than assumed:

- as a RATE LIMIT input: the aggregate fill can slow K's motion toward the
  side that would deepen the fast actuators' books (a one-sided
  conditional integration), which cannot be driven anywhere by an
  attacker because it can only ever reduce K's motion;
- as a SATURATION flag: when the actuators are at their bounds, the error
  K sees is the true one, and the case for K moving is stronger.  Measure
  it; do not schedule a gain on it (the gamma knob exists for this and
  stayed at 0 for the same reason).

** 5.4 Where optimal control and estimation belong

The branch's offline derivation (=kdesign.py=) is worth keeping for what
it actually established, which is not a set of gains:

- *It is an identification harness.*  Step tests against a control cell
  that shares the keyed random history, differential fits, the plant's
  own constants.  Every number in F4 came from it, and every number in
  F4 must be re-measured on the merged tree.  Keep and re-run it.
- *It found the structural obstruction* (F3) rather than a tuning.
- *Its answer about the optimal law was feedforward.*  The flow state's
  LQR gain was large (L_d = 210 K per unit of fill per day) while its
  position feedback gains railed the lever.  An optimal controller here
  mostly wants to know the DISTURBANCE, not to react harder to the error.

So the estimator is the piece to build, and its job is the SPLIT:

: F_hat   the fused net BUCK flow at par (BUCK/day, sold positive), from the channels the chain already emits:
:         the facilities' fills, the desk's net inventory, direct-mint net flow, borrower draws and repayments, pool reserves
: F_p     its persistent part      (a slow state)
: F_t     its transient part       (F_hat - F_p)
: K       = K0 + clip(-F_p / g, rails) + Kp e + Ki Int(e)

with g the supply flow per unit K, identified (F4) rather than assumed,
and ONE integrator covering the error in g -- the internal model
principle doing the single job it is needed for.  With the disturbance
modelled as persistent plus transient, the steady-state Kalman filter
reduces to an alpha-beta tracker: two exponential averages and two
constants, which is an on-chain law.  The persistent estimate lags by the
transient's own time constant (days to weeks) instead of the 90-day price
integral -- that is the whole gain, and it is a gain in the SPEED OF THE
DECISION, not in the gain of a loop.

The release rule completes it: K leaves the rail when the supply still in
the pipe (g times the uptake lag times the K excursion) equals the flow
remaining to absorb.  That is the landing, and it is measurable: one sign
change, no limit cycle.

Three limits to state plainly, because they are what makes this different
from a rocket landing:

1. *The plant's gain is uncertain by a factor of 2 and signed.*  Rises
   offer, cuts withdraw the offer (F4); neither calls anything.  A single
   linear g is wrong; the law needs a per-sign g, and the cut side needs
   a rate limit because its immediate effect is a price move that the
   premium-sellers answer at once.
2. *The disturbance can exceed the lever by 5-8x.*  During a large attack
   the feedforward is at the rail and the facilities are doing the work.
   The estimator earns its keep on the RELEASE, deciding how much of the
   excursion was permanent -- which is precisely the question level 2
   asks.
3. *The estimator's inputs are adversary-visible.*  Every one of them is a
   number an attacker can push.  The persistence test and the rate limit
   are what make that unprofitable; a proportional gain on the same input
   is what made it profitable in F2.  This constraint is not a detail of
   implementation: it is the reason the seam is discrete.

What estimation is NOT for here: inventory (measured exactly on chain --
no filter needed), and price (deterministic once quoted from the pool's
own state, F9).  And no quadratic program on chain: for a box-constrained
scalar plant, explicit MPC is piecewise affine, which is a linear law plus
rail logic -- what a cascade with anti-windup already is.

** 6.2 The one design consequence: D8 is answerable now

=CARRY-CONVEXITY.org= 9.1 records the facility's worst risk as D8: because
credit creation was permissionless and zero-premium credit is exempt from
the funding gate, a participant with a fictitious insured asset could
issue free BUCK into every premium with a retirement obligation that never
bites -- the oversupply flood, automated.  The remedy it names ("restrict
the facility to vetted insurers' credit, or to a whitelisted set in sim
and pilot") was a placeholder for a mainnet blocker.

Master now provides the surface: an insurer must hold an attested
envelope, proven against a regulator's accumulator, that covers the face
band, the depreciation model and rate, the premium rate and the declared
scope of what it is insuring, with an expiry; a false scope claim is
evidence.  And a client must have opted in to that insurer.  The gate is
per-deployment (unset registry = no envelope required), so the
simulation can run either side of it deliberately.

That turns 9.1 from a caveat into an experiment: run the facility's flood
cell with the insurer registry BOUND, and measure the flood as a function
of attested capacity rather than of an assumed whitelist.  It is the one
place where master's security work makes a wave-3 question cheaper to
answer than it was.

*Ruled 2026-09-26 (design owner):* the flood question is STRUCTURAL, so
the phase rule admits it now; and the flood is bounded -- by the rising
cost of insurance funding as bvib inflates: the cheaper BUCK gets, the
harder it is to accumulate the capital required to issue insurance.

Checked against the contracts as merged, that bound binds an HONEST
insurer and not yet a fictitious one:

1. The funding factor, max(0, 1 + 10 (basket - BUCK) / basket), does
   rise as BUCK cheapens -- but it gates the MINTER's pre-mint reserve,
   scaled to the mint's insurance principal, not the insurer's capital,
   and =Buck.mint= skips it when =poolPrincipal == 0= ("zero-cost
   insurance => exempt").  D8's flood is exactly the zero-premium mint.
2. The envelope bounds each POLICY (face band, depreciation model and
   rate, maximum premium rate, scope, expiry).  It has no premium FLOOR
   and no aggregate exposure, so an attested insurer can write
   zero-premium policies without limit on their number.
3. The insurer's capital -- the solvency buffer =CREDIT-IMPROVEMENT.org=
   D8 names as the intended shape -- is the regulator's off-chain
   judgment at attestation; nothing on chain holds or measures it.

The owner's mechanism is the right bound for an insurer whose capital
accumulates from premiums paid in BUCK: in real terms that capital grows
more slowly the more BUCK inflates, a counter-cyclical brake on credit
creation.  It reaches the fictitious insurer given either of two
contract changes -- a premium floor in the envelope (every attested
credit then has =poolPrincipal > 0= and the funding factor applies), or
an attested aggregate capacity per insurer tied to its capital (issuance
bounded by capital, capital by premiums).  So the cell has three sides:

| side | registry | what bounds the flood                                                   | what it measures                                   |
|------+----------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------|
| a    | unbound  | nothing but theta x apr (today's mainnet blocker)                       | the baseline flood                                  |
| b    | bound    | attestation only; zero premium admitted                                 | how much vetting alone buys                         |
| c    | bound    | a premium floor, and a sim-side capacity per insurer that grows with the premiums it collects | the owner's mechanism: flood size and its equilibrium discount against bvib |

Side c is the test of the claim: does the flood stop short of theta x
apr because insurance capital cannot keep pace with an inflating BUCK?
Its answer selects between the two contract changes (or both).

   discount or premium, the funding factor) over a fixed menu (hold,
   deposit, pledge, retire external debt, retire BUCK credit, spend).

   facilities and the ladder.
