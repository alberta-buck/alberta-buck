* 1. The proposal, and the rulings of record

The design owner's proposal (2026-09-26): the monetary operations issue
and absorb BUCK at a premium or discount, but none of them arbitrages --
the system depends on arbitrageurs who know nothing of BUCK to keep its
venues consistent.  Why should the BuckBasket not watch the BUCK/TOKEN,
USDC/TOKEN and BUCK/USDC prices it already depends on, and close the
gaps itself?  With a WORK WHEEL -- a bounded state machine that any
caller advances, many calls per block amortizing the monitoring, calls
after the block's work is done nearly free -- and an Alberta Buck agent
that simply keeps calling the basket's tick.

Rulings, the same day:

1. *Every chain.*  The basket will eventually be deployed on L1 and the
   L2s alike; the gas price on each chain sets the action margin.
2. *No USDC position.*  Place only the USDC exposure a cycle needs to
   close a gap; never BUILD a USDC position.
3. *Consistency cycles only.*  The wheel absorbs arbitrage between
   venues.  It does not trade BUCK against its basket value (bvib) --
   that leg changes the supply and belongs to the undertakings and the
   desk under their own rules.
4. *Investigate* whether the arbitrage profit can pay for the gas, or
   more, to make calling the tick worth someone's while (section 4).

* 2. What the basket is today

- *It is the TOKEN/BUCK liquidity.*  The venue facet
  (=src/basket/BuckBasketUniswapV3.sol=) owns full-range positions in
  every TOKEN/BUCK pool; a deposit is an LP add at spot with minted
  partner BUCK.  So every move of a TOKEN's USD price opens a gap on the
  BASKET'S OWN position, and whoever closes it takes the basket's
  loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR; Milionis, Moallemi, Roughgarden and
  Zhang, 2022) -- today, the sim's =AnonymousArbAgent= cycles
  (USDC -> TOKEN -> BUCK -> USDC, and the reverse) and the excursion
  arbs.  Pool fees are the basket's only compensation.
- *It reads only its own pools* (slot0 or a TWAP, behind the
  spot-vs-TWAP =Slippage()= guard).  It never reads a USDC price.
- *The work-wheel pattern exists, at epoch cadence.*
  =RebalanceDirectorBase.poke(maxWork)= is a permissionless epoch wheel
  the shell advances with =poke(1)= on every deposit and redeem;
  =BuckKController*.compute()= runs a PID cycle when its interval has
  elapsed and is called from every mint, burn, deposit and redeem.
  Nothing in =src/= keys work to =block.number= yet.

* 3. What a consistency cycle is, and why it is safe

A triangle through TOKEN_i: USDC/TOKEN_i, TOKEN_i/BUCK (the basket's
pool) and BUCK/USDC.  When the three prices disagree by more than the
cycle's cost, going round the triangle in the profitable direction
closes the gap and ends with more of the asset it started with.

- *No capital, no inventory.*  The opening leg can be a FLASH MINT of
  BUCK by the basket (MakerDAO's flash-mint pattern): mint, go round,
  burn the principal, keep the profit.  USDC exists only between two
  swaps inside one transaction -- ruling 2 holds by construction.
- *No supply change.*  The flash-minted BUCK is burned in the same
  transaction; the profit is existing BUCK (or TOKEN) moved from the
  venues to the basket.  Not a monetary operation (ruling 3).
- *Manipulation-safe.*  The cycle checks REALIZED output against input
  and reverts below a minimum profit.  An attacker who moves a pool
  first only pays the basket to move it back.  Only non-cycle trades on
  spot prices are manipulable, and the wheel makes none.
- *It moves bvib, correctly.*  =basketValueInBuck= is a TWAP of the
  TOKEN/BUCK pools.  Closing their gaps to the USD venues makes bvib
  read the external valuation instead of whatever the last trade left
  -- the same effect the external arbs have today, without their lag.

* 4. Who can take which gap: the fee-recycling band

A searcher going round the triangle pays three pool fees.  The basket
pays two: on its own TOKEN/BUCK pool it is (nearly) the only LP, so the
fee it pays comes back to it.  With the sim's tiers (TOKEN/USDC 0.30%,
TOKEN/BUCK 0.30%, BUCK/USDC 0.05%):

| gap                                    | who takes it                                                    |
|----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| below 0.35% + gas / size                | nobody: the cycle costs more than it earns                      |
| 0.35% .. 0.65% + gas / size             | ONLY the basket: its fee on its own pool comes back to it       |
| above 0.65% + gas / size + a margin     | searchers, directly, with flash swaps; they pay the basket's fee |
| any gap the basket's own trade opens    | the basket, in the same transaction (no race)                   |

That band is the wheel's natural domain, and it is why the tick needs a
bounty: a gap in it is worth nothing to a searcher directly, so the
only way anyone monetizes it is by calling the basket's tick.  Above
it, a searcher always prefers the whole gap to any share of it; on
today's Uniswap v3 pools the basket cannot stop them, and keeps only
its fees.  Capturing the large external gaps too needs control of the
swap path -- a Uniswap v4 hook that runs the wheel before the first
swap of each block, or an auction for the right to go first (the
auction-managed AMM, Adams, Moallemi, Reynolds and Robinson, 2024; CoW
AMM's batch clearing is a cousin).  Recorded here as the upgrade path,
not the first version.

*What the sim shows today* (the banked cycle-1 none arm, design A,
days 30-730; =../alberta-buck-grid/build/sim/star/d7-c1/none-design-A.json=):
the residual triangle gap at the day's end has a median of 117-126 bp
on every pool and a 90th percentile of 196-257 bp.  That is the cast's
threshold, not the market's: =AnonymousArbAgent= wants a 60 bp net edge
on top of ~65 bp of fees (=MARGIN_BP=, "so arb profit is visible, not
competed to ~0").  Real searchers compete that margin toward gas, so
the sim's cast leaves the band -- and more -- open.  A wheel prototype
measured against this cast would overstate its take; the prototype
must also be measured with the arb margin at its competitive level.

* 5. Paying the caller: the incentive

The design question is how the captured profit pays for the call, on
every chain, without the basket needing to know the gas price.

- *No cure, no pay.*  The tick pays only when a cycle executes.  Callers
  simulate first (=eth_call=) and send only when a cycle is there, so
  the basket never pays for an idle tick; an idle call is the caller's
  loss.
- *A proportional bounty: a share s of the realized profit*, paid in the
  cycle's proceeds.  A caller calls when s x profit exceeds its own gas
  cost, so the action margin sets ITSELF per chain -- on L1 the wheel
  acts on large gaps, on an L2 on small ones -- with no gas-price
  oracle and no ETH price inside the basket (ruling 1).  s trades the
  speed of consistency (a lower threshold) against the profit kept.
- *If a floor is wanted, reimburse at the base fee.*  A fixed part equal
  to gas used x =block.basefee= (EIP-1559, set by the protocol, unlike
  =tx.gasprice=, which the caller chooses and a builder-searcher can
  inflate) plus the share.  This is MakerDAO's Liquidations 2.0 keeper
  pay in shape -- a flat =tip= plus a proportional =chip= -- and the
  flat part needs the gas token's price in BUCK, which the share alone
  does not.
- *Competition is fine.*  Callers racing for the bounty bid priority
  fees up to (bounty - gas); that transfer is the builder's, out of the
  bounty.  The basket's cost is fixed at s x profit.
- *Piggybacking is what makes ticks nearly free.*  Inside another
  transaction a tick whose block's work is done costs a storage read
  and a return -- a few thousand gas.  A standalone tick still pays the
  21,000-gas transaction base.  So the Alberta Buck agent should fold
  ticks into transactions it sends anyway, and the shell's own
  deposit / redeem / rebalance calls should advance the wheel (as they
  already =poke= the director).  The wheel re-arms within a block when a
  basket-touching trade lands, so a gap opened after the wheel ran is
  not skipped until the next block.

*Is the pie big enough?*  LVR on a constant-product pool accrues at
about V sigma^2 / 8 per unit time.  On the sim's ~$31.6M of basket-owned
TOKEN/BUCK liquidity with its implied TOKEN/BUCK volatility of 50-67%/yr
that is ~$3.3k/day (~$1.2M/yr); the sim's volatility is inflated by its
own noise, and at a more ordinary 20% the same pools leak ~$400/day.  A
three-swap cycle is ~350-450k gas: on L1 at 2-20 gwei and ETH at $3,000,
~$2-27 a cycle; on the current rollups, cents.  (Ballparks, to be
refreshed when a deployment is costed.)  So on an L2 the band pays for
many ticks a day; on L1 it pays for the larger gaps only -- exactly
what the proportional bounty does without being told.  Whether the
share also leaves a surplus to fund other keeper work (the director's
epochs, the ops quadrants, the controller's compute) is the question
the prototype answers: the profit is the basket's, so it can pay for
the basket's upkeep.

* 6. USDC: the minimum, never a position (ruling 2)

The flash-minted cycle holds no USDC at rest.  A basket-owned BUCK/USDC
range would save the cycle one fee leg -- 0.05% at the sim's tier -- at
the price of a standing USDC position with its own LVR.  Not worth it
while the BUCK/USDC pool's depth carries the cycles; the prototype
records when depth binds (a cycle cut short by slippage), and only then
is a minimal, transient range the question.

* 7. The prototype, and what it must measure

WAVE3's pattern: a Python agent over the real contracts first, Solidity
last.  =BasketWheelAgent= acts for the basket through SimLP authority
(as the undertakings did in WP-2), once per tick:

1. For each TOKEN_i, the triangle's gap from the three pools' slot0
   (=gauge.py=, decision 8 -- never balances).
2. If the gap exceeds the basket's cost (its two external fee legs) plus
   a per-chain gas cost (a scenario knob: L1 / L2 cost per cycle), go
   round it: flash-mint BUCK (a sim stand-in: the SimLP's BUCK float,
   restored in the same act), size to close the gap to the basket's
   threshold, check realized profit, and book it to the basket.
3. Pay the caller share s (booked, not paid to an actor, in the first
   version).

Measured on the catalogue's none arm and the dump arms, with the
external arb cast (a) as today, (b) at a competitive margin, (c) halved:

- the profit captured, per pool and per day, against the V sigma^2 / 8
  estimate and against what =AnonymousArbAgent= earned in the control;
- the residual-gap distribution (it should collapse toward the basket's
  threshold);
- the within-day reversion of bvib with the external arbs thinned -- the
  thin cast's second assumption (their unlimited capital) replaced by a
  protocol property;
- the ticks a day each gas level buys, and the surplus after the share.

The phase rule: consistency cycles change no supply and are not a
monetary operation (ruling 3), so the prototype can be built beside the
stand; its quantitative verdict, like everything else, is read on the
stand's rungs.

* 8. The chassis: activities as task kinds

The design owner's question: can the work wheel be abstracted, so a
basket is derived from the basic BuckBasket and the arbitrage -- or any
other activity -- is added in a modular fashion?  Yes, and the codebase
already holds the pattern: =RebalanceDirectorBase= is a policy-agnostic
chassis (epoch clock, round-robin cursor, =poke(maxWork)=) whose policy
arrives through two hooks, =_isStale= / =_pokeOne=.  The wheel is that
chassis generalized from "constituents x epochs" to "tasks x clocks".

** 8.1 The pieces

| piece           | what it owns                                                                                  |
|-----------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| the chassis     | the slot table over every kind, the cursor, the per-block scan and idle memo, the re-arm, the caller's pay |
| a task kind     | how many slots it has, whether a slot is due (a view), how to run one (bounded), what it was worth |
| the reserve     | the gas offset: a slice of basket yield, paid as kappa of its balance per working tick          |
| the clock       | a per-chain adapter: =block.number= on L1 and the OP stack; =ArbSys(100).arbBlockNumber()= on Arbitrum, whose =block.number= is the L1's and would idle the wheel for many of its own blocks |
| the capabilities | what a kind may ask of the basket when the wheel is not inside it: a flash mint for the arb kind |

The kinds the codebase already has, as tasks: the director's signal
refresh (=poke=), the treasury sweep (=sweepTreasury=, which no sim
agent calls today), the ops quadrants (=monetaryOperation=), the Fence
re-strike (=fenceRebalance=), the controller's =compute=.  The new one:
the consistency arbitrage, one slot per constituent.

** 8.2 Scheduling: amortizing the scan, not just the work

On chain the expensive part of an arb slot is deciding whether it is due
-- three cold =slot0= reads, ~14k gas -- so an idle scan of six
constituents is ~85k: too much for the shell's piggybacked =tick(1)=.
So a call examines at most =maxScan= slots, the cursor moves past every
slot examined (not only the ones run), and the block is memoized idle
only when consecutive calls have found every slot not due.  Many small
calls in a block share one scan; after it, each further call is one
read.  Any basket-touching trade re-arms the wheel.

** 8.3 The incentive, in the chassis

A working tick pays its caller two things: each value-producing kind's
share of what it captured (the arbitrage pays for its own calls), and
kappa of the reserve (upkeep that captures nothing).  The reserve is the
design owner's gas offset: a small slice of the basket's yield (and a
fraction of the arb's value) flows in; each working tick takes kappa of
the balance; so it BUILDS UP when ticks are under-called and PAYS OUT
LESS when they are over-called.  At equilibrium a paid tick earns about
funding / calls, and callers call until that equals their gas -- the
calling rate sets itself per chain, with no gas oracle, like the arb's
share.  An idle tick is paid nothing; a cap on the reserve returns the
excess to depositors.  (An elapsed-time drip -- pay rho x the time since
the last paid tick -- has the same equilibrium and pays only the first
caller in a block; kappa-of-balance is the owner's form, and needs no
clock.)

** 8.4 Solidity: kinds as mixins over slot ranges

#+BEGIN_SRC solidity
abstract contract WorkWheel {
    uint256 public cursor;
    uint256 public idleBlock;          // the clock value of a block found idle
    uint256 internal _scanned;         // consecutive slots found not due
    uint256 internal _scanBlock;

    function tick(uint256 maxWork, uint256 maxScan) external returns (uint256 work, uint256 pay);
    function _clock() internal view virtual returns (uint256) { return block.number; }
    function _markDirty() internal { idleBlock = 0; _scanned = 0; }

    // the kind seam: the slot table is the concatenation of the kinds' ranges
    function _slotCount() internal view virtual returns (uint256) { return 0; }
    function _due(uint256) internal view virtual returns (bool) { return false; }
    function _run(uint256) internal virtual returns (uint256 work, uint256 value, uint256 share);
}

/// A kind claims the range after its bases' and hands the rest to super:
/// the C3 order of the derived contract fixes the slot layout.
abstract contract ArbKind is WorkWheel {
    function _slotCount() internal view virtual override returns (uint256) {
        return super._slotCount() + _constituents();
    }
    function _due(uint256 s) internal view virtual override returns (bool) {
        uint256 b = super._slotCount();
        return s < b ? super._due(s) : _arbDue(s - b);
    }
    function _run(uint256 s) internal virtual override returns (uint256, uint256, uint256) {
        uint256 b = super._slotCount();
        return s < b ? super._run(s) : _arbRun(s - b);
    }
}

contract ProRataWheel is WorkWheel, DirectorKind, SweepKind, ArbKind { }
#+END_SRC

Two deployments from one chassis, which is the "derive from the basic

- *Inside the shell* where it fits -- =BuckBasketProRata= (~16.2 KB, ~8.4 KB

- *Beside the shell* where it does not -- the ops shell (~3.2 KB) and the

** 8.6 The Solidity v1 (2026-09-27)

=src/wheel/WorkWheel.sol= (the chassis), =UpkeepKinds.sol= (=ComputeKind=,
=DirectorKind=, =SweepKind=, =OpsKind=), =ArbKind.sol= (=CycleMath= and the
arbitrage), =BasketWheel.sol= (the concrete wheel beside the shell; a kind
whose target is unset has no slots, so one contract serves the savings
basket and the monetary one by configuration).  Tests:
=test/wheel/WorkWheel.t.sol= (8: layout, round-robin, the amortized scan and
its per-block memo, re-arm, the reserve's build-up and draw-down, the cap)
and =test/wheel/BasketWheelArb.t.sol= (8, on real V3 pools: nothing to do
when consistent; a gap closed in either direction and credited to the
depositors; a capped cycle closing the gap over blocks; the BUCK start
funding the reserve then the treasury; the credit entry points and the
callbacks closed to everyone else).

What building it settled:

1. *No flash mint is needed -- for either start.*  The design owner asked
   how the basket issues flash liquidity for its closed paths.  V3 pays a
   swap's output before it collects the input: the first leg funds the
   cycle, the other two run inside its callback, and what they return
   pays the first.  So a cycle needs no inventory from ANY start token,
   and =Buck.sol= is untouched.  The basket's mint authority is what the
   NAV leg uses (issuing at a premium, absorbing at a discount) -- the
   undertakings' leg, now in the demonstration.
2. *The start token decides where the profit lands, not how much it is.*
   The same three swaps in the same rotation earn the same.  TOKEN start
   (=startMode= 0): the profit is the constituent, credited to the
   DEPOSITORS.  BUCK start (=startMode= 1): the profit is BUCK -- the
   reserve's own asset -- so it funds the callers' gas offset up to the
   reserve's cap and the rest goes to the treasury: the wheel pays for its
   own calls without touching the basket's yield.  Both are measured.
3. *The harvest reaches the receipts through the stress fee's door.*
   ProRata's doctrine of record pays depositors in TOKEN only and keeps
   BUCK profit in the treasury.  The TOKEN a cycle captures is re-LP'd as
   DEPOSITOR liquidity with minted partner BUCK booked in
   =stressBonusPrincipal= (=creditDepositors=), exactly the stress fee's
   mechanics, so =totalOutstandingBuck == sum buckPrincipal +
   stressBonusPrincipal= holds and every receipt's claim grows pro rata.
4. *Hosted by the venue facet, for the Fence's sake.*  =setWheel=,
   =creditDepositors= and =creditTreasury= in ProRata would have left the
   Fence (which inherits it) 36 bytes under EIP-170.  The shell's
   fallback already delegates every unknown selector to the venue facet
   with =msg.sender= preserved, so they live there.  Sizes after:
   ProRata 16,235 B, Ops 21,435 B (3,141 to spare), Fence 23,612 B (964),
   the venue facet 19,039 B, =BasketWheel= 12,335 B.  The shells pay only
   the public =wheel= getter (56 B).
5. *BUCK/USDC liquidity is not the basket's.*  The owner asked whether
   BUCK/USDC liquidity must be architected into the basket.  No: the
   cycles route through whatever BUCK/USDC pool exists (the market's, or
   the treasury seeder's temporary seeding, D5); owning a slice would
   save its fee leg (0.05%) at the price of the standing USDC position
   ruling 2 forbids.  What must be architected is its DEPTH: a thin
   BUCK/USDC pool caps every cycle, so the demonstration seeds it and the
   measurements record when it binds.
6. *Gas, measured:* a working arbitrage tick is ~630-780k gas (the
   depositor credit's V3 mint is a third of it), an idle memoized tick a
   few thousand.  The L1 margin is correspondingly higher than 7.1's
   estimate assumed.
7. *v1 takes DIRECT profit only* (gaps beyond all three fees).  The band
   only the basket can take needs a TOKEN float or the wheel inside the
   shell; it is v2.  The cycle cap is =capBp= of the FIRST leg's input
   reserve: against a small own pool a large gap closes over several
   blocks, one bounded cycle per scan.
