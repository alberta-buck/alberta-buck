#+BEGIN_ABSTRACT
Public money is going programmable, and programmability invites
surveillance: every proposed central-bank digital currency couples the
convenience of digital cash to a ledger its issuer can read, freeze, and
mine.  Physical cash resists this but offers the opposite extreme -- no
accountability at all, no issuance trail, no recourse.  This paper
presents BUCK Notes, a bearer-instrument layer for an identity-aware
stablecoin that occupies neither pole.  /Bulk/ traceability is defeated
-- no analytics firm or omnibus subpoena can reconstruct who paid whom --
while /bilateral/ disclosure is preserved: the two parties to any payment
always retain the cryptographic means to prove, under compulsion and with
*no trusted third party*, exactly who the counterparty was.  The
load-bearing property is the *non-deniable-receipt invariant*: there is
no path, even for a /colluding/ payer and payee, to construct a payment
for which no court-compellable receipt naming the KYC-bound counterparty
exists.  We formalise the invariant, give a construction meeting it for
public-issuer notes (shipped and evaluated on Ethereum), prove its
security, and characterise precisely the boundary at which the guarantee
stops being free: when /both/ parties are anonymous at their respective
binding moments, a collusion-resistant receipt provably requires
in-circuit set-membership over the identity registry.  We give the
negative result that the obvious cheaper fix is vacuous, and an
incentive-compatible mechanism that bounds the residual risk to the
always-identifiable recipient.
#+END_ABSTRACT

  An /Identity/ is a KYC-certified point \( M \in \mathbb{G}_1 \) bound to
  a registered account by an issuer's Pointcheval-Sanders credential and
  stored ElGamal-encrypted as \( E_{\text{addr}}[X] = (R_X, C_X) = (r_X G,
  M_X + r_X\, pk_X) \) with \( pk_X = sk_X G \).  A /payment/ moves value
  \( v \) from payer \( P \) to recipient \( D \).

** Adversaries

   #+begin_definition
   Three properties, held against adversaries of increasing power:
   - *(Bulk-tracing resistance.)*  /Mallory/, a passive observer with full
     chain history and unbounded compute but no party's secrets, cannot
     reconstruct who paid whom, how much, or link a mint to its spend(s).
   - *(Third-party non-disclosure.)*  Nothing on chain reveals either
     party's Identity point \( M \) to a non-participant, even in
     aggregate across many payments -- /including to the issuer/ (below).
   - *(Mutual decryptability / accountability.)*  Each of the two
     participants, from data they hold plus public chain state, can
     produce a sound receipt naming the other's registered \( M \); and a
     /colluding/ pair cannot arrange a payment for which no such receipt
     exists.
   The first two properties must hold against the strongest realistic
   deanonymiser, the *Issuer*: the KYC trust anchor that holds, for every
   client it certified, the plaintext identity \( (\mathrm{id}_i, m_i, M_i) \)
   and the credential \( \sigma_i \) it issued -- strictly more than Mallory
   knows.  We treat a /compelled or wholly breached/ issuer in
   Sec. [[#sec:issuer-blind]].
   #+end_definition

** The non-deniable-receipt invariant

   #+begin_definition
   <<def:ndr>>
   /(Non-deniable receipt.)/  A protocol satisfies the *non-deniable-receipt
   invariant* if for every accepted payment there exist a receipt
   \( \mathcal{R} \) and an efficient public predicate \( \mathsf{RcptVerify} \)
   such that:
   - *(Completeness.)*  The recipient \( D \) can compute \( \mathcal{R} \)
     from data they hold plus public chain state, and
     \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}) = 1 \) names a registered Identity
     \( M_P \) and value \( v \).
   - *(Soundness / non-frameability.)*  No PPT adversary produces
     \( \mathcal{R}' \) with \( \mathsf{RcptVerify}(\mathcal{R}') = 1 \)
     naming an Identity that did not author the payment.
   - *(No deniability.)*  No PPT payer can cause an accepted payment to
     \( D \) for which /no/ accepting receipt naming the payer exists --
     even in collusion with \( D \).
   #+end_definition

   The third clause is the operative one and the departure from prior art:
   it is the collusion-resistant form.  Completeness alone protects a
   self-interested recipient; the invariant additionally forbids a
   /willing/ pair from constructing an untraceable payment.

** Issuer-blindness: accountability without a bulk opener
   :PROPERTIES:
   :CUSTOM_ID: sec:issuer-blind
   :END:

   The KYC issuer is the system's only trust anchor, and the natural
   objection to any /accountable privacy/ claim is that the issuer is a
   bulk-deanonymisation backdoor.  It is not.  Issuance is /address-blind/:
   the issuer signs the identity scalar \( m = H(\mathrm{id}) \) once (a
   Pointcheval-Sanders credential over \( m \) alone) and is thereafter
   done; the client derives each on-chain account /offline/ -- a fresh key
   pair \( (sk, pk) \), a self-formed ElGamal credential
   \( E_{\text{addr}} = (rG,\ M + r\,pk) \), and a /rerandomised/ credential
   \( \sigma' \) -- without the issuer ever seeing \( pk \).  The issuer's
   view is therefore, per certified client \( i \), the tuple
   \( (\mathrm{id}_i, m_i, M_i, \sigma_i) \) -- the plaintext identity --
   plus public chain state; it holds /no/ chain addresses.

   #+begin_theorem
   <<thm:issuer-blind>>
   /(Issuer-blindness.)/  Under statistical unlinkability of PS
   rerandomisation (Thm. 2 of the companion /Proofs/) and IND-CPA security
   of ElGamal on \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) (equivalently DDH), an issuer -- even
   one compelled or wholly breached, in possession of
   \( \{(\mathrm{id}_i, m_i, M_i, \sigma_i)\}_i \) and the entire chain --
   has negligible advantage in matching any issued identity \( M_i \) to
   its on-chain registration \( (pk_j, E_j, \sigma'_j) \).  Compulsion or
   breach yields the /set/ of certified identities but no /map/ to chain
   accounts; deanonymisation remains per-account, requiring that account's
   \( sk \) or a counterparty receipt.
   #+end_theorem

   #+begin_proof
   Two locks.  /Credential lock./  \( \sigma'_j \) is a rerandomisation of
   some issued \( \sigma_i \); by statistical unlinkability the two carry
   zero mutual information, so even an unbounded issuer cannot recognise its
   own signature.  /Plaintext lock./  The issuer /knows/ \( M_i \), so its
   task is not decryption but /plaintext-checking/: does \( E_j = (R, C) \)
   encrypt \( M_i \)?  That is deciding whether \( (G, pk_j, R, C - M_i) \)
   is a DDH tuple, and an algorithm answering it for a chosen \( M_i \)
   breaks IND-CPA (submit \( m_0 = M_i \), \( m_1 \) random).  Knowing the
   plaintext thus confers no advantage.  With both locks shut and no stored
   \( pk \), the issuer is reduced to an independent DDH decision per
   (identity, account) pair.
   #+end_proof

   This is the load-bearing form of /no trusted opener/: the closest thing
   to an authority cannot open transactions in bulk, even against itself --
   under compulsion it can only /verify/ a link some party surrenders (map
   a receipt-disclosed \( M \) to the real person), never /produce/ one.
   Two caveats bound the theorem, and we state rather than bury them:

   - *Computational, not information-theoretic.*  The plaintext lock is
     DDH; only the credential lock is statistical.  A discrete-log break --
     in particular a quantum computer running Shor against
     \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) -- turns the harvested \( \{M_i\} \) plus the
     archived chain into a wholesale, retroactive deanonymisation of all
     /past/ identity and A2-Note traffic.  The breached or compelled
     issuer's KYC records are precisely that harvest corpus (companion
     /Proofs/, Implementation Notes: /Harvest-Now, Decrypt-Later/).
   - *Retroactive, not prospective.*  Address-blind issuance protects
     /past/ registrations unconditionally against /future/ issuer
     compromise; but a coerced issuer can change the protocol going forward
     (demand \( pk \) at KYC, record addresses, backdoor credentials).
     Issuer-blindness is a statement about issuances already performed
     under the honest protocol.

   Placement note: this is an identity-LAYER guarantee (substrate), stated
   here because it is what makes "no trusted opener" true and because it is
   the strongest privacy result against the most powerful realistic

    A bearer note's redeemer is unknown at mint -- it is whoever comes to
    hold rho, and rho circulates publicly among holders.  For the redeemer
    to name the issuer, the note must carry an issuer-identity binding the
    redeemer can open.  The issuer creates it when the redeemer is unknown,
    so it is openable under a key the eventual redeemer will hold.  Two

    cannot target a key it has never seen; (2) a note-bound key derived
    from rho -- openable by EVERY holder, so the issuer is revealed to the
    whole bearer chain (no privacy).  A binding openable by no one fails
    decryptability.  Hence a hidden issuer requires a DESIGNATED recipient
    -- an addressed (A) note.  Bearer and private-issuer are mutually
    exclusive.  []
  Note the contrast with A2 (reveals the issuer to exactly one chosen
  party).  This theorem fixes the realisable set to {A1, A2, B1}.

  /(No deniability under collusion -- public issuer.)/  No payer/recipient
  coalition can produce an accepted public-issuer payment with no accepting
  receipt: =mint= rejects any leaf lacking a valid issuer binding, so every
  spendable leaf carries one, recoverable by the holder of the opening.
  #+end_theorem

  #+begin_corollary
  /(Mutual decryptability, public issuer.)/  Combining the issuer half
  (Schnorr binding at mint) with the depositor half
  (=verifyDepositorForIssuer= at deposit), both parties recover the other's
  registered Identity and each can prove it under compulsion -- with no
  third party.
  #+end_corollary

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Cost of Anonymity: the Private-Issuer Boundary (A2)
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: sec:cost
  :END:

  The public-issuer result is free of any SNARK over identities because, at
  the binding instant, one party is /named/.  We now show that removing the
  last named anchor -- making /both/ parties anonymous at their respective
  binding moments (the A2 setting: issuer named only at mint, recipient
  named only at spend, mint and spend unlinkable) -- is exactly what makes
  collusion-resistance expensive, and we locate the boundary precisely.

** The EOA-transfer mirror: the cost is the anonymity

   #+begin_comment
   EXTRACT -- D:1131-1213.  The mapping table D:1176-1186 is the thesis in
   one line: A2 mint `issuer_reenc` IS `verifyApprove` with the recipient's
   key BLINDED; that one change is the entire gap.  A direct EOA transfer
   is collusion-resistant FOR FREE because mandatory CP-bound approve
   (I:1571-1681) reads BOTH parties' registered records at one instant.
   A2 blinds pk_rec for privacy -> drops "under the recipient's REGISTERED
   key".  Structural diagnosis D:1188-1204: the two anchors are never
   co-present on chain.
   #+end_comment

   A direct private-to-private transfer achieves mutual decryptability with
   no SNARK and free collusion-resistance, because the mandatory
   Chaum-Pedersen-bound =approve= reads /both/ parties' registered records
   in a single call -- both anchors co-present, both named.  A2 splits the
   anchors in time by anonymity: the issuer's anchor exists only at mint,
   the recipient's only at spend.  No on-chain check reads both at once, so
   the A2 binding /blinds/ the recipient's key to bridge them -- and
   blinding it is precisely what drops the "under the recipient's
   /registered/ key" guarantee.

** The obvious fix is vacuous   *** NEW (rigorous, ready to formalise) ***

   #+begin_comment
   This is the paper's strongest single technical result and it is FULLY
   WORKED at D:973-995.  State as a Lemma with the explicit witness.  It is
   a clean impossibility-of-the-obvious-fix: constraining only that eIss
   and E_note share a key is absorbed by the free recipient witness.
   #+end_comment

   #+begin_lemma
   <<lem:vacuous>>
   /(Vacuity of key-equality coupling.)/  Let an A2 mint constrain only
   that the issuer payload \( eIss \) and the note ciphertext
   \( E_{\text{note}} \) are encrypted under the /same/ key \( pk_{\text{rec}} \),
   with the recipient's identity point \( M_{\text{rec}} \) a free witness.
   Then for any issuer-chosen \( pk_{\text{bogus}} \) the constraint is
   satisfiable while the note remains spendable by the intended recipient
   \( D \) and \( eIss \) is encrypted under \( pk_{\text{bogus}} \neq pk_D \).
   #+end_lemma

   #+begin_proof
   Set \( pk_{\text{rec}} := pk_{\text{bogus}} \) and \( M_{\text{rec}} :=
   M_D + r_n(pk_D - pk_{\text{bogus}}) \).  Then \( E_{\text{note}} = (r_n
   G,\ M_{\text{rec}} + r_n\, pk_{\text{rec}}) = (r_n G,\ M_D + r_n\, pk_D)
   \), a valid encryption of \( M_D \) under \( D \)'s registered key, so
   =verifySpendCP= accepts and \( D \) spends; while \( eIss = (r'G,\
   M_{\text{iss}} + r'\, pk_{\text{bogus}}) \) is under \( pk_{\text{bogus}}
   \), so a compelled \( sk_D \) decrypts \( eIss \) to \( M_{\text{iss}} +
   r'(pk_{\text{bogus}} - pk_D) \neq M_{\text{iss}} \).  The free witness
   \( M_{\text{rec}} \) absorbs the constraint.
   #+end_proof

** Closing the clause: the coupled construction
   :PROPERTIES:
   :CUSTOM_ID: sec:coupled
   :END:

   Lemma [[lem:vacuous]] shows that binding \( eIss \) to /a/ key is
   worthless; the binding must be to the recipient's /registered/ key, and
   the issuer -- who does not hold \( sk_D \) -- can only assert that key
   /exists in the registry/.  This yields the coupled mint circuit
   \( \mathcal{C}^\star \).  Write the registry as an accumulator with
   public root \( \mathsf{rt} \) over leaves \( \ell_X = \mathrm{Poseidon}(
   \mathrm{addr}_X, pk_X, E_{\text{addr}}[X]) \); the issuer is
   \( I = \texttt{msg.sender} \) with public \( E_{\text{addr}}[I] = (R_I, C_I) \);
   the note ciphertext is \( E_{\text{note}} = (R_n, C_n) \) and the issuer
   payload \( eIss = (R_e, C_e) \) is a public output (the leaf-tie of
   Sec. [[#sec:construction]]).  Per A2 leaf, with private witnesses
   \( (sk_I, M_{\text{iss}}, pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D]=(R_D,C_D), \pi_D, r', r_n) \),
   \( \mathcal{C}^\star \) constrains:

   #+begin_export latex
   \begin{align}
   \textbf{(C1, issuer opening)}\quad & pk_I = sk_I G,\qquad M_{\text{iss}} = C_I - sk_I R_I; \notag\\
   \textbf{(C2, membership)}\quad & \mathsf{MerkleVerify}\bigl(\mathsf{rt},\,\pi_D,\,\mathrm{Poseidon}(\cdot, pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D])\bigr) = 1; \notag\\
   \textbf{(C3, re-randomisation)}\quad & R_n = R_D + r_n G,\qquad C_n = C_D + r_n\, pk_D; \notag\\
   \textbf{(C4, payload coupling)}\quad & R_e = r' G,\qquad C_e = M_{\text{iss}} + r'\, pk_D. \notag
   \end{align}
   #+end_export

   (C1) ties \( eIss \) to the issuer's /registered/ identity (the public
   \( E_{\text{addr}}[I] \)); (C2) certifies the recipient is a /real/
   registered account without revealing which (leaf private, root public);
   (C3) makes \( E_{\text{note}} \) a re-randomisation of that account's
   registered credential -- hence an encryption of \( M_D \) under
   \( pk_D \); (C4) encrypts \( eIss \) under the /same/ \( pk_D \).  The
   spender's key is pinned to \( pk_D \) only later, at spend, by
   =verifySpendCP=; soundness is therefore a /composition/ of the mint
   membership proof and the spend equality proof -- the only two moments at
   which the issuer's and the recipient's anchors are respectively live.

   The construction rests on one property of registration, already present
   in the identity layer for unrelated (determinism / unlinkability)
   reasons, and now load-bearing.

   #+begin_definition
   <<asm:grind>>
   /(Non-grindable identities.)/  Each registered identity point is
   \( M = H(\mathrm{id})\cdot G \), where \( \mathrm{id} \) is the
   KYC-verified attribute record and \( H \) (keccak256 over a canonical
   encoding) is preimage-resistant, modelled as a random oracle.  No PPT
   adversary registers an identity point of its choosing; in particular it
   cannot register an account whose \( M \) equals a prescribed target
   \( M_D + \delta G \) for an adversarially chosen \( \delta \neq 0 \).
   #+end_definition

   #+begin_proposition
   <<prop:sufficiency>>
   /(Sufficiency.)/  Under Assumption [[asm:grind]], with =verifySpendCP=
   enforced at spend, the coupled construction \( \mathcal{C}^\star \)
   closes the no-deniability clause (Def. [[def:ndr]]) for A2: for every
   accepted mint-and-spend, a compelled spender recovers the issuer's
   registered \( M_{\text{iss}} \) from \( eIss \).
   #+end_proposition

   #+begin_proof
   By (C3), \( E_{\text{note}} \) is a re-randomisation of
   \( E_{\text{addr}}[D] \), so for every \( sk \),
   \( C_n - sk\,R_n = M_D + (r_D + r_n)(sk_D - sk)\,G \), where
   \( sk_D = \mathrm{dlog}\,pk_D \).  At spend, =verifySpendCP= establishes
   that the spender holds \( sk_s \) with registered key \( pk_s = sk_s G \)
   and that \( M_s := C_n - sk_s R_n \) equals the spender's /registered/
   identity.  Substituting, \( M_s = M_D + (r_D + r_n)(sk_D - sk_s)\,G \).
   If \( sk_s \neq sk_D \), then \( M_s = M_D + \delta G \) with
   \( \delta = (r_D+r_n)(sk_D-sk_s) \neq 0 \); by Assumption [[asm:grind]]
   the probability that such a point is a registered identity distinct from
   \( D \)'s is negligible (the adversary would have to register an account
   at \( M_D + \delta G \), i.e. find KYC data hashing to \( m_D + \delta \),
   contradicting preimage resistance).  Hence \( sk_s = sk_D \) and
   \( pk_s = pk_D \) except with negligible probability.  By (C4),
   \( C_e - sk_s R_e = M_{\text{iss}} + r'(pk_D - pk_s) = M_{\text{iss}} \),
   and by (C1) \( M_{\text{iss}} \) is the issuer's registered identity.
   The compelled spender therefore produces a receipt naming \( I \).
   #+end_proof

   #+begin_proposition
   <<prop:privacy>>
   /(Recipient privacy preserved.)/  In \( \mathcal{C}^\star \) the values
   \( pk_D, E_{\text{addr}}[D], M_{\text{iss}} \) are private witnesses; the
   only public artefacts are the fixed root \( \mathsf{rt} \) and the
   ciphertext \( eIss \) (semantically secure absent \( sk_D \)).  By
   Groth16 zero-knowledge and ElGamal semantic security, no third party
   learns the recipient's identity or key, and two A2 mints to distinct
   recipients are computationally indistinguishable.
   #+end_proposition

   #+begin_comment
   POLISH: tie the cost here -- non-native BN254 G1 in F_r (C1,C3,C4 are
   emulated F_p), ~10^6 R1CS/leaf, defer the number to Sec eval (D:997-1027,
   D:1073-1105).  One sentence is enough; the eval section carries the table.
   #+end_comment

   This sufficiency is what reserves A2 rather than abandons it: the
   construction exists and is sound, but its non-native \( \mathbb{G}_1 \)
   arithmetic (C1, C3, C4 are emulated \( \mathbb{F}_p \) operations on the
   proof system's own curve) costs \( \sim\!10^6 \) R1CS per leaf
   (Sec. [[#sec:eval]]) -- an order of magnitude beyond the public-issuer
   path, and the reason A2 ships under the mechanism of
   Sec. [[#sec:burden]] in the interim.

   *Remark (the cost is prover-side; interactive A2).*  The \( 10^6 \) R1CS
   is a /prover/ cost: the EVM verifies the resulting proof in constant gas,
   so the construction is expensive to /prove/ but cheap to /check/.  Where
   the wallet runs on a free-compute substrate that already holds the
   registry tree (here, a Holochain layer), the prover cost is absorbed off
   chain and the tree need not live on chain beyond its root.  A variant we
   note for completeness: when both parties are co-present off chain at note
   creation (/interactive A2/), they run the full bilateral handshake and
   prove it at mint -- recovering the EOA transfer's free,
   collusion-resistant mutual decryptability (Sec. [[#sec:cost]]) while still
   depositing unlinkably -- at the cost of an online recipient.  Neither
   observation relaxes Prop. [[prop:necessity]] (the membership proof is
   still required); they relocate /where/ it is proved and confirm its
   on-chain verification was never the bottleneck.

** Is the SNARK necessary?   *** NEW (conditional result + open problem) ***

   The sufficiency above is an upper bound.  The matching question -- /must/
   a collusion-resistant A2 receipt use registry set-membership? -- is the
   paper's central open problem.  We resolve it under one restriction and
   leave the unconditional statement open.

   #+begin_proposition
   <<prop:necessity>>
   /(Conditional necessity.)/  Let \( \Pi \) be a protocol that, in the A2
   setting, (i) preserves bulk-tracing resistance and third-party
   non-disclosure (issuer named only at mint, recipient only at spend,
   mint and spend unlinkable), (ii) closes the no-deniability clause, and
   (iii) achieves the binding by referencing a registered party at a
   /single/ protocol step.  Then \( \Pi \) performs a zero-knowledge proof
   of membership in the identity registry -- over the recipient set at mint,
   or the issuer set at spend.
   #+end_proposition

   #+begin_proof
   By Lemma [[lem:vacuous]], (ii) forces \( \Pi \) to certify that \( eIss \)
   decrypts, under the addressed recipient's registered key, to a registered
   /issuer/ identity -- binding to some key alone does not close the clause.
   This certificate references a registered party: the recipient's key
   \( pk_D \) (at mint), or the issuer's identity (at spend).  By (iii) it is
   a single step's reference, either in the clear or in zero knowledge.

   /In the clear is ruled out./  Revealing the referenced key or identity on
   chain names a party and makes them linkable across mint and spend,
   violating (i): naming \( pk_D \) at mint exposes the recipient, and
   revealing the decrypted \( M_{\text{iss}} \) at spend exposes the issuer.
   Importing \( E_{\text{note}} \) (or a deterministic commitment to it) to
   the mint to bind there instead re-links mint and spend, also violating (i).

   /Zero-knowledge is set-membership./  A reference that certifies the
   referenced party is registered without revealing /which/ -- the
   recipient's \( pk_D \) at mint, or the issuer that \( eIss \) re-encrypts
   at spend -- is, by definition, a zero-knowledge proof of membership in the
   registry.  As (i) excludes the in-clear case, \( \Pi \) performs this.
   #+end_proof

   #+begin_comment
   The conditional is the honest, provable statement.  The unconditional
   lower bound is the OPEN PROBLEM -- enumerate the escape routes the
   conditional excludes (these are exactly the future-work threads, keep in
   sync with Sec discussion / D:1286-1322):
     - bindings amortised across MULTIPLE steps (drops restriction (iii));
     - TWO-PARTY mints: the recipient contributes a blinded
       verifiable-decryption witness at mint without exposing E_note;
     - witness-encryption / functional-encryption style constructions;
     - accountability that does NOT bind eIss at all (threshold/selective
       escrow of M_iss to a regulator key) -- a different trust model.
   A clean impossibility settles that the SNARK is necessary; a loophole is
   a cheaper construction.  This is the paper's headline forward question.
   #+end_comment

   The restriction (iii) -- a single binding step -- is what the proof
   leans on, and relaxing it names the open frontier.  A binding amortised
   across steps, a two-party mint in which the recipient contributes a
   blinded witness without exposing \( E_{\text{note}} \), a
   witness-encryption construction, or an accountability mechanism that does
   not bind \( eIss \) at all (threshold escrow of \( M_{\text{iss}} \) to a
   regulator key -- a weaker trust model) each escape the case analysis.
   Whether any achieves collusion-resistant A2 naming without registry
   set-membership is open (Sec. [[#sec:discussion]]); a clean impossibility
   would prove the SNARK necessary, a loophole would be a cheaper design.

** A mechanism instead of a proof   *** NEW (model to formalise) ***
   :PROPERTIES:

   :END:

   #+begin_comment
   Turn the prose backstop (D:1230-1266) into a game.  Crucial structural
   input from non-frameability (Thm receipt-soundness): collusion can only
   DENY (render un-nameable), never FRAME (substitute a victim), because
   the binding is always over msg.sender's OWN credential.  And the
   recipient is ALWAYS KYC-identifiable (msg.sender at spend).  So the
   residual risk is self-limiting to the identifiable recipient.
   #+end_comment

   #+begin_proposition
   <<prop:burden>>
   /(Recipient-burden incentive compatibility.)/  Model an A2 deposit by
   recipient \( D \): audit probability \( q \), AML penalty \( L \) for
   holding funds from an unaccountable source, delivery-verification cost
   \( \varepsilon \) (decrypt \( eIss \), run the \( \gamma \)-coupling),
   and collusion benefit \( b \).  If \( qL > b \) and \( \varepsilon \) is
   small, the unique sequential equilibrium is "verify at delivery, reject
   an un-nameable note", so accepted A2 notes are nameable with high
   probability.  By non-frameability the only off-equilibrium outcome is an
   un-nameable note (never a framed third party), and \( D \) -- always
   KYC-identifiable -- bears the penalty; the residual risk is therefore
   self-limiting to the recipient.
   #+end_proposition

   audit/penalty).  Show verify-then-accept dominates accept-blind when
