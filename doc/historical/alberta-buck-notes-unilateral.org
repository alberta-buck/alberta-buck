#+BEGIN_ABSTRACT
A direct EOA transfer /deduces/ identity from the two accounts in play -- the
account is primary, the Identity point \( M \) is derived.  A Note is the
opposite object: it /names/ the identities and treats accounts as interchangeable
envelopes that need only /match/ the expected \( M \).  This document specifies,
and reports a working implementation of, the *identity-targeted A2 Note*: a
private-issuer cheque addressed to the recipient's Identity \( M_{\text{rec}} \)
rather than to one of the recipient's accounts.  The issuer encrypts its /own/
registered Identity \( M_I \) under the recipient's Identity /point/, so the one
secret every Fountain account of the recipient shares -- the identity scalar
\( m_{\text{rec}} \) -- opens it, and the issuer needs only \( M_{\text{rec}} \),
never an account.  At deposit the recipient proves eligibility to the EVM with an
Okamoto sigma that reveals no Identity; and from the same \( m_{\text{rec}} \) the
recipient /alone/ decrypts \( M_I \) and produces a plaintext receipt naming both
parties and the amount.  A colluding issuer cannot mint a spendable-but-unnameable
note, because the decrypted issuer Identity must be a member of the
registry-Identity tree.  Every step is implemented and tested
(=alberta_buck.wallet.unilateral_a2=, =IdentityRegistry.verifyDepositCoupling=)
([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-notes][Notes]], [[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-notes-decryptability][Decryptability]])

* The Inversion: Address the Identity, Not the Account

  In the standard A2 construction (/alberta-buck-notes/) the issuer reads /one
  specific/ registered account of the recipient and re-randomises /that/
  account's credential; the depositor must then spend from that same account.
  The identity-targeted construction drops the account from the addressing
  entirely.  The issuer knows only the recipient's Identity point
  \( M_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{rec}} G \) -- learned out of band, exactly as a
  payer learns "who" they are paying -- and the note is redeemable by /any/
  account the recipient controls that is bound to \( m_{\text{rec}} \).

  This is not merely more convenient; it is /lighter to make collusion-resistant/
  and it yields a receipt the recipient can produce /alone/.  The whole design
  turns on one substitution: the issuer encrypts under the recipient's Identity
  /point/ \( M_{\text{rec}} \), whose decryption secret \( m_{\text{rec}} \) is
  shared by every Fountain account of the recipient, instead of under one
  account's key \( pk_{\text{rec}} \).

  *What was built.*  This document is matched by a working reference:
  - =alberta_buck/wallet/unilateral_a2.py= -- the mint, the deposit-coupling
    sigma, and the unilateral receipt (the registry-Identity Merkle tree is the

    here a thin point-centric facade over it).
  - =IdentityRegistry.verifyDepositCoupling= -- the on-chain, EIP-196
    verification of the deposit-eligibility sigma.
  - =Notes.spendCoupledA2= -- the deposit entrypoint that co-verifies the

* Parties, Keys, and the Identity Tree

  #+begin_definition
  Notation.  \( G \) is the BN254 \( \mathbb{G}_1 \) generator; \( H \) a second
  nothing-up-my-sleeve generator (= =issuer_reenc.H_POINT=).  An /Identity/ is a
  KYC-bound scalar \( m = H(\text{id}) \) and its point \( M = m G \).  A
  /registered account/ is \( (\text{addr}, pk = sk\,G,\ E_{\text{addr}} =
  (R, C) = (rG,\ M + r\,pk)) \): an ElGamal encryption of its Identity \( M \)
  under its own key.  The Identity Fountain lets one \( m \) back unlimited
  unlinkable accounts \( (sk_i, pk_i, E_{\text{addr},i}) \), all encrypting the
  same \( M = mG \).
  #+end_definition

  Two principals:

  - *Issuer* \( I \): identity \( (m_I, M_I) \), with a registered account
    \( (pk_I, E^{\text{reg}}_I = (R^{\text{reg}}, C^{\text{reg}})) \).  Holds
    \( sk_I \).  The issuer recovers its own \( M_I = C^{\text{reg}} - sk_I
    R^{\text{reg}} \).
  - *Recipient* \( D \): identity \( (m_{\text{rec}}, M_{\text{rec}}) \), with one
    or more registered accounts.  The depositing account is
    \( (pk_{\text{dep}}, E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d)) \); \( D \) holds
    \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) and \( m_{\text{rec}} \).

  *The identity provider and its tree.*  The registry maintains a Poseidon Merkle
  tree whose leaves are registered Identity /points/, \( \ell(M) =
  \mathrm{Poseidon}(M.x, M.y) \) (=identity_leaf=).  Its root \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \)
  is published on chain; the full tree lives off chain (the Alberta Buck wallet's
  Holochain layer -- no size limit).  Membership of a point \( M \) in this tree
  is the statement "\( M \) is a genuine, KYC-registered Identity," and it is the
  anchor each party uses to certify the /other's/ decrypted Identity.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Mint, Step by Step (Issuer)

  The issuer holds \( (sk_I, E^{\text{reg}}_I) \) and knows \( M_{\text{rec}} \).
  It performs four operations.

** Step M1 -- recover the issuer's own Identity

   \[ M_I \;=\; C^{\text{reg}} - sk_I\, R^{\text{reg}}. \]
   /Required:/ the issuer's secret \( sk_I \) and its registered credential
   \( E^{\text{reg}}_I \).  /From the registry:/ nothing new (the issuer reads its
   own record).

** Step M2 -- encrypt the issuer Identity under the recipient Identity point

   With fresh randomness \( r' \),
   \[
     eIss \;=\; (R_e, C_e) \;=\; \bigl(r'\,G,\ \; M_I + r'\,M_{\text{rec}}\bigr).
   \]
   This is an ElGamal encryption of \( M_I \) under \( M_{\text{rec}} \) /treated
   as a public key/.  Its decryption secret is \( m_{\text{rec}} \), since
   \( M_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{rec}} G \): for any holder of \( m_{\text{rec}} \),
   \[
     C_e - m_{\text{rec}} R_e
       = M_I + r' M_{\text{rec}} - m_{\text{rec}}(r' G)
       = M_I + r'(M_{\text{rec}} - m_{\text{rec}}G) = M_I.
   \]
   /Required from the issuer:/ \( M_I \), \( r' \).  /Required of the recipient:/
   only \( M_{\text{rec}} \) -- *not* an account, a key, or which account will
   deposit.

** Step M3 -- the anti-framing binding (so \( eIss \) names the true minter)

   The issuer proves, with the shipped recipient-blinded re-encryption sigma
   (=issuer_reenc=, here with the "recipient key" slot fed the Identity point
   \( M_{\text{rec}} \)), that \( eIss \) re-encrypts the issuer's /own registered/
   \( M_I \) -- without revealing \( M_I \) or \( M_{\text{rec}} \).  This forecloses
   /framing/: the issuer cannot key \( eIss \) over a victim's Identity, because the
   proof is taken against \( E^{\text{reg}}_I = E_{\text{addr}}[\texttt{msg.sender}] \).
   Verified on chain by =IdentityRegistry.verifyIssuerReenc=.

** Step M4 -- commit the note

   \[
     idHash = \mathrm{Poseidon}(R_e.x, R_e.y, C_e.x, C_e.y), \qquad
     cm = \mathrm{Poseidon}(\text{A2},\, v,\, \rho,\, idHash,\, predicate).
   \]
   The target Identity is /not/ committed separately -- it is implicit in
   \( eIss \)'s key, since only \( m_{\text{rec}} \) decrypts \( eIss \) to a
   registered point (Section /Security/).

** What the mint publishes, holds, and transmits

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Datum                                  | On chain (mint tx)            | Issuer keeps          | Sent to recipient     |
   |----------------------------------------+-------------------------------+-----------------------+-----------------------|
   | \( cm \) (opaque Poseidon commitment)  | yes (calldata)                | --                    | (implied by opening)  |
   | \( eIss = (R_e, C_e) \)                | yes (leaf-tie public output)  | \( r' \)              | yes                   |
   | anti-framing binding (=verifyIssuerReenc=) | yes                       | --                    | yes (for the receipt) |
   | opening \( (\text{A2}, v, \rho, idHash, predicate) \) | no              | --                    | yes (off chain)       |
   | \( M_I, M_{\text{rec}}, sk_I, r' \)    | *never*                       | yes                   | --                    |
   #+LATEX: }

   To Mallory the mint is: =msg.sender= (the issuer account) escrowed \( v \)
   BUCK and folded an opaque \( cm \), publishing a ciphertext \( eIss \) and a
   zero-knowledge binding.  Neither \( M_I \) (issuer Identity) nor
   \( M_{\text{rec}} \) (recipient Identity) is revealed.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The A2 Note Payload (What Travels)

  The note is the opening tuple plus the issuer payload, delivered off chain
  (message, QR, or wallet-to-wallet):

  #+begin_example
  note = (
     flavor = A2,  v,  rho,  idHash,  predicate,     # the commitment opening
     eIss   = (R_e, C_e),                            # issuer M under recipient identity point
     binding,                                        # verifyIssuerReenc anti-framing proof
     mint_tx_ref,                                    # where cm was folded (anchors the receipt)
  )
  #+end_example

  It carries /no/ account address of either party and /no/ plaintext Identity.
  Anyone intercepting it learns nothing: \( eIss \) is semantically secure absent
  \( m_{\text{rec}} \), and the binding is zero-knowledge.

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* Delivery and Recipient Verification (Off Chain, Before Accepting)

  On receipt, \( D \) verifies the note /before/ treating it as money:

  1. *Decrypt the issuer.*  \( M_I = C_e - m_{\text{rec}} R_e \) (=elgamal_decrypt=
     under \( m_{\text{rec}} \)).
  2. *Membership.*  Check \( \ell(M_I) \in \mathsf{rt_{id}} \) -- \( M_I \) is a
     registered Identity (=IdentityTree.contains=).
  3. *Binding.*  Check =verifyIssuerReenc= accepts the binding against the named
     issuer account's registry record -- \( eIss \) really re-encrypts /that
     account's/ registered Identity.

  If any check fails the recipient /rejects/ the note (it is un-nameable, so its
  value could never be accounted for).  An honest recipient thus only ever accepts
  notes it can later receipt -- the cryptographic form of the recipient-burden
  posture.  (Tested: =test_delivery_recipient_decrypts_issuer=.)

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Deposit, Step by Step (Depositor)
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: sec:deposit
  :END:

  \( D \) chooses /any/ registered account bound to \( m_{\text{rec}} \) as the
  payout vehicle and proves eligibility to the EVM while revealing no Identity.

** The eligibility relation

   The depositor proves knowledge of \( (m_{\text{rec}}, sk_{\text{dep}}, b) \)
   such that, with \( eIss = (R_e, C_e) \), the chosen account's registered
   \( E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d) \), its on-chain key \( pk_{\text{dep}} \), and a
   published commitment \( P_I = M_I + b H \):

   \begin{equation*}
   \begin{aligned}
     &\textbf{(E4)} & pk_{\text{dep}} &= sk_{\text{dep}}\,G
       &&\text{— the real account key;}\\
     &\textbf{(E2)} & C_d &= m_{\text{rec}}\,G + sk_{\text{dep}}\,R_d
       &&\text{— the account is bound to } M_{\text{rec}} = m_{\text{rec}}G;\\
     &\textbf{(E3)} & C_e - P_I &= m_{\text{rec}}\,R_e - b\,H
       &&\text{— } eIss \text{ decrypts under } m_{\text{rec}} \text{ to } P_I - bH.
   \end{aligned}
   \end{equation*}

   (E4) pins \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) to the registered key; (E2) then pins
   \( m_{\text{rec}}G \) to the account's Identity; (E3) ties that /same/
   \( m_{\text{rec}} \) to \( eIss \)'s decryption, with the recovered issuer
   Identity \( M_I \) /committed and blinded/ in \( P_I \).  This is a five-point
   Okamoto sigma over EIP-196 (=deposit_couple_prove= / =deposit_couple_verify=,
   on chain =verifyDepositCoupling=) -- the same family as =verifyApprove= /
   =issuer_reenc=, and equally cheap.

** The membership half (the SNARK piece)

   \( P_I \) commits the decrypted issuer Identity.  The depositor additionally
   proves, in zero knowledge, that \( P_I \)'s point is a member of the
   registry-Identity tree: \( \exists\, b:\ P_I - bH = M_I \wedge \ell(M_I) \in
   \mathsf{rt_{id}} \).  The membership is a /native/ Poseidon-Merkle proof; the
   tie to the EC commitment \( P_I \) is the one non-native scalar relation, and
   the only remaining circuit work (Section /Implementation Status/).  Its effect:
   a note whose \( eIss \) is keyed to anything but \( M_{\text{rec}} \) decrypts to
   a non-member and the spend /reverts/.

** Completing the spend

   The deposit calls (schematically) =Notes.spend(proof, root, nullifier, face,
   recipient, eIss, depositCouplingProof, membershipProof)=, where the contract:
   verifies the note's Merkle inclusion and nullifier (unspent), calls
   =verifyDepositCoupling(depositor, eIss, dc)= (E2/E3/E4), checks the membership
   proof against \( \mathsf{rt_{id}} \), records the nullifier, and pays =face=
   BUCK out of the pool to the depositor's chosen account via the Carrying path.
   The nullifier is \( \mathrm{Poseidon}(\rho, idHash, \text{A-tag}) \)
   (=nullifier_a=), unlinkable to \( cm \).

** What the deposit publishes -- and why Mallory is blind

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Datum                          | Revealed at deposit                | Hidden                          |
   |--------------------------------+------------------------------------+---------------------------------|
   | depositor account (=msg.sender=) | yes (it receives the BUCK)        | its Identity \( M_{\text{rec}} \) (DDH in \( E_{\text{dep}} \)) |
   | \( pk_{\text{dep}}, E_{\text{dep}} \) | yes (already in registry)     | \( sk_{\text{dep}}, m_{\text{rec}} \) |
   | \( eIss \), \( P_I \)          | yes                                | \( M_I \) (blinded in \( P_I \)), \( M_{\text{rec}} \) |
   | nullifier, face                | yes                                | which mint this redeems         |
   | issuer account / tx            | *never* (not an input at deposit)  | the entire issuer side          |
   #+LATEX: }

   The deposit names /no/ issuer account, tx, or Identity.  \( eIss \) and
   \( P_I \) are the only Identity-derived values, and both are blinded: \( eIss \)
   is semantically secure, \( P_I = M_I + bH \) is perfectly hiding.  So Mallory
   cannot link the depositor's account to the issuer's mint, nor to other accounts
   of the same recipient.  (Tested: =test_deposit_proof_hides_identities=,
   =test_two_mints_unlinkable=; soundness:
   =test_deposit_coupling_{tamper,wrong_account,wrong_chainid}_rejected= and the
   seven Solidity parity tests.)

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* The Unilateral Receipt (Recipient Alone)
  :PROPERTIES:
  :CUSTOM_ID: sec:receipt
  :END:

  The recipient holds the /one/ secret \( m_{\text{rec}} \) that names /both/
  parties, and needs no cooperation from the issuer at spend time.

** Construction

   \begin{align*}
     M_I            &= C_e - m_{\text{rec}} R_e && (\text{the issuer Identity}); \\
     M_{\text{rec}} &= m_{\text{rec}} G         && (\text{the recipient's own}).
   \end{align*}

   The recipient attaches a /verifiable decryption/ (=verifiable_decrypt=, a
   Chaum-Pedersen DLEQ) proving \( eIss \) decrypts under \( M_{\text{rec}} \) to
   \( M_I \) -- revealing both points but not \( m_{\text{rec}} \).  The receipt is
   \begin{align*}
     \mathcal{R} = \bigl(M_I,\ M_{\text{rec}},\ v,\ eIss,\ \text{vd},\
       \text{binding},\ \text{issuer addr},\ \text{chainid}\bigr).
   \end{align*}

** Third-party verification (no secret)

   A court, auditor, or counterparty checks four links (=verify_receipt=):

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Link | Check                                                                  | Establishes                                   |
   |------+------------------------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------|
   | 1    | =verifyIssuerReenc= on the binding vs. the issuer account's record     | \( eIss \) re-encrypts the issuer's /registered/ \( M_I \) (no framing) |
   | 2    | =verifiable_decrypt_verify(eIss, M_rec, M_I)=                          | \( eIss \) decrypts under \( M_{\text{rec}} \) to \( M_I \) |
   | 3    | \( \ell(M_I) \in \mathsf{rt_{id}} \)                                   | the issuer Identity is registered -- /the coupling/ |
   | 4    | \( \ell(M_{\text{rec}}) \in \mathsf{rt_{id}} \)                        | the recipient Identity is registered          |
   #+LATEX: }

   Link 3 is the load-bearing one.  Composing 1 and 2: \( M_I = C_e -
   r' M_{\text{rec}} \) (link 2, since \( R_e = r'G \)) while the binding gives
   \( C_e = M_I^{\text{reg}} + r' Q \) for the issuer's registered Identity
   \( M_I^{\text{reg}} \) and the issuer-chosen point \( Q \).  Hence \( M_I =
   M_I^{\text{reg}} + r'(Q - M_{\text{rec}}) \).  If \( Q \neq M_{\text{rec}} \)
   the recovered \( M_I \) is a pseudorandom offset of a registered point and is
   /not/ itself in the tree; so link 3 /forces/ \( Q = M_{\text{rec}} \) and thus
   \( M_I = M_I^{\text{reg}} \).  The receipt is therefore VALID exactly when it
   names the true issuer.  (Tested: =test_unilateral_receipt_names_both=.)

** Why it is unilateral, and why collusion fails

   Completeness needs no issuer participation: \( m_{\text{rec}} \) yields both
   identities.  Collusion-resistance is the contrapositive of link 3: a colluding
   issuer who keys \( eIss \) to a throwaway point (so the recipient "cannot"
   decrypt to a registered Identity) produces a note whose decrypted \( M_I \) is a
   non-member -- so the /receipt is INVALID/ and the /spend membership reverts/.
   The pair cannot obtain a spendable-but-unnameable note.  (Tested:
   =test_collusion_bogus_eiss_unnameable= -- the bogus note's binding still
   verifies, yet the decrypted point is provably absent from the tree and
   =verify_receipt= returns INVALID at link 3.)

* Security Properties (and the Tests that Witness Them)

  #+begin_theorem
  /(Completeness.)/  For an honest mint to \( M_{\text{rec}} \), any account of the
  recipient bound to \( m_{\text{rec}} \) satisfies E2/E3/E4 and the membership;
  and the recipient produces a VALID receipt naming \( (M_I, M_{\text{rec}}, v) \).
  #+end_theorem

  #+begin_theorem
  /(Collusion-resistance.)/  Under the non-grindable-identities assumption
  (\( M = H(\text{id})G \), \( H \) a random oracle), no issuer/recipient coalition
  produces a spendable note whose decrypted issuer Identity is absent from the
  tree: spendability requires \( eIss \) to decrypt, under the depositor's
  authenticated \( m_{\text{rec}} \), to a tree member, which is nameable.
  #+end_theorem

  #+begin_theorem
  /(Non-frameability.)/  The mint binding forces \( eIss \) over the minter's own
  registered \( M_I \); the only reachable outcomes are "names the true minter"
  (VALID) or "names no one" (INVALID).  A victim cannot be substituted.
  #+end_theorem

  #+begin_theorem
  /(Privacy.)/  The deposit sigma is honest-verifier zero-knowledge and \( P_I \)
  is perfectly hiding; \( eIss \) is IND-CPA.  No third party learns
  \( m_{\text{rec}} \), \( M_{\text{rec}} \), or \( M_I \), and the deposit names no
  issuer account or transaction -- so mint and spend stay unlinkable.
  #+end_theorem

  The cross-artifact tests pin the Solidity verifier to the Python reference
  byte-for-byte (the established =alberta_buck.wallet= \( \to \) =vm.readFile=
  pattern): =scripts/gen_unilateral_a2_vectors.py= emits
  =test/vectors/unilateral_a2.json=; =test/UnilateralA2.t.sol= reads it and
  asserts =verifyDepositCoupling= accepts the canonical proof and rejects every
  tamper.
