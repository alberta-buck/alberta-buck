#+DATE: 2026-10-10
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

Measurements of [[file:BASKET-MACHINE.org][the basket machine]] on the real contracts in pyrevm. Every number here is rebuilt
by the commands in [[#regenerate][the last section]]; none of the output is committed. Open decisions and
remaining work are in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-TASKS.org][the task list]].

* Summary

- /Rebalancing, on prices built to revert./ With every actor active the pair policy adds 3.2% to
  4.2% to the basket's value in 120 days, in BUCK, in USD and at reference alike. The first
  sizing added 0.4%. A wallet of the same TOKENs under a simple turn rule would earn about 8%.
- /Rebalancing, on commodity-like prices./ 0.5% to 0.7% over a run that stands for a decade. The
  limit there is the 2-point allocation band, which is open 4% of the time; a 1-point band
  doubles the result.
- /It stays out of the way./ No transfer on flat noisy prices, under any setting. When a TOKEN
  reprices and never returns it gives up 0.1% to 0.3%.
- /The money./ On the commodity profile BUCK follows its reference basket through a 42% rise
  in USD cost with a mean parity error of 0.37%, with no desk operation.
- /Owned arbitrage is not a return yet./ It is the larger number in a six-hour-step lab and
  falls 5.6 times at hourly steps. It measures the feed's discontinuity and an uncontested
  arbitrageur.
- /One side effect to rule on./ Each transfer turns some wallet TOKEN into BUCK inside
  liquidity. Where no one supplies that BUCK, it becomes 6% to 8% dear and K runs to its rail.

* The lab in brief

One run is one isolated world: three TOKENs, TOKEN/USDC pools driven to an external feed, and
TOKEN/BUCK and BUCK/USDC pools that float. It lasts 120 days at four market steps a day, after a
120-day prelude that settles the basket's liquidity at mean prices. The basket starts with one
million of equity; each pool has one million of outside liquidity.

/Stages/ add actors one at a time: feeds, monitor, monitor with K, linked markets (arbitrage),
basket, K, desk, buyer only, seller only, both, all. "Isolated basket" below is the basket stage:
no K movement, no desk, no private flow. "All actors" is the last.

/Profiles/ are the price paths:

| Profile | Path |
|---------+------|
| three-phase | Each TOKEN a 12% sine, 30-day period, 120 degrees apart |
| noise20, noise80 | The same with 20 or 80 bp of fresh noise per sample |
| flat-noise80 | Flat prices with 80 bp of noise |
| mixed, shock | Pulses, a ramp and noise; a ten-day 30% step in one TOKEN |
| historical-bridge | Gold, food and energy over 120 real days, pinned to end where they began |
| two-step | One TOKEN reprices by 15% twice and never returns |
| commodities | Three uncorrelated prices around a USD inflation trend ([[#commodities][below]]) |

/Units./ The contract's equity is in BUCK at its own marks. USD converts it at the BUCK/USDC
spot. /At reference/ revalues the recorded book: TOKEN at the external feed, net BUCK at the
reference basket's USD cost. Where nothing regulates BUCK its spot drifts with the basket's own
trades, and BUCK and USD marks can disagree about a sign for that reason alone; the reference
mark is the one to read there. With all actors the three agree. All are marks: no caller gas, no
cost of unwinding.

Effects are differences against a /maintenance/ control: the same basket with liquidity
placement, daily bookkeeping and the risk fallback, and no paired transfer.

* Rebalancing

** How much there is to take

A frictionless calculation ([[file:review/rebalance-benchmark.py][rebalance-benchmark.py]]) holds one million of TOKEN in a wallet,
charges 0.3% per swap and applies simple rules to the clean cycle:

| Rule | Return over 120 days |
|------+----------------------|
| Hold | 0.00% |
| Rebalance to equal weights every 6 hours | -0.10% |
| Rebalance when a weight is 2 points from target | +4.44% |
| Rebalance when a weight is 3 points from target | +6.88% |
| Rebalance after a pair retreats 1% from its extreme | +8.47% |
| Rebalance every 10 days | +12.87% |

Continuous rebalancing of a smooth closed cycle earns nothing. The harvest comes from leaving
a gap open until near the turn. The basket cannot reach these figures as built: with most of
its TOKEN in liquidity its allocation gaps are about 60% as wide as a wallet's.

** Why the first sizing took a twentieth of it

On the clean cycle the first sizing made 133 transfers moving 36,307 BUCK of TOKEN out of a
1.72 million book: median 231 BUCK, largest 800. Every one sold the dearer TOKEN. Three causes:

1. The braking release was 10% of the gap a day, times a braking factor that cannot exceed
   =T / (2T + dt/2)= for a smooth turn: 0.44 at a 12-hour response and 6-hour steps.
2. The release halved at a fixed 20 bp/day of outward speed. These pairs move at up to 435
   bp/day and the estimate lags a turn by about 1.7 days, so around the true extreme the release
   factor was 0.06 to 0.25.
3. Closure was confirmed about four days after a pair's extreme, when one leg is already back
   inside its allocation, and was paid by closing speed, which peaks as the spread vanishes.

** The sizing campaign

Seven profiles; a maintenance control, five sizings and two narrower bands each. Gain against
maintenance over 120 days, book at reference. /Chosen/ is 4 gaps a day with the release and
closure judged on the rate's own allowance.

| Profile | First | 1/day | Chosen | 8/day | Chosen, all actors |
|---------+-------+-------+--------+-------+--------------------|
| three-phase | +4,059 | +17,063 | +35,300 | +37,912 | +36,808 |
| noise20 | +4,162 | +18,060 | +36,947 | +37,526 | +38,002 |
| noise80 | +3,807 | +20,004 | +34,495 | +36,588 | +39,878 |
| flat-noise80 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| historical-bridge | +597 | +812 | +970 | +1,222 | +349 |
| two-step | -232 | -738 | -1,265 | -1,597 | -2,074 |
| commodities | +210 | +1,527 | +4,506 | +3,826 | +6,915 |

With all actors the chosen sizing's gain on the cyclic profiles is 36,808 to 39,878 at
reference, 38,915 to 41,710 in USD and 31,600 to 32,799 in BUCK. It makes fewer transfers than
the first (71 against 122 on the clean cycle; median 4,600 BUCK). The gain recurs in each 30-day
window: +9,628, +10,610, +9,118, +7,453.

Doubling the rate again adds 2% to 7% on the cycles and a quarter to the two-step cost. On the
historical path the effect is within 0.1% in every unit and its sign depends on the unit.

Checked on every response of the clean and noisy runs: each planned leg improves both
allocations, stays within its capacities, has both routes ready and appears in the receipts;
no leg sells the cheaper TOKEN; every TOKEN is traded both ways in each mature cycle. All
executed legs are braking releases. By the time closure is confirmed the gap has been moved.

* Commodity-like prices
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: commodities
:END:

** The hypothesis and the data

A basic commodity's price is bounded below by what it costs to produce and above by what can
replace it, so over years it follows the pricing currency's inflation, each with its own delay.
[[file:review/commodity-inflation.py][commodity-inflation.py]] checks the vendored monthly series against US CPI, 1976 to 2026.
/Departure/ is the log distance of the real price from its own trend line.

| Series | Real drift, % a year | Spread | Lowest | Highest | Half-life | M2 lag, months |
|--------+----------------------+--------+--------+---------+-----------+----------------|
| Gold | +1.74 | 42.7% | -91% | +123% | 127 mo | none clear |
| Energy, US retail | -0.19 | 13.8% | -30% | +41% | 53 mo | 13 |
| Food, US retail | +0.23 | 6.4% | -12% | +15% | 17 mo | 23 |
| Construction, US PPI | -0.02 | 10.2% | -18% | +30% | 106 mo | 10 |
| Labour, US hourly | +0.29 | 5.3% | -9% | +13% | 299 mo | 16 |
| Energy, BCPI | -0.19 | 39.1% | -138% | +103% | 32 mo | 10 |
| Metals, BCPI | -0.01 | 26.3% | -59% | +70% | 82 mo | none clear |
| Agriculture, BCPI | -1.62 | 21.4% | -50% | +51% | 63 mo | 10 |

- Six of the eight drift within 0.3% a year of CPI over fifty years. Gold gains 1.7% a year
  and the agriculture index loses 1.6%.
- Departures are large and last years. Ceilings are further from trend than floors.
- The delay is against money, not against CPI: year-over-year changes line up with CPI at
  zero to five months and trail M2 by 10 to 23.
- Over fifty years the departures move together (0.3 to 0.8). Inside a ten-year window, against
  that window's own trend, gold, energy and food are close to uncorrelated (-0.05 to 0.14).

Inside ten-year windows (medians), which is what a 120-day run stands for:

| Series | Real drift in the window, % a year | Spread | Furthest | Months between crossings |
|--------+------------------------------------+--------+----------+--------------------------|
| Gold | 4.57 | 12.1% | 28.5% | 12.0 |
| Energy, US retail | 2.46 | 7.1% | 18.5% | 10.9 |
| Food, US retail | 0.80 | 3.0% | 9.3% | 6.7 |
| Construction, US PPI | 1.04 | 3.6% | 8.4% | 13.3 |
| Labour, US hourly | 0.66 | 1.2% | 2.7% | 20.0 |

Within one decade a real price need not return: gold's typically drifts 4.6% a year one way
or the other. The hypothesis is a statement about decades.

** The profile

=control_lab/commodities.py= builds each price in closed form:
=base × exp(inflation + lag gap + departure)=.

- /Inflation/ is 3% a year with a burst at 9% from day 36 to day 60; the level ends at 1.505.
- /Lag gap./ Each TOKEN follows that level through a first-order lag. Through the burst a slow
  TOKEN falls behind, then catches up.
- /Departure/ is a bounded oscillation of the TOKEN's own: ten sinusoids with seeded phases,
  the slower ones larger, squashed by a soft floor and a higher soft ceiling. The three are
  made uncorrelated over the run.

| TOKEN | Lag, months | Spread | Months between crossings | Floor | Ceiling |
|-------+-------------+--------+--------------------------+-------+---------|
| A: fast, gold-like | 3 | 12% | 12 | -24% | +34% |
| B: mid, energy-like | 13 | 7% | 11 | -16% | +22% |
| C: slow, labour-like | 16 | 1.2% | 20 | -2.3% | +3.2% |

One lab day is one month, so the shapes are a decade's and the pools see them thirty times
too fast. History before day zero is the same process in day-zero dollars. The profile takes no
random walk; the food and construction rows above are ready to substitute in =control-lab.toml=.

** What it shows

With all actors:

| Day | Reference basket, USD | BUCK/USDC | K |
|-----+-----------------------+-----------+---|
| 0 | 0.963 | 0.998 | 0.754 |
| 30 | 1.080 | 1.079 | 0.753 |
| 60 | 1.235 | 1.228 | 0.749 |
| 90 | 1.374 | 1.379 | 0.744 |
| 120 | 1.421 | 1.422 | 0.741 |

Mean parity error is 0.37% and the desk does not operate. The buyer has used 866 thousand of
its million USDC and the seller has 86 thousand BUCK of credit left: a longer run needs a
funding rule for them.

The wallet benchmark's rules earn 1.6% to 2.8% over holding on this path, against 4% to 13% on
the clean cycle. The basket's allocation is more than 2 points from target in 4% of the samples
and more than 1 point in 70%. Thirteen of its 16 transfers are at two extremes of TOKEN A: sold
17% to 20% above trend, bought 17% to 18% below. The timing is right and the opportunity rare.

A decade's harvest of 0.7% to 1.7% of the initial equity is small beside a 50% rise in the
price level. On this evidence the basket's case rests on holding value through inflation, with
rebalancing a modest addition, unless its constituents swing far more than these do.

* Arbitrage

With the chosen sizing, isolated basket, clean cycle, 120 days:

| Unit | Maintenance | Rebalancing | Owning arbitrage | Interaction | Combined |
|------+-------------+-------------+------------------+-------------+----------|
| At reference | +8,529 | +35,300 | +18,739 | +947 | +63,516 |
| USD | +9,728 | +45,956 | +19,021 | +434 | +75,138 |
| BUCK | -2,142 | -24,217 | +18,128 | +5,539 | -2,692 |

The arbitrage column is not a return:

- 18,792 of the 18,843 BUCK of clean-cycle profit is taken in the pass right after a feed
  step. The driver moves TOKEN/USDC by up to 0.63% per six-hour step and the BUCK pools are
  stale until arbitrage links them.
- With 24 steps a day the same cycle yields 3,394 BUCK on the same volume. With 80 bp of fresh
  noise per step it is 25,845 against 28,230: that income is the noise each step is given.
- The basket holds about a fifth of each TOKEN/BUCK pool. The owned arm takes the whole
  receipt, most of it from the outside liquidity provider, and is never contested.

Rebalancing does not share the dependence: at hourly steps the chosen sizing adds 39,506 at
reference on the clean cycle against 35,300. The basket's own transfers create about 500 BUCK
of arbitrage in 120 days, which it could close in the same transaction.

* What a transfer does to the book

A transfer sells the donor's wallet TOKEN before it touches a position, and places the
recipient's share as liquidity: half buys TOKEN, half stays BUCK beside it. Each wallet-sourced
transfer therefore moves half its value from TOKEN to net BUCK. The lien does not change.

At the chosen sizing the three wallets, about 945 TOKEN each after the prelude, are empty by day
90 to 100. TOKEN held falls from 10,019 to 9,120 and net BUCK rises from +2,441 to +135,193:
roughly 87% TOKEN and 13% net BUCK by value. After that a sale burns liquidity and the
composition is stable. The harvest continues: +7,225 at reference in the last 30 days isolated,
+7,453 with all actors.

Where that BUCK must come from matters. Clean cycle, 120 days, chosen sizing:

| Stage | Ending K | Mean parity error | BUCK/USDC at the end | Desk operations | Return at reference |
|-------+----------+-------------------+----------------------+-----------------+---------------------|
| Basket (isolated) | 0.750 | 4.66% | 1.084 | 0 | +4.38% |
| K moves | 0.950 | 3.85% | 1.062 | 0 | +4.04% |
| Desk | 0.950 | 2.98% | 1.031 | 21 | +4.11% |
| Buyer only | 0.950 | 4.71% | 1.069 | 0 | +3.87% |
| Seller only | 0.682 | 0.99% | 0.983 | 0 | +5.10% |
| Buyer and seller | 0.762 | 0.19% | 1.008 | 0 | +4.53% |
| All actors | 0.762 | 0.19% | 1.008 | 0 | +4.53% |

Without a supplier of BUCK the basket's demand makes BUCK 6% to 8% dear and K runs to its upper
rail. With one, K ends 0.009 above the maintenance run and parity holds. Returns are over the
whole run including the prelude, and include the maintenance baseline of about +0.85%.

The desk feels it too. Across the interactive campaign it operates 209 times, all of them in
the desk stage, where it is the only supplier of BUCK: 69, 66 and 74 operations on the
three-phase, mixed and shock profiles. At the first sizing the whole campaign had 14. With the
private flows present it does not operate at all.

This is not new behaviour, but it is now material. If a fully paired book is not the intended
resting state, a wallet-sourced sale should buy the recipient's TOKEN into its wallet and leave
pairing to the idle placement step, which acts only against credit headroom.

* Limits of this evidence

- One seed per profile, one adverse shape, 120 days.
- The three-phase cycle is far faster and wider than commodity relatives; the commodity profile
  is compressed thirtyfold.
- No caller gas and no unwinding cost. Transfer counts are reported so gas can be applied.
- One arbitrageur, which never competes and holds its profit.
- The older machines have not been run against this one on the same inputs.

cached by their inputs, the simulator's sources and the compiled contracts, so any change to a
contract recomputes everything. =CONTROL_LAB_JOBS= sets the workers.
