#+TITLE: The basket machine: what the lab shows
#+DATE: 2026-10-10
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

This is what we learned by running [[file:BASKET-MACHINE.org][the basket machine]] on the real contracts. The work still to
do is in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-TASKS.org][the task list]]. Every number can be rebuilt with the commands at the end.

* The short version

The basket now makes money from prices that swing apart and come back. It did not before: it
moved too little, too late. With one change to how much it moves, it earns about ten times more.

On realistic prices it earns much less, and the reason is a single threshold. The basket only
rebalances a TOKEN once its share is 2 points off target. Realistic prices rarely push it that
far. At 1 point it earns twice as much.

The BUCK itself held up well. When prices rose 42% in dollars, the BUCK rose with them.

Two things to be careful about. The income from arbitrage is mostly an artifact of how the lab
steps prices. And rebalancing slowly turns the basket's spare TOKEN into BUCKs, which pushes the
BUCK's price up when nobody is issuing new ones.

* How to read the numbers

A run is 120 days with three TOKENs and a basket that starts with one million.

Every gain below is measured against the same basket doing no rebalancing. So +36,808 means the
basket ended 36,808 richer than it would have by sitting still.

We value the basket "at reference": its TOKEN at outside prices, its BUCKs at what the BUCK is
supposed to be worth. We do this because in some runs nothing holds the BUCK's price steady, and
then gains measured in BUCKs and in dollars can point in opposite directions.

No gas is charged. Keep that in mind wherever a result depends on many small trades.

* Rebalancing

The basket sells a TOKEN that has become expensive against another and buys the cheap one. It
waits for evidence that the gap has stopped widening.

The first version waited too long and then moved too little. Over 120 days it moved 36,307
BUCKs out of a book of 1.72 million. The typical trade was 231 BUCKs.

We made the amount it moves a setting, and raised it. Gain over 120 days:

| Prices | Before | Now | Now, whole system running |
|--------+--------+-----+---------------------------|
| Clean cycles | +4,059 | +35,300 | +36,808 |
| Cycles with noise | +3,807 | +34,495 | +39,878 |
| Flat, noisy | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| One TOKEN reprices for good | -232 | -1,265 | -2,074 |
| Commodity-like | +210 | +4,506 | +6,915 |

Three things to notice. On cycles the gain is about 3.7% in 120 days. On flat prices it does
nothing, which is right. And when a TOKEN moves and never comes back, rebalancing loses a
little, because it sold on the way.

For scale: someone holding the same TOKENs in a wallet, rebalancing by a simple rule, could
have made about 8% on the clean cycles. The basket gets less because most of its TOKEN sits in
liquidity pools, which damp how far its shares move.

* The 2-point threshold

This is the limit that matters on realistic prices. Shares must be 2 points off target before
the basket acts. On commodity-like prices that is true 4% of the time. They are more than 1
point off 70% of the time.

| Prices | 2 points | Trades | 1 point | Trades |
|--------+----------+--------+---------+--------|
| Clean cycles | +36,808 | 71 | +42,836 | 469 |
| One TOKEN reprices for good | -2,074 | 4 | -4,196 | 13 |
| Commodity-like | +6,915 | 16 | +13,931 | 94 |

Lowering it to 1 point doubles the gain on realistic prices. It also doubles the loss when a
TOKEN never comes back, and makes five to seven times as many trades. With gas charged, some of
those small trades would not pay. So this is a real choice, not a free improvement.

* Realistic prices

The idea behind the BUCK is that basic commodities follow inflation over the long run, each
with its own delay. We checked this against fifty years of data. Six of eight series stayed
within 0.3% a year of US inflation. Gold ran 1.7% a year ahead. They wander a long way in
between, for years at a time.

So we built a test with three made-up commodities that behave this way: a fast one like gold,
a middle one like energy, a slow one like wages. They share a rising price level and wander
around it independently. One day in the test stands for one month, so a run covers a decade.

Prices rose 42% in dollars over the run. The BUCK followed, staying within 0.4% on average, and
the desk never had to act. That is the main result.

Rebalancing added only 0.7% over the decade. Real prices do not swing far enough, often enough.
On this evidence the basket's job is to hold value through inflation. Rebalancing is a small
bonus unless the things in the basket swing much more than these do.

* Arbitrage

Giving the basket the arbitrage profit looked like the biggest win: +18,739 on clean cycles.
Do not count on it. Nearly all of that profit appears right after the lab moves prices, which it
does in six-hour jumps. With hourly jumps the same profit falls to a fifth. A real market moves
continuously and has competing arbitrageurs.

* A side effect

When the basket rebalances, half of what it buys goes in as BUCKs beside the TOKEN. Over about
90 days this turns its spare TOKEN into BUCKs: it ends up holding 13% of its value in BUCKs.

Those BUCKs have to come from somewhere. Clean cycles, 120 days:

| Who else is in the market | K at the end | BUCK price at the end |
|---------------------------+--------------+-----------------------|
| Nobody | 0.750 | 1.084 |
| K alone | 0.950 | 1.062 |
| K and the desk | 0.950 | 1.031 |
| A seller of BUCKs | 0.682 | 0.983 |
| Buyers and sellers | 0.762 | 1.008 |

With someone issuing BUCKs, all is well. Without, the BUCK becomes 6% to 8% too expensive and K
runs to its limit. We need to decide whether the basket should do this at all.

* What we do not know yet

- How the old basket and desk compare on the same tests.
- What gas does to the results.
- Whether one seed and one bad case are representative.
- How the machine behaves at real speed. The cycles are far faster than real commodities.
