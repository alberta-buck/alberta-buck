#+TITLE: The Alberta Buck sandbox
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil ^:nil

A private, simulated Alberta Buck world in your own browser tab.  Certify people, give them
wallets, insure their (imaginary) homes and cars, activate credit against them, pay one another
and trade BUCKs for dollars -- then look at the chain the way a stranger would, and see what it does
and does not give away.  Nothing leaves the tab.

Everything here is simulated: the identity issuer, the insurer, the dollars, the clock, and every
key, which lives in the page in the clear.  What is *not* simulated is the machinery: the real
Alberta Buck contracts on a real Ethereum virtual machine, the real identity cryptography, and
Uniswap's own contracts for the market.

* Run it

Open [[https://sandbox.albertabuck.ca/]]: the hosted copy is the same static page, built from
=master=, and runs entirely in your tab like a local one.

To run it yourself, with [[https://nixos.org/download][Nix]] (flakes enabled), from a clone of
this repository:

#+BEGIN_SRC sh
make nix-sandbox          # build, then serve http://localhost:8000/
#+END_SRC

Open [[http://localhost:8000/]].  The first build fetches the npm dependencies and compiles the
identity kernel to WebAssembly, which takes a few minutes once; later builds take seconds.  The
page then deploys a fresh world in a second or two.  =SANDBOX_PORT=8080 make nix-sandbox= serves
on another port.

Without Nix you need Node 22 or later, a Rust toolchain with the =wasm32-unknown-unknown= target,
and Foundry's =forge=; then =make sandbox=.  The flake pins all of these, and is the supported
path.

* A first story

1. *Issuer*: /Fill a sample person/ and /Issue credential/, twice -- Carol and Bob.
2. On each card, /New wallet for .../: a wallet is created and registered with the card -- W1 and
   W2.
3. *Wallets*: /Introduce/ W1 to W2.  Bob's wallet now shows Carol's name; Carol's still shows only
   W2 and its address.  Introduce W2 to W1 as well: paying BUCKs between two private wallets takes
   both.  Try paying Bob 100 BUCKs: refused, Carol has none.
4. /Open trading/ on Carol's wallet, then /Buy BUCKs/ with 500 USDC, and pay Bob 100 BUCKs -- and
   12.50 USDC, which needs no introduction at all.
5. *Credit*: insure Carol's home for 400,000 and ask to activate 50,000.  The quote shows the
   premium deposit and her shortfall; /Buy ... BUCKs first/, then /Activate/.
6. /+30 days/ in the world bar: Bob's BUCKs owe a little demurrage, and the home is worth a
   little less.
7. *Observer*: everything that happened, as anyone can see it.  Look for a name.

* The tools

** The world bar

Always in view: the simulated day and date, the block, BUCK's price in the BUCK/USDC pool,
=BUCK_K= (how much credit each unit of insured value supports), the BUCK supply and the insurance
pool.  The clock moves only when you move it: /+1 hour/, /+1 day/, /+30 days/.  /Journal/ lists
every transaction with its outcome and gas -- refusals included, since they are part of the
story.  /Export/ saves the whole world to a file, /Import/ loads one, /Reset/ starts again.

** Issuer

[[file:../images/sandbox/issuer.png]]

The sandbox's identity issuer, trusted on chain by governance.  It checks a person's documents
(here, three form fields) and writes a short /core record/: the name at first certification, the
date of birth, a person number it never reuses, and the date.  It signs the record's fingerprint
and hands over a /credential card/, which you can copy, download, import elsewhere, or turn into a
registered wallet in one click.  Issuance is off-chain: the chain learns nothing.

** Wallets

[[file:../images/sandbox/wallets.png]]

Each wallet is an account with its own keys, starting with 10 ETH for gas and 10,000 USDC.  It is
named by its number, the label you give it (if any) and, once registered, its holder's registered
name, in green: =W1=, =W1: Savings=, =W1: Savings: Carol Nakamura=.

- *Register* with a credential.  On chain this puts a fresh identity key, the identity encrypted
  to it, a re-randomized signature and a zero-knowledge proof -- enough for the registry to know a
  trusted issuer certified the holder, and nothing about who.
- *Introduce* a wallet to another: it re-encrypts its identity for that wallet alone, which can
  then say whom it dealt with -- and learns the registered name.  An introduction goes one way:
  until W1 introduces itself to W2, W2's dropdowns show W1 by number, label and address only.
- *Pay* in BUCKs, USDC or ETH: the amount and both addresses are public.  BUCKs also carry a
  receipt each side can decrypt, so paying BUCKs between two private wallets takes an introduction
  each way, and both must be registered; USDC and ETH go to any wallet, registered or not.
- *Open trading*, once: an introduction to the BUCK/USDC pool (whose operator must be able to say
  who traded with it) and approvals for Permit2, Uniswap's approval contract.  Then *buy* and
  *sell* BUCKs.  The pool takes BUCKs straight from the wallet and pays straight back, so the
  Uniswap router never holds BUCKs and needs no identity of its own.
- *Deposit* more USDC, as a bank transfer would.

Balances show BUCKs held (after demurrage), credit drawn, what is spendable (held BUCKs plus unused
credit), the credit limit, the demurrage owing, USDC and ETH.  Every amount in the sandbox shows six
decimal places, lined up on the decimal point, with its currency in a column of its own.

** Credit

[[file:../images/sandbox/credit-quote.png]]

Sandbox Mutual insures simulated assets.  Pick an owner and an asset -- a home, a vehicle,
equipment, farmland or gold -- and its value; the depreciation schedule and the premium fill in
from the asset class, and every one can be changed.  /Insure/ issues a BuckCredit NFT to the owner.

Activating credit is where the premium comes in, as a deposit rather than a cost: activation
issues ten years' worth of premium to the insurance pool, which invests it -- at an assumed 10 % a
year -- to earn the premiums, and returns it when the insurance is dropped.  At 0.35 % a year on a
home, activating 50,000 BUCKs of spendable credit deposits about 1,813 BUCKs -- and the contract
lets that through only if the owner /already/ holds as much.  So a first-time owner buys the
shortfall in the pool; the quote offers to.  Once one credit is active, its unused credit covers the
next one's deposit.

[[file:../images/sandbox/credit.png]]

The credits table shows each asset's value depreciated to today and what the activated part is
worth now; move the clock and watch them fall on their schedules.

** Observer

[[file:../images/sandbox/observer.png]]

The chain as anyone sees it, Mallory included: every transaction, newest first, with its sender,
contract, function, arguments and events.  Addresses, amounts and credit terms are in the open.
Identity is not: it appears only as keys, ciphertexts and proofs, each marked /opaque/ with a note
on what opening it would take.  No name, birth date or person number appears anywhere -- unless
you tick /Show what only you know/, which labels addresses with the sandbox's own names for them.
Mallory has no such list.

| =BuckCredit=, =Buck=, =BuckKControllerDirect=       |                                        |

| PS signatures, ElGamal, the registration and        | every wallet's keys, kept in the page  |

Not in the sandbox (yet): Notes and their private transfers; the commodity basket and the
controller that moves =BUCK_K= (it stays at 0.75 here -- the equilibrium demo,
=core/js/demo/eqworld.html=, runs that loop); the insurer attestation gate; revocation; receipts.
The Groth16 verifiers in the published contracts use a development trusted setup until v1.0.0.

* Your world

The world saves itself after every action in the browser's own storage (IndexedDB), private to
this page, and comes back when you reload.  /Export/ writes it all -- the chain's state, the
credentials, every wallet's private keys -- to one JSON file; /Import/ loads such a file into any
browser, replacing the current world only if the file loads.  A world saved by an older version
of the sandbox is refused, and a new one started, with a note saying so.  If the browser refuses
storage (some private windows), the world lasts until the tab closes, and the page says so.

* Under the hood

- =core/js/sandbox/= -- the page: =src/app.js= (=SandboxApp=, all the chain logic and none of the
  page), =src/store.js=, =src/main.js=, and =src/ui/= (one module per tool).
- =core/js/src/= -- what it is built from: =buckworld.js= (the ceremonies, whole and by role),
  =market.js= (the BUCK/USDC market through Permit2), =observer.js=, =codec.js=, and the Tevm
  snapshot in =backends.js=.
- Tests (=make nix-core-test-js=): the ceremonies by role, the market and its premiums, the whole
  story through =SandboxApp= with no page, and the built page in headless Chromium
  (=test/sandbox.browser.test.js=; the browser comes from the flake).
- =make nix-sandbox-screenshots= regenerates the pictures above.
- The design and its history: =doc/review/sandbox-plan.org=.
