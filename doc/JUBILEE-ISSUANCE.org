*The core fix is built* (section 8), on branch =fix/jubilee-accounting=:
step 1 of section 7, with the recommended options of 6.1.1-6.1.5.  The
defects were proven by =test/JubileeBasis.t.sol= (commit e177c79, each a

The basket's side (section 4, rulings 6.2) is not built: the basket's
issuance is recorded, and its relief is not yet paid.  6.1.6 (re-judging
the T15 and octl results) is still asked.

The owner's principle (2026-09-29): *the Jubilee relief must equal the
demurrage accrued.*  Demurrage accrues on BUCK in circulation, so relief
may accrue only on BUCK actually issued, never on credit that could be
issued.  Every account carries its own accrued demurrage.  And the
BuckBasket should be a real BuckCredit issuer, earning real relief on the
BUCK it issues, with a BuckCredit overseeing its issuance.

* 1. The defects (proven)

The fund takes in 2%/yr of =totalSupply=.  Relief is meant to pay back
exactly that to the issuers.  Seven places where the two sides part
company today, each pinned by a test in =test/JubileeBasis.t.sol=:

| # | defect                                                                 | test_defect_...                              | effect                                                                     |
|---+------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | relief accrues on activated coverage, drawn or not                      | undrawnCoverageEarnsRelief                   | 1000 activated, 100 drawn: a year's quote is 20, the fund took in 2         |
| 2 | a drawn credit earns on its coverage, not on the BUCK it issued          | drawnCreditEarnsOnCoverageNotIssuance        | fully drawn at K = 0.75: quote / fund = 1/K                                |
| 3 | a quote the fund cannot cover is forfeited, not deferred                 | theFundCapForfeitsRelief                     | the unwind consumes its coverage-seconds; the fund pays a fraction; first come, first paid |
| 4 | aged BUCK that repay a lien repay it in full                             | ageCarriedIntoALienGoesUncollected           | the fee rides into a negative account, where it reads zero                 |
| 5 | a fee locked in a balance is spent into credit as if it were BUCK        | aFeeSpentIntoCreditShrinksTheLien            | 98 held + 52 drawn leaves a lien of 50                                     |
| 6 | burned basket BUCK leave their age on the basket                         | burnedBasketBuckLeaveTheirAgeBehind          | 900 of 1000 burned after a year: the last 100 carry 20%; the next recipient pays it |
| 7 | a transfer that changes the supply does not checkpoint the fund          | supplyChangesDoNotCheckpointTheFund          | a year at 100, then a draw of 900: the fund accrues a year at 1000          |

And by construction: *8. BUCK the basket issues earn no relief at all*
(=mintFromBasket= touches no credit).

Defects 1-3 over-claim the fund; 4-6 under-collect it; 7 mis-accrues it
either way.  1 and 2 are why the relief quote overstates what the fund can
pay; 3 then makes settlement a race.  4 and 5 are the same leak from two
sides: a fee is deferred whenever an account is at or below zero, and
collected only if it ever climbs back.

* 2. The principle, and the identity it rests on

*Every BUCK carries its fee to the end; every issuer earns back the fee its
issuance collected.*

- A fee accrues on each BUCK while it circulates, wherever it goes.  It is
  *realized* -- taken out of circulation -- whenever the BUCK carrying it
  would otherwise shed it: when it repays a lien, when its holder spends
  past it into credit, and when it is burned.
- Relief accrues on each issuer's *outstanding issuance*: an account's lien
  (its negative balance), and the basket's issued BUCK.  It is paid from the
  fund and reduces what closing the issuance costs.

The fund balances because of an identity Buck already maintains.  Only
three things change the sum of the (non-Jubilee) signed balances: the
basket's hooks, relief paid out, and fees realized (under this design).
Premium deposits, refunds and every transfer conserve it.  So

#+BEGIN_SRC text
totalSupply = sum(liens) + basketIssued + reliefRealized - feesRealized
#+END_SRC

and the BUCK /issued/ is

#+BEGIN_SRC text
totalIssued = sum(liens) + basketIssued = totalSupply - reliefRealized + feesRealized
#+END_SRC

computable from two counters that move only on the cold paths.  The fund
accrues 2%/yr on =totalIssued=; relief claims accrue 2%/yr on each issuer's
issuance; the two are equal by construction.  The fees holders owe accrue
on =totalSupply=; the difference, 2%/yr of (relief realized - fees
realized), is demurrage on BUCK whose issuer has already been relieved of
them.  It is second order, it never lets the fund pay relief it did not
accrue, and the invariant test measures it.

* 3. The core fix

** 3.1 The demurrage side: every BUCK carries its fee to the end

- *D1. Checkpoint the fund on every supply change* (defect 7).  A transfer
  that draws credit or repays it calls =_accrueJubilee= before the supply
  moves.  It costs one SLOAD, and the fund's writes once per block.
- *D2. Realize the fee at zero* (defects 4, 5).
  - An account spending past its held BUCK into credit first pays its fee:
    the lien absorbs it (=raw -= fee=, =buckSeconds = 0=).  The lien is then
    exactly the credit drawn.
  - Aged BUCK arriving at an account at or below zero pay their carried fee
    on arrival: they repay =value - fee=.
- *D3. A burn carries out its fee* (defect 6).  =burnFromBasket(amount)=
  realizes the burned BUCK's share of the basket's age
  (=liveBs * amount / raw=), so what remains keeps its own age.
- *The fund accrues on issuance* (section 2): =_accrueJubilee= uses
  =totalSupply - reliefRealized + feesRealized=.

Demurrage stays a lien, never a movement, everywhere else: the hot path is
unchanged for positive accounts.

** 3.2 The relief side: relief on issuance, in Buck

- *R1. The account's age is signed.*  With D2, an account at or below zero
  always holds =buckSeconds = 0= in fee terms.  So while it is negative the
  same field accumulates its /issuance-seconds/, =|raw| x dt=.  One field,
  read by sign: fee-seconds while positive, issuance-seconds while
  negative.  No new storage, and the account slot stays one SSTORE.
  (Delegated demurrage routes only positive rectangles already.)
- *R2. Relief is quoted live and realized at three points*:
  - at the crossing to zero or above (the lien is repaid: its relief pays
    out with it);
  - in =burn= (closing coverage, as today);
  - on a permissionless =settleRelief(a)= poke, like =settleDemurrage=.

  Realizing moves =min(relief, lien, fund)= from the fund into the
  account's raw, and bumps =reliefRealized=.  The views are
  =reliefOf(a)= and =redeemCost(a) = lien - reliefOf(a)=, per account.
  =balanceOf= does not count unrealized relief: the limit is checked
  against the raw lien, as now.
- *R3. The cap stays at the lien*: a position carried ~50 years still
  redeems free, and relief never becomes income beyond it.
- *R4. BuckCredit stops aging.*  =_covSeconds=, the =JUB_= constants,
  =_foldCoverage=, =_covSecondsLive=, =jubileeRelief(tokenId)= and
  =redeemCost(tokenId)= go.  =deactivateFromBuck= returns nothing.
  BuckCredit becomes purely the record of collateral and coverage, and
  still never calls Buck.

With issuance-based relief the fund always covers the outstanding claims
(section 2), so the fund cap stops binding and defect 3 disappears with
defects 1 and 2: settlement is no longer a race.

** 3.3 The invariants (=test/JubileeBasis.t.sol=, inverted)

- The fund equals the outstanding relief claims, to rounding, less relief
  forfeited to the lien cap.
- Every defect test inverts: undrawn coverage earns nothing; a drawn credit
  earns 2%/yr of its lien; a lien repaid with aged BUCK falls by value less
  fee; a burn leaves the survivors' age unchanged; the fund accrues each
  period at that period's issuance.
- =sum(raw) == totalSupply + jubileeActual= holds across every realization.
- A fuzzed handler (draws, repayments, carrying transfers, burns, basket
  hooks, warps) holds all of these.

* 4. The basket as a real BuckCredit issuer

** 4.1 The design: a credit holder, like any other

The first proposal gave the basket a /ledger/ credit that Buck activated
one-for-one with =mintFromBasket=, so BuckCredit's coverage-seconds would
age its issuance.  With the fix, relief lives on the issuer's lien in Buck,
and the natural completion is that *the equity basket is simply a credit
holder*:

- *It is bound public and non-Carrying* (as =SimLP= already is), so it can
  hold a lien.
- *It holds one BuckCredit*, created by Buck when the basket is wired
  (=setBasket=): the basket is its insurer, the premium is zero (no
  deposit; outside the funding-factor gate by the core's own rule), it is
  fully activated and non-transferable.
- *Its value is a mark*: a new valuation mode, =MARKED=, in which the
  credit's present value is the insurer's latest mark (capped by the face)
  instead of a depreciation schedule.  Like depreciation it moves the limit
  without touching the coverage, so "an insurer may not reappraise below
  activated coverage" still holds.  The basket marks its equity at the
  conservative marks at every entry point, before any issuance.
- So =creditLimit(basket) = K x equity= -- ruling 13.3's limit, enforced
  by Buck.

Everything else is Buck's native credit model:

| ruled (BASKET-EQUITY 13.6)                        | as a credit holder                                                          |
|---------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| limit = K x equity                                | =creditLimit(basket)= = K x mark                                            |
| a deposit mints its K                             | the mark rises by the deposit: K x value of headroom, at once                |
| room from K rises and earnings, minted on demand  | headroom, spent when needed                                                  |
| no margin calls                                   | a K cut lowers the limit, never the lien (the system's rule)                 |
| liquidity = BUCK held + headroom                  | =balanceOf(basket)=                                                          |
| debt; =mintFromBasket= / =burnFromBasket=          | the lien: =-signedRawBalanceOf(basket)=; no hooks                            |
| =owed=: an exit's debt share, burned later         | gone: BUCK received repay the lien as they arrive; Trim's target is the lien's band |
| relief: none                                       | native: 2%/yr on the lien, realized daily (=settleRelief=)                  |
| burned BUCK's age                                  | gone: nothing is burned; aged BUCK repaying the lien pay their fee (D2)     |
| held BUCK's demurrage                              | paid in place, as a non-Carrying account                                     |

The equity basket's own books lose =debt=, =idleBuck=, =owed=,
=mintedTotal=, =burnedTotal=, =_mint=, =_burn= and =_settle=; it reads its
lien and its held BUCK from Buck.  Equity is =gross + signedRaw +
reliefOf(basket)=.  Relief accretes to the share price, so every holder
earns it for exactly the time they hold, with no bookkeeping.

*What it is worth.*  At the limit the lien is K/(1+K) of gross.  At K =
0.75 relief is 0.86%/yr of gross, 1.5%/yr of equity -- as much as the
director's lean harvests.

** 4.2 The alternative: the ledger credit

Keep the hooks; Buck activates and deactivates a basket-owned ledger
credit one-for-one with them, and tracks the hooks' issuance-seconds for
relief; the basket stays Carrying, D3 makes its burns carry out their fee,
and its books gain a relief line.  Smaller change to the basket; but the
basket remains a parallel issuer beside the credit model, with its own
debt books, and the NFT carries no relief once relief lives on liens.  I
recommend the credit holder.

** 4.3 The pro-rata baskets

=BuckBasketProRata=, =BuckBasketOps= and =BuckBasketFence= (the sim's
baselines) keep the hooks.  D3 fixes their burns.  Their hook issuance can
earn relief through a Buck-side issuer record, paid into the basket at
=burnFromBasket= and booked to the treasury -- or none, leaving the fund a
small surplus (a ruling, 6.2).

* 6. Rulings asked

** 6.1 The core

1. *Relief on BUCK issued* (the lien), system-wide: undrawn coverage earns
   nothing, a drawn credit earns 2%/yr of its lien, and the quotes move from
   BuckCredit (per credit) to Buck (per account).  (The owner's principle;
   asked to confirm the consequences.)
2. *Every BUCK carries its fee to the end* (D2, D3): realized when it
   repays a lien, when it is spent past into credit, and when it is burned.
3. *Where relief is realized*: at the crossing to zero, at =burn=, and on
   a =settleRelief= poke (recommended); or continuously, which would put a
   fund write on every touch of a negative account.
4. *The fund accrues on issuance* (=totalIssued=), so it always equals the
   claims; the residual (demurrage on already-relieved BUCK) stays with
   the holders.
5. *The cap at the lien* stays ("carried ~50 years, redeems free").
6. *The results that used coverage-based relief*: rerun the T15 retiree
   cells and the octl debtors' close decisions after the fix, or annotate
   them as superseded.

*What changed in =Buck=, piece by piece:*

- *Storage*, appended last: =feesRealized=, =reliefRealized=,
  =basketIssued= (signed), the basket's issuance-seconds and its fold time.
- *=_crystallize=*: the rectangle is =|raw| x dt= either side of zero;
  above zero it is fee-seconds (routed to a payer as before), below it is
  issuance-seconds (never routed).
- *=_debit=* (new; the non-Carrying sender and the minter's deposit): a
  spend within the held BUCK is unchanged; one that reaches past them pays
  the locked fee into the lien first.
- *=_credit=* (new; every receipt): a recipient at or above zero takes the
  BUCK and their age, as before; below zero, the BUCK repay the lien net
  of their fee, and repaying it all pays the lien's relief.
- *=_carryingTransfer=*, *=_nonCarryingTransfer=*: the sender sides are
  unchanged; the recipient sides are =_crystallize= + =_credit=; each
  checkpoints the fund first when it moves the BUCK issued.
- *=_accrueJubilee=* accrues on =totalIssued()=.
- *Relief*: =reliefOf(a)=, =redeemCost(a)=, =settleRelief()= (the
  caller's own), realized in =_burnAllocated= before the solvency check.
- *Basket hooks*: =mintFromBasket= records the issuance and credits like
  any receipt; =burnFromBasket= carries out the burned BUCK's age;
  =basketRelief()= reports what the basket has earned.
- *=BuckCredit=*: the aging is gone (its storage slot kept, retired);
  =deactivateFromBuck= returns nothing.

*Where the build departed from sections 3-4, and why:*

1. *A zero balance is a holder's.*  3.1 said aged BUCK "arriving at an
   account at or below zero" pay their fee on arrival.  At exactly zero
   there is no lien to repay, so the receipt keeps the age as a positive
   account's does (the fee stays a lien in the balance, as before).  Only
   a negative account pays on arrival.
2. *=settleRelief()= is the holder's own.*  3.2 proposed a permissionless
   poke.  Relief accrues linearly on the lien; paying it early shrinks the
   lien it accrues on, so a third party poking daily would compound a
   holder's relief down (from 20% to about 18% over ten years).  The
   holder settles; everything else is realized at repayment or burn.
3. *A basket burn's fee comes out of the burn* (D3).  Taking it on top
   would leave the baskets' own books (=idleBuck=, =treasuryBuckPending=)
   claiming BUCK Buck no longer holds.  Instead the burn of =amount=
   retires =amount - fee= of the basket's recorded issuance: aged BUCK
   retire less than face, as they repay less than face of a lien.  No
   basket code changes; the difference lives in Buck's record of the
   basket's issuance, and the basket's relief, when it is paid (6.2),
   squares it.
4. *The basket's relief is recorded, not paid.*  Paying it needs the
   basket to book it (6.2.4, 6.2.5).  The fund accrues on the basket's
   positive issuance meanwhile, so the relief is there when it is.
5. *A basket that retired more than it issued does not dilute the
   accounts.*  A desk's buy-back burns BUCK it did not issue, so
   =basketIssued= can go negative; the fund's base then counts the liens
   alone (=totalIssued()= adds back the negative part).  That demurrage is
   owed by the basket (=basketRelief= < 0), to be charged when its relief
   is settled.
6. *Relief the fund cannot pay at a crossing is forfeited.*  A positive
   account cannot hold issuance-seconds.  With the fund accruing on
   issuance this binds only if governance has drawn the fund down.
   =settleRelief= and =burn= defer instead.
7. *The core kernels had to follow* (missed in section 5).
   =core/rust/buck-math='s =carrying_transfer= mirrors
   =_carryingTransfer=, and the Rust, Python (PyO3) and JS (wasm) suites
   replay =test/vectors/math-vectors.json= bit for bit.  Two carry rows
   changed (aged BUCK into a lien) and one was added (a crossing that pays
   a year's relief); the vector harness plants a funded Jubilee, as the
   fund covers relief by construction.
8. *Size.*  =Buck= grows from 18,783 to 21,690 bytes: 2,886 left under
   the 24,576 limit (5,793 before).  =BuckCredit= shrinks by 701.  The
   basket's credit creation in =setBasket= (section 4) must fit in that.

*Tests.*  The whole forge suite passes (762 before the fuzz case, from
757).  =test/JubileeBasis.t.sol= now pins every defect inverted, the two
books identities, and =testFuzz_theJubileeBalances=: 32 random draws,
repayments, basket mints, burns and payouts, coverage burns, settlements
and warps per run, the books checked after each (256 runs).  Gas: the

*The pieces:*

- *MARKED credits* (=BuckCredit=): present value = the insurer's latest
  mark (=mark(tokenId, value)=), capped by the face.  Self-issued only
  (insurer == holder) and MARKED from creation or never, because a mark may
  fall below the activated coverage and an insurer marking someone else's
  credit down would be revoking a purchased policy.  Gated in production
  by the insurer envelope's =depTypes= bit, like any depreciation model.
- *Buck* carries no basket code; its identity is =totalSupply = sum(liens)
  + reliefRealized - feesRealized=.  The hooks, the basket's issuance
  record and =basketRelief= live on in =BuckWithBasketHooks=, which only
  the sims and tests deploy (for the pro-rata baselines).
- *The equity basket* holds one MARKED credit (=openCredit=, by
  governance; the face caps its issuance at K x face).  It marks the credit
  at its equity (the exiters' LOW marks, plus the desk's book) before it
  spends -- every deposit, exit and wheel step -- and activates it with an
  ordinary =Buck.mint= at the first mark above zero.  Its debt is its lien;
  relief is collected on Daily and counted, accrued, in the share price.

*Where the build departed from section 4, and why:*

1. *Liquidity is unused credit, so the lien is smaller.*  Before, a deposit
   minted its whole K at once and the wheel held liquidity as idle BUCK.
   Now nothing is issued until the wheel places it, and liquidity is the
   credit left unspent: placing 300k of BUCK leaves a lien of about 110k,
   not the old 225k of debt.  Same economics (idle BUCK paid demurrage, an
   issued lien earns relief), less issued.  A K cut therefore has to be
   deeper to put the basket under water.
2. *After a K cut, Trim deleverages to the new limit.*  An account below
   zero cannot hold BUCK -- what it receives repays the lien -- so its
   liquidity comes back only once the lien is under K x equity.  Trim's
   refill (liquidity below its floor) now includes the amount the basket
   is over its limit, paced at 2 x =swapCapBp= of a pool a step; until
   then, exits pay in kind.  This buys BUCK when a K cut says it is weak,
   which is the controller's intent; the alternative (no refill while under
   water, exits in kind until equity or K recover) is one line of
   =_trimPlan=.  *For the owner to confirm.*
3. *An exit is marked first.*  =_exit= marks the credit at the equity that
   stays (=eLow - pay=) and pays in BUCK only if Buck then lets the account
   spend it -- the ruled "K x (E - pay)" test, enforced by Buck.
4. *The in-kind exit's two corners.*  Short of its lien share (leverage
   above one), it leaves TOKEN behind worth the shortfall at the LOW marks.
   Owed more BUCK than an under-water account can spend, the rest stays in
   the receipt as shares at the post-exit price: the exit completes, and no
   claim moves ahead of another's.  No exit charge in kind (nothing is
   converted).
5. *The desk is a sub-account.*  Its BUCK is explicit counters
   (=monetaryBuckHeld=, =monetaryOutstanding=) inside the shared account;
   equity's BUCK is the account less that position.  Its issuance spends
   the basket's credit, bounded by what the account can spend beyond the
   liquidity target; its purchases book the BUCK that actually arrived
   (their fee on arrival is the desk's, not equity's); and it keeps its own
   relief accumulator on its own issuance, folded at every desk swap -- a
   share of the account's relief by the current split misattributed relief
   whenever the split moved.
6. *The facet reads the desk by a self-call* (=deskPosition=): it runs
   its own code under delegatecall and cannot see the shell's overrides.
7. *A bug found and fixed in the build:* the in-kind exit valued what
   stays, and marked the credit, from the snapshot taken before its
   positions were burned -- overstating the remaining equity by the burned
   positions, shorting the exiter's left-over shares and, worse, marking
   the credit above its collateral.  It now reads fresh; a test pins the
   post-exit mark to the equity that stays.
8. *The suites run on the real Buck, BuckCredit and the identity
   harness*, in 6-decimal BUCK (the mock ERC-20 could show no lien, limit,
   relief or fee).  Placement's weight tolerance is one capped Fund step
   (500 bp): with no director, Fund places only while the spare passes
   half the target.
9. *Size.*  =BuckBasketEquityOps= is 24,131 bytes, 445 under the limit.
   The next addition to the shell or the desk must first move code to the
   components facet (=_exitInKind= is the natural candidate; the facet has
   13 KB to spare).

** 9.0 The plan, as written before the work

The owner asked to remove the orphaned slots and put the rest in a sensible
order.  Nothing is deployed, so layouts may change; what can break is
anything that reaches a slot by *number* rather than by name -- a
=vm.store= / =vm.load= at a hard-coded index, or a mapping slot computed as
=keccak256(key, N)=.  The compiler cannot see those: a moved slot makes
them read and write the wrong place, silently.

** 9.2 The procedure

1. *Name every positional access.*  One test helper (=test/harness/BuckSlots.sol=)
   holds each slot number once, as a named constant, with the slot
   arithmetic for its mappings; every file above calls it instead of
   spelling a number.  A layout test pins each constant against the real
   layout: write through the public API, =vm.load= the helper's slot,
   compare.  After this step a slot move changes one file, and a wrong
   constant fails a test instead of corrupting one.
2. *Remove orphans, one at a time*: BuckCredit's =__retiredCovSeconds= first.
   After each removal, renumber every access to a slot above it down by
   one (with step 1, in the helper alone), run the whole suite, and diff
   =forge inspect <contract> storageLayout= against the table above.
3. *Reorder, one move at a time*, into groups a reader can follow:
   accounts (=_state=, =_totalSupply=, =_allowances=, =_receiptFragments=),
   credit bookkeeping (=mintsBacked=, =mintsPrincipal=), the Jubilee
   (=_jubileeLastUpdate=, =feesRealized=, =reliefRealized=), delegated
   demurrage (=demurragePayer=, =demurragePayerRequest=, =sponseeCount=).
   Each move: update the helper, run the suite, diff the layout.  The
   comments that explain today's contortions ("housed here, out of order,
   to reclaim the slot...") go with them.
4. *The equity basket's layout* (=BuckBasketEquityStorage=, shared by the
   shell and both facets through inheritance) is compiler-derived and
   reached by number nowhere; it was rewritten in the credit-holder change.
5. Record the final layouts in this section.
