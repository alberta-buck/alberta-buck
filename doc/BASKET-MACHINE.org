* Overview

The equity basket is one Buck credit holder (=BuckBasketEquity=) and the
monetary desk is a second (=EquityDesk=). Each has its own account and its
own marked =BuckCredit=. The wheel paper describes a work wheel per machine.
What this tree actually deploys is one wheel. =alberta_buck/sim/deploy.py=,
for =equity= and =equity-ops=, deploys one =BasketWheel=, then =setWheel=
and =setEquity= on the basket. It never deploys a second wheel and never
calls =setOps= on =EquityDesk=. =EquityDesk= has no wheel. The desk runs
because =MonetaryKeeperAgent= calls =monetaryOperation=
(=alberta_buck/sim/director_agent.py=).

What the two machines also do not have is one signal. The basket's turn
detector (=EquityTurnDirector=) and the desk's common-mode reading
(=PairsRebalanceDirector.commonMode= / =monetaryEffort=, the sim's =mk_cm=)
are two filter banks, updated by two different callers, and neither answers
the other's question.

On the basket side the response is starved. =deposit= books a TOKEN deposit
as =idleToken= and does not mint a position. =_fundPlan= and =_trimPlan=
then return =(NONE, 0)= while any pool's idle TOKEN is above the grain.
Deploy is not under that veto: it is due when that pool's idle TWAP value
exceeds the grain, and it can clear the idle by at most =swapCapBp= (1% of
pool BUCK depth) per step. Fund and Trim therefore do not run on a basket
that is still receiving deposits, including a leg the leash has already
released. Section 14 of =doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= records the savings world
placing 1.49M BUCK of deposits in 45 days with Fund and Trim never once due,
and with legs past the 30% leash (cbBTC 13.8% vs 6%, PAXG 15.3% vs 8%, LABR
19.0% vs 24%).

This design splits the work into two machines, and names the contract
boundary between them.

Machine A is the signal. It samples pool TWAP ticks once a day, keeps one
differential ladder per leg and a separate common-mode ladder, and publishes
readings. It holds no TOKEN, no LP, and no BUCK, and it does not set =K=.
Machine B is the basket's response. It reads those per-leg readings plus how
far each pool's response weight sits from its leaned target, and it does one
paced action: sell one pool and buy another, sell only, buy only, or hold.
The step is specified below as balance deltas. A buy draws credit headroom
under =K= times the mark and mints liquidity on the existing full-range
position. A sell unwinds liquidity and sells TOKEN; the BUCK that comes out
is measured as the increase in =balanceOf=. Idle TOKEN is inventory that
action may use. It is not a state that disables the machine.

The credit mark is a third, smaller step. A Respond that trades marks on the
opening snap, then again from a new =_snap()= after the sell is booked, and
again from a new =_snap()= after the buy is booked. A second =_markS= on the
opening snap is not a re-mark: =s.m= and =s.lien= stay at their pre-trade
values, and =_markS= refreshes only =s.spend=. A Respond with nothing to
trade marks when =|equity(LOW) - markOf| > grain=. The owner's equity is
"the total value of all TOKEN and BUCK in all the pools, minus the BUCK
issued into existence against the basket's BuckCredit NFT." The code's
=_equityS= is wider than that sentence. The difference (idle wallet TOKEN,
the wallet's BUCK, relief, owed fees, LOW versus TWAP) is written down below
as a decision. Including idle TOKEN in the mark is a confirmation of today's
=_equityS=, not a new formula.

* Background & Motivation

** What is built

=BuckBasketEquity= is the shell. It is not proxied. =setEquityWheel=,
=setWheel=, and =setEquityDirector= replace the facet, the wheel, and the
director. They do not replace the shell. =BuckBasketEquityWheel= is the
components facet, reached by delegatecall for =wheelDue= / =wheelStep=.
Kinds, fixed in =EquityKind._equityOf= (=src/wheel/UpkeepKinds.sol=), are
Daily (0), Sync × N (1), Deploy × N (2), Fund (3), Trim (4): =2N + 3= slots.
=WorkWheel.tick= walks them round-robin and skips a slot that is not due. A
bare revert inside =_run= aborts the tick without advancing the cursor; the
try/catch lives in the kind, not in =tick=.

=BasketWheel='s base order is the slot order (=src/wheel/BasketWheel.sol=):
Compute, Director, Sweep, Ops, Equity, then Arb. A kind whose target is
unset has no slots. On the equity deployment Ops is unset, so =OpsKind=
contributes zero slots. The equity basket has no =monetaryOperation=; the
shell's fallback delegatecalls the venue and reverts, and =OpsKind._run=
swallows that revert. =BasketWheelAgent= calls
=setOps(d.basket, d.director)= only when the kinds list contains ="ops"=
(=alberta_buck/sim/basket_wheel_agent.py=). The equity wheel's kinds list
does not.

=EquityTurnDirector= is a separate contract the basket names with
=setEquityDirector=. It holds no balances. The basket's Daily step calls
=observe()=; anyone else may too. The desk does not call it and does not
read =mayTrim= / =mayFund=.

=EquityDesk= inherits =MonetaryDesk= and is its own credit holder
(=doc/DESK-LP-RESERVE.org= states the same; =doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §13.8
recorded an earlier mixin shape, =BuckBasketEquityOps=, which is not in this
tree). =monetaryOperation= requires =IMonetaryDirector.epochNow()= and
stores =lastMonetaryEpoch = e + 1= before it reads =monetaryEffort()=
(=src/basket/MonetaryDesk.sol=). Zero effort reverts =NoAdvice=. That effort
is produced today by =PairsRebalanceDirector._updateMonetary=, from a ladder
of =log(basketValueInBuck)= against par. The sim snapshots that rung as
=mk_cm=. =MonetaryKeeperAgent.act= reads
=d.director.functions.commonMode(2)=, not =monetaryDirector=.
=DirectorKeeperAgent= does not poke equity baskets. The keeper pokes the
pairs director itself; the comment dated 2026-09-30 records that before that
poke the equity desk read an unsampled director and got =NoAdvice= every
day.

=MonetaryDesk= is the mixin of =EquityDesk= and of the pro-rata ops basket
(=BuckBasketOps=). Editing =monetaryOperation= couples both. The desk is
re-pointed through an adapter that implements today's =IMonetaryDirector=.
The mixin is not edited.

** Why rebalancing does not run

=_fundPlan= (=BuckBasketEquityWheel=) returns =(NONE, 0)= when
=_anyTokenWaiting= is true: any pool with =_tokenValue(s, i) > _grainS(s)=.
=_grainS= is =gross(TWAP) * grainPpm / 1e6=, and =grainPpm= is 100, so 0.01%
of gross, with a floor of =1e3= raw units. =_trimPlan= returns none on the
same test, and also when =_fundPlan= itself returns a pool. =mayTrim= is
consulted only after both vetoes, inside the loop that picks a pool. A leg
past the leash never gets that far while any idle TOKEN exceeds the grain.
Deploy is not inside that veto.

The veto stays closed for three reasons, all in the current facet:

1. =deposit= (=BuckBasketEquity.deposit=) does =idleToken[idx] += amount=
   for TOKEN and then =_markS=. It does not mint. A depositor population
   keeps some pool over the grain on most days (=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org=
   §14.1). The mark already includes the deposit, because =_grossS= reads
   =idleToken= from storage and =_markS= runs after the increment.
2. Deploy is paced. =_deploy= pairs pool =i='s idle TOKEN with that pool's
   pro-rata share of =_usableS= (spend above the liquidity target) and, if
   still short of BUCK, sells TOKEN of at most =_swapCap= =
   =depth * swapCapBp / 10000= (100 bp, 1% of the pool's virtual BUCK depth,
   all LPs). =positionMint= takes the scarcer side (=_liquidityForAmounts=
   is the min of the two amounts; one side zero returns 0 liquidity). A
   deposit that is large against its pool stays above the grain for many
   steps.
3. Sync writes fee TOKEN back into =idleToken= (=_sync=:
   =idleToken[i] += t=). Fund does the same on purpose: =_fund= swaps half
   the planned BUCK for TOKEN and leaves it in =idleToken= for a later
   Deploy, which re-closes the gate. Trim can too: TOKEN that =monetaryLeg=
   does not sell is added back to =idleToken=.

There is a second ordering even after the wallet is empty. While Fund has a
plan, Trim does not run. A basket with spare credit and a weight gap funds
and does not trim. The wheel paper's sentence that a leg more than 30% off
its declared weight trades regardless (=alberta-buck-ethereum-wheel.org=,
"The director") is true of =EquityTurnDirector.mayTrim= only. It is not true
of =_trimPlan=.

Section 7 of =doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= is the prototype, where the same idea
is named "settled": Rebalance is due only when nothing worth placing waits
in the wallet. The Solidity gate is the descendant of that rule. It is the
rule this design removes. Section 14.2 mitigations 1 and 2 (a per-pool gate,
and letting the leash through) are a patch to that rule. They are not this
design. Mitigation 4, placing a deposit's value toward the leaned targets,
is adopted below as part of the response, with the signal gate kept.

** Why the signal is not already shared

=alberta-buck-ethereum-wheel.org=, "One filter bank, two modes", splits one
vector of log prices:

#+begin_example
differential:  c_i - mean(c)        which commodity is rich against which
common:        log(basket in BUCK)  what the whole basket costs, against par
#+end_example

The differential mode is the director's signal. The common mode is the
desk's signal and the K controller's process variable. The same section, and
=PairsRebalanceDirector.commonMode=, record why the common mode is not the
mean of the legs: an equal-weighted mean of log moves drifted from the
basket by +1,000 to +1,700 bp over two years (=doc/CONVERGENCE.org= 5.1,
cited in that function's notice). The common mode is a ladder of
=basketValueInBuck()= against par. It is not =equity()=.

The two ladders are not one window vector. =EquityTurnDirector= uses
=[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 1280]=, last rung the anchor.
=PairsRebalanceDirector.WINDOWS= is =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320]=. Index 2
is 20 on both, which is why the deployed measuring rung matches. Sharing the
array would retune every other common-mode EMA, and 1280 is not the common
mode's long window.

=EquityTurnDirector.observe= implements only the differential half, as a
deviation from the legs' mean. It has no =basketValueInBuck= ladder, no
deadband, and no persistence counter. Its =turned= is a quorum of velocities
toward the anchor. The desk must not use that test for its outright
quadrants. The wheel paper ("The four quadrants") and
=PairsRebalanceDirector._updateMonetary= both say persistence is a duration
past the leash, because a velocity test changes sign on noise and, in the
earlier model, never once reported "persisting", leaving Q2 and Q4 inert.

So the existing director is the basket's half of the filter bank. It is not
the machine both consumers can read. The desk's half is a different
contract, with a different window vector, poked by the keeper on equity-ops
(and by =DirectorKind= on a pro-rata wheel). It still also computes a
pairwise differential the equity basket does not use.

* Goals & Non-Goals

** Goals

- Document the machines that exist, including the starvation transition,
  before describing the ones proposed.
- One signal contract, read by the basket per leg and by the desk on the
  common mode, matching the paper's two modes without collapsing the two
  turn tests into one boolean.
- A basket response that sells, buys, or transfers weight in one paced step,
  written as balance deltas, sized by the expressions in §5, and that treats
  a deposit as equity and as inventory.
- Recompute equity from prices and write the mark on the rule in §6, so
  headroom tracks the book, including on a price move that does not trade.
- Remove the global idle-TOKEN veto. A deposit or a newly minted position
  increases what the response can do. It cannot switch the response off.
- Keep one credit holder for the basket, one for the desk, and none for the
  signal. Keep the no-margin-call rule. Do not move =K= into the signal.

** Non-goals

- Redesigning the desk's four quadrants, its three bounds (=maxLegBp= 40,
  =maxPositionBp= 1000, =maxOutrightBp= 1000, =enabled= false until
  =setOpsParams=), or its book (=monetaryBuckHeld=, =monetaryTokenHeld=,
  =monetaryOutstanding=).
- A new AMM, or the better-route swap (TOKEN/USDC then BUCK/USDC). That
  route is named in =doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §13.8 departure 2 as not built.
  Pacing stays on the basket's own pools, capped, with the wheel's existing
  arbitrage re-pinning them between steps.
- Changing redemption, the 25% treasury cut, the entry charge, or Jubilee
  relief.
- Changing =ArbKind='s cycle or its credit call. =creditDepositors=
  (=BuckBasketEquity.creditDepositors=) still does =idleToken[i] += amount=.
  Its comment says Deploy places that TOKEN. After Deploy is gone the
  capture is response inventory and the response may sell it out of the pool
  it was captured in. "Arb unchanged" means the kind's code, not the pool
  the TOKEN ends in.
- Fitting Bertram thresholds. Quorum stays 4 of 6 and =leashBp= stays 3000,
  both ruled by the owner on 2026-09-30. This design does not retune them.
  The band-versus-leash rule is the one in §5.
- Starting, restarting, or retargeting experiments E2--E4, including the
  equity twins running outside this worktree. This document authorizes no
  campaign.

** Contract boundary

The owner's suggestion is a third wheel-based contract whose only job is to
look for signals in the pricing noise, which both the desk and the basket
read. Evaluated against the code, the suggestion is right about the contract
and wrong about a private wheel.

It is right about the contract because the two readings already live in two
contracts, and the basket's contract is missing the reading the desk uses.
Extending =EquityTurnDirector= and deploying that new bytecode at its own
address gives one sample and two views, without forking a second
differential ladder and without leaving the desk on
=PairsRebalanceDirector=. A "satellite the desk is also allowed to read"
would mean pointing =monetaryDirector= at today's =EquityTurnDirector=. That
contract cannot answer cheap/dear: it never reads =basketValueInBuck=, and
its =turned= is the velocity quorum the desk's outright quadrants were built
to avoid.

It is wrong about a private wheel because =observe= is already
permissionless and idempotent per day, and the catch-up is closed form
(=e' = x + (e - x) q^n=, gap capped at 3650). The sample captures nothing,
so a third =WorkWheel= would need a third reserve to pay a caller, or it
would not be called. =DirectorKind= already shows the pattern the basket
side uses: a slot on an existing wheel calls a contract that holds no funds,
and =_run= try/catches the call the way =DirectorKind= try/catches =poke=.
The signal gets one =SampleKind= slot on the one =BasketWheel=, placed after
=ArbKind= so arbitrage triangle indexes do not move, and only when a signal
address is set. An unset signal adds zero slots, the existing "unset kind
has no slots" rule.

The desk side has no wheel to put a slot on. =deploy.py= does not deploy
one, and =EquityDesk= is not an =OpsKind= target. =MonetaryKeeperAgent=
calls =signal.observe()= and then =monetaryOperation=. That is the sample. A
day on which the basket wheel is idle is still sampled when the keeper runs,
and =observe='s catch-up repairs a gap in one call, which is the property
the wheel paper states under "Time without a scheduler." The signal contract
holds no reserve and no credit.

What the signal owns:

- The daily sample of each constituent's TWAP tick, oriented as BUCK per
  TOKEN (=EquityTurnDirector._tick=, the same orientation as today).
- Per leg, the differential ladder on windows
  =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 1280]=: deviation from the mean, seven EMAs,
  seven velocities, the 1280-day anchor, the excursion, the vote count,
  =turned= / =running=.
- The common-mode ladder on its own windows =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320]=,
  copied from =PairsRebalanceDirector.WINDOWS=. EMAs of
  =log(basketValueInBuck())= against par, the measuring rung, the deadband
  comparison, the persistence counter. The two vectors are not aliased.
  Index 2 is 20 on both by coincidence.
- The sample clock (=lastDay=) and the published views below. The signal's
  day is =uint32(block.timestamp / 1 days)=, the day =observe= already uses.

What the signal must not own:

- TOKEN, LP, BUCK, a =BuckCredit=, a mark, or =K=. No =openCredit=, no
  =transfer=, no approval.
- =idleToken=, =liquidityOf=, the lien, or the response's step size in BUCK.
  It publishes strength (vote count, excursion, common-mode magnitude). The
  consumer sizes the trade.
- The desk's quadrant. =kappa= and =capBpPerEpoch= live on the adapter, not
  on the signal and not inside =MonetaryDesk=. The basket's lean and leash
  live on the basket. Today =mayTrim=, =mayFund=, =targetsBp=, and
  =_leashed= live inside =EquityTurnDirector= and read =weightsBp()= and
  =targetWeightBp= off the basket. They move to the basket. The new basket
  calls =IFilterBank= and does not call =mayTrim= / =mayFund=. The old
  deployment keeps those three functions so a basket still pointed at it
  does not break.
- The decision that the common mode has "turned" in the velocity-quorum
  sense. The desk's reverting/persistent bit stays a duration past the
  leash, computed in =observe= from the common-mode rung, and published as a
  flag. It is not =EquityTurnDirector.turned=.

The basket pulls the sample out of Daily's exclusive ownership. Daily may
still call =observe= (it is idempotent). It must not be the only caller. The
basket reads =running=, =votes=, and =excursion=, and applies the leash and
the lean itself, because both compare against the basket's weights and its
declared targets.

The desk reads the common-mode view through an adapter that implements
today's =IMonetaryDirector=: =epochNow()= is the signal's day, and
=monetaryEffort()= is built from =monetaryReading()= with the adapter's
=kappa= and cap. =MonetaryDesk.monetaryOperation= is not edited. Its
quadrant branches stay. The desk does not read =targetsBp=, =mayFund=,
=mayTrim=, or per-leg =turned=.

=epochNow= on =RebalanceDirectorBase= is
=(block.timestamp - genesisTime) / epochSeconds=. A calendar-day sample
equals that epoch only while =epochSeconds= is 86400 and =genesisTime= is
aligned to a day boundary. Deployed pairs directors use =epochSeconds= 86400
(=deploy.py=). The adapter does not assume the equality in general. Its
=epochNow= is the signal's day, the same clock =observe= uses. This design
does not =setOps= the equity desk. If a later deployment does, that
=OpsKind= epoch source is the adapter, so the desk does not carry two
clocks. Leaving =opsDirector= on the pairs director while =monetaryDirector=
is the adapter is the two-clock bug.

=basketValueInBuck()= on the venue (=BuckBasketUniswapV3.basketValueInBuck=)
is the declared basket amounts times TWAP prices, the controller's process
variable, centred near =1e18= at par. It is not =equity()=. Marking the
monetary signal off equity would fold the lien into cheap/dear: a draw would
look like a move in BUCK. The common mode stays =log(basketValueInBuck)=
against par, in tick·1e9, the same =_logTick1e9= the pairs director uses.
Positive =cm= means the basket costs more BUCK, so BUCK is cheap. The
shell's fallback already sends =basketValueInBuck= to the venue, so the
signal reads it from the basket address the same way
=PairsRebalanceDirector= reads =IBasketValue=.

** 1. Current signal: =EquityTurnDirector=

Purpose.* Once a day, turn each leg's TWAP into a lean and two gates.

Callers: the basket's Daily step (=_daily= calls =IEquityDirector.observe=),
or anyone. The gates are consulted by =_mayFund= / =_mayTrim= during Fund
and Trim planning. The director never moves tokens.

State it owns.* =primed=, =lastDay=, and per leg =deviation=, =_ema[7]=,

=_vel[7]=. Parameters: =windows = [5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 1280]= days (last
is the anchor), =quorum = 4=, =tiltWad = 1e18=, =leashBp = 3000=. No
balances.

Inputs and the mark.* For each pool, =IV3Observe.observe= over

=basket.twapWindow()=; on failure, =slot0='s spot tick. The tick is negated
when =buckIsToken0= so the sign is BUCK per TOKEN. The input is a TWAP tick
when the pool can report one, otherwise spot. There is no LOW/HIGH in this
contract. The sample then subtracts the equal-weighted mean of those ticks.
That mean is the differential's numeraire cancellation. It is not the desk's
common mode.

Transitions.*

| From                                         | When               | Effect on the ladder                                                                                                                                                    | Effect on idle TOKEN, positions, lien, mark |
|----------------------------------------------+--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| Unprimed                                     | =observe=, any day | Each EMA seeded to today's deviation =x=; velocity 0; =primed = true=                                                                                                   | None                                        |
| Primed, same day                             | =today <= lastDay= | Return. Do nothing                                                                                                                                                      | None                                        |
| Primed, gap of =n= days (=n= capped at 3650) | =today > lastDay=  | For each rung, =e' = x + (e - x) q^n= and =v' = a q^(n-1) (x - e)=, with =a = 2/(w+1)= and =q = 1 - a=. One day apart this is the plain step =e += (x - e) * 2 / (w+1)= | None                                        |

=turned=, =running=, =targetsBp=, =mayTrim=, and =mayFund= are views over
that state. They are not transitions and they do not write.

A leg's reading, once primed:

- Excursion =e = deviation - ema[anchor]=. Zero excursion is not a turn
  (=turned= returns false when =e == 0=).
- Each of the six shorter rungs votes when its velocity points back toward
  the anchor: =v < 0= while =e > 0=, or =v > 0= while =e < 0=.
  =votes >= quorum= is a turn. A leg not turned is still running
  (=alberta-buck-ethereum-wheel.org=, "The director": "A leg not turned is
  still running, and a run is let run").
- =running(i, +1)= is excursion above the anchor and not turned (still
  running up). =running(i, -1)= is the mirror (still running down).
- =mayTrim= is =!running(i, +1)= or the weight leash. =mayFund= is
  =!running(i, -1)= or the leash. The leash reads =basket.weightsBp()=
  against =targetWeightBp=: over-weight if
  =w > declared + declared * leashBp / 10000=, under-weight if
  =w + declared * leashBp / 10000 < declared=. =weightsBp()= sums =posTwap=
  only. Idle TOKEN is invisible to the leash.
- =targetsBp= scales each declared weight by =exp(-tilt * excursion)= (the
  excursion converted through =ln(1.0001)=) and renormalizes. That is the
  lean. It is a target, not a gate.
- =_mayFund= returns false when =equityDirector= is unset. Fund then runs
  only through the parking fallback. The proposed response does not keep
  that fallback, and its unset-signal behaviour is stated in §5 so it is not
  read as this one.

#+begin_src mermaid
stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Unprimed
  Unprimed --> Seeded: observe seeds every rung to x, vel = 0
  Seeded --> Seeded: same day, observe returns
  Seeded --> Updated: a later day, catch-up e' = x + (e-x) q^n
  Updated --> Reading
  state Reading {
    [*] --> Flat: excursion = 0
    Flat --> RunningUp: excursion > 0 and votes < quorum
    Flat --> RunningDown: excursion < 0 and votes < quorum
    RunningUp --> Turned: votes >= quorum
    RunningDown --> Turned: votes >= quorum
    Turned --> RunningUp: votes fall below quorum, excursion still > 0
    Turned --> RunningDown: votes fall below quorum, excursion still < 0
    RunningUp --> Flat: excursion returns to 0
    RunningDown --> Flat: excursion returns to 0
  }
#+end_src

The do-nothing transition is the same-day return, and also a reading of
Flat, or of Running in the direction the gate forbids. Neither writes the
basket.

** 2. Current basket wheel

Purpose.* Place deposits, collect fees, and (when the veto allows) move

weight. Caller: =BasketWheel= via =EquityKind._run=, which delegatecalls
=wheelStep(kind, i)= on the shell. =wheelStep= reverts unless
=msg.sender == wheel=. A user never calls a component. =deposit= and
=redeem= are the shell's verbs and are not wheel kinds.

State it owns.* On the shell, through the shared storage in

=BuckBasketEquityStorage=: =idleToken=, =liquidityOf=, =flowMs=, =dayFlow=,
=lastDay=, =lastSyncDay=, =creditId=, =creditLive=, the receipt books, and
=eq=. The lien is not a basket variable. It is
=max(0, -signedBalanceOf(basket))= at =Buck=. The mark is
=BuckCredit.markOf(creditId)=.

Inputs and the mark.* =_snap= reads every pool's

=marks(i, liquidityOf[i])=, =K= from =controller.currentBuckK()=, and the
account. =IBuckBasketVenue.Marks= carries =posLow= / =posTwap= / =posHigh=,
the three prices, and =depth=. Position value is twice the BUCK side of the
basket's own full-range liquidity at that price
(=BuckBasketUniswapV3.marks=: =posTwap = 2 * _buckSideAt(...)=). Spot and
TWAP are both read; HIGH is the greater position value, LOW the lesser. TWAP
is the price input to gross for the grain and the liquidity target. LOW is
the price input to the mark.

The equity step the components already run.* Deploy, Fund, and Trim call

=_markS= before spending. =_markS= marks at =_equityS(s, MARK_LOW)= and
re-reads =balanceOf= into =s.spend=. Daily marks at the end. Sync does not
mark. =_markAt= writes =credit.mark= when the value changed. The first time
the value is above zero it calls =Buck.mint(type(uint256).max, [creditId])=
once and sets =creditLive=. That mint activates the whole face (zero
premium, so no deposit). Later marks do not mint. =Buck.creditLimit= is
=totalCurrentValue * currentBuckK / 1e18=. For a MARKED credit
=depreciatedFaceValue= is =min(markOf, face)= (=BuckCredit.currentValue=,
=BuckCredit.mark=). =balanceOf= for this non-Carrying account is held BUCK
plus unused credit (=creditLimit - lien=) (=Buck.balanceOf=). Incoming BUCK
that only repays a lien increases =balanceOf= by the full amount when the
lien was already inside the limit, because a smaller lien frees an equal
amount of credit. When the lien is still over the limit after the receipt,
unused credit stays zero and =balanceOf= does not rise. =_deploy= records
that case: after the sale it clamps =have= to =balanceOf= ("under water:
nothing"). Spending past held BUCK is what issues. One account. A mark
change moves the limit because =creditLimit= reads =markOf= live. It does
not open a second account, and it does not call the lien back. =K= moving
changes =creditLimit= on the next =Buck= read with no NFT write. The lien
does not move. There is no margin call.

=_fundPlan='s veto and the parking fallback are part of the current machine,
so they are stated exactly. After the waiting-TOKEN veto and the
=usable > g= test, the plan picks the largest gap past =weightBandBp= (200

exceeds the parking room (=parkBp= 30000, that is 3× the liquidity target;
=bandBp= if no director is set), it picks the largest gap *without*
=mayFund=. That fallback can buy a leg the director says is still running
down. The new response does not keep it.

=_trimPlan='s real step, when it is allowed to exist, refills liquidity
below =target * (1 - bandBp)= (and adds the underwater shortfall =-headroom=
when headroom is negative), or unwinds the director's overweight leg. The
size is =min(need, pos * stepBp / 10000, 2 * swapCap)=. =stepBp= is 500. The
factor of two is because the TOKEN half of the unwind is what gets sold.
That size is the precedent the response keeps, typed out in §5.

larger of =floorBp= (100, 1% of gross at TWAP) and a flow target =z=
standard deviations of daily net flow times =(1+K)=, capped at =ceilBp=
(2000, 20% of gross). =_headroomS= is =s.k * markOf / 1e18 - lien=. That
target is the BUCK kept so exits can be paid. It is not, in this design, a
reason to refuse every trade.

whatever was deposited, and never runs the two kinds that would move it
back.

Under water (=_headroomS < 0=, lien above =K * mark= by the division above),

not due. Trim's refill is the path that sells and repays the lien, at most

cannot pay go in kind (=_exit= / =_exitInKind=). That split --- buys stop,
sells that repay may continue, no margin call --- is the ruled behaviour
(=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §13.3, =doc/JUBILEE-ISSUANCE.org= 8b.2). The veto
blocking the refill is not part of the ruling. The response keeps the split
and drops the veto.

** 3. Current desk, only as far as the signal

Purpose.* One monetary operation per director epoch, on the common mode.

Caller on equity-ops: =MonetaryKeeperAgent=, which =pokeAll=s =d.director=
and then calls =monetaryOperation= on =d.desk=. =OpsKind= is not that caller
in this deployment. =monetaryOperation= reads
=IMonetaryDirector.epochNow()=, stores =lastMonetaryEpoch = e + 1= before
reading effort, and reverts =NoAdvice= when effort is 0. =EquityDesk= marks
its *own* credit at =netValue()= before it trades (=_mark= from
=_swapAcross= and from =_deskIssue=). Nothing it holds is in the basket's
=_equityS= (invariant D2 in the contract notice and in the wheel paper, "A
separate machine"). =_deskNav= is =basket.equity()=, which is
=_equity(MARK_TWAP)=, and that sizes =maxPositionBp= and =maxOutrightBp=. It
is not the basket's credit mark. =_mark=, =netValue=, and =_deskNav= stay as
they are.

State it owns.* =opsParams=, =monetaryBuckHeld=, =monetaryTokenHeld=,

=monetaryOutstanding=, =lastMonetaryEpoch=. The signal it acts on is *not*
its state. =effortBp= and =outright= are stored on =PairsRebalanceDirector=
(=monEffortBp=, =monOutright=, =monOverEpochs=).

Inputs and the mark.* =monetaryEffort()= returns =(effortBp, outright)=.

Sign: =effortBp < 0= means BUCK is cheap (common mode at =measIdx= is
positive: the basket costs more than a BUCK). =effortBp > 0= means BUCK is
dear. =outright= is =monOverEpochs >= persistEpochs=. The rung is warmed for
=WINDOWS[measIdx]= epochs before any effort is published; until then the
effort is 0 and =monOverEpochs= is not touched. =capBpPerEpoch == 0= returns
from =_updateMonetary= before any write. The desk's own mark is LOW prices
of the TOKEN it holds, plus its signed balance, plus relief
(=EquityDesk.netValue=). That mark is not the basket's mark and not the
signal.

Transitions.* Disabled, already-done this epoch, or zero effort: revert, no

trade. Otherwise =_absorb= (cheap) or =_supply= (dear). Quadrants, matching
=_absorb= / =_supply= and =EquityDesk= because =_deskRetire= is empty ---
BUCK that arrives while the account is drawn repays the lien on arrival, and
the book entry is separate from that repayment:

Supply unchanged.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Q3. Sell held BUCK for TOKEN. Supply unchanged. Q3 runs before Q4.                                                     |

reserve. Bounds stay =maxLegBp= 40, =maxPositionBp= 1000,
=maxOutrightBp= 1000.

** 4. Proposed signal: one sample, two views

Purpose.* Publish the differential reading the basket's response uses and

the common-mode reading the desk uses, from one sample. Callers:
=SampleKind= on the basket's one wheel when a signal address is set;
=MonetaryKeeperAgent= before =monetaryOperation=; Daily (idempotent);
anyone. The first call on a day does the work. A later call the same day
returns. There is no desk-wheel sample slot, because there is no desk wheel.

State it owns.* Everything =EquityTurnDirector= owns for the differential

ladder, plus a common-mode ladder copied from =PairsRebalanceDirector=:
=bvM= over the common-mode windows, =bvFirstEpoch=, =bvLastEpoch=,
=monOverEpochs=, and the last common-mode sample. The differential windows
stay =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 1280]=, anchor 1280, quorum 4. The
common-mode windows are =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320]=. They are a second
constant. They are not the differential array with 1280 swapped out, and
they are not a shared storage vector.

Parameters that define a *reading* stay here, with the guards =setMonParams=
already enforces (=measIdx > 3= reverts; =leash1e9 < deadband1e9= reverts).
Rung 3 (40 days) is the last safe measurement: past the 80-day rung the
outright quadrants invert. Rung 4 and above is not a published measurement.
Defaults are the deployed =MonParams= from =deploy.py= (the
=director_impl == "pairs"= block, used for ops and equity-ops), so the four
ported tests run against the numbers a wired desk already uses:

| Parameter       | Default                                       | Where it lives |
|-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+----------------|
| =deadband1e9=   | =100e9= (100 bp)                              | signal         |
| =leash1e9=      | =200e9= (200 bp)                              | signal         |
| =persistEpochs= | 30                                            | signal         |
| =measIdx=       | 2 (the 20-day rung on the common-mode vector) | signal         |
| =kappa1e9=      | =250_000_000= (0.25)                          | adapter        |
| =capBpPerEpoch= | =SIM_OPS_CAP_BP=, default 40                  | adapter        |

The defaults in the table are the deployed nonzero =MonParams=, and those
are the numbers the signal and the adapter are constructed with.
=setMonParams= reverts when =leash1e9 < deadband1e9=. =leash == deadband= is
legal, including =(0, 0)=, so a later zero does replace a stored zero.
=insideDeadband= follows =_updateMonetary='s =absCm < deadband=. A zero
deadband contains no nonzero =cm=, so once the rung is warm the adapter
publishes a nonzero effort on every move. That is a live desk. Cap 0 on the
adapter forces effort 0. That is the off switch, and it does not suppress
=observe=. Turning the desk off must not freeze the ladder. The adapter's
constructor sets =kappa1e9 = 250_000_000= and =capBpPerEpoch = 40=.
Governance can set the cap to 0.

Parameters that size a basket trade do not stay here. =tiltWad= and the
basket =leashBp= move to the basket.

Inputs and the mark.* Same TWAP tick sample as today, plus one

=basketValueInBuck()= read, converted with =_logTick1e9= (a tick is =log=
base 1.0001, about 1 bp; the common mode is stored in tick·1e9). The
differential excursion stays in tick·1e18, as today. No LOW and no HIGH. The
signal does not read =idleToken=, =weightsBp=, or the lien. A pushed spot
moves it only to the extent the TWAP window admits, which is the existing
director's exposure.

Transitions of one leg* are the current director's transitions. =observe=

additionally folds the common-mode ladder with the same sample-and-hold,
once per day. Persistence matches =_updateMonetary='s order, with one
relocation: =capBpPerEpoch == 0= is the adapter's early return (effort 0, no
ladder write skipped), not a reason for =observe= to return before sampling.

On the signal, after the fold:

- If the measuring rung is not warm (=WINDOWS_CM[measIdx]= epochs since
  =bvFirstEpoch=, the same =e + 1 < bvFirstEpoch + w= test), publish a cold
  reading: effort will be 0, =persistent= is false, and =monOverEpochs= is
  not written.
- Else if =|cm| > leash=, increment =monOverEpochs= by one. Otherwise zero
  it.
- The deadband comparison is a view over =cm=. It does not itself clear the
  counter. A dip through the deadband is also at or below the leash, so the
  leash test does reset the counter. =Persistent → Deadband= is that edge.
- Catch-up of either ladder uses =q^n= across a gap. The persistence counter
  increments once per =observe= that lands on a new day, not once per missed
  day inside the fold. A gap is one sample, and the counter moves by one.
  That matches =_updateMonetary=, which runs once per poke epoch, not once
  per elapsed day inside =_foldBasket=. A later change must not "fix" the
  counter into counting the gap.

Two views of one sample.* One =observe= reads the pools once and

=basketValueInBuck= once. The differential view is
=x_i = tick_i - mean(tick)=, then the ladder, the excursion against the
1280-day anchor, and the six-rung vote. The common view is not that mean and
not the anchor residual. It is the separate ladder of
=log(basketValueInBuck)= against 0, on the 5..320 vector. The paper's phrase
"one filter bank, two modes" is this split. The later correction --- common
mode is the basket's own value, not the mean of the legs --- is part of the
same machine, not a second bank.

** 5. Proposed basket response

Purpose.* One paced change to the basket's positions, or a hold that still

refreshes the mark when the mark is stale by more than the grain. Caller:
the basket's wheel, one slot, =wheelStep= of a single Respond kind. Deploy,
Fund, and Trim cease to be kinds. Sync and Daily stay. The slot layout
changes once, in the same PR as the shell. See Data Model.

State it owns.* No new books. It reads =idleToken=, =liquidityOf=, the

signal's views, =eq= (including =tiltWad= and =leashBp=), and the snap. It
writes the same fields Deploy, Fund, and Trim write today: =idleToken=,
=liquidityOf=, and, through =_markAt=, the credit mark.

Inputs and the mark.* A step that will trade calls =_markS= first, so the

mark is LOW equity and the assets about to move are still in the mark.
Weights for the decision are not today's =weightsBp()=. Today's
=weightsBp()= is positions only (=BuckBasketEquity.weightsBp= sums
=posTwap=), so a deposit does not look overweight until something mints it.
The response weight includes idle at TWAP. The lien is not in the weight.

Let =dec_i= be the constituent's decimals. Define, in BUCK:

#+begin_example
value_i = posTwap_i + idleToken_i * pTwap_i / 10**dec_i
B       = Σ_i value_i
#+end_example

If =B == 0= there are no weights. The step holds. It may still mark under
the stale-mark rule in §6. It does not divide.

=responseWeightsBp()= is the integer analogue of =weightsBp()=:

#+begin_example
q_i = value_i * 10000 / B          // integer division
#+end_example

The remainder =10000 - Σ q_i= is dealt out one basis point at a time to the
legs with the largest =value_i * 10000 % B=, and on a tie to the lowest
index, so the vector sums to 10000. The leash and the band read this vector.
They do not read a 0--1 ratio, and they do not read =weightsBp()=.

The leaned target is the basket's: declared weight times
=exp(-tiltWad * excursion)=, renormalized, the same function as
=EquityTurnDirector.targetsBp=, computed here from =excursion(i)=. =tiltWad=
lives on the basket. Write =T_i = leanedBp_i * B / 10000= for the leaned
target in BUCK, and =D_i = declaredBp_i * B / 10000= for the declared weight
in BUCK.

The leash and the band are different units, and the leash wins inside the

band.* =weightBandBp= 200 is 2 percentage points of the whole book:
=band = B * weightBandBp / 10000=. =leashBp= 3000 is 30% of that leg's
declared weight, in the same integer basis points as =responseWeightsBp()=:

#+begin_example
leashedOver  = wBp_i > declaredBp_i + declaredBp_i * leashBp / 10000
leashedUnder = wBp_i + declaredBp_i * leashBp / 10000 < declaredBp_i
#+end_example

On the savings basket cbBTC is declared at 600 bp (6%). The leash opens at
780 bp, 7.8% of the book, a 1.8 pp gap, which is inside the 2 pp band. A 200
bp leg (2%) is leashed at 260 bp, a 0.6 pp gap, and stays inside the band
until it is a full 2 pp off --- 100% of declared, not 30%. Section 14's
prints (13.8% vs 6%, 15.3% vs 8%, LABR 19.0% vs 24%) are outside the band,
so those days trade under either rule. The day the 6% leg first crosses 7.8%
does not. A rule that says "the band wins even if leashed" would leave that
leg untraded at the moment the leash opens. That is the opposite of the
mandate this design keeps: a leg that has left the declared basket trades.

The lean makes the two regions able to disagree. The band sits around the
leaned target. The leash sits around the declared weight. A leg can be
leashed against the mandate and inside the band, or outside the band and not
leashed. The rule is one sentence. A leashed leg trades even inside the
weight band. When the leg is leashed, the step aims at the declared weight
(=D_i=), and that aim may lie inside the band. When the leg is not leashed,
the step aims at the band edge only, the Davis and Norman (1990) form: trade
to the boundary of the no-trade region, not through it, and not to the point
target. The pairs director's measurement that =boundaryBp = 0= won at a 30
bp cost is not imported. Whether =weightBandBp= should later be a Bertram
(2010) threshold is open question 8 in the wheel paper and stays open.

Per-leg eligibility*, not a stored state. =running= and =turned= come from

the signal. An unset signal is the triple in "Unset signal" below, not a
silent false from =_mayFund=.

|                     | Sell                                                                  | Buy                                                                     |
|---------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Forbidden when      | =running(i, +1)= and not =leashedOver=                                | =running(i, -1)= and not =leashedUnder=                                 |
| Also forbidden when | inside the band and not =leashedOver=                                 | inside the band and not =leashedUnder=                                  |
| Therefore a sell    | =leashedOver=, or (overweight past the band and not =running(i, +1)=) |                                                                         |
| Therefore a buy     |                                                                       | =leashedUnder=, or (underweight past the band and not =running(i, -1)=) |

A running-up leg has =running(i, +1)= and not =running(i, -1)=. If it is
underweight past the band, it is a buy. Positive excursion leans the target
down and does not make underweight impossible. There is no "in practice this
class is not a buy." A running-down leg that is overweight past the band is
a sell, because =running(i, +1)= is false. Inside the band and not leashed,
neither side, including a turned leg: the turn does not punch through the
band. The leash does. A turned leg is not running, so outside the band a
turn opens the corresponding side.

Aim and the step*, in BUCK. One sell leg and one buy leg: the eligible sell

with the largest aim, then the lowest index; the same tie-break on the buy.

#+begin_example
// Leashed: aim at the declared weight, even inside the band.
// Not leashed: aim at the band edge.
aimSell = leashedOver  ? (value_i - D_i) : (value_i - (T_i + band))
aimBuy  = leashedUnder ? (D_j - value_j) : ((T_j - band) - value_j)

// Turned: scale by votes/6. Not turned: full step.
// A failed vote is not a multiplier. Do not write votes/6 on a leg that is not turned.
sellStep = turned_i ? value_i * stepBp * votes_i / 10000 / 6
                    : value_i * stepBp / 10000
buyStep  = turned_j ? value_j * stepBp * votes_j / 10000 / 6
                    : value_j * stepBp / 10000

sellCap = min(aimSell, sellStep, 2 * swapCap_i)
buyCap  = min(aimBuy,  buyStep,      swapCap_j)
#+end_example

=swapCap_i= is =_swapCap= of that pool (1% of its virtual BUCK depth, all
LPs). The buy-side base is =value_j=, that leg's position plus idle at
=pTwap=. There is no =buyBook=. =stepBp= is 500, the current default. The
=2 *= on the sell cap is the unwind precedent: the TOKEN half of a position
is what gets sold, so a position of value =2 * swapCap= yields one =swapCap=
of TOKEN. A side whose cap is =<= grain= is dropped. The other side may
still run. Both sides dropped is a hold.

Unset signal.* This is explicit, and it differs from today. Today

=_mayFund= returns false when =equityDirector= is unset, and Fund runs only
through the parking fallback. That fallback is deleted. With
=equityDirector == address(0)= the response uses:

- =excursion(i) = 0=, =votes(i) = 0=, =turned(i) = false=,
  =running(i, +1) = false=, =running(i, -1) = false=
- leaned target = declared weight (=T_i = D_i=)
- the full-step formula (=value * stepBp / 10000=), not =votes/6=
- sells and buys are the band plus the leash, under the predicates above

An unset signal does not freeze the basket. Turn-scaled trades require a
signal. =B == 0= is still a hold.

The sell, as one swap.* Idle TOKEN of the sell leg is sold first. Burn LP

only for the TOKEN that idle did not cover. Burn cannot exceed
=liquidityOf=. One =monetaryLeg(sell, false, dx)= sells the TOKEN. There is
no second swap and no reserved-proceeds primitive.

The TOKEN value sent to that one swap is at most one =swapCap_i=. Idle fills
that budget first. The position value burned is =2 *= the TOKEN value still
needed after idle, because the TOKEN half is what is sold, and that burn is
also capped by =sellCap= and by =liquidityOf=. Concretely, with
=one = 10**dec_i= and =p = pTwap=:

#+begin_example
tokenBudget = swapCap_i                         // BUCK value of TOKEN sold
idleVal     = min(value of idleToken_i, tokenBudget, sellCap)
dxIdle      = idleVal * one / p                 // counted into dx; not a storage write yet
still       = min(tokenBudget, sellCap) - idleVal
burnVal     = min(2 * still, posTwap_i, sellCap - idleVal)
burned      = min(liquidityOf_i, liquidityOf_i * burnVal / posTwap_i)
#+end_example

When idle is zero this is the existing trim: position value up to
=2 * swapCap=, TOKEN sold up to one =swapCap=. When idle covers the whole
=swapCap=, nothing is burned. The burn's BUCK and the swap's BUCK both land
in the same account. They are not tracked separately. =positionBurn= pays
the position's BUCK side into the account before =monetaryLeg= sells the
TOKEN.

What a snap freezes.* =_markS= marks =_equityS= of the =Snap= it is given.

=s.m[i]= is =marks(i, liquidityOf[i])= from the moment =_snap()= ran
(=_marks=). =s.buckEq= and =s.lien= are =_account= at that same moment.
=_grossS= reads =idleToken= from storage, so a later idle write is visible
to gross and invisible to =s.m=. =_markS= then writes the mark and refreshes
=s.spend= only. =_headroomS= subtracts =s.lien=, which =_markS= does not
refresh. The only post-trade marks in the wheel today are fresh snaps:
=_daily= calls =_markS(_snap())=, and =redeem= does the same after the
positions are gone. A second =_markS= on the step's opening snap still
includes the burned position and the pre-sale lien, while =balanceOf= has
risen by the proceeds. The buy would then spend the proceeds and the credit
that was backed by the assets just sold.

The step as balance deltas.* This is the order. A step that is due for a

trade runs it even when the mark moved by less than the grain. A step that
is not due does not run and does not mark; the wheel skips the slot. Every
mark after the opening one is =_markS(_snap())= taken after the storage
write-backs. The opening snap is not marked a second time.

1. =s0 = _snap(); _markS(s0)=. Equity is LOW. Idle and positions about to
   move are still inside =s0.m= and in storage. Record =spend0 = s0.spend=
   (=balanceOf= after the mark), =usable0 = max(0, spend0 - _targetS(s0))=,
   and =headroom0 = s0.k * markOf / 1e18 - s0.lien= (=_headroomS(s0)=).
2. If a sell leg survived the grain test, burn, swap once, and book storage
   the way =_trim= does, with the idle that entered the same swap subtracted
   once:

#+begin_example
(tokenOut, ) = positionBurn(sell, burned)          // skip the call when burned == 0
liquidityOf[sell] -= burned
dx = tokenOut + dxIdle
(spent, ) = monetaryLeg(sell, false, dx)           // exact input; spent <= dx; skip when dx == 0
idleToken[sell] = idleToken[sell] - dxIdle + (tokenOut + dxIdle - spent)
#+end_example

=monetaryLeg= returns =spent=, which is below =dx= when the swap stops short
of the requested input. The unspent TOKEN, from idle and from the burn
together, is back in =idleToken=. When =dxIdle= is 0 this is =_trim=:
=liquidityOf -= burned=, then =idleToken += tokenOut - spent=.
=proceeds = max(0, balanceOf_after - balanceOf_before)=, measured across the
burn and that one =monetaryLeg=. =proceeds= is 0 when the sale only repays a
lien that is still over the limit: =balanceOf= is unchanged, which is
=_deploy='s "under water: nothing." 3. =s1 = _snap(); _markS(s1)=, before
any new credit is drawn. =s1.m= is valued at the post-burn =liquidityOf=.
=s1.lien= is the post-sale lien. Headroom and =balanceOf= for the buy are
=_headroomS(s1)= and =s1.spend=. Do not call =_markS(s0)=. 4. Buy budget. If
=headroom0 < 0=, the budget is 0: the basket started under water, so it does
not buy. Otherwise =buyBudget = min(proceeds, buyCap)=. If
=_headroomS(s1) >= 0=, add at most the pre-trade =usable0= that still fits
inside =buyCap= (=min(usable0, buyCap - buyBudget)=). Then
=buyBudget = min(buyBudget, s1.spend)=. If headroom on =s1= is negative, do
not add =usable0=. That is "already under water before the buy, so do not
draw." It is not a mid-step state that reserves proceeds to the lien. 5. The
buy runs only when =buyBudget > 0= and a buy leg survived. Split that budget
in half, the Fund precedent, so =positionMint= receives both sides. Book the
mint the way =_deploy= does:

#+begin_example
half = buyBudget / 2
(, received) = monetaryLeg(buy, true, half)
idleToken[buy] += received
(liquidity, tokenUsed, ) = positionMint(buy, idleToken[buy], buyBudget - half)
idleToken[buy] -= tokenUsed
liquidityOf[buy] += liquidity
#+end_example

=_liquidityForAmounts= is the min of the two amounts. One side zero makes
=positionMint= return =(0, 0, 0)=, so the last two writes are zero and the
TOKEN stays in =idleToken=. That is =_deploy='s =l == 0= return, written
out. Swapping the whole =buyBudget= leaves the kept half at 0 and mints
nothing, which is Fund's behaviour, and this design deletes it. Pre-existing
idle on the buy leg is paired with the kept half, beside =received=. Idle
alone is not a budget: =buyBudget == 0= does not call =positionMint=, and
that idle is not sold into the buy pool. Leftover BUCK stays on the account.
The step does not loop. 6. If step 5 ran, =s2 = _snap(); _markS(s2)=. =s2.m=
sees the new =liquidityOf=, and =_grossS= reads =idleToken= after
=tokenUsed= was subtracted, so the minted TOKEN is not in the mark twice. If
step 5 did not run, =s1= is the end mark.

No-sell (buy only) uses the same sequence with =proceeds = 0=. The opening
mark is the pre-buy mark, because nothing has been burned. The budget is the
=usable0= term, and the mint runs only when that budget is positive. No-buy
(sell only) books the sell and stops on =s1=. Hold is a step that ran
because the mark was stale: =_markS= on the snap just taken, no swap, no
second call on that snap.

The post-trade test.* Two checks, and no third. A step with =headroom0 < 0=

does not increase the lien. The BUCK spent on the buy is at most =s1.spend=,
the post-remark =balanceOf=, and at most =buyBudget=. The end mark does not
pull a lien back. A fresh snap before the spend keeps the buy inside the
limit that existed at =s1=. It does not keep the lien inside
=K * equity(LOW)= of the book after the fee. =deploy.py= sets
=FEE_BUCK = 3000=, a 30 bp TOKEN/BUCK fee, taken from the swap input. BUCK
has 6 decimals, so a 30 bp fee on 1 BUCK (=1e6= raw) is 3000 raw units.
=_grainS='s floor of =1e3= is 0.001 BUCK and is the least-work floor. It is
not a slack on this test, and the test does not use it.

There is no =Transfer --> SellOnly= edge. A sale reduces the lien. Headroom
gets worse on the buy, or was already negative before the step. The
underwater case is =headroom0 < 0= at the start, or =headroom < 0= after the
re-mark, and in both the response does not draw new credit.

The do-nothing transition of the wheel is a slot that is not due: =tick=
skips it, emits nothing, and does not call =wheelStep=. A Respond that runs
and holds emits =Responded= with =action = hold=. That event means the step
ran because the mark was stale. It does not mean every skipped slot.

A deposit that arrives mid-way.* =deposit= is unchanged in what it books:

shares at the high equity, TOKEN into =idleToken= (BUCK straight onto the
account), =dayFlow += value=, =_markS=. The mark rises on the deposit's LOW
value before any placement, because idle TOKEN is already in =_grossS=.
There is no Deploy after this design. On a later Respond the deposit's TOKEN
is part of =value_i=. If that leg is a sell, the TOKEN is sold, idle first,
inside the one =swapCap=, and the proceeds buy an underweight leg the
predicates allow. If that leg is a buy, the TOKEN is the TOKEN side of
=positionMint=, paired with the buy budget. If that leg is still running
down and not =leashedUnder=, the TOKEN is not bought and not minted into it.
It waits as idle, and the rest of the machine keeps trading other legs.
Waiting in one pool does not veto another.

That is mitigation 4 of =doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §14.2, with the gate the
section already required: do not buy a leg that is still running down,
unless =leashedUnder=. The arrival pool is not privileged. The entry charge
still only partly covers the extra swap (§14.2.4). The cap is what keeps one
deposit from moving the pool. Pricing the mix into the entry charge
(mitigation 5) is not part of this machine.

Why the new position and the new credit are the ammunition.* A buy draws

=balanceOf= after the re-mark, which rose when the deposit raised LOW
equity, by about =K= times the deposit, subject to the face cap and to
=usable0=. A sell's step is the expression above. Minting LP raises =pos= on
the next snap, which raises the next step's =value_i=, and raises =depth= if
the basket is the LP in that pool, which raises =swapCap=. The machine uses
both on the next step. Nothing in the classification looks at whether other
pools still hold idle TOKEN.

The liquidity target is not a settled-wallet gate.*

=usable0 = max(0, spend0 - target)= remains the ceiling on *new* credit
drawn to mint LP, so a buy does not spend the BUCK the exit path keeps
(=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §13.6: liquidity is what pays a BUCK redemption).
It is taken from the pre-trade snap and added only when headroom after the
re-mark is =>= 0=, and only inside =buyCap=. A sell does not consult
=usable=. A transfer spends the sale's proceeds first. Idle TOKEN of a leg
the predicates allow does not need =usable0 > 0= to be sold. Sync's fee
TOKEN lands in =idleToken= as today and is classified on the next response.
It does not close a gate.

Under water.* If =headroom0 < 0=, the buy budget is 0. Sell-only runs, and

the BUCK repays the lien. The mark has already fallen with LOW equity. The
lien is not recalled. =K= is read from the controller inside =_snap=. The
signal does not set it. This is the existing rule (=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org=
§13.3, =doc/JUBILEE-ISSUANCE.org= 8b.2). It is not re-opened.

** 6. Equity and the mark

Purpose.* Keep the single =BuckCredit= equal to equity at LOW, so headroom

tracks the book between trades as well as inside them. Callers that mark,
and the test each one uses:

- A Respond due for a trade marks on the opening snap, then
  =_markS(_snap())= after the sell is booked, and =_markS(_snap())= again
  after the buy is booked, regardless of grain. =_markAt= writes when
  =equity(LOW)= is unequal to =markOf= and has no grain of its own. The
  later snaps are new. The opening snap is not passed to =_markS= again.
- =wheelDue= for a trade-less Respond is =|equity(LOW) - markOf| > grain=.
  That step calls =_markS= once. =Responded(action = hold)= is emitted only
  in this case.
- A slot that is not due emits nothing and does not write. The wheel skips
  it. There is no running step that snaps and then refuses to mark.
- Daily, =deposit=, and =redeem= mark as they do today. =deposit=, =redeem=,
  and =_daily= call =_markS= even when the move is below the grain.

The first mark above zero calls =Buck.mint(type(uint256).max)= once (the
whole face, zero premium) and sets =creditLive=. Later marks only write
=credit.mark=.

Today a price move re-marks when Daily runs, when Deploy, Fund, or Trim are
actually due, and when =deposit= or =redeem= runs. The waiting-TOKEN veto
can suppress Fund and Trim. It cannot suppress Deploy, which is due whenever
that pool's idle TWAP value exceeds the grain and which marks. The veto
therefore blocks the underwater refill. It does not block Deploy's mark.

State it owns.* =credit.markOf(creditId)= and the cached =creditLive= flag.

Equity itself is not stored. It is recomputed from the snap.

Inputs and the mark.* The snap's three prices. The mark *written* is LOW.

Response weights, the grain, and the liquidity target use TWAP. HIGH remains
the incumbents' side of an entry (=deposit= prices shares off
=_equityS(sn, MARK_HIGH)=).

Three sums, already different in the code.*

#+begin_example
gross(mark)   = Σ_i pos(mark, i) + Σ_i idleToken_i * p(mark, i) / 10**dec_i
buckEq        = signedBalanceOf(basket) + reliefOf(basket)
equity(mark)  = max(0, gross(mark) + buckEq)
lien          = max(0, -signedBalanceOf(basket))
mark written  = equity(LOW)                         // argument to _markAt
headroom      = s.k * markOf / 1e18 - lien          // _headroomS
spend         = balanceOf = held + max(creditLimit - lien, 0)
creditLimit   = K * min(markOf, face) / 1e18        // once the whole face is activated
usable        = max(0, spend - liquidityTarget)
#+end_example

=pos= is twice the BUCK side of *the basket's* liquidity (=marks=:
=posTwap = 2 * _buckSideAt=). It is not =token.balanceOf(pool)= and not
=buck.balanceOf(pool)=. Those balances include every other LP. Counting them
as the basket's equity would mark the credit on assets the basket does not
hold. =depth= is the pool's virtual BUCK reserve, all LPs, and it is an
impact cap for =swapCap=. It is not an asset.

      B->>C: _markS(_snap())
    end
  else leg i still running down, not leashedUnder
    B->>B: leave idleToken[i], trade some other eligible leg
  end
#+end_src

* Data Model Changes

=idleToken=, =liquidityOf=, =flowMs=, the credit id, and the receipt books
stay where they are. =EquityParams= is packed into the one storage slot =eq=
(24 bytes used, 8 free). =eq= is the last variable in
=BuckBasketEquityStorage=, so an extra slot is an append. A =uint16 leashBp=
fits in the 8 free bytes. An =int256 tiltWad= occupies the next slot.
=setEquityParams= already replaces =eq= in one assignment; the setter writes
the whole struct, including the new fields, and reverts when =leashBp == 0=.

The shell is not proxied. A facet against an old shell would read
=tiltWad = 0= and =leashBp = 0=. The shell, the setter, the facet, and the
wheel ship in one PR. A newly deployed shell is a different basket. Pointing
=setWheel= / =setEquityWheel= / =setEquityDirector= back at old bytecode
restores an old facet on an old shell. It does not roll back a new shell.
Running experiments keep the old shell, the old facet, and the old director.
They are not migrated.

Signal state lives entirely in the new signal deployment: the existing
director mappings, the differential windows, the separate common-mode
windows, and =monOverEpochs=. Nothing is copied out of a live
=PairsRebalanceDirector=. The equity desk is pointed at the adapter, and the
adapter is pointed at the new signal. The old director's storage is
abandoned with the old address. A ladder seeded on a different basket is not
this machine's history. The new differential ladder warms for 1280 days
before the anchor means what the paper says it means; until then the
excursion is against a short history, which is the existing director's
behaviour from =primed= onward (the anchor starts equal to the first sample,
so the first day's excursion is zero). The common-mode rung stays cold until
it has =WINDOWS_CM[measIdx]= days (20 at the default), and while it is cold
the adapter publishes effort 0 and the signal does not touch
=monOverEpochs=.

The lien, the mark, and the receipt books are not migrated.

=kappa1e9= and =capBpPerEpoch= are storage on the adapter, not on the signal
and not in =MonetaryDesk=. The adapter's constructor writes the deployed
defaults. Governance can set the cap to 0.

* Alternatives Considered

** 1. Delete the idle-TOKEN veto and keep the rest

Section 14.2 mitigations 1 and 2: gate Trim on =idleToken= of the leg being
trimmed, let Fund spend =usable= minus the BUCK needed to pair waiting
TOKEN, and let the leash through the gate. This is the smallest code change.
It is rejected as the architecture, not as a false description of the bug.

It leaves the desk and the basket on two signals. =EquityTurnDirector= still
has no common mode, and =PairsRebalanceDirector= is still what the desk's
=mk_cm= is. It leaves placement in Deploy, which mints the deposited TOKEN
into the pool it arrived in, so a deposit still pushes the weight toward the
depositor's mix and only later, if Trim is allowed to run, unwinds it. The
deposit is not ammunition for the underweight leg. It is a queue. Fund and
Trim remain two steps ordered against each other: =_trimPlan= returns none
while =_fundPlan= has a pool, and =_fund= writes the idle TOKEN that
re-closes =_anyTokenWaiting=. A leash that can fire is necessary and is not
sufficient for the machine the owner described, which sells one pool and
buys another in one step, and places a deposit toward the legs the signal
allows.

Mitigation 1 is also the wrong end state for a different reason. "Reserve
pairing BUCK for waiting TOKEN, then let Fund spend what remains" still
treats the arrival pool as the destination. The response treats the arrival
pool as a candidate sell or a candidate buy, depending on the response
weight and the predicates.

** 2. A third signal contract on its own wheel, versus the director as a basket satellite the desk may read

|                    | Satellite: desk reads today's director                                                                                                    | This design: evolved director, one sample slot, keeper calls =observe=                                                  |
|--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Common mode        | Absent. The desk cannot decide cheap/dear.                                                                                                | Present, as =log(basketValueInBuck)= against par, on windows =[5..320]=, separate from the 1280-day anchor.             |
| Desk's persistence | Would be tempted to reuse =turned=, which is a velocity quorum. That is the test the wheel paper says never fired the outright quadrants. | Duration past the leash, copied from =_updateMonetary=, including the cold early-return.                                |
| Who samples        | Daily, inside the basket wheel. A stalled basket wheel freezes the desk's signal.                                                         | =SampleKind= on the one deployed wheel, the keeper before =monetaryOperation=, Daily, and anyone. Catch-up is one call. |
| What it can steal  | Nothing today, and nothing after.                                                                                                         | Nothing. No tokens, no credit.                                                                                          |
| Cost               | No new address. Does not work.                                                                                                            | One new signal deployment and one adapter. No new reserve. =PairsRebalanceDirector= remains for the pro-rata path.      |

The own-wheel variant with a private reserve was considered and rejected. A
=WorkWheel= that captures nothing does not get called unless a reserve pays
=kappa=. The sample does not need to be the only thing in its block. Putting
a Sample slot on a desk wheel would describe the paper. =deploy.py= does not
deploy that wheel, and this design does not add one.

Leaving the lean, the leash, and =mayTrim= inside the signal was considered
and rejected. They read the basket's weights. Putting them in the shared
contract would make the desk's process depend on a function that answers a
basket question, and it would keep the leash on =weightsBp()=, which ignores
the idle TOKEN the response has to see. The signal publishes prices. The
basket applies its mandate to =responseWeightsBp()=.

** 3. Trim and fund the current pools, versus mint new LP with drawn credit

These are the two halves of one action. The current facet already does both,
in the wrong order, with a veto between them, and with Fund's swap leaving
=positionMint= nothing to pair.

- Selling is an unwind. =positionBurn= returns TOKEN and pays the position's
  BUCK into the account; one =monetaryLeg(i, false, dx)= sells the TOKEN,
  idle first; =liquidityOf= and =idleToken= are booked as =_trim= books
  them; =proceeds= is the increase in =balanceOf=. That is Trim, typed as
  the sell in §5.
- Buying is a draw, after =_markS(_snap())= on the post-sale book.
  =s1.spend= is unused credit under =K * mark= plus held BUCK, computed on
  that snap. Half of the buy budget is swapped for TOKEN. =positionMint=
  adds liquidity to the existing full-range position, and
  =idleToken -= tokenUsed=, =liquidityOf += liquidity=, as =_deploy= does.
  It does not require the wallet to be empty first.
- A transfer is that sell, then the fresh snap, then that buy funded by
  =min(proceeds, buyCap)= plus pre-trade =usable0= only when headroom on
  =s1= is still non-negative, clamped to =s1.spend=. Neither half waits for
  another kind to become due.

Minting a second position per pool is not what "mint new LP" means here. The
basket's book is one =liquidityOf[i]= per constituent, full range
(=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §3.1, and =marks= valued on that liquidity). New LP
is more of that position, funded by the credit the mark just rose to allow.

What is rejected is the current split into three kinds. Deploy can only mint
into the deposited commodity's pool. Fund swaps the whole planned BUCK,
leaves TOKEN in the wallet, and mints nothing. Trim refuses to run while
either of those is true, and also while Fund has a plan. The response is one
kind so those three cannot veto each other.

* Security & Privacy Considerations

The signal holds no assets. A compromised or merely wrong signal can
mis-time trades inside =swapCapBp= and inside =stepBp=. It cannot withdraw,
cannot mark either credit, and cannot set =K=. That is the bound the wheel
paper already states for the director ("Its outputs are hints, not
authority"). Governance points the basket at it with =setEquityDirector= and
points the adapter at it; the desk's =setMonetaryDirector= points at the
adapter. Changing those pointers is the same authority as changing the
director today. The signal must not be passed to =openCredit=.

=observe= is permissionless. A second call on the same day returns. The
catch-up exponent is capped at 3650. There is no value to extract by calling
it, and no way to move =lastDay= backward.

The mark is LOW. A spot push up does not raise =creditLimit= above TWAP
equity. A spot push down can shrink headroom and stop buys. It cannot pull
the lien back, and it cannot move tokens out. That is the existing E2
direction. The response's re-mark between sell and buy is =_markS(_snap())=
after =liquidityOf= and =idleToken= are written. That is what keeps a
transfer from issuing against assets the sell just removed. A second
=_markS= on the opening snap does not.

The response runs only from the wheel (=msg.sender == wheel=), inside the
facet, as Deploy, Fund, and Trim do. Pool callbacks stay in the venue
(=onlySelf= on =positionMint= / =monetaryLeg=). This design adds no entry
point that pulls BUCK to an arbitrary address. The BUCK spent on the buy is
at most the post-remark =balanceOf= and at most =buyBudget=. A step that
started under water does not increase the lien. The end mark is not asked to
pull that lien back inside =K= times the post-fee book.

A depositor can no longer freeze Trim by leaving dust above the grain. They
can still deposit a large amount of one TOKEN. The response will sell it
toward the aim at one =swapCap= per step when the predicates allow, which is
the intended flow rebalance, and will not buy a leg that is still running
down unless =leashedUnder=. The entry charge is unchanged and only partly
covers that sell. The loss from selling a depositor's mix sits on the book,
as §14.2.4 already says, and is capped per step by the same 1% depth cap
that bounds every other wheel swap.

The basket and the desk remain two accounts. A test that the signal's TOKEN
and BUCK balances stay zero, and that it has no credit id, belongs in the
signal's forge suite. The desk's existing wall (D2, and
=test/basket/EquityDeskInvariant.t.sol= as the wheel paper cites it) stays:
inflating the basket's mark must not move the desk's limit.
=EquityDesk._mark= / =netValue= / =_deskNav= are not edited. =_deskNav=
remains =basket.equity()= at TWAP, idle included.

No new personal data. The signal stores prices and EMAs. Receipts, identity,
and the approve-before-exit path are untouched (=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org=
§13.8, "Identity").

* Observability

Existing events stay: =WheelWork(kind, i, amount)=, =Deposited=,
=CreditMarked= / the desk's =DeskMarked=, =MonetaryOperation=. After the
cut, equity =WheelWork= kind 2 is Respond. Kinds 3 and 4 are not emitted.

Add one event on the response, beside =WheelWork=:

    uint8 action,          // 0 hold, 1 sell, 2 buy, 3 transfer

    uint256 buyLeg,

#+end_src

=action = hold= is emitted only when the step ran because
=|equity(LOW) - markOf| > grain=. A skipped slot emits nothing. =wheelDue=
is false and =tick= does not count it as work.

The sim's snapshot already stores =mk_cm=. After the desk is re-pointed,
=mk_cm= is the signal's measuring rung (=monetaryReading='s =cm=,
common-mode index 2), not =d.director.commonMode(2)=. That change is in
=MonetaryKeeperAgent= and ships with the adapter PR. Reading =commonMode(2)=
on the pairs director after =setMonetaryDirector= would keep the column on
the old bank.

Counters worth plotting, not new on-chain storage: Respond actions per day
by kind, days a leg spends past the leash, days =headroom < 0=, max idle
TOKEN value as a fraction of gross (it may be large; it is inventory, and
Fund/Trim are gone), and =markOf= against =equity(LOW)=. The section 14.3
diagnostic --- waiting TOKEN, which kinds were due, weights, targets,
=mayTrim= --- is the list to extend, not a campaign to start.

Alerts, off-chain, from those counters:

- Signal =lastDay= more than two days behind the chain. Catch-up repairs the
  ladder. It does not repair a desk that traded on a stale sign. The
  keeper's =observe= before =monetaryOperation=, and the one =SampleKind=
  slot, are what make a stale day rare.
- A leg past the leash for more than a day while the basket wheel's =tick=
  is landing and Respond is due and not succeeding. That is the section 14
  failure mode returning.
- =headroom < 0= persisting. This is the ruled underwater state, not a page.
  The alert distinguishes a prolonged outage (sells also not running) from
  the rule (sells running, buys not).

* Rollout Plan

There is no feature flag in these contracts. The order is the PR plan. Each
step is independently deployable. Running experiments are not migrated.

1. Land the state-machine chapter in the repo. No contract change. Running
   cells are unaffected.
2. Deploy the signal beside the old director. Do not point the equity basket
   or the desk at it. The forge tests include the four common-mode tests
   ported onto the deployed =MonParams= (deadband =100e9=, leash =200e9=,
   kappa =250_000_000=, cap 40, =persistEpochs= 30, =measIdx= 2) and onto
   the separate window vectors. The old director remains the address the
   running experiments call. =targetsBp= / =mayTrim= / =mayFund= stay on
   that old bytecode.
3. Deploy a new shell, the new facet, and the new wheel together.
   =EquityKind= is Daily, Sync × N, Respond (kind 2). =SampleKind= sits
   after =ArbKind=. =EquityParams= carries =leashBp= and =tiltWad=, the
   constructor sets =1e18= and =3000=, and =setEquityParams= rejects
   =leashBp == 0=. This basket is a new deployment. Running experiments keep
   the old shell, the old facet, and the old director. Rolling back the new
   shell is deploying or retaining the old basket. It is not three pointer
   writes. Do not point the new facet at an old shell, and do not run the
   old wheel against the new facet: the old wheel sends kind 2 as Deploy,
   and the new facet reads kind 2 as Respond.
4. Deploy the adapter with the constructor defaults. =setMonetaryDirector=
   on the equity desk points at the adapter. =monetaryOperation= is
   unchanged. The keeper calls =signal.observe()= before =monetaryOperation=
   and reads =mkCm= from the signal's measuring rung. Re-pointed equity-ops
   cells stop poking the pairs director. Cells that have not re-pointed keep
   that poke. Pro-rata desks stay on =PairsRebalanceDirector=. Pointing
   =setMonetaryDirector= back at the pairs director restores today's =mk_cm=
   for that cell.
5. Sim snapshot counters for the response. Still no campaign.

Bytecode pressure is a check, not a redesign. §13.8 measured the wheel facet
at 9.0 KB and the shell at 16.7 KB. The response replaces three external
flows with one; the common-mode math moves into the signal, which has no 24
KB problem of its own (=EquityTurnDirector= is not the contract that was
tight). If the facet crosses the limit, the split is still one Respond
function in the facet, not a return to three kinds.

* Key Decisions

1. *The signal is a third contract, evolved from =EquityTurnDirector=, not a
   satellite and not a new ladder.* The current director is only the
   differential half, on =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 1280]=. The desk's common
   mode lives in =PairsRebalanceDirector= on =[5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320]=
   and is a different turn test. One new deployment carries both views, on
   those two vectors, with =measIdx <= 3= and =leash >= deadband=. The old
   director stays for anything already pointed at it. =targetsBp= /
   =mayTrim= / =mayFund= stay on the old interface.

2. *No private wheel and no third reserve.* =deploy.py= deploys one
   =BasketWheel=. =SampleKind= is one mixin slot on that wheel, after
   =ArbKind=, try/catch around =observe=, and only when a signal address is
   set. An unset signal adds zero slots. The desk is advanced by
   =MonetaryKeeperAgent= calling =observe= and then =monetaryOperation=.
   This design does not add a desk wheel, and it does not put a Sample slot
   on a second wheel.

3. *The two turn tests stay different.* The basket uses the six-rung
   velocity quorum (=turned= / =running=) against the 1280-day anchor. The
   desk uses the sign of the 20-day common-mode rung on its own =[5..320]=
   vector, a deadband, and a duration past the leash. Unifying them
   reintroduces the bug the wheel paper documents: a velocity test that
   never reports persistence. Index 2 is 20 on both ladders by coincidence.
   The vectors are not shared.

4. *The signal is price-only.* The lean, the basket leash, and the step size
   live on the basket. =kappa1e9= and =capBpPerEpoch= live on the
   =IMonetaryDirector= adapter, not on the signal and not inside
   =MonetaryDesk=. The signal does not hold tokens and does not set =K=.
   =monetaryOperation= is not edited.

5. *One response kind replaces Deploy, Fund, and Trim.* Kind 2 becomes
   Respond. The transfer is the balance-delta procedure in §5: mark the
   opening snap, one sell (idle first, one =swapCap=, one =monetaryLeg=),
   book =liquidityOf= and =idleToken= the way =_trim= does, then
   =_markS(_snap())= and read headroom from that snap. Never a second
   =_markS= on the pre-trade snap. The buy budget is =min(proceeds, buyCap)=
   plus pre-trade =usable0= only when headroom on the new snap is still
   non-negative, clamped to that snap's =balanceOf=. Half the buy's BUCK is
   swapped and half is kept for =positionMint=, then
   =idleToken -= tokenUsed= and =liquidityOf += liquidity= the way =_deploy=
   does, then =_markS(_snap())= again. The global =_anyTokenWaiting= veto,
   the "Trim yields to Fund" ordering, and the parking fallback are removed.

6. *Idle TOKEN in the mark confirms today's =_equityS=.* It is not a formula
   change. Three sums stay distinct: the collateral mark is =equity(LOW)=
   via =_markAt=; desk NAV and =sharePrice()= are =equity()= at TWAP; the
   response weight is =responseWeightsBp()= at TWAP with the lien left out.
   All three include idle. The owner ruled on 2026-09-30 that idle stays in
   the mark and is not excluded. Wallet BUCK and unpaid relief stay in
   equity. Owed fees stay out until Sync. The mark stays LOW. Outsider pool
   balances stay out.

7. *The liquidity target caps new credit drawn for a buy. It does not gate
   sells, and it does not gate the machine.* =usable0= is the pre-trade
   value. It is added to the buy budget only when headroom on the post-sale
   snap is =>= 0=, and only inside =buyCap=. Under water at the start, the
   buy budget is 0 and sells that repay the lien continue. That rule is
   unchanged. The checks are: a step that started under water does not
   increase the lien, and the BUCK spent on the buy is at most the
   post-remark =balanceOf= and at most =buyBudget=. There is no end-of-step
   test that the lien is within =K * equity(LOW)=. The grain floor =1e3= is
   not a slack on that test.

8. *Step size is the integer expression in §5, in BUCK.* With
   =value_i = posTwap_i + idle_i * pTwap_i / 10**dec_i= and =B = Σ value_i=:
   if =B == 0=, hold. Otherwise =wBp_i = value_i * 10000 / B=, remainder
   assigned so the sum is 10000. A turned leg steps
   =value * stepBp * votes / 10000 / 6=. A leg that is not turned steps
   =value * stepBp / 10000=. A leashed leg aims at the declared weight, even
   inside the band. A leg that is not leashed aims at the band edge.
   =sellCap = min(aimSell, sellStep, 2 * swapCap_i)= and
   =buyCap = min(aimBuy, buyStep, swapCap_j)=. A side =<= grain= drops only
   that side. Tie-break is the largest aim, then the lowest index. The buy
   splits its BUCK in half before =positionMint=.

9. *Flow rebalancing is mitigation 4 with the signal gate.* A deposit is
   placed toward the underweight leg the predicates allow, not into the pool
   it arrived in. A leg still running down is not bought unless
   =leashedUnder=. The arrival pool is not privileged. Arb captures that
   =creditDepositors= parks in =idleToken= are the same inventory.

10. *This document does not start E2--E4.* Equity cells that score the
    starved wheel become invalid when the response ships. Desk cells that
    score =mk_cm= become invalid only when the adapter PR moves
    =monetaryDirector=. Pro-rata cells that never call
    =BuckBasketEquityWheel= stay valid while their director address is
    unchanged. None of them is relaunched from here.

* Refinements raised 2026-10-01

The owner raised four questions against the machines above. None is ruled.
The PR plan does not grow a PR for any of them. What follows is the reading
of the current text, and the condition under which each would become a
design.

** Arb stays a kind on the wheel

This design does not fold =ArbKind= into the response. =BasketWheel='s slot
order is Compute, Director, Sweep, Ops, Equity, Arb, and the slot cut
appends Sample after Arb so the triangle indexes stay where they are.
Changing =ArbKind='s cycle is a non-goal.

=doc/BASKET-EQUITY.org= §13.8 departure 1 already placed the profit in the
basket and left the code on the wheel. The cycle needs no float: the V3
callback funds it, and the profit is credited through =creditDepositors= /
=creditTreasury=. "In the basket" is where the TOKEN lands, not where the
kind runs. The one new coupling is a consequence of deleting Deploy.
=creditDepositors= does =idleToken[i] += amount=. That TOKEN becomes
response inventory, and the response may sell it out of the pool it was
captured in. The cycle itself is unchanged.

** The signal does not price the outside route

The better-route swap is a named non-goal. Respond sells and buys with one
=monetaryLeg= on the basket's own TOKEN/BUCK pool. The signal reads those
pools' TWAP ticks and one =basketValueInBuck()=. It does not read TOKEN/USDC
or BUCK/USDC, and it does not compare the cost of acquiring a TOKEN, or the
proceeds of divesting it, against the two-hop route through USDC.

=ArbKind= does read all three pools. It takes a cycle whose direct profit is
positive, in whichever direction pays, with USDC existing only between two
of the swaps (=src/wheel/ArbKind.sol=). That search is a consistency trade.
It is not a quote the response consults before it sizes a sell or a buy.

§13.8 departure 2 deferred the route for a different reason: the wheel's
arbitrage re-pins the basket's pools between capped steps, so an own-pool
trade is priced near what the outside route would have cost. That is an
argument about the pool being pulled back between steps. It is not a
comparison, inside one step, of which path is cheaper while the gap is still
open.

Open, and not designed here. A route comparison would be a third reading
beside the two ladders. For each leg, the BUCK cost of buying its TOKEN on
the own pool against the cost of BUCK to USDC to TOKEN, and the BUCK
proceeds of the reverse. The response would send that step's sell or buy
down the better path, inside the same =sellCap= and =buyCap=. The signal is
the place that already samples once a day and holds no inventory, so it is
the place that would publish the two quotes. =ArbKind='s triangle search
stays a different function. Departure 2 remains the record that the route is
unbuilt.

** The desk's reserve, and whether the basket runs a quadrant

=doc/DESK-LP-RESERVE.org= (2026-09-30) is the existing discussion of the
desk saving its grant in the basket. This design does not adopt it. The desk
remains its own credit holder. =monetaryOperation= stays on =EquityDesk=.
The equity desk is not =setOps='d. Invariant D2 stands: nothing the desk
holds is in the basket's =_equityS=.

The question splits in two.

The desk deposits the grant, holds the receipt's shares, and its own
BuckCredit is marked at a net value that includes that receipt at the
basket's LOW share price. The note's assessment: promising as the place the
reserve waits, because the shares earn, a cheap BUCK makes the shares buy
more BUCK, and an in-kind exit delivers the TOKEN Q1 sells. Weak as the
issue and retire lever itself, because a deposit and an exit change pool
depth and do not move BUCK's price, and an exit during an inflation attack
thins the pools the desk is supposed to be defending. The spiral bound in
that note is the one that applies to any loop of this shape: a mark at
gross, drawing, buying TOKEN and depositing it again, grows as
=K / (1 - K)=, three times at =K = 0.75=. A mark at net does not grow. The
four questions at the end of that note --- the treasury cut on the desk's
receipt, how much of the reserve stays plain TOKEN, whether the desk's exit
queues behind depositors, and the size of a standing book --- are still
open.

The basket performing a quadrant is the other half, and it is a different
machine. Respond already draws the basket's own credit to mint LP, and sells
TOKEN to repay the basket's own lien. That step is paced by =stepBp= and
=swapCapBp=, and it is sized to a weight gap. It is not Q1--Q4, and it is
not sized to move bvib. Handing the basket a quadrant would turn a weight
trade into a supply trade, and it would make the basket's TOKEN the desk's
reserve. The quadrant table refuses that: Q1 spends TOKEN the desk already
holds, and the basket's TOKEN is not that reserve. PR 4 re-points the desk's
reading. It does not move the operation.

** A BuckCredit on LP, drawn while K is quenching

The basket's one BuckCredit is already a mark on its LP. =equity(LOW)= is
the positions plus idle TOKEN, minus the lien, and =_markS= writes that to
the NFT. A second NFT marked on those same positions counts them twice.

The loop used by lending platforms --- borrow against an asset, post what
was borrowed, borrow again --- is the gross mark =DESK-LP-RESERVE.org=
refuses. Each new NFT is another turn of =K / (1 - K)= when the collateral
it marks is still inside a position the basket's mark includes.

An NFT on LP that has left the basket is a relocation, not a loop. Moving
the position out drops the basket's equity, and its line, by about =K= times
the value moved. The new holder marks what it received. When both marks use
the same =K= and the same LOW value, system-wide credit capacity moves from
one account to the other. It increases only if the new mark is gross, or
uses a higher factor, or the LP never left =_equityS=.

The case where a relocation does something the basket cannot: under water
the basket's buy budget is zero and sells that repay continue. While BUCK is
cheap (=basketValueInBuck= above par, the inflation K exists to quench) the
basket's headroom can be exhausted and K can still be moving. A holder of
segregated LP, with its own line and the same no-margin-call rule, could
draw and acquire through that stretch, and unwind after the persistent error
has been quenched and the basket's headroom has returned. A price fall may
leave that holder's lien above =K= times its mark. Buys stop. Sells that
repay continue. That holder is a third credit account. It is not the signal.
It is the desk only if the desk is the holder, which is the standing book
already asked in =DESK-LP-RESERVE.org=.

Open. The condition for it becoming a design: the LP inside the new NFT is
absent from the basket's =_equityS=. Until that is true, the NFT is a second
mark on the basket's own positions.

** The residual bvib K's integral needs

K is a PID on raw bvib (=doc/K-AND-OPS.org=, decision 10). The proportional
term is that error. The integral time in the sim defaults is 90 days, with
=e_max= 0.10, =dk_rail= 0.5 and =kp_frac= 0.02
(=alberta_buck/sim/experiment.py=). Held for one integral time, a constant
error =e= moves K by about =5 e=: a 1% error moves K by about 0.05, and a 2%
error by about 0.10. The proportional term on the same error is about
=0.1 e=, so 0.001 at 1%. The authority is the integral. A residual of 2--4
bp, which is where the calm pro-rata E3 sat (=doc/CONVERGENCE.org=: bvib
mean 1.0002--1.0004, inside the 100 bp deadband, the desk publishing no
advice for 731 days), moves K by about 0.0015 over a season.

The equity calm twins on record at =star-71ccec2= =conv-e3-eq= (all four
cells FAIL) sit in a different place. The tail bvib is 0.9805--0.9826 and K
is 0.950, the rail, for 93--98% of that tail. A residual near 2% is the
desk's leash (=leash1e9= 200e9). It is large enough for the integral to have
wound, and K is already at =kmax=, where conditional integration stops.

=doc/K-AND-OPS.org= §2 and F3 are the other half of the same observation.
The desk's job is to hold the price inside its band on a timescale of days,
so the flow sits in the desk's inventory and the price gauge reads what the
desk did not take. K reads raw bvib, not the desk's book. While the desk
holds the price, K's error is the residual. One lever zeros one of the two
integrals.

This design copies the deployed band onto the new signal and the adapter:
deadband =100e9= (100 bp), leash =200e9=, =persistEpochs= 30, =measIdx= 2,
=kappa1e9= =250_000_000=, cap 40 bp per epoch. Inside the deadband the
adapter publishes effort 0. Outside it, 40 bp a day is fast beside a 90-day
integral. The owner asks whether that band is too tight. Some error has to
remain, large enough and long enough that K's proportional term is not
nothing and the integral winds, and the runs are not showing that wind.

Widening the deadband, the leash, or the cap is a retune of the desk. PR 2
ports these numbers so the four common-mode tests match a desk that is
already deployed, and that port stands. The question is a later ruling. It
is the seam in K-AND-OPS: how much of the error the fast actuator is allowed
to remove before the slow one can see it.
