#+TITLE: The basket machine, as built
#+DATE: 2026-10-11
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

This describes the contracts that turn the equity basket: what each one is, what it reads, what
it decides and what bounds it. It replaces the proposal that carried this name and the working
notes written while the machine was built. What the machine earns, and on what evidence, is in
[[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org][the results]]. What is left to do is in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-TASKS.org][the task list]].

Two older machines still ship beside this one: the equity basket's earlier upkeep (the Deploy /
Fund / Trim facet and its turn director), kept so the two can be compared, and the pro-rata
basket with its ops shell, its director and the shadow observer. The original basket, the Fence
and the other director are gone (2026-10-11). Two parts of the machine still
build on them (see [[#names][names]]): the venue facet on the older V3 venue, the desk on the ops mixin.

This document covers the machine that turns the basket. What a depositor is promised (shares,
the exit, the basket's quarter of a gain) is the shell's, and is unchanged: see [[#record][the older record]].

In short. The basket is a credit holder whose limit counts only the BUCKs it has let out, so it
pairs every TOKEN with BUCKs of its own issue the moment it arrives, and pays an exit in BUCKs
before it sells anything. It earns by reversion: each constituent has a target that leans
against its price, and is sold above it and bought below it, on its own, when its price has
stopped parting from the others. While the BUCK is cheap the book holds some. While it is dear
the book lets none out for that.
A desk beside it, with its own grant and credit, acts only on excursions of the BUCK beyond a
band. K sets how much the basket may let out, and is the slow lever under both.

* The parts

A /facet/ is code the basket shell delegatecalls, so it runs on the shell's storage. A /service/
is a contract with its own state that the others call.

| Contract | Kind | What it owns | Who calls it |
|----------+------+--------------+--------------|
| =BuckBasketEquity= | Shell | The books: receipts, shares, wallet TOKEN, liquidity counts, the credit | Depositors, governance, the wheel |
| =BasketVenueFacet= | Facet | Nothing: Uniswap V3 reads, swaps and positions with strict price checks | The shell and the upkeep facet |
| =BasketUpkeepFacet= | Facet | Nothing: the Daily, Sync and Respond steps | The wheel, through the shell |
| =PairRebalancePolicy= | Service | Each pair's last rate and change, each constituent's urgency and allowance, the basket's own recent price impact | The upkeep facet |
| =PairSignals= | Service | One level-and-rate tracker per TOKEN per quote route, and their error statistics | Anyone (=observe=); read by the policy |
| =BasketReference= | Service | The last valid reference-basket value in BUCKs, its freshness and day count | The wheel's Sample slot, the desk policy, K's reference |
| =BasketDesk= | Service | The desk's own account, TOKEN, credit, turnover budget and capital floor | The wheel's Ops slot |
| =OuterBandDeskPolicy= | Service | Which side of parity the basket is on and for how many days | The desk and the wheel |
| =BasketMachineWheel= | Wheel | The cursor, the reserve, the arbitrage triangles | Any keeper (=tick=) |

The upkeep facet is one contract: the three step entry points, the pairing of what waits, the
book and its plan, and the sell and buy primitives. Each service and facet has its interface in
its own file (see [[#names][names]]); they depend on those, not on each other's contracts.

* The books and the mark

The basket is an ordinary BUCK credit holder. It holds one self-issued credit that it marks
itself, so Buck enforces its limit: it can draw at most K times that mark.

- /Gross/ is every TOKEN/BUCK position at fair value (twice its BUCK side, full range) plus the
  wallet's TOKEN, at a chosen price mark.
- /Equity/ is the gross plus the signed BUCK account plus the relief accrued on it. A negative
  account is credit drawn, a claim on the basket's own assets: the lien. Equity already deducts it.
- Marks are TWAP, HIGH (the higher of spot and TWAP, an entrant's price) and LOW (an exiter's).
  Every spend is preceded by a fresh mark of the credit, from equity(LOW).
- A constituent's /holding/ is the TOKEN in its position and its wallet, in BUCK. Its /target/
  is its leaned share of the book the equity asks for (see [[#targets][targets]]).

Every TOKEN sits inside liquidity. A position's value follows the square root of price, so a
holding moves about half as far as a wallet's would for the same price change.

Marked at equity, the credit bounds every BUCK drawn: lien <= K x equity. A BUCK drawn and
paired beside the basket's own TOKEN then uses the limit as if it had been spent, so credit can
pair only K of the book, and the rest must be sold or left waiting. That is how the shell is
made, and how the older upkeep runs it.

The machine marks /net/ (=netMark=, set by governance). The credit is marked at equity plus the
BUCK side of the positions over K, which makes Buck's own limit read:

#+begin_example
lien - BUCKs held in the positions  <=  K x equity
#+end_example

K then bounds the BUCKs the basket has let out, not those it still holds, which carry no price
risk against the lien they came from. Three things follow.

- Pairing uses none of the line. Every TOKEN is paired with issued BUCKs as it arrives.
- An exit is paid in BUCKs from the line, before anything is sold. One exit of up to K/(1 + K)
  of the book fits.
- The most TOKEN the basket can come to hold is what it was, (1 + K) x equity. Counting gross
  assets as collateral, the loop of lending protocols, would allow 1/(1 - K) and is not done.

Buck never calls anything back. Beyond the limit the account simply cannot spend, and an exit is
then paid in kind. Relief accrues on the whole lien and is counted in equity as it accrues. The
demurrage on BUCKs held in a pool rides with them and is borne when they come back; equity does
not deduct it until then, so a young basket's share price shows the relief first.

All of this holds while the BUCK stays near its basket. It fails when the BUCK rises against its
basket and keeps rising, as it does in half of the five-year matrix once K is at its upper limit
([[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org::*The five-year matrix][the results]]). Two things then work against the basket at once. Equity is counted in BUCKs, so
a dearer BUCK lowers what the basket may have let out. And a position gives up BUCKs for TOKEN
as the TOKEN cheapens, so what it has let out rises with no decision of its own: the BUCKs it
holds are not a fixed sum. The upkeep then sells TOKEN for BUCKs to come back under its ceiling,
which bids for BUCKs when they are scarcest. Nothing in the machine prevents this yet. It is the
first open item in [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-TASKS.org::*Toward the merge][the task list]].

* The question at K's limit
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: k-limit
:END:

This section states an open question. Nothing in it is decided. The measurements are in
[[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org::*The five-year matrix][the results]].

/What K is for./ K is how the system makes more BUCKs when they are scarce. Everyone who holds
credit may owe K times its worth. When the BUCK is dear the controller raises K, people can
issue more, and the new BUCKs bring the price down. K stops at 0.95. Past that the controller
has nothing left to do.

/What happened./ In seven of the fourteen five-year worlds K reached 0.95 with the BUCK still a
little dear. From there the BUCK rose for years, to between 4 and 12 times its basket. On the
pro-rata basket the same seven worlds stayed at parity.

/Why it should not happen./ A dear BUCK is an offer. Anyone with assets can pledge them, issue
BUCKs that are worth more than their basket, and buy other assets cheaply with them. That
selling is what brings the BUCK back. In these worlds the offer went unanswered, and the
portcast world shows why ([[file:BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org::*Who could issue][the results]] have the count). It holds about 18 million of issuing
capacity against 75 million BUCKs in circulation, and most of it was shut out or used up before
the rise began:

- The 30 mortgage debtors could not pass the funding gate. To mint against an insured credit
  the minter must already hold BUCKs worth the insurance deposit times a factor, and the factor
  is near one at parity and falls to zero only when the BUCK is 9% dear. The model gave these
  debtors no savings to buy that reserve with, which is a fault of the model, since mended. They
  were refused 59 times in 63 attempts while the BUCK sat up to 4% dear and K climbed to its
  limit.
- The three credit arbitrageurs, the only people in the world who issue because the BUCK is
  dear, had 11 million of pledgeable wealth among them and had used it all by day 600.
- The desk is small, and under the older upkeep nobody in this world turns it.

So these worlds run out of issuers, and one rule of Buck's, the gate, keeps willing ones out in
exactly the band where they are wanted. Neither is the basket's doing. Whether the BUCK holds
once there are issuers enough is being run.

/What the basket did meanwhile./ This is the part that belongs to the basket. While the BUCK
rose, the basket bought BUCKs, all the way up. Three of its rules made it.

1. What it may have let out is K times its equity, and equity is counted in BUCKs at the pools'
   prices. A dearer BUCK makes the same TOKEN worth fewer BUCKs, so the limit falls under it.
2. Its positions give up BUCKs for TOKEN as the TOKEN cheapens. So what it has let out rises
   with no decision of its own, and the upkeep buys BUCKs to come back to what it aims at.
3. The older upkeep also keeps a stock of BUCKs, sized as a share of its book.

In the portcast world the older upkeep's lien fell from 9.5 million BUCKs to 2.0 million over
the rise, and the machine's from 18.2 million to 1.3 million. Every BUCK of that was bought.

/The question./ Was the basket's buying a cause of the rise, or only a consequence of it?

If a cause, the basket turns a shortage it could have waited out into a spiral. Each purchase
lifts the BUCK. That lowers its limit. That forces the next purchase. It is a margin call, and
the design set out to have none. If only a consequence, the worlds are short of issuers and the
basket is one more of the people caught.

The pro-rata basket is the evidence that it may be a cause. It owes nothing that can be called
back, it never buys BUCKs, and the same worlds hold with it. But it also issues a BUCK for every
BUCK's worth deposited, so it is the largest issuer in those worlds as well. The runs so far
cannot tell the two apart.

/An example of rule 1./ A basket has taken in TOKEN worth 100 baskets and let out 30 BUCKs,
which it spent on 30 baskets' worth more. It holds TOKEN worth 130 baskets and owes 30 BUCKs. K
is 0.95.

| | The BUCK at parity | The BUCK doubled | Doubled, limit counted in baskets |
|---+--------------------+------------------+-----------------------------------|
| Its TOKEN, in BUCKs | 130 | 65 | 65 |
| It owes, BUCKs | 30 | 30 | 30 |
| Equity, BUCKs | 100 | 35 | 35 |
| Equity, baskets | 100 | 70 | 70 |
| Equity the limit is worked from | 100 | 35 | 100 |
| It may have let out, K times that | 95 | 33 | 95 |
| The machine's ceiling, half of it | 47.5 | 16.6 | 47.5 |
| It must buy back, BUCKs | 0 | 13.4 | 0 |

When the BUCK doubles the depositors have lost 30 baskets in a hundred, whichever way the limit
is counted: the 30 BUCKs owed now cost 60 baskets. The difference is the last row. Counted in
BUCKs, the machine must sell 27 baskets' worth of TOKEN to buy 13.4 BUCKs, in a market that is
already short of them. If that lifts the BUCK again, the sum is done again from a worse start.

/The candidate./ Count the limit in baskets. Value the basket's TOKEN at what the basket is
declared to cost, one BUCK, and not at what the pools say. A rise in the BUCK's price then
lowers no limit, and rule 1 buys nothing.

It does not make the basket safe. It still owes BUCKs that cost more than they did, and if the
BUCK went on to 4.3 times its basket this one would owe more than it holds. It gives up a forced
purchase now and accepts a larger loss later, should the BUCK never come back. A basket that
lets out few BUCKs has less at stake either way, which is what the lean on the cheap side only
now does. Rule 2 remains.

/How to tell./ Run a failing world with the limit counted in baskets. If the BUCK stops rising,
or rises far less, the buying was a cause. Separately, count who could still issue in that world
when K reached its limit, and why they did not.

* Signals

** Relative price: =PairSignals=

A price-only observer of every TOKEN on every registered quote route (BUCK and USDC in the lab).
It never trades and values no book.

- /Observation./ Each call reads every pool's cumulative tick. The difference from the previous
  call, over the time between them, is the interval's mean log price; no outlier is rejected.
  Within a route the mean across TOKENs is removed, so a move in the quote currency cancels.
- /Tracking./ Per TOKEN and route, an alpha-beta filter keeps a level and a rate. Its gains come
  from one response time (12 hours in the lab) and the actual time between samples.
- /Allowance./ Per pair and route it keeps a running mean of the prediction error and of the
  rate's acceleration. The rate's allowance is a floor, plus the error over the response time,
  plus the lag this filter is known to have for that acceleration. It is empirical, not a
  confidence interval.
- /Readiness./ A route is ready after three samples and a warm-up (two days), and falls out when
  a pool cannot be read or the gap exceeds a day. A route that fails restarts from nothing.
- /Pair./ For TOKENs a and b it reports the level and rate of a against b averaged over ready
  routes, and the envelope (lowest low, highest high) of their rates. Routes are not treated as
  independent evidence.

** Parity: =BasketReference=

The reference basket's cost in BUCKs from each pool's TWAP, refused when any pool has no
liquidity or its spot is more than a tolerance (5%) from its TWAP. It publishes the last valid
value, whether it is fresh, and how many consecutive days have had a valid sample. K reads the
same quantity through the venue. No offset or filter is applied to either.

It keeps nothing else. The seven-window EMA ladders it once carried, their imported history and
the trend gates read from them were removed on 2026-10-10: nothing in the machine reads a trend
from the parity reference.

The sign is easy to get backwards. The value is BUCKs per reference basket. Above one, a basket
costs more BUCKs: the BUCK is cheap, the desk's side is +1 and K falls. Below one the BUCK is
dear, the side is -1 and K rises.

* The rebalance policy: =PairRebalancePolicy=

The policy owns no assets and authorizes no spending. It answers two questions. Which gaps may
be worked at all is /urgency/. How much of an eligible gap to move, and when, is /desire/.

** Urgency

A fixed band is the wrong shape for eligibility: a share 1.9 points off for a month never
qualifies, and one that touches 2.1 for an hour does. So each constituent keeps a count.

#+begin_example
urgency  <-  urgency + (|gap| - slack) x time elapsed      kept between zero and patience
urgency  <-  zero       when the gap changes side
may be worked while urgency = patience
#+end_example

=gap= is the constituent's distance from target as a share of the book. The upkeep facet reports
its gaps at every response; between reports the policy carries the count forward on the gap it is
shown, for at most a day, so the wheel's view and the response agree. With a slack of a quarter
point and a patience of two point-days, a gap of 2.25 points qualifies in a day, 1.25 in two,
and a quarter point never does. A policy built with no patience keeps no count, and the basket's
fixed allocation band decides as it did before.

A trade does not restart the count. A gap that has become eligible stays so while it is
worked off, and closes the moment it is back inside the slack, because there the count drains.

This is a CUSUM, the cumulative-sum test for a small shift that persists. It decides only
whether. Desire still decides when and how fast.

** Desire

For an ordered pair (sell a, buy b) the policy returns a rate: the fraction of what remains of
an eligible gap to close per day. A rate of ln 2, 0.693, halves the gap each day.

1. /Evidence must be current./ Every route ready, and the policy's motion sample taken from the
   observer's latest sample. Otherwise zero.
2. /Separation./ The pair's log price must stand more than 1% above its accepted reference
   (governance-supplied historical means), on the filtered level and on every live pool. A ratio
   that has crossed its reference is a renewed departure, not a reason to keep selling.
3. /Motion./ =rate= is the pair's rate, positive when a is still gaining on b. =change= is its
   change over one response time. The basket's own recent price impact widens the allowance,
   decaying over two days.
4. /Braking release./ =releaseRate × release × braking=. =braking= is the fall in the rate over
   one response time against the allowance, at most 1; a rate within the floor of zero counts
   as one fifth. =release= halves at an outward speed of =releaseSpeed=, or at the rate's own
   allowance when =releaseSpeed= is zero.
5. /Closure./ When even the upper allowance is below zero the pair is closing. It adds
   =closureGain= per unit of that closing speed, and =closureRate= times its confidence (the
   closing speed against the allowance, at most 1).
6. /Ceiling./ The sum is capped at =maxRate=.

The =Sizing= (the five rates and scales above, the share of own impact counted, the slack and
the patience) is fixed at construction.

** Release

A rate becomes an amount through a constituent's /allowance/: the rate it has been worked at,
summed over time since it last traded.

#+begin_example
allowance  <-  allowance + rate x time elapsed       at every response; an unobserved stretch earns a day at most
allowance  <-  zero                                  when it trades, or is no longer worked
share of the gap to move now  =  1 - exp(-allowance)
#+end_example

This composes exactly. A day at a rate of ln 2 moves half the gap whether the basket is turned
once that day or in every block, and a turn too small to trade loses nothing: its allowance
carries to the next. Each constituent keeps its own.

* Targets
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: targets
:END:

A liquidity position sells a TOKEN as its price rises and buys as it falls. So a constituent's
share of the book moves half as far as it would in a wallet, and a basket that only restored
its declared weights would find little left to do after a large move. Its pools would already
have sold, at every price on the way.

So a target leans against price. The shell holds a /tilt/ and, for each constituent, a
/reference price/: the price it is taken to return to.

#+begin_example
target  =  declared weight x (reference price / price) ^ tilt      shared out over the book
           and held within the lean cap of the declared weight
#+end_example

Only one price against another matters. A move in the BUCK itself, or in every TOKEN together,
leans nothing. As a share's response to its own price:

| How the TOKEN is held | Share moves with price to the power |
|-----------------------+-------------------------------------|
| In a wallet, untouched | +1 |
| In the pools, untouched | +1/2 |
| Restored to its declared weight (tilt 0) | 0 |
| Tilt 1/2 | -1/2 |
| Tilt 1 | -1 |

With a tilt the basket sells a TOKEN that stands above its reference to below its declared
weight, and buys it back as the price returns. That is a bet on the price returning. The
reference price is the constituent's anchor, the price its reference quantity was struck at,
unless governance sets another (=setReferencePrice=). The tilt and the cap are governance's too
(=setResponse=); the cap must be inside the leash, so the leash never fights the lean. The
leash keeps the declared weights.

The book those shares are of is not what the basket happens to hold. It is what its equity asks
for, leaned against parity on one side:

#+begin_example
book  =  equity + aim
aim   =  - parity gain x (the basket's cost in BUCK - 1) x equity      where the BUCK is cheap
aim   =  0                                                           where it is dear
#+end_example

Where the basket costs more than one BUCK the BUCK is cheap, the aim is below zero and the book
is smaller than equity: TOKEN is sold and BUCKs held, down to the ceiling. The basket has then
taken its BUCKs while they were cheap, before the fall in K that a cheap BUCK brings. Where the
basket costs less than one BUCK the BUCK is dear, and the book is the size of its equity: it
lets no BUCKs out because of that. With no fresh reading of parity the book stays the size it
is. A cut in K moves nothing by itself; it lowers the ceiling.

The two sides are not treated alike because they are not alike (decided 2026-10-12). A basket
holding cheap BUCKs can lose what it paid for them. A basket that has let out dear BUCKs loses
without bound while they go on rising, and in the five-year matrix that was most of what its
depositors lost. A second gain, zero as deployed, restores the dear side for a measurement.

* The upkeep facet: =BasketUpkeepFacet=

The wheel turns three kinds of step on the basket.

- /Daily (0)./ Once a day: settle relief against the lien, re-mark the credit.
- /Sync (1, pool i)./ Once a day per pool: collect the position's fees into the wallet and the
  account, re-mark.
- /Respond (2)./ At most once per interval: observe, then the three things below, in order.

It runs only on a shell marked net. Nothing in it rations credit: there is no liquidity target,
no cash need and no order of who may be placed first.

** Pair what waits
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pairing
:END:

Every constituent's wallet TOKEN is paired with issued BUCKs, at once and in full. This swaps
nothing and spends no allowance. The BUCKs stay in the position, so each marking gives the room
back, and a deposit larger than the room is paired in a few passes. Nothing waits past the
next turn but the fees a position has just returned.

** Size the book and find each gap

The book is equity plus the aim (see [[#targets][targets]]). Each constituent's target is its leaned share
of that book, and its gap is the TOKEN it holds, in its position and its wallet, less that
target. The gaps are reported to the policy as shares of the book. A gap is /eligible/ when the
policy says it is urgent or, for a policy with no patience, when it exceeds the allocation band.

** Work each constituent on its own
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: working
:END:

A constituent over its target is sold, and one under it is bought. Neither needs the other:
the line carries the difference.

1. /The rate./ For a sale, the policy's desire for this constituent against each of the
   others, weighed by their declared weights. For a purchase, the same with the pair turned
   round. A constituent that nothing is closing against is left.
2. /The amount./ The gap times the constituent's =share= at that rate, then capped by the step
   (5% of its holding, or of its target for a purchase) and its pool's depth (1% a swap).
   Pieces under twice the grain are not asked for.
3. /The line./ Purchases are scaled back together so that what is issued, after the turn's
   sales, does not pass the ceiling.
4. /Execution./ A sale takes wallet TOKEN first, then burns liquidity: a position gives up
   TOKEN and BUCKs in equal value, so twice the remainder is burned and its TOKEN sold. A
   purchase buys TOKEN with half its budget and pairs it with the other half. The credit is
   marked after the sales and after each purchase.
5. The measured tick change of each pool is reported to the policy as own impact, and each
   constituent that traded has spent its allowance.

Two things act with no view on prices and ask the policy nothing.

- /The leash./ A constituent more than 30% of its declared weight from its declared share is
  worked toward its target at once, in full, within the caps. A book of BUCKs alone holds
  nothing of anything, so this is also how a cash deposit is bought into the book.
- /The ceiling./ BUCKs issued beyond half of K x equity are sold down at once, every
  constituent giving its share. The other half of the line is what exits are paid from. A
  large exit is paid first and sold down over the following turns.

Failing all of this the facet re-marks a stale credit.

* The venue: =BasketVenueFacet=

The Uniswap V3 venue with nothing optional. A missing TWAP is an unavailable price, including on
K's reference path. A swap must execute within the price tolerance of the TWAP and leave the
spot within it, or the step reverts. A position is minted only when spot is within tolerance.

* The desk: =BasketDesk= and =OuterBandDeskPolicy=

The desk is its own credit holder beside the basket: its own account, its own marked credit, its
own TOKEN. Nothing it holds or has drawn enters the basket's books. It mirrors the basket's
constituents and trades the same pools through the same venue facet.

The desk and the basket are separate concerns. The basket earns on its constituents returning
to one another. The desk deploys its own equity on excursions of the BUCK itself, which K will
in time reverse, so it is paid when the BUCK returns to its basket. It is the backstop beyond
the band that profit-seeking holders are expected to keep, and it offers depth there, at a
premium or a discount, up to the limits of its reserves. Inside the band it does nothing: that
is left to those holders, to the basket's own lean and to K.

- /Side./ It enters a side when the raw parity error exceeds the entry band (3%) and leaves when
  it returns inside the release band (2%).
- /Persistence./ It counts consecutive valid days beyond the entry band on the same side. A
  missed day, a reversal or a return clears the count.
- /Effort./ A share (25%) of the error beyond the release band, capped (40 bp of the desk's
  size basis per operation), and only while TWAP and spot agree on the side.
- /Authority./ The desk sells BUCKs it holds, or buys BUCKs with TOKEN, on any advice. It draws
  credit to issue only on advice that has persisted (30 days).
- /Guard./ After the trade the terminal spot must still be outside the release band on the same
  side, or the operation reverts. The desk cannot spend through K's band.
- /Limits./ A finite turnover budget that nothing replenishes, a capital floor on the desk's net
  value, caps on its position and on the credit it has drawn, and Buck's own limit on its lien.

* The wheel: =BasketMachineWheel=

One permissionless =tick= advances a cursor over slots, each due or not: Sample (the parity
reference, through the desk policy when there is a desk), Compute (K's cycle), Ops (the desk),
the basket's Daily, Sync per pool and Respond, and Arb (triangles between TOKEN/BUCK, TOKEN/USDC
and BUCK/USDC, taken only at a realized profit). Sample comes first so that every other kind
acts on a market already read, and no trade of the same turn can spoil the reading. Ops
publishes its observation dependency again before it acts. A failing target cannot trap the
cursor.

Arbitrage profit goes to a configured creditee. The basket accepts income from its wheel and
from one governance-appointed arbitrage creditor, which can deposit and nothing else.

* How it is deployed today

There is no single recipe yet. The lab builds the older equity machine with the simulator's
=deploy=, then swaps in the venue, the upkeep facet, the parity reference and the desk policy
(=control_lab/engine.py=), and wires the pair observer and policy separately
(=control_lab/block_pairs.py=). Both set the net mark, and the facet takes the parity reference
and its gain at construction. The general simulator builds the same parts when an experiment
names =basket = "equity-machine"= (=basket_machine.py=), sized by the experiment's =[machine]=
table, which starts from the lab's values, and with each constituent's declared price as the
policy's reference. =SIM_EQUITY_MACHINE=1= beside the older =equity-ops= does the same for a
scenario that carries no experiment. The shell is not a proxy with upgradeable storage: a new
facet needs a shell built from these sources.

The order that matters: seed and age every pool through a TWAP window; wire venue and upkeep
facet; open and fund the desk's credit; deploy the observer, then the policy with the accepted
reference means; set the desk's policy, guard, budget and floor; point the wheel's Ops at the
desk, Sample at the desk policy, Equity at the basket.

* What earlier measurement settled

Three choices in this machine were not made here. The carry-convexity program measured them on a
66-cell grid of shocks and attacks, and [[file:K-AND-OPS.org][K-AND-OPS.org]] ranks what it found.

- /K reads the price alone./ Every design that fed a position into K pinned K at its ceiling
  somewhere, and at the recommended gain an attacker who loaded the book came out ahead. So K
  here is the direct controller on the basket's value in BUCKs, and nothing the desk holds or
  has drawn reaches it.
- /The seam between the desk and K is persistence, not size./ The desk trades on any advice
  with what it holds. It draws credit, which changes supply, only on advice that has lasted.
- /K is a slow, weak lever./ It moves the stock of credit over months. Holding the price inside
  the band, now, was the job of faster facilities. This machine has one of its own, on one side:
  the basket holds BUCKs while they are cheap. Otherwise it expects profit-seeking holders to do
  it: see [[file:BASKET-MACHINE-TASKS.org][the task list]].
