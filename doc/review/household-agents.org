* Audit and choice

| Existing agent | What it models | What is missing |
|----------------+----------------+-----------------|
| Python =ExternalDebtRetireeAgent= | Partial refinancing, carrying-cost comparison, real funding gate, renewal penalties, external premiums, two cash-flow ledgers | Continuing arrivals, adaptive surplus allocation, investment from reusable credit |
| Python =BuckCreditDebtorAgent= | Same refinance kernel in carry mode; older paced/atomic modes; staggered arrivals; discounted retirement or recurring investment | Several historical policies in one class; finite cohort; no premium-driven investment |
| Python =FatCreditBorrowerAgent= | Aggregate credit use, adoption and regime schedules | No mortgage counterfactual; simulated insurance gate; burns capacity after buyback and never reactivates |
| Python =SaverAgent= | Cash-backed purchases below basket value and sales above it; randomized action probability | No insured asset, credit issuance or basket-investment ledger |
| JS =MortgageRetiree= prototype | Real insured credit, funding gate, income and mortgage ledgers | Older issuance rule; dollar-par buybacks; no recurring basket deposits or carrying-cost kernel |
| JS =BasketSaver= | Cash-backed constituent purchase, deposit, mark and timed exit | No credit or mortgage; one saving cycle |
| JS =Farmer= prototype | Mortgage and operating line, seasonal business cash flow, basket assets | Specialized business model, not a common household implementation |

The Python carrying-cost kernel is the decision baseline. The new household model keeps real
contract enforcement and separate accounts for borrowing and savings. It has the same pure
decisions, seeded parameter sampling, arrival schedule and cash-flow convention in both
languages. Common vectors test those rules; each implementation also executes against contracts.

The existing agents remain available for reproducing old experiments. New economy presets use
the household population instead of synthetic large borrowers. This avoids interpreting a
changed population, balance sheet and decision rule as a controlled change to an old result.
The older savings and trading agents still serve actors without credit; capital alone does not
grant a BuckCredit line. The new credit saver explicitly brings an insured asset.

* Decisions and accounting

The external mortgage and its untouched counterfactual receive the same starting cash, income,
living expenses and mortgage terms. Interest accrues daily; income, expenses, insurance and
scheduled payments settle every thirty days from arrival. Funds paid to the external lender
leave the agent's real USDC account. Refinancing uses actual proceeds, not a notional quote.
Insurance principal remains an asset; cash premiums, execution, switching and penalty costs
are recorded separately. Basket principal, market value and pending tokens are distinguished.
The insurance asset's mark subtracts its share of the insurance pool's accrued demurrage,
which a refund would carry back. The liability's accrued Jubilee relief is counted separately.
Unpaid living expenses and insurance remain payable in both books; a cash shortage cannot
improve net worth by silently dropping an expense. Unpaid mortgage payments remain in the
outstanding balance. No extra income is invented for the savings from refinancing: the same
income simply has less external debt service to pay.

Four surplus policies are explicit:

- =adaptive=: retire liabilities at a basket-relative discount after costs; invest at a premium;
  hold cash within the household's individual neutral band.
- =basket=: invest available surplus regardless of parity, after funding worthwhile issuance.
- =retire=: spend available surplus retiring the existing BUCK liability regardless of parity.
- =discount=: retire only at a discount and otherwise keep cash, for the previous comparison.

Refinancing may accept an execution discount when interest savings justify it. Investment
issuance additionally requires a premium after fees and impact, available insured credit, the
funding reserve, and a favorable cost comparison. It is disabled for retirement-only policies.
Buying BUCKs repays a liability by receipt; it does not burn reusable coverage. Falling K limits
new spending and never triggers a forced liquidation by this agent.

Issuance funding has priority over all four discretionary surplus policies. A household first
retains USDC for its cash buffer, switching charge and quoted prepayment penalty. It then buys
the BUCKs needed by the live funding gate, keeping partial purchases across decisions and
paydays. While a worthwhile issuance remains unfunded, =surplus_action= is =fund-issue= and
cash is not invested or spent on discretionary buybacks. The gate BUCKs stay in the borrower's
real account; they are not a synthetic escrow or a permanently locked reserve. Issuance still
requires the contract's current funding factor and credit availability.

Every decision recomputes the cost comparison; activation reads the live funding quote.
Successful issuance releases the remaining cash to the selected surplus policy. A newly
unfavorable verdict also releases cash; it does not force a sale of BUCKs already acquired.
Pending savings TOKENs can still be deposited without spending new cash. Telemetry records
=funding_pending= (JavaScript: =fundingPending=), alongside the existing =cash-wait= and
=gate-wait= reasons.

Cash invested at a premium buys a basket constituent through TOKEN/USDC and deposits it. It
does not immediately buy back the expensive BUCKs just issued. Borrowing and saving have
separate accounts so deposits cannot accidentally extinguish a liability or depend on redraw.

* Population and integration

A population is a collection of separate household books and credit accounts, with an initial
population and arrivals measured in households per year. Each has independently keyed
thresholds, patience and decision timing. The arrival stream continues through the configured
horizon, bounded by an explicit population limit. It is not a single unlimited credit account
whose balance is multiplied by a nominal population count.

Since branch =feature/household-economy= (=doc/review/household-economy-plan.org=) the
population has three roles -- debtors, insured savers and investors who hold BUCKs (and convert
them into the basket while the BUCK is dear) -- each with its own opening count and rate. The coordinator stays in the cast and decides
each morning who arrives, from shared keyed thresholds and an intensity drawn by the averaged
price gap: debtors and savers faster while the BUCK is dear, investors while it is cheap. With
=gain= 0 the same draws give a Poisson stream at the base pace. The older keys (=initial=,
=arrival_rate=, =saver_share=) still work and now split the total between savers and debtors
deterministically. Passages below that describe the population as expanded before the run, or
roles drawn per member, predate this.

The savings sandbox's background economy runs in Python. Its JavaScript controls select the
same policy and population settings used by the five-year presets. The JavaScript library also
provides the household and population agents for the in-browser EVM; both use the same policy
contract and conformance vectors. Existing attacks and shocks remain separate agents.

* Migration cost

The decision kernel and settings are straightforward to port. Most of the work is execution
and accounting: fresh funding-gate reads, separate savings custody, real lender payments,
retrying rejected deposits without losing assets, and keeping both ledgers synchronized.
Replacing the synthetic borrower is a population migration, not a one-line correction to its
burn call. Old results remain tied to their old cast. New five-year worlds need new measurements
before making equilibrium claims; unit agreement between languages is not such evidence.

** Converting the mature matrix

There are seven mature variants: =debtors-mature=, =basketeers=, =originate=, =endog=,
=endog-excursion=, =endog-raid= and =endog-excursion-raid=. Their current mortgage settings
already select the carrying-cost kernel and a mixed surplus policy, but their casts still use
=BuckCreditDebtorAgent= and five =FatCreditBorrowerAgent= instances. The canonical household
preset is a new economy with different counts and two ticks/day; the mature presets use four.
Its results cannot stand in for rerunning those seven worlds.

| Area | Already available | Work needed for a faithful migration |
|------+-------------------+--------------------------------------|
| Mortgage decisions | Cost-based partial refinancing, real lender payments, both cash-flow books | Carry over the mature APR, face, LTV and premium ranges explicitly; reconcile cash and expenses |
| Credit savers | Premium issuance and reusable coverage, separate basket custody | Replace synthetic face and cash with an explicit insured-saver cohort |
| Surplus mix | Four individual policies in both languages | Add population policy sampling for the previous 50:50 =discount= / =basket= comparison |
| Continuing arrivals | Independent Poisson stream in both languages | Set rates by incoming mortgage principal and insured face, not household count alone |
| Mature opening | Legacy Python has OTC placement | Add audited household placement and equivalent JS operations, or label a warm-up experiment separately |
| Endogenous arrivals | Legacy debtors have a market-responsive clock | Port a continuing hazard clock to both household populations, including shared vectors |
| Shocks and traders | Existing separate actors remain usable | Preserve excursion and raid controls; replace synthetic adoption/utilization shocks with explicit household shocks |
| Reporting | Household books, five-year PDF generation and linked campaign index | Add the new matrix names and replace synthetic-credit summary columns |

The most consequential choices are these:

1. Preserve the meaning of /mature/. The new household's =bootstrap= is empty: even its initial
   population refinances through the market after day 0. Simply swapping classes reintroduces
   an opening issuance event. For a matched mature arm, implement an opening placement with
   real insured activation, real funding and lender transfers, consistent actual/counterfactual
   books, and an exactly offsetting reduction in the seeder's liability. Assert that pool
   reserves and prices do not move. Put the equivalent operations in the JavaScript adapter.
   A market warm-up is another useful experiment, but must specify the measurement window and
   the counterfactual's history rather than silently calling new arrivals mature.
2. Replace the large borrowers' economic size explicitly. Their face is $2--5 million each and
   funding cash $3--6 million each; the canonical households are much smaller insured accounts.
   The canonical 24 initial members include savers, whereas the mature world has 24 mortgage
   borrowers /plus/ the five large borrowers. Match opening insured face, mortgage principal,
   cash and outstanding BUCK liabilities as separate quantities. Do not grant fictional
   mortgages to savers or multiply one account's books to mimic many households. Separate
   initial =CreditHouseholdAgent= and =CreditSaverAgent= cohorts can use their existing class
   settings; population-specific profiles would make more complex cohorts easier to configure.
3. Keep policy mix distinct from role mix. =saver_share= chooses whether a member has a mortgage;
   it does not implement the requested even split between discounted retirement and investment.
   The household population currently accepts one surplus policy for everyone. Add a stable
   keyed policy assignment shared by Python and JavaScript before claiming to reproduce the
   old mixed-policy arm. Keep all-adaptive and the four uniform-policy arms as comparisons.
4. Preserve the arrival experiment. Thirty finite mortgage borrowers over five years suggest
   roughly six new borrowers/year as a starting count comparison; the principal and capacity
   flows must still be measured. The new constant-rate Poisson stream does not reproduce
   =endog=: that arm responds to K and the BUCK's basket price. Use shared exponential arrival
   thresholds with cumulative market-dependent intensity so the constant and responsive arms
   share their underlying draws. Keep the arriving depositors' separate peg-stability response.
   Provision the population bound for the maximum hazard; never silently exhaust the stream.
   Python currently expands the population into members before the run and discards the
   wrapper. A live shared arrival clock therefore needs a retained coordinator or equivalent
   once-per-day state. JavaScript retains its wrapper. In both, income and mortgage accounting
   must start on the actual arrival day, not the provisional neutral-rate date.
5. Keep the rest of the economy comparable. Retain the 120 TOKEN depositors, 30 BUCK depositors,
   and, where present, 60 arriving depositors in the first migration comparison. Household
   investments are an additional measured flow. Keep the $50 million BUCK/USDC depth, four
   ticks/day, price path, upkeep and raid parameters. Avoid treating =save_rate= as the new
   =living_frac=: the former allocates old-model surplus, the latter pays real living expenses.
   Adding expanded populations changes later agents' numerical indices, so audit their sampled
   settings as well; a shared seed alone does not guarantee the same non-household cast.
6. Keep shocks meaningful. Legacy =uptake_shock= changes =extra_cap=, which the household policy
   does not read. Synthetic borrowers also have utilization regimes and scheduled adoption
   jumps. Define household equivalents in arrivals, income, credit eligibility or costs and
   test them; attaching the old fields would silently do nothing. The separate whale raid
   can remain the same market actor.

Recommended sequence: finish the household funding comparison, implement and test placed
openings and policy mixtures in both languages, then add separately named mature-household
and mature-household-basketeer presets. Measure those on =prorata=, =equity-ops= and
=equity-machine= before adding continuing/endogenous arrivals and the excursion/raid variants.
This is modest configuration work once the missing opening and population semantics are built;
those shared behaviors and their accounting tests are the substantive implementation work.
Keep legacy classes and TOMLs for reproduction until their consumers have migrated.
The Python household still inherits contract helpers and telemetry from =BuckCreditDebtorAgent=;
extract those helpers before deleting that class. Setting its population to zero needs no such
refactor and is enough to remove its old decisions from a migrated world.

The complete replacement matrix is seven worlds by three baskets (21 five-year runs per
seed and policy configuration). At minimum compare multiple seeds, inspect year-five issuance,
discounted buybacks, retained coverage, funding waits, mortgage costs, K and BVIB, and reconcile
every active household's real/counterfactual books. Separate opening placement from market
issuance. Run from frozen sources and require a completed vector plus its matching PDF before
marking a run successful. The household PDF already works; =matrix-baskets.py= has a fixed list
of the older world names, and the HTML index still reports the synthetic borrowers' limit.
Neither is a reason to change a contract or the lab's fingerprinted modules.

* Available implementations

- Python: =alberta_buck/sim/credit_households.py= exports =CreditHouseholdAgent=,
  =CreditSaverAgent= and =HouseholdPopulationAgent=. The ordinary simulator expands a population
  into individual accounts; the resulting members appear in =octl= and per-agent telemetry.
- JavaScript: =core/js/src/agents/credit-household.js= exports the same three classes from the
  package's main entry point. =buildEquilibriumWorld= installs their contract operations.
  =EqWorldApp= now uses these households and offers role and surplus-policy controls.
- The new five-year preset is =rebalancing-eq-5yr-households.toml=: 24 initial households,
  12 arrivals/year and a 50% probability of being an insured saver. Roles are independently
  sampled, so a particular finite population need not be exactly half savers.
- =demo-savings.toml= uses 10 initial households and 6 arrivals/year. Its JavaScript savings
  sandbox selects all four policies, arrival rates, initial population and saver share when
  creating a world. Existing sessions retain the parameters with which they were created.

The population's =agents.HouseholdPopulationAgent= table accepts the single-household settings,
plus =initial=, =arrival_rate=, =max_members= and =saver_share=. Monetary parameters ending in
=_k= are thousands of USD; thresholds and APRs are fractions, not percentages. The defaults
and validation live in =household_policy.py= and its JavaScript counterpart. Adding a setting
does not move another setting's random draw. Reaching =max_members= fails explicitly rather
than silently stopping an arrival stream.

For a JavaScript population, provide a distinct account for each member:

#+begin_src js
const households = new HouseholdPopulationAgent({
  makeAccount: () => privateKeyToAccount(generatePrivateKey()),
  seed: 0xA1BC, days: 1827, initial: 24, arrival_rate: 12, saver_share: 0.5,
  settings: { surplus_policy: "adaptive" },
});
// Add households to the world's normal agent loop after its clock and market agents.
// Its setup and act methods manage every member. Do not add a second income agent.
#+end_src

Run the new Python preset from a frozen checkout using the ordinary simulator command:

#+begin_src sh
python -m alberta_buck.sim \
  --experiment alberta_buck/sim/experiments/rebalancing-eq-5yr-households.toml \
  --out /absolute/path/households.json
#+end_src

Overrides such as =--set agents.HouseholdPopulationAgent.surplus_policy=basket= select comparison
arms. A completed five-year run automatically writes the adjacent PDF; its household pages
include credit availability, issuance, buybacks, mortgage balances and policy allocations.
The savings page retains a compact household summary in its lite frames, including actual and
counterfactual financial net worth. Pro-rata receipt values use principal as an approximation;
equity shares use the basket's share price before exit costs. Neither is a guaranteed exit quote.

* Validation and limits

Shared vectors compare policy verdicts, all cost components, sampled settings and arrival
dates across Python and JavaScript. Contract tests exercise both basket implementations,
partial refinancing with actual USDC lender payments, premium issuance by savers, discounted
repayment and reuse of the resulting headroom without burning coverage, separate savings
custody, and retrying rejected deposits without spending twice. The cash-flow books are also
compared between languages to one micro-dollar rounding tolerance.

The four policy runs at =00dfb2f= completed all 1827 daily frames on the basket machine, seed
41404, with 90 arrived households (48 mortgage borrowers and 42 credit savers). All four PDFs
were generated and matched to their vectors. Their index is
[[file:../../build/sim/households/index.html][the original household campaign]]. Adaptive and discount-only borrowers retired all external
mortgages. Always-invest left $3.351 million outstanding: thirteen borrowers never refinanced
because investment repeatedly consumed the cash needed for issuance fees. That is the funding
priority defect corrected on 2026-10-17; these saved results remain the /before/ comparison.
They are one path on one basket, not validation of the older mature matrix.

The corrected four-policy comparison uses a separate frozen campaign under
=build/sim/households-funding/=. Its [[file:../../build/sim/households-funding/index.html][report index]] lists completed results only, each with
an adjacent PDF and source vector. Do not substitute an in-progress run for a completed result.

Regression tests in both languages exercise an initially cash-constrained always-invest
household through several paydays: it saves fees, buys and retains gate BUCKs without activating
unfunded credit, refinances, then resumes basket investment. Another case withdraws the economic
justification for refinancing and checks that the cash becomes available to invest again.

A 60-day run of the new preset completed with 27 arrived households: about $2.49 million paid
to external lenders, $385,238 spent on basket investments and 109,820 BUCKs bought back toward
liabilities. Twenty deposits succeeded. Some deposits temporarily hit the basket's slippage
guard; their TOKENs remained in custody and none was still waiting at day 59. The run's PDF
is =build/cleanup/households-60d.pdf=. These are integration observations, not a five-year
equilibrium finding.

Issuance currently uses the deep direct BUCK/USDC route. Savings acquire TOKENs through their
TOKEN/USDC route. The existing arbitrageurs connect the markets; the household does not yet
optimize a split across direct and multihop routes. Credit investment is capped by utilization
and a premium after execution costs; it does not assume a guaranteed future basket return.
Defaults describe a population of insured asset holders, not the entire economy. Existing
cash-only savers, market makers, attacks and shocks remain independent of this population.
