#+TITLE: Net-balance demurrage: audit and redesign
#+DATE: 2026-10-27

* Status and scope

This is an audit of =src/Buck.sol= and its Rust math mirror, not a claim that
net-balance demurrage is implemented. The existing contract charges the raw
positive balance and advances the Jubilee on outstanding issuance. The desired
model treats accrued demurrage as already belonging to Jubilee, and bases
gross demurrage and linear relief on outstanding issuance, including BUCK in
the fund. Jubilee's demurrage paid to itself is gross activity but does not
increase its assets. The signed
balance guard described below has been corrected; the accrual and Jubilee
redesign remains to be implemented and validated. Do not use the old 50-year
relief assumptions as proof that the redesigned system is funded.

* What the current contract does

Each =AccountState= contains a signed raw balance, a =buckSeconds= accumulator,
a timestamp and flags. The sign chooses the interpretation of the accumulator:

| Raw balance | Meaning | Accumulator | Important qualification |
|-------------+---------+-------------+-------------------------|
| positive | gross BUCK held | fee-seconds | includes a fee already attributed to Jubilee |
| zero | neither held BUCK nor a drawn lien | zero by invariant | undrawn credit may still be available |
| negative | actual BUCK issued against credit | issuance-seconds | time integral of the outstanding raw lien, not a fee |

This multiplexing works only if every transition settles the old meaning before
the accumulator is reused. =_debit= crystallizes the old holding, realizes its
fee when spending into credit, then starts issuance-seconds. =_credit= first
crystallizes a lien; when the receipt crosses zero it realizes relief, clears
issuance-seconds, and starts a holding. A partial receipt into a lien retains
the existing issuance history. A zero-balance transition must leave zero
seconds. Merely activating credit is not issuance: a draw is what makes the
raw balance negative.

For an ordinary, non-Carrying positive account, the current formula is

#+begin_example
live_seconds = stored_seconds + raw * elapsed
fee          = live_seconds * BASE_RATE_PER_SEC / SCALE
net_held     = max(0, raw - fee)
#+end_example

=balanceOf= additionally includes unused credit headroom; it is a spendable
amount, not a holding or debt measure. =signedBalanceOf= excludes headroom:
for a non-Carrying account it now returns the nonnegative net holding for raw
positive, zero at zero, and the raw lien for negative. It does not subtract
unpaid relief from a lien; =redeemCost= is the relief-adjusted quote, subject
to actual fund availability. The previous positive-raw calculation did not
clamp at zero and could produce a negative signed result without a lien. The
burn solvency check now reads the actual signed raw state directly. This only
repairs the sign and solvency interpretation; it does not repair the fee
formula or authorize an undercollateralized exit.

Carrying accounts, including AMM pools and the fund, report raw via
=balanceOf= and =signedBalanceOf= so conventional pool reserve accounting can
work. The sender passes a pro-rata portion of accumulated fee-seconds along
with nominal BUCK. A recipient already below zero receives only nominal amount
less its carried fee toward repayment. Thus a Carrying balance is a nominal
quantity, not an unencumbered economic mark; pool users must understand the
fee carried on outflow. =balanceOfFees= for a Carrying account is not capped
at raw, unlike its non-Carrying counterpart.

[[file:../../alberta-buck-demurrage.org][Demurrage and the Jubilee Fund]] describes the intended model and the
reference simulation. [[file:../JUBILEE-ISSUANCE.org][Jubilee on issuance]] documents the earlier matching
assumptions; its implementation details must be revisited for the corrected
net-balance model.

* Arithmetic and the long-idle cases

At a nominal continuous rate r = 0.02/year, if no funds are added, spent,
sponsored, or otherwise moved, a natural interpretation of the requested model
is dN/dt = -r N, hence N(t) = N(0) exp(-rt) and the transferred amount is
N(0) - N(t). This is a proposed convention: the existing per-second constant
is nominal, while an effective 2% per year or discrete charging schedule would
produce slightly different results. The latter need an explicit calendar and
rounding rule. A 2% annual rate on the remaining balance does *not* imply the
whole balance has been transferred after 50 years.

For 100 initially held BUCK, ignoring rounding:

| Years | Current raw-linear net | Proposed continuous net | Proposed transferred |
|-------+------------------------+-------------------------+----------------------|
|     1 |                     98 |               98.019867 |             1.980133 |
|    25 |                     50 |               60.653066 |            39.346934 |
|    49 |                      2 |               37.531110 |            62.468890 |
|    50 |                      0 |               36.787944 |            63.212056 |
|    51 |                      0 |               36.059494 |            63.940506 |
|   100 |                      0 |               13.533528 |            86.466472 |

The current =feeOwing= continues beyond 100 at 51 years even though
=balanceOfFees= caps the ordinary account's displayed fee at 100. Before the
signed guard, =signedBalanceOf= reported about -2 BUCK: an invented lien.
The cap alone does not fix accounting. For example, just after the old fee has
reached 102 on raw 100, adding 10 fresh BUCK produces raw 110 and still owes
102, so the visible holding rises by only 8, not 10. A real incoming fresh
payment must not be eaten by fees attributed to a prior exhausted holding.
The same risk appears much sooner if an account has spent most of its net
holding and has a large accumulated fee locked in a small raw remainder. As an
example, raw 294, fee 293.5 and net 0.5 currently accrue about 5.88 BUCK in
the next year, versus approximately 0.0099 BUCK on the remaining net amount
under the proposed continuous model. The particular gross amount and fee can
change with rounding; the discrepancy is structural.

A naive change that adds =net_at_last_touch * rate * elapsed= at each touch is
not enough: frequent permissionless =settleDemurrage= calls then change the
amount charged. For constant rate, exponential decay has the semigroup property
D(t+u)=D(t)D(u), making arbitrary checkpoints economically equivalent apart
from a specified, bounded fixed-point rounding error. The implementation must
also bound precision and gas at very long idle times, dust, the =uint40=
timestamp horizon, and accumulator/storage limits; rounding must not allow
repeated tiny transfers or pokes to create value or erase charges.

* Jubilee and relief cannot be changed independently

Currently =_accrueJubilee= advances the fund by approximately
=totalIssued() * rate * elapsed=, where =totalIssued()= is outstanding liens
computed from supply and realized counters. =reliefOf= separately quotes the
same linear rate on each raw lien, capped at the lien; =settleRelief= shrinks
the lien and reduces later accrual. Relief is capped by fund cash at payment.
This matching was designed around the old raw-linear fee assumptions.

A minimal closed example demonstrates the incompatibility. Suppose 100 BUCK
is issued against a 100 BUCK lien, held untouched outside Jubilee for 50
years, with no relief paid and no other activity. Under net exponential
holder demurrage, the holder has 36.787944 and has transferred 63.212056 to
Jubilee. The current linear fund advancement and relief quote both reach
approximately 100. Crediting the fund with 100 while also leaving the holder
36.787944 makes about 36.787944 of unbacked extra value. The fund cannot both
receive only actual holder transfers and promise a 100 BUCK fixed relief in
this example without a separately identified source of funds. Distributing
fund money along the way changes the path and must be modeled explicitly; it
does not invalidate the accounting constraint.

The intended relief remains linear in each credit position's outstanding
issuance and is capped at the principal being retired. The reference model
apportions accumulated issuance-seconds proportionally on a partial reduction.
The fund's gross self-demurrage cannot be credited as additional assets;
liquid assets and realizable investment positions must support the relief
payments. If they do not, the protocol must define the treatment of the
unfunded obligation. In particular, =redeemCost= currently subtracts the
quoted relief even if payment is capped by available fund cash; this becomes
materially misleading if funding is no longer guaranteed. A redesign must
give callers an executable quote or explicitly distinguish the accrued claim
from currently available relief.

An O(1) global index and aggregate normalized economic holdings could permit
lazy account and fund accounting without enumerating holders. One account
needs sufficient state to reconstruct its gross nominal quantity and its net
economic value; proportional Carrying transfers must move both consistently.
This is a design direction, not a verified implementation. The current
=totalIssued()= is a lien aggregate. It can form the gross system-wide fee
base, but it does not by itself determine the incremental BUCK transferred
from holders to the fund: that requires separating self-demurrage. Account
mint/draw, transfer, repayment, settlement, rounding, the fund's own balance,
and simulator basket hooks all need explicit conservation equations.

* Delegation and old balances

A sponsored account routes its elapsed fee-seconds to a consenting payer,
up to the payer's remaining capacity; excess stays with the sponsored account.
This is lazy and best effort. Pending sponsored exposures are not enumerated in
the payer's live view until the sponsee is touched, and the payer may spend
before that touch. Under net decay, routing the old raw rectangle to the payer
is incorrect. The redesign must define whose net balance forms the fee base,
when capacity is consumed, how to split an interval in which capacity runs out,
and how multiple sponsees and touch ordering affect fairness, conservation,
and the results. Simply exponentiating each account independently does not
solve this allocation problem. Consider removing or redesigning delegation
only after measuring its use and explicitly deciding its economics.

An already stored positive account can have historical =buckSeconds= implying
a fee greater than raw. The past net balance cannot be reconstructed exactly
from that one linear accumulator: different deposits and withdrawals can have
the same final raw and fee-seconds but different net-decay histories. A
migration must specify a snapshot cutoff, treatment of excess legacy fees and
fund advances, and how to reconcile outstanding relief and fund assets. A new
contract deployment can instead start from a documented clean genesis. Neither
approach should silently reinterpret old seconds as the new accumulator.
Existing deployed immutable contracts, if any, do not change merely because
this source is updated.

* Required verification before deployment or long simulations

- Compare contract and Rust math with an independent high-precision reference;
  define the same convention in any Python and JavaScript model which computes
  demurrage independently.
- Cover 1, 25, 49, 50, 51 and much longer idle years, large accrued fees with
  tiny net holdings, dust, fresh deposits, withdrawals, zero and both sign
  crossings, partial and full debt repayment, relief, and fund exhaustion.
- Prove accounting invariants across positive holders, Carrying pools, fund,
  liens and realized relief. Check the fund's self-demurrage is recorded
  without being credited as new assets, and that relief is capped at the
  principal being retired and paid from identified assets.
- Fuzz time-splitting, arbitrary third-party settlement, transfer splitting,
  precision, accumulator overflow and maximal supported timestamps. Check
  Carrying transfers conserve gross and economic value to bounded rounding.
- Exercise sponsored payer depletion, multiple sponsees, ordering and
  permissionless settlement, then verify pool reserve and AMM quote behavior.
- Re-run the five-year simulations only after contract, simulations and
  reporting use the same model; existing runs use the old fee model and cannot
  establish the new model's behavior. The signed-balance correction alone does
  not validate or repair their economics.
