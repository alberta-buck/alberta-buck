* Assessment

The policy campaign finished, but it exposed a household exit defect that must be fixed before
using these runs to select a policy or certify a release. The machine holds the measured price
band in all sixteen worlds. That is useful evidence, but some of those worlds contain unintended
buy/sell flows from households whose asset sales never finish.

Keep =adaptive= as the agreed behavioral default. It is not an established performance winner.
The exit repair below is implemented in Python and JavaScript; fresh five-year validation remains
required. Next remove the old machine from the deployment path. Retire legacy contracts with
their callers and replacement checks, rather than carrying another complete legacy economy into
the release.

The original assessment is of =feature/household-economy= at =c7ef815=; the repair section records
the subsequent work. It supplements the current
[[file:household-economy-plan.org][household plan]]; older merge checklists describe earlier casts and package states. No merge,
push, tag, package publication or hosted-service update was performed for this review.

** The exit defect is economically material

All thirteen error observations say =BUCK: insufficient coverage to unwind=. They occur after
the affected member's scheduled asset sale. All thirteen affected member instances remain
unexited at day 1826, retaining a combined 2.94 million BUCK of credit limits.

The defective sequence in both implementations at =c7ef815= was:

1. [[file:../../alberta_buck/sim/credit_households.py][CreditHouseholdAgent._divest]] and
   [[file:../../core/js/src/agents/household-ops.js][household-ops divest]] set =released= before attempting the coverage burns.
2. A burn failure breaks out, leaving that flag set. Later days therefore skip release.
3. The exit sale is sized from =balanceOf=. This is spendable balance, including unused credit;
   it is not just positively owned BUCKs. With coverage still open, the sale can draw a new lien.
4. Subsequent exit steps buy that lien back, then sell from the still-open line again.

For example, =smid-dmid-revert-41404= member =0/debtor/11= starts its sale on day 747 and reports
the burn failure on day 758. Its credit limit remains roughly 426--433 thousand BUCK. Its drawn
balance falls to nearly zero and rises again; at day 1826 it is still about 42 thousand BUCK.
That world's cumulative exit buybacks reach 18.49 million BUCK, versus 0.72 million in its
always-invest counterpart. Those world totals include other exits; the member's repeated
liability and unchanged coverage independently establish the failed exit.

Some error frames display an older action such as =save= or =pay-down=: account marks and action
labels are cached for five days, while the error is sampled daily. The displayed action is not
a reliable call-site trace. Record the failing operation and its arguments with the error.

Repair requirements identified in the original review:

- Release is complete only after verifying the remaining activated coverage and deposit. Retry
  a refused release from fresh state; do not mark a failed attempt complete.
- Refresh/quote the controller-dependent burn amount. =Buck._burnAllocated= calls =compute()=
  before sizing release, whereas the agent estimates from an earlier limit and subtracts 0.1%.
  A K change between sizing and execution was the suspected trigger; the replay below confirms it.
- Sell only positively owned BUCKs when closing an account. An exiting household must not create
  fresh liability to sell; check lien, coverage, deposits and receipts before declaring it gone.
- Test a K change during release, a failed first release, aged insurance refunds, and a partial
  basket exit in both engines. Preserve any receipt shares left by an in-kind/partial redemption;
  current receipt bookkeeping removes a receipt after any successful redemption.
- Acceptance should detect overdue unfinished exits and exit buy/sell cycling, even when no
  exception remains on the final frame. Re-run the comparison after the repair.

These are agent/account-management findings. The error is not, by itself, proof that the Buck
contract's coverage check is wrong. Do not weaken it or classify this error as harmless slippage.

** Exit repair and reproduced cause (October 26)

The exact first failure is reproducible with the frozen historical source: seed 45517, history,
=retire=, member =0/debtor/10=, day 289. K changes inside the release transaction from
0.811122133739745280 to 0.809028911545070080. The agent asks to unwind 87,174.500095 BUCK;
capacity at the transaction's K is only 87,013.288510, a 161.211585 BUCK shortfall. The old
0.1% allowance does not cover the movement. The contract correctly rejects the stale amount.

The household defect is a settlement bookkeeping error, not a rational refusal to pay an
expensive BUCK liability. A household can choose when to repay while keeping its insured asset.
Once it sells that asset, it must settle the secured obligations before leaving. It cannot treat
unused borrowing capacity as sale proceeds or silently assume cancellation succeeded.

Both engines now follow the same settlement sequence:

1. Record the asset sale only after receiving its proceeds, and pay the external mortgage.
2. Collect the payment wallet and redeem savings, retaining partial receipt claims. Sell TOKENs
   in bounded trades. Successful redemptions update the books immediately, so a later failure
   cannot make a retry redeem a destroyed receipt or discard a remaining claim.
3. Buy back the actual BUCK lien from available cash, with bounded market impact. Settlement is
   required regardless of the discretionary surplus policy or the BUCK's premium/discount.
4. Refresh K and quote the exact per-credit release amount, including depreciation and deposit
   accounting. A stale-coverage rejection gets a fresh quote and up to two attempts per day.
   Other errors remain visible. Verify zero activated coverage, backing and deposit before
   setting =released=; otherwise try again the next day.
5. Sell only positive =signedBalanceOf= BUCKs. Negative balances return to repayment. Mark the
   household exited only after settlement completes; exhausted cash leaves it pending and
   records a shortfall. Small positive execution reserves remain recorded, not forgiven debts.

An aged JavaScript exit also exposed full-balance transfers racing demurrage between reading and
execution. Savings and payment-wallet collections now retain half a BUCK when transferring a
balance larger than one BUCK. The final sale uses the same existing allowance. Pending TOKEN
liquidation leaves at most one dollar per constituent. JavaScript expected-success proxy calls
now use the session's actual =ok= mode, so a failed transfer is reported instead of ignored.

Daily telemetry includes the exit start, current stage, release retries, shortfall and confirmed
release/completion. The campaign judge rejects asset-sale settlements pending for more than
30 days and completed accounts with obligations or claims left. The 30-day threshold reports
slow settlement; it never forces liquidation, deletes a claim or forgives a liability. These
checks require the new telemetry and cannot be inferred from old vectors lacking it.

The matched 315-day replay closes the formerly affected household on day 290, six days after
the sale begins, with no household errors. Its final mortgage, lien, credit limit, credit deposit
and receipt count are all zero. The original run remains unexited at day 314 with 96,728.713108
BUCK of credit limit and 9,021.485813 BUCK of deposit. Total exit buybacks over this window fall
from 270,925.870740 to 88,654.480714 BUCK. This confirms removal of the repeated borrowing and
repayment in this case; it is not five-year equilibrium acceptance.

** What the policy comparison does and does not establish

Refinancing is active, and the final accounts report no living/premium arrears. Actual mortgage
interest totals are lower than the same households' counterfactual totals in every policy group.
Some mortgages remain, including those of recent arrivals; this is a continuing population, not
the older finite cohort in which every mortgage eventually cleared.

Two qualifications prevent a simple ranking:

- =household_policy.issuance= disables post-mortgage investment issuance for both =retire= and
  =discount=. Insured savers in those arms therefore supply no such credit. The comparison
  changes supply behavior as well as the destination of surplus income. Separate the issuance
  option from the surplus option before attributing the high K to repayment policy alone.
- Arrivals respond to prices and K, so policies create different populations and exposure times.
  Compare matched members/arrival cohorts and per-household outcomes, alongside the endogenous
  economy totals. Aggregate marked wealth is not a controlled policy comparison, and marks
  are not realized exit proceeds. The exit defect further compromises these totals.

Adaptive and always-invest have very similar mean price errors here. Discount has the smallest
mean, while retirement-only consumes the most K headroom. None of that proves a universally best
household policy. Retain the settings in one model, with comparisons as diagnostics.

** Simulations and interfaces

Keep one household runner, the cross-language contract/policy tests, routing tests, a focused
control lab, and the wallet/savings interface. The separate vector viewer and PDFs answer different
questions and can remain small views of the same data. The JavaScript household CLI/demo is a
conformance and interaction example, not a second five-year acceptance engine.

The tree still has 63 top-level experiment TOMLs, and substantial legacy debtor/borrower code.
The canonical cast avoids those decision classes, but their old stress consumers remain.
Consolidate these consumers onto named economy interventions, then delete their old presets,
registrations, report fields, Make targets and obsolete tests together. Preserve attack actors
when their distinct attack has not yet been reproduced.

The new 32 stress arms are configured and tested for construction; no completed campaign for
them is present here. They cover demand/supply pressure, commodity prices, rates, income, arrivals
and investor departures. They do not yet replace whale raids, LP withdrawal or book loading.
The general five-year judge also lacks shock-specific peak, duration, recovery and attacker-P&L
criteria. A brief severe disturbance can disappear inside a five-year average or percentile.

Reduce routine work by purpose: a small centre/probe for iteration, the final population grid
for release evidence, policies when policy changes, and selected stress cases for each failure
mechanism. Keep the full control-lab sweep available for controller changes instead of running
310 diagnostic worlds after every UI or household change. The old identity demos and the JS
equilibrium page are optional consolidation candidates after their unique UI checks are mapped.

* Findings that suggest better stability and shock response

1. *Fix unintended flows before tuning K.* Failed exit cycling adds artificial buying and issuance.
   Disabling savers' issuance in policy arms also changes the supply response. Correct and isolate
   those effects before changing controller gains or increasing the K ceiling.
2. *Keep the paced basket response.* The October 19 grid on =d73f047= held mean error at
   0.38--0.66% and the 99th percentile at 1.4--1.9%. The paced book move also improved all basket
   stages in the control lab. It trades some prompt issuance for smoother price response: nine
   low-supply cells failed K headroom. Study issuer capacity, funding and arrival response in
   those corners rather than undoing pacing merely to lower K.
3. *Vary lasting money demand.* The present demand axis varies adoption time: four months, one
   year or three years. Eventually households hold the same target balances. Replace that axis
   with balance size (=money_months= or payment share); keep adoption speed as one transition
   diagnostic. This removes weakly distinct grid cells and tests a more meaningful equilibrium.
4. *Exercise the desk deliberately.* The current control-lab audit verifies 310/310 worlds but
   records zero desk operations. Test a sustained excursion crossing its entry and persistence
   thresholds, then reversal, depletion, stale observations and adverse execution. Measure recovery
   time, overshoot, reserve usage and realized P&L net of keeper costs before adjusting its bands
   or persistence. Inactivity in the current lab is not evidence the desk can be deleted.
5. *Preserve K's price-only input.* Earlier position-coupled controllers saturated and rewarded
   book loading. Those measurements predate accounting changes, so their precise profits are not
   current evidence. They still motivate a dedicated adversarial test and caution against wiring
   inventory directly into K. Prefer bounded market response and tested persistence to a new
   untested high-gain loop.
6. *Keep decisions heterogeneous and execution-aware.* Household profiles already randomize phases,
   periods and issuance/retirement thresholds. Recheck executed discount after fees and impact,
   receipt valuation, and turnover under common shocks. The adaptive urgency parameters are
   common by default; test modest dispersion and hysteresis only if corrected runs show synchronized
   churn. Include keeper fees and live liquidity reductions in the stressed execution budget.
