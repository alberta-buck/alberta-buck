#+TITLE: Simplified Buck demurrage and one Jubilee basket position
#+DATE: 2026-10-29

* Status and decision

The account and relief portions of this proposal are superseded by
[[file:credit-facility-accounting-design.org][Buck, credit facilities, Jubilee, and insurer pools]]. Activated,
issued-but-unspent BUCK accrue demurrage; an account can hold BUCK and have
outstanding credit simultaneously; and each NFT owns its issuance and relief
history. In particular, do not implement the signed-account and undrawn-credit
claims below as the intended model. The basket receipt, net deposit, callback,
and fund-checkpoint analysis remain useful with those corrections.

The basket findings below describe the code before the implementation branch:
=BuckBasketEquity= now has same-asset =increasePosition= and exact-share
=redeemShares= entry points, and uses credited net equity for deposits. See
the [[file:bucknet-economy-implementation.org][unified replacement plan]] for current validation and outstanding
manager, fixed-point, and contract integration work.

This is a historical proposal, not an implemented or audited contract. It follows
[[file:../../alberta-buck-demurrage.org][Demurrage and the Jubilee Fund]] and the available-relief reference in
[[file:../../alberta_buck/sim/demurrage_reference.py][demurrage_reference.py]]. The intended behavior is net-balance demurrage,
Carrying transfers with their proportional fee and a full-value Jubilee payment.
Its proportional-sale payout was superseded by the [[file:jubilee-marked-relief.org][marked-relief design]]:
credit reduction uses net cash plus a position mark, pays only from liquid
cash, and does not redeem basket shares in the same transaction.

The smallest practical design keeps one Buck token and the existing equity
basket, adds a small Jubilee position manager, and lets an existing basket
receipt be topped up. A separate manager adds one contract, but allows the
basket to call Buck while redeeming without re-entering a locked Buck
transaction. Do not add a second economic basket or an independent LP
accounting system.

* Findings in the present code

- =BuckBasketEquity.deposit= always mints a new ERC-721 receipt; there is no
  existing receipt argument or top-up API. =redeem(id, bp)= partially
  reduces the same receipt, retaining it if shares remain, and burns it when
  emptied. It returns BUCK paid, not the adjusted ID and actual shares retired.
- The current equity basket is the =equity-machine= economy's basket. It is a
  BuckCredit holder in its own right. It also prices its equity using unpaid
  =reliefOf=, and the wheel calls =settleRelief=.
- A deposit computes value and shares from the *nominal* BUCK amount before
  =transferFrom=. A Carrying sender's BUCK can have a smaller net value. Simply
  passing its gross amount would overstate a deposit and dilute existing
  receipt holders. A Jubilee top-up must credit its *actual net* BUCK.
  A temporary real-contract probe on this branch deposited 1,000 nominal
  BUCK after a year of Carrying age: the basket's net balance rose by
  980.000001 BUCK, but the receipt recorded a 1,000 BUCK basis and minted
  997.375 shares. The gap is separate from the ordinary entry charge.
- A receipt stores shares and BUCK cost basis in =holdings[id]=. A top-up can
  add to both. Partial retirement reduces =holdings[id].basis=, but the
  metadata in =deposits[id]= is not currently updated on partial equity
  redemption; it must not be treated as the remaining basis.
- Existing redemption uses whole basis points and treats =bp == 0= as a full
  exit. Small requested fractions must not accidentally pass zero. The basket
  can revert on zero equity or return TOKEN and/or leave shares outstanding in
  an impaired exit, not necessarily the requested amount of net BUCK.
- Buck has a single transient =nonReentrant= guard on =transfer=, =burn=,
  and =settleRelief=. Basket deposit and redemption call Buck transfers;
  the basket also owns a BuckCredit. Calling the basket from a locked Buck
  operation would re-enter Buck. The current Buck contract also does not
  implement =onERC721Received=, needed to own a safe-minted basket receipt.
- The first deposit invokes =receipt.mint=, which uses =_safeMint= and can
  call the receiver before the basket writes the new holding and total shares.
  The new manager and basket entry points need an explicitly tested callback
  order and reentrancy boundary, while preserving required V3 venue callbacks.
- BuckCredit coverage is activated per NFT, but actual BUCK draws are an
  aggregate negative signed balance per account. =mintsBacked= records
  coverage and =mintsPrincipal= records insurance deposits, not the BUCK each
  NFT individually issued. A per-NFT issuance-age claim cannot be recovered
  from those fields alone.

* State and accounting

Use BUCK's six-decimal base unit for external amounts. Amounts are represented
at higher fixed-point precision internally; all rounding rules below require
implementation and tests. Let r be the chosen nominal continuous rate and
D(dt) = exp(-r * dt).

** Account state

Keep the signed-account idea, but make its meaning explicit:

| Stored sign | Principal meaning | Second field's meaning |
|-------------+-------------------+------------------------|
| positive | nominal raw BUCK held, G | remaining net BUCK N at the checkpoint, in fractional units |
| zero | neither holdings nor debt | zero |
| negative | actual BUCK issued against credit, P = -raw | accumulated issuance-seconds for that debt |

For positive ordinary and Carrying accounts, 0 <= N <= G. Fees already
attributed to Jubilee are F = G - N. Over an idle interval:

#+begin_example
N1 = N0 * D(dt)
F1 = G - N1
#+end_example

The existing signed =int80= balance and =uint120= accumulator could remain
packed if the positive accumulator uses, for example, 40 fractional bits:
max positive =int80= times 2^40 fits in 120 bits. Negative state keeps
issuance-seconds. This is a candidate layout, not a proof of required
precision, overflow bounds, or migration compatibility. The fund's gross
nominal history and global cumulative counters should use 256-bit storage; a
long-idle Carrying fund need not fit the ordinary 80-bit account range.

Do not apply the rate again to F. Net can reach zero through fixed-point dust;
new incoming full-value BUCK increases N immediately and must not be consumed
by historical fees. When an ordinary account exhausts its net balance, clear
its fee-only remainder before starting a negative lien. A negative signed
balance is actual drawn credit, never a holder whose accumulated fees happen
to exceed its raw amount. Credit headroom remains a separate quantity.

** Two transfer forms

- An ordinary sender transfers full-value net BUCK. It leaves its existing
  fee behind; if it spends past N, it first settles the remaining fee-only
  representation and draws only the excess from credit.
- A Carrying sender transfers x nominal BUCK with a proportional amount of N
  and F. A Carrying recipient receives x nominal with that age attached; an
  ordinary recipient or an existing lien receives the corresponding net value.
  If the recipient is a lien, discard the transferred fee history only after
  accounting for it as already attributed to Jubilee. Do not credit the same
  fee to Jubilee a second time.
- Pool and other Carrying =balanceOf= must stay nominal to preserve their
  recorded reserve. A non-Carrying =balanceOf= currently includes unused
  credit; add an explicit net-held view for accounting instead of confusing it
  with debt or total issuance. Recipient net may be less than an ordinary
  ERC-20 transfer's nominal amount; wallets, AMM routes and integrations must
  expose that fact.

The single N/G ratio treats a Carrying account's BUCK as a fungible mixture:
every outflow receives the same proportional mixture of its fee history. It
does not allow selecting individual old or new BUCK. Equal net-decay rates
make the aggregate sufficient for this pro-rata rule; test mixed-age deposits
and outflows rather than assuming a token-by-token storage structure.

Provide a narrowly defined net-value transfer for the manager's deposit and
relief paths: specify desired net BUCK, maximum nominal BUCK, and return both
the actual nominal and actual net. The allowance and identity checks must
apply to the nominal amount and the real sender/recipient. This avoids asking
an existing basket =deposit= to pretend a fee-bearing gross transfer has full
value. An implementation can use an internal equivalent when the fund pays a
lien, but both paths must use the same arithmetic and events.

For continuous proportional allocation, to deliver net R from a Carrying
account with gross G and net N, send R*G/N nominal, if R <= N. Integer
rounding must deliver the required net without overdrawing. The reference's
integer helper assumes a direct proportional fee with floor rounding; current
Solidity first apportions buck-seconds and then computes the fee, so that
helper is not proof of Solidity parity.

** O(1) fund checkpoint

Maintain I, the total *actually issued, outstanding* BUCK, as a 256-bit
counter. Update it whenever a negative lien is drawn or repaid; activating an
unused credit limit alone is not issuance. Track the fund's direct Carrying
BUCK as gross Gf and net Nf. BUCK held in the basket's pool accounts are
outside the fund's direct cash, even where the fund owns a basket receipt.

Assume between checkpoints no issue, repayment, transfer into or out of the
fund, or other external source changes I or Nf. With U = I - Nf, the sum of
all *other* net BUCK held, the continuous solution is:

#+begin_example
D       = exp(-r * dt)
net in  = U * (1 - D)
Nf1     = Nf0 + net in
Gf1     = Gf0 + r * I * dt
self fee accrued = r * I * dt - net in
#+end_example

The raw increment includes self-demurrage, but the matching self fee is
already attributed to the same fund. Its net increase is only =net in=.
For 100 BUCK issued to a wallet, initially no fund cash, after 50 years the
formula gives Gf = 100, Nf = 63.212056, and fund-carried fee = 36.787944.
It does not create the extra 36.787944 of economic value. If all 100 start in
the fund, after 50 years Gf = 200 and Nf remains 100.

This saves an enumeration of holders, provided the invariant below is proved
for every path:

#+begin_example
I == sum(net economic holdings of non-fund accounts) + Nf
#+end_example

Include BUCK in the basket, pools, Notes and the insurance pool in the sum;
exclude unused credit. Since the fund's position is a claim on basket assets,
do not add its marked value a second time to the token-supply identity. For
example, demurrage on BUCK actually held by a basket pool reaches fund cash;
it can also reduce the mark of the fund's basket receipt. It is an internal
movement in the fund's aggregate economic wealth, not an extra return.

Expose I explicitly as =totalIssued()=. A candidate is to have =totalSupply()=
also report I as the economic supply, but that changes the existing API and
needs an integration audit. In either case, summing ERC-20 =balanceOf= values
does not reproduce I: Carrying views are nominal and ordinary views currently
include unused credit headroom. The distinction is already present in the
existing contract; pool, wallet, explorer and accounting consumers must be
tested against the chosen public supply semantics.

Checkpoint the fund before a change to I or to the fund's own net cash.
Normal transfers between non-fund holders that neither draw nor repay credit
need no separate global checkpoint. The account-side accrual must still occur
before their balances change. This optimization is contingent on the invariant,
correct handling of all transfer paths, and an explicit rounding budget.

Keep separate 256-bit lifetime counters for gross demurrage, net demurrage
cash actually received, net relief paid, net invested, and net withdrawals.
Their differences can be negative after investment gains. Use separate
unsigned counters or guarded signed arithmetic, not unchecked subtraction.
Account-level raw units and global book counters need not use the same type.

* One basket position

The existing equity receipt is a suitable identity for one pooled position.
Add a top-up path that shares the existing deposit pricing and guards:

#+begin_example
increasePosition(id, BUCK, requestedNet, maxNominal, minShares) -> id, sharesAdded
  1. id == 0: create and safe-mint the first receipt to the caller.
     Otherwise require the caller owns the receipt and it belongs to this basket.
  2. Capture the basket's pre-deposit snapshot and apply existing entry guards.
  3. Transfer enough Carrying BUCK to deliver requestedNet; measure actual net.
  4. Price additional shares from that net amount at the existing entry mark.
  5. Add shares and their net BUCK cost basis to the existing holding; update
     totalShares, credit mark, flow, metadata and events. Return the same ID.
#+end_example

The existing three-argument =deposit= may remain as a wrapper creating a new
receipt; avoid changing behavior for other depositors. Initially restrict the
new top-up to BUCK, which is all Jubilee needs. Mixing TOKEN deposits in one
receipt requires a separate decision about =token=, =tokenPrincipal= and
=depositTime= metadata. Check all =uint128= conversions and addition before
writing. Maintain a truthful remaining basis after both top-up and redemption,
including correcting or explicitly deprecating stale =deposits[id]= metadata.

For reductions, reuse the existing exit logic but add an exact-shares entry
point that returns the actual BUCK paid, shares retired, shares remaining, and
ID. Unlike the legacy basis-point API, zero shares must never mean redeem
everything. The new entry point can retain an empty receipt when the manager
asks to keep the same ID through a full exit; its shares and basis are zero
until the next top-up. The legacy =redeem= can keep burning empty receipts for
existing clients. If merely holding *at most one* receipt is enough, retaining
the legacy burn and creating a new ID after a full exit is simpler; decide
which identity semantics the manager needs.

A zero-proceeds execution is not a reason to assume that shares were retired:
inspect what the basket actually retired. If in-kind TOKEN is received, it is
an asset of the fund, not spendable BUCK until exchanged; track it separately.
Do not change the basket's low-K solvency rules to manufacture an exit.

One position means average cost basis. That is consistent with the requested
pooled behavior, but it changes the 25% realized-gain cut versus separate
receipts when old and new deposits have different gain/loss histories. Test
and document this as an economic policy choice. One receipt removes an
unbounded *receipt* loop; pricing and redemption can still scan the basket's
constituent pools, and an actual withdrawal can incur slippage or return
in-kind assets.

The alternative of putting special =jubileeShares= and basis directly in the
basket avoids an NFT, but couples the basket to one privileged depositor and
does not by itself solve Buck reentrancy. Reusing =treasuryShares= would mix
Jubilee assets with the basket's own 25% gain cuts and is not an equivalent
position. A wrapper around many existing receipt NFTs hides their number but
still has to manage or enumerate them. One adjustable ordinary receipt keeps
the existing pooled-share accounting and avoids those duplications.

For ordinary deployment, set the desired reserve to the administrative
fraction of the selected benchmark, initially 10%. On each eligible touch,
the manager schedules a fraction =1 - 2^(-elapsed / 1 day)= of any net cash
above that target for a top-up. Accumulate scheduled work until it reaches 1%
of the target reserve; then top up the same receipt. Clamp scheduled work to
available excess when cash or the target changes. Below target, do not deploy;
future cash income or an explicitly required redemption can replenish it.
Updating the time checkpoint while discarding sub-threshold work on every
touch would prevent deployment, so the pending amount must survive such
touches. This is a proposed controller to test, not a reason to promise a
particular realized ROI.

* Jubilee manager and settlement order

A small, public-identity Carrying manager holds the one receipt, implements
=onERC721Received=, and has narrow authority to manage its own assets and pay
credit relief. It keeps the configured reserve fraction, receipt ID and
necessary cumulative accounting. Ordinary holders keep control of their own
credit and identity consent. Deployment needs one-time wiring of the fund,
Buck, the basket and the identity registry; resolve the constructor cycle
without leaving an unprotected setter.

A credit reduction must be orchestrated from *outside* Buck's locked token
call:

1. The authorized credit holder requests a reduction. Buck checkpoints and
   computes its baseline target R0 from actual issuance-seconds; it is capped
   at the principal to be reduced. Obtain the Jubilee benchmark B.
2. The manager requests a fraction min(1, R0/B) of its receipt, when B > 0.
   For zero target there is no sale. Do not call legacy =redeem(id, 0)=.
3. The basket redeems while no Buck lock is held. Measure the actual net BUCK
   delivered and actual shares retired; retain any unretired position and
   separately account for non-BUCK output. If a redemption cannot execute,
   preserve the receipt and compute the relief from currently available net
   cash; report the sale outcome. This does not bypass low-K exit rules.
4. Re-check the relevant issuance and benchmark after the sale, because the
   equity basket's own BUCK transfers can change its lien. Use the specified
   pre-sale fraction and the agreed post-sale benchmark; stale quotes must not
   be mistaken for executed results.
5. Compute the effective relief using post-redemption fund net cash, the
   administrative reserve target and the baseline request. Cap it at the
   reduction principal and the available net cash. Send enough nominal
   Carrying BUCK to deliver that full-value relief; the holder supplies the
   rest. If the holder lacks its share the transaction cannot complete.
   Zero sale proceeds and zero relief are valid, not a fund shortfall error.

The present Python experiment makes the proposed rule concrete as
=reserveTarget = 0.1 * B=, =multiplier = netCash / reserveTarget=, and
=actualRelief = min(principal, netCash, R0 * multiplier)= when the target is
nonzero. Thus a 10.25 BUCK reserve against a 10 BUCK target uses multiplier
1.025. The multiplier is a *cash-to-target signal*, not necessarily the
portfolio's mark or annual ROI: deposits and redemptions themselves change
cash. Test split versus combined reductions, multiple creditor ages, and
different reserve deployment cadences before treating this rule as final.

The router/manager must be authorized to act for the holder without changing
identity receipt or allowance semantics. Its entire operation should be atomic:
if the debtor cannot pay, the receipt, cash, and all counters revert as well.
Alternatively, a two-transaction reservation requires explicit expiry,
ownership, accounting and cancellation; it is not the simple default.
Never call the basket on an implicit transfer into a negative account while
Buck's lock is held. Separate ordinary repayment from the explicit operation
that settles the associated relief, preserving the relevant issuance-seconds
until the chosen settlement point. This transition, including partial
repayments and whether relief follows the account or a selected NFT, must be
specified before implementation; the current aggregate signed lien does not
identify which NFT issued a particular BUCK.

If repayment precedes the explicit settlement, preserve the repaid portion's
earned target as a separate pending claim, instead of retaining all its age
against the smaller remaining lien or discarding it. A later fund payment may
then refund the holder rather than extinguish the same already-paid debt
twice. The relation of that pending claim to coverage release and to an NFT is
part of the required specification.

The fund's own basket may be the credit holder being settled. Basket
redemption can itself change that lien or trigger Buck transfers. This case
must not recursively redeem the Jubilee position or settle the same relief
again. The current equity wheel's =settleRelief= and its treatment of the
basket's accrued relief in equity marks must be updated to the executable
relief policy; otherwise marks can include relief that the fund cannot pay.

* Benchmark and simplification boundaries

The reference defines B as the sum of each open credit position's capped
unadjusted relief. It can simply enumerate its Python objects; the contract
cannot assume that sum is O(1). A global issuance-seconds accumulator can be
updated in O(1) when total issued changes and when a position is reduced,
but its *uncapped* total is not generally the same as the sum of separately
capped claims after 50 years. A saturated-position index or maturity buckets
would add complexity. For example, a sole 100 BUCK lien held 100 years has a
200 BUCK uncapped target but a 100 BUCK capped claim: the former would request
half the fund position and the latter all of it. An O(1) uncapped benchmark
is a possible simplification only if that different economic behavior is
accepted and tested. Decide the exact benchmark and rounding before claiming
an O(1) implementation; also handle zero B and a cumulative gross-demurrage
minus actual-relief balance that can become negative after investment gains.

Existing delegated demurrage moves fee-seconds between accounts; it does not
implement net-decay sponsorship. Searches found its API in Buck, dedicated
contract tests and design documents, but not the current Python or sandbox
agents. Retiring it could remove meaningful complexity, but it is a user-facing
feature. Discuss the loss of that capability before removing it. If retained,
payer capacity, multiple sponsees, settlement order and global conservation
need their own net-based design.

Keep the active equity basket, its real credit and the routing/contract tests.
The pro-rata basket and =BuckWithBasketHooks= are still exercised by older
simulation variants; retire them only after their distinct checks have a
replacement. Do not silently remove identity checks, pool reserve semantics,
the insurance deposit, or low-K solvency checks to simplify the core.

* Verification and implementation order

1. Prove the global checkpoint equations and fixed-point bounds against an
   independent high-precision model, including no-touch versus many touches,
   49, 50, 51, 100 and 1,000 years, dust, many accounts, transfers and all
   positive/negative sign transitions. Do not reuse =PortfolioMath.expWad=
   as-is: it clamps its exponent at -4, so an idle balance stops decaying
   after roughly 200 years at this rate.
2. Implement the basket top-up and exact-share redemption independently.
   Test same ID, no dilution, cost basis, 25% gain cut, third-party ownership,
   small fractions, full exit and re-entry, zero value, in-kind and residual
   shares, carrying-fee transfers and recipient net. The existing Solidity
   tests establish today's behavior, not these new behaviors.
3. Prototype the coordinator and fund with the existing reference scenarios;
   test actual post-sale proceeds, zero proceeds, fund gross-up, source and
   recipient net, negative-liability transitions, rollback, identity and
   reentrancy, and the basket paying relief on its own credit.
4. Replace Buck's accrual and reconcile BuckCredit semantics, Rust math and
   Python/JavaScript consumers. Define any migration or clean genesis; old
   raw-linear accumulators cannot reconstruct historical net accrual exactly.
5. Re-run full Solidity and Python/JS tests and the five-year economic
   campaigns with fresh frozen snapshots and PDFs. Old runs use the old
   contract and cannot establish the redesigned system's stability.
