* Purpose and status
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: purpose
:END:

This document is the internal half of phase 6 of
[[file:a-prime-plan.org][the A' plan]]: the security argument for the hiding
registration presentation implemented on branch =feature/a-prime=, written
in full so that an independent reviewer can confirm or refute it. The
external half, the review itself, has not happened. Until it has, every
result below is a claim of this branch, not a property of the system, and
the four papers say so.

What is claimed, in one paragraph. A holder of a Pointcheval--Sanders
credential on an identity scalar =m= registers an account by publishing a
blinded pair =(A, B)= and a Schnorr-family proof. The pair and the proof
together reveal nothing about =m= beyond what the account's ElGamal
ciphertext reveals, unconditionally on the credential side and under DDH
on the ciphertext side (Theorems 2, 3, 6). An accepting registration
implies possession of a credential on the encrypted =m=, and neither a
party who has seen any number of the holder's registrations nor one who
has learned =m= can produce one: registration forgery reduces to PS
unforgeability in the committed-message key form, in the random-oracle
model, with the usual forking loss (Theorems 4, 5). Fresh randomness per
registration is security critical (Section [[#fresh]]).

What the reviewer is asked to do is in Section [[#checklist]].

** Keys and issuance

Issuer secret =(x, y)=, public key

#+begin_example
X = x*g2,   Y = y*g2,   Y1 = y*G
#+end_example

The registry accepts a key only if =Y1 != O= and =e(Y1, g2) = e(G, Y)=
(=trustIssuer=). Issuance is unchanged from the plain scheme: on identity
scalar =m= the issuer samples =h = t*G= for uniform nonzero =t= it does not
disclose and returns =sigma = (h, (x + m*y)*h)=. The credential never leaves
the wallet.

** Presentation

For each account the wallet samples independent uniform nonzero =a, b, sk, r=
and publishes

#+begin_example
A  = a*sigma_1
B  = a*sigma_2 + b*Y1        = x*A + y*(m*A + b*G)
pk = sk*G
E  = (R, C) = (r*G, m*G + r*pk)
#+end_example

and discards =a, b= after proving. Write =P = m*A + b*G=; then
=B = x*A + y*P=.

** Registration proof

Witness =(m, b, r, sk)=, nonces =(m_t, b_t, r_t, sk_t)= uniform in =Z_q=,
context =ctx = (registrant, chainid, registry, DOMAIN)= with
=DOMAIN = keccak("AlbertaBuck/FiatShamir/IdentityRegistry/Register/v3")=:

#+begin_example
C1    = m_t*A + b_t*G
T_C   = m_t*G + r_t*pk
T_R   = r_t*G
T_key = sk_t*G
e     = H(A, B, R, C, pk, C1, T_C, T_R, T_key, ctx) mod q
s_m   = m_t + e*m,   s_b = b_t + e*b,   s_r = r_t + e*r,   s_sk = sk_t + e*sk
#+end_example

Published: =(A, B), pk, E, (e, s_m, s_b, s_r, s_sk, C1, T_C, T_R, T_key)=,
plus the issuer's address, which selects the key.

** Verifier

#+begin_example
(g)   reject unless A, B, pk, R are not O; all points on the curve;
      e, s_m, s_b, s_r, s_sk canonical (< q); registrant unregistered;
      issuer trusted
(fs)  e == H(A, B, R, C, pk, C1, T_C, T_R, T_key, ctx) mod q
(b)   s_m*G + s_r*pk == e*C + T_C
(c)   s_r*G          == e*R + T_R
(k)   s_sk*G         == T_key + e*pk
(a')  e(s_m*A + s_b*G - C1, Y) * e(e*A, X) * e(-e*B, g2) == 1
#+end_example

Credential-based contract binding uses the identical proof with the target
contract as =registrant=.

* Assumptions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: assumptions
:END:

- *DLog on G1.* Standard.
- *DDH on G1.* Distinguishing =(G, uG, vG, uvG)= from =(G, uG, vG, wG)=.
  Used only for the ciphertext lock (Theorem 6).
- *PS-CM: EUF-CMA of the Pointcheval--Sanders single-message scheme with
  the committed-message public key.* Pointcheval and Sanders, "Short
  Randomizable Signatures", CT-RSA 2016, Section 6.1, publish
  =(G, Y = y*G; g2, X = x*g2, Y~ = y*g2)= and let the signer sign
  commitments =C = t*G + m*Y=; the user unblinds to =(h, (x + m*y)*h)=. A
  plain-message signing oracle is the special case =t = 0=, so an
  adversary against our scheme, whose oracle signs plain messages under the
  same public-key distribution, is weaker than the Section 6.1 adversary.
  The paper proves Section 6.1 EUF-CMA under its Assumption 2, an
  interactive assumption in type-3 groups. The 2018 reassessment (CT-RSA
  2018) gives non-interactive q-type assumptions for modified variants;
  whether one of them covers this exact key form is a question for the
  reviewer (checklist item 1). Nothing below depends on q-SDH.
- *Random-oracle model* for =H= (keccak256 over the transcript words),
  including the programming used by the simulator.

No assumption is made about the issuer beyond honesty of issuance: the
issuer holds =(x, y)= and can forge signatures at will, so registration
unforgeability is stated against every party /except/ the issuer, and
issuer-blindness is stated against the issuer.

* Definitions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: definitions
:END:

** Presentation hiding (game HIDE)

The adversary chooses the issuer key =(x, y)= (it may be the issuer), two
scalars =m_0, m_1=, and valid credentials =sigma^0, sigma^1= on them. The
challenger samples uniform nonzero =a, b=, a bit =beta=, and returns
=(A, B) = (a*sigma^beta_1, a*sigma^beta_2 + b*Y1)= together with
=P = m_beta*A + b*G=. The adversary outputs a guess. Advantage
=|Pr[guess = beta] - 1/2|=.

Returning =P= makes the game at least as strong as anything the
transcript reveals (Theorem 3 shows the transcript is a function of
=(A, B, P)= plus the ciphertext).

** Registration unforgeability (game RU)

Challenger samples =(x, y)=, publishes =(X, Y, Y1)=. The adversary =A= has:

- =Sign(m)=: a credential on =m= of its choice; the set of queried messages
  is =Q=.
- =Present(m, ctx)= for any =m=, including ones not in =Q=: a complete
  honest registration transcript of =m= with fresh =(a, b, sk, r)= and the
  given context, i.e. =(pk, E, A, B, pi)=. The adversary also receives
  =m= itself. This models a party who has seen the target's registrations
  and has learned the target's identity scalar (every counterparty does).
- The random oracle.

=A= wins if it outputs an accepting transcript
=(ctx*, pk*, E*, A*, B*, pi*)= whose extracted witness (Theorem 4) has
=m* not in Q=. Note that =m*= may be a message it obtained /presentations/
of; only credentials count as queries.

This single game covers both "registration soundness" (no credential on
the encrypted identity) and the review's R3 (harvesting: =m= known,
transcripts seen).

** Issuer-blindness (game IB)

The adversary is the issuer: it holds =(x, y)= and chooses two certified
identities =m_0, m_1= with their credentials. The challenger registers one
of them at random with fresh =(a, b, sk, r)= and honest randomness,
returning the full public transcript =(pk, E, A, B, pi)= with a context of
the adversary's choice. Advantage as in HIDE. Many registrations are
handled by a hybrid over them.

* Theorems
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: theorems
:END:

** Theorem 1 (completeness)

For every valid credential, every nonzero =a, b= and every =sk, r=, the
honest transcript is accepted. Proof: =s_m*A + s_b*G - C1 = e*(m*A + b*G)
= e*P=, and by bilinearity =e(B, g2) = e(a*sigma_2, g2) * e(b*Y1, g2) =
e(A, X + m*Y) * e(b*G, Y) = e(A, X) * e(P, Y)=; raising to =e= gives (a').
(b), (c), (k) are the Schnorr identities. =A != O= because =sigma_1 != O=
and =a != 0=.

** Theorem 2 (hiding)

Every adversary in HIDE, unbounded, has advantage at most =1/q=.

/Proof./ Fix =beta=. =A = a*sigma^beta_1= is uniform on =G1 \ {O}= because
=t -> t*sigma_1= is a bijection of =Z_q^*= onto =G1 \ {O}=. Conditioned on
=A=, the map =b -> m_beta*A + b*G= is a bijection =Z_q -> G1=, so for =b=
uniform on =Z_q^*= the point =P= is uniform on =G1 \ {m_beta*A}=. Finally
=B = x*A + y*P= is a function of =(A, P)= (Theorem 1's identity). So the
distribution of =(A, B, P)= for =beta = 0= and for =beta = 1= differ only
in which single point =P= avoids; their statistical distance is at most
=1/q=. Nothing else in the challenger's output depends on =beta=.
=QED=

/Remarks./ (i) The adversary knows =x, y=: the result is unconditional
and holds against the issuer. (ii) The exclusion =b != 0= is deliberate;
=b = 0= would publish a rerandomized signature, which is verifiable on
=m= by anyone holding =m=. (iii) For every candidate =m_i= the point
=P - m_i*A= is a well-formed =b_i*G=, so no candidate is distinguished by
any relation among public values.

** Theorem 3 (zero knowledge)

Given =(A, B, P, pk, E, ctx)= and a challenge =e=, the simulator draws
=s_m, s_b, s_r, s_sk= uniform in =Z_q= and sets

#+begin_example
C1 = s_m*A + s_b*G - e*P,   T_C = s_m*G + s_r*pk - e*C,
T_R = s_r*G - e*R,          T_key = s_sk*G - e*pk.
#+end_example

The simulated transcript is distributed identically to an honest transcript
with the same challenge. In the random-oracle model the NIZK is
zero-knowledge: the simulator programs =H= at the transcript to =e=,
aborting if that point was queried before, which happens with probability
at most =q_H / q= because =C1= is uniform and fresh.

/Proof./ In an honest transcript with fixed witness and challenge, each
response is a uniform nonce plus a constant, hence uniform and independent
of the others, and each commitment is the unique point solving its
verification equation for those responses. The simulator samples the same
response distribution and solves the same equations. =QED=

/Remark (what the transcript reveals)./ =P= is public:
=P = e^-1 * (s_m*A + s_b*G - C1)=. The transcript is therefore a
deterministic function of =(A, B, P, pk, E, ctx)= and simulator
randomness; combined with Theorem 2, the credential side of a registration
carries no information about =m=.

** Theorem 4 (special soundness and composition)

From two accepting transcripts with the same
=(A, B, pk, E, C1, T_C, T_R, T_key, ctx)= and challenges =e != e'= one
computes =m* = (s_m - s'_m)/(e - e')= and likewise =b*, r*, sk*= such that

#+begin_example
pk = sk* * G,   R = r* * G,   C = m* * G + r* * pk,
e(A, X + m* * Y) = e(B - b* * Y1, g2).
#+end_example

/Proof./ Divide the two instances of (a') and raise to =(e - e')^-1=:
=e(m* * A + b* * G, Y) * e(A, X) = e(B, g2)=. Since =Y1 = y*G= (enforced at
=trustIssuer=), =e(b* * G, Y) = e(b* * Y1, g2)=, which rearranges to the
stated equation; =A != O= by (g). Relations (b), (c), (k) extract =r*,
sk*= and the /same/ =m*= because the nonce =m_t= is shared between =C1= and
=T_C= and =r_t= between =T_C= and =T_R=. =QED=

Consequently =(A, B - b* * Y1)= is a plain PS signature on the =m*=
encrypted in =E= under a key whose secret is =sk*=.

** Theorem 5 (registration unforgeability, from PS-CM)

Let =A= be an adversary in RU making =q_H= random-oracle queries and =q_P=
presentation queries, whose accepting output has extracted =m* not in Q=
with probability =eps=. There is a PS-CM forger =B= with success
probability at least =eps^2 / q_H - (q_P + q_H + 1)/q - negl=.

/Proof./ =B= receives the PS-CM public key =(X, Y, Y1)= and a signing
oracle =S=, and forwards the key to =A=.

- /Sign queries./ =B= forwards them to =S= and records =Q=.
- /Presentation queries./ =B= must simulate registrations of identities
  it holds no credential for. It queries =S= once, on a message =m_d=
  chosen uniformly at random, obtaining =sigma_d = (h_d, (x + m_d*y)*h_d)=.
  For =Present(m, ctx)= it samples fresh =a, b, sk, r=, sets
  =A = a*h_d=, =B = a*sigma_{d,2} + b*Y1 = x*A + y*(m_d*A + b*G)=,
  =P := m_d*A + b*G=, =pk = sk*G=, =E = (r*G, m*G + r*pk)= using the real
  =m= (which is public to =A= anyway), and simulates =pi= with the
  simulator of Theorem 3, programming =H= at the transcript.
- /Forgery./ When =A= outputs an accepting transcript whose challenge was
  the =i=-th oracle query, =B= rewinds =A= to that query with a fresh
  answer (the forking lemma); with probability about =eps^2 / q_H= it
  obtains a second accepting transcript with the same commitments and a
  different challenge, extracts =(m*, b*)= by Theorem 4, and outputs
  =(m*, (A*, B* - b* * Y1))=.

/Indistinguishability of the simulation./ In a real =Present= answer,
=(A, B, P)= has =A= uniform nonzero, =P= uniform on =G1 \ {m*A}=, and
=B = x*A + y*P=. In the simulation =A= is again uniform nonzero (a
rerandomization of =h_d=), =P= is uniform on =G1 \ {m_d*A}=, and
=B = x*A + y*P=. The two differ by at most =1/q= per query (Theorem 2's
argument). The ciphertext =E= is formed identically. The proof is
simulated, which by Theorem 3 is exact given =(A, B, P, pk, E, ctx)= up to
the oracle-programming abort. The relation "=P = m*A + b*G= for the =m=
inside =E=" is not checkable by anyone without =b=, since every =m=
admits some =b=; so nothing in =A='s view distinguishes the simulated
world from the real one beyond =q_P/q + q_H/q=.

/Validity of the forgery./ By Theorem 4 the pair verifies as a PS signature
on =m*=. =A= wins only if =m* not in Q=. The one message =B= queried that
=A= does not see is =m_d=; since =A='s view is independent of =m_d= up to
the statistical distance above, =Pr[m* = m_d] <= 1/q + q_P/q=. Hence the
output is a forgery on a message never queried to =S=. =QED=

/What this closes./ A party that has learned =m= from a receipt and holds
every registration of the target (review finding R3), a party that copies
a registration to another address or chain (=ctx= is in the transcript,
and the proof needs =sk= and =b= anyway), and a party with no credential
at all (soundness) are all instances of =A=. The earlier corollary in the
proofs document that argued non-reusability through a CDH instance is
superseded by this reduction; the CDH remark remains true but is no longer
load-bearing.

/What it does not close./ The issuer, who can sign. Two credentials on the
same identity issued to different people (duplicate issuance) are a
certification-policy problem, not a cryptographic one.

** Theorem 6 (issuer-blindness, from hiding, zero knowledge and DDH)

Every PPT adversary in IB has advantage at most =Adv_DDH + 1/q + q_H/q=
per registration.

/Proof (hybrids)./ =H_0=: the real game with =beta=. =H_1=: replace =pi= by
the simulated proof from =(A, B, P, pk, E, ctx)=; identical up to the
programming abort (Theorem 3). =H_2=: replace =(A, B, P)= by
=(U_1, x*U_1 + y*U_2, U_2)= with =U_1= uniform nonzero and =U_2= uniform;
statistical distance at most =1/q= (Theorem 2), and computable by the
challenger since the adversary supplied =(x, y)=. In =H_2= the only object
that depends on =beta= is =E = (r*G, m_beta*G + r*pk)= with =pk = sk*G=
for secret =sk, r=: the ElGamal encryption of a known plaintext under a
fresh key, whose indistinguishability is the plaintext-checking problem,
i.e. deciding whether =(G, pk, R, C - m_beta*G)= is a DDH tuple. =QED=

/Scope./ The theorem is about the registration transcript. It says nothing
about the issuer's address (which identifies the anonymity set), about
timing or gas, or about identity-derived Note keys (review findings R5,
R6).

* Fresh randomness is security critical
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: fresh
:END:

Each of the following is an explicit break if the wallet reuses a value:

- *=b= reused across two registrations of one identity:*
  =P_1 - P_2 = m*(A_1 - A_2)=, so a holder of a candidate =m_i= tests
  =P_1 - P_2 - m_i*(A_1 - A_2) == O=. Links the accounts and identifies the
  identity.
- *=a= reused with the same credential:* =A_1 = A_2=; links the accounts.
- *=m_t= reused across two proofs:* =s_m - s'_m = (e - e')*m= reveals =m=
  to everyone.
- *=b_t= reused:* reveals =b=, after which =B - b*Y1= is a verifiable
  signature on =m=, i.e. the credential is published.
- *=r_t=, =sk_t= reused:* reveal =r= (hence =M= from =C - r*pk=) and =sk=.

The wallet draws all six from the platform CSPRNG (=rand_scalar=). A
reviewer may prefer deterministic derivation of the nonces from the
witness and the transcript in the style of RFC 6979, which removes the
RNG from the trusted base for =m_t, b_t, r_t, sk_t=; =a= and =b= must
still be fresh per registration.

* Verifier-level checks and why each exists
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: checks
:END:

- *=A != O=* (critical). With =A = O= the term =e(e*A, X)= vanishes and
  (a') reduces to =e(s_b*G - C1, Y) = e(e*B, g2)=, which =B = b*Y1=,
  =C1 = b_t*G= satisfy for every =m=: a registration with no credential.
  Reachable by choosing =a = 0=.
- *=B != O=* (defensive). =B = O= means =x*A = -y*P=; producing such a pair
  needs =x/y=. Rejected anyway so that no degenerate encoding is accepted.
- *=pk != O=* (critical). Relation (k) is vacuous at =pk = O=: no account
  key ownership.
- *=R != O=* (critical). =r = 0= makes =C = m*G=, publishing the identity
  point.
- *Canonical scalars.* Two encodings of one proof would otherwise be
  accepted; hygiene, since =ctx= already prevents replay.
- *On-curve and subgroup.* =G1= has prime order on BN254, so on-curve
  suffices for =G1= inputs; the pairing precompile rejects =G2= points
  outside the subgroup. =X, Y= are governance-set.
- *=Y1= consistency at =trustIssuer=.* Theorem 4's last step needs
  =Y1 = y*G=. A wrong =Y1= cannot help a forger because the verifier never
  multiplies by =Y1=; it would only make honest presentations fail, and
  the check prevents that.
- *Fiat--Shamir coverage.* The transcript hashes every public point of the
  statement, all four commitments, the registrant, the chain id, the
  registry address and the versioned domain. The issuer address is /not/
  hashed: the proof is bound to the issuer's key through equation (a')
  itself, so a proof cannot verify under a different key, and two issuers
  publishing the same key are indistinguishable by construction.
- *Registrant = =msg.sender= (or the target contract for binding).* The
  proof is useless to any other address.
- *Storage, not calldata, for later use of =E= and =pk=.* Downstream
  proofs (approve, spend) read the registered values.

* What this argument does not cover
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scope
:END:

- The identity-derived A1/A2 Note receiving keys (R5) and the issuance,
  policy and accumulator metadata (R6) of the follow-up review.
- Duplicate issuance by a corrupt issuer; the optional holder secret =u=.
- Wallet-side leakage: RNG quality, side channels, backup formats that
  might export =sigma= (any such export reintroduces R3).
- The pre-launch launch gate is unaffected: nothing is on chain to
  migrate, and nothing should be until the review closes.
- Post-quantum: the ciphertext lock is DDH; the credential lock is
  information-theoretic but the signature scheme is pairing-based.

* Review checklist
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: checklist
:END:

For the independent reviewer, in the order that matters.

1. *Assumption.* Confirm that the key form =(G, Y1; g2, X, Y)= with plain
   signing is the Section 6.1 scheme of PS 2016 with =t = 0=, and that
   its EUF-CMA proof under Assumption 2 applies; say whether a
   non-interactive assumption from PS 2018 covers it.
2. *Theorem 2.* Check the bijection argument and the =1/q= accounting;
   confirm that returning =P= to the adversary makes the game at least as
   strong as the real transcript (Theorem 3's remark).
3. *Theorem 3.* Check that the four verification equations determine the
   four commitments from the responses, and the ROM programming abort
   bound.
4. *Theorem 4.* Check the division step and that =Y1 = y*G= is the only
   place =Y1= enters; check the shared-nonce composition.
5. *Theorem 5.* This is the load-bearing reduction. Check the simulation
   of presentations from a signature on an unrelated message, the
   statistical-distance accounting, the treatment of =m_d=, and the
   forking-lemma application (which query index is forked; the
   commitments are fixed before the challenge).
6. *Theorem 6.* Check the hybrid order and that the final hybrid is
   exactly ElGamal plaintext-checking under a fresh key.
7. *Code against specification.* =src/IdentityRegistry.sol=:
   =_verifyCredential= (guards, canonical scalars, revert strings),
   =_fsRegister= (word order and count against =nizk.py=),
   =_checkPresentation= (the three pairs, the negations, the =-e*B=

   the =pk != O= and =R != O= rejections are present in the Solidity path,

   overload still rejects nonzero leaves.
8. *Ports.* =core/rust/buck-identity/src/nizk.rs= and
