* Purpose and current state

This is the single authoritative sequence, checklist and handoff for replacing
=src/Buck.sol= with the improved =BuckNet= implementation, migrating the
existing basket machine and wheel, and validating the five-year economy and
both sandboxes. The immediate next milestone is *Buck API compatibility*.
Completed items and evidence are recorded here, not independently in the
older checklists. Update this plan and commit each independently reviewable,
tested milestone; preserve the historical progress table below.

Continue in =feature/bucknet-economy= at
=.claude/worktrees/bucknet-economy=, or a fresh branch from it if isolation is
needed. Do not modify the unrelated working-tree changes in the main checkout.
At =475b2427=, =src/Buck.sol= is still the signed-balance legacy token;
=src/BuckNet.sol=, its ledger, per-NFT credit and separate Jubilee fund are
an experimental clean-genesis implementation. The legacy token is still used
by the actual economy and sandboxes. No leveraged BuckNet five-year campaign
has run, and the new token has not been deployed as a production replacement.

A Python/Anvil and JS/Tevm cash-only stack, real-identity diagnostic, market
and household operation primitives exist. The last combined smoke recorded in
the log covers 26 Python and 20 JS cases; newer position-mark tests are
standalone. The production-registry net basket is not yet bound for deposits,
the existing household lifecycle and durable restore are incomplete, and the
net basket cannot yet operate its own credit. The cash-only setup is a
diagnostic, not the mature leveraged economy.

* Decisions and invariants to preserve

- Ordinary =balanceOf= is actual net spendable BUCK, including issued but
  unspent BUCK; unissued capacity is separate. An ordinary receipt or transfer
  never silently retires the recipient's NFT principal. Holding and issuance
  can coexist and cannot be faithfully collapsed into one signed balance.
- Ordinary and Carrying accounts decay on net value at the implemented
  continuously applied 2% annual rate. Carrying services expose nominal
  ERC-20 balances, transfers, allowances and events, passing their pooled
  carried fee proportionally; users bear that fee. Restrict this mode to
  approved BUCK-neutral services after the allowlist is designed and tested.
  Such an approved service may own a BuckCredit NFT and issue and retire
  credit itself. The current prototype rejects Carrying facility owners; this
  is an implementation requirement, not an existing capability.
- A successful compatible =mint(N)= increases the caller's actual
  =balanceOf= by at least =N= raw units; a successful =burn(N)= decreases it
  by at least =N=, after the insurer premium or refund and Jubilee relief.
  Ordinary callers use net units and approved Carrying callers use nominal
  units. Otherwise the operation reverts atomically; do not silently deliver a
  smaller balance change. The =uint256.max= sentinel retains its separate
  maximum-available meaning. Issued wallet BUCK and unissued facility room
  are distinct: this balance change does not promise an equal change in room.
- Each NFT owns its issued principal and capped issuance-seconds; baseline
  relief grows linearly at 2% per year of issued principal, up to 100% at
  average issuance age 50 years. Actual relief is the selected claim's share
  of Jubilee's *net* cash plus its marked position, capped at retired
  principal; it can differ from the target. Gross fund self-demurrage is not a
  second source of net value. The claim benchmark includes deferred matured
  principal until actual burn.
- A normal reduction or renewal pays full-value relief only from already
  liquid net Jubilee BUCK, and *reverts* if its calculated amount breaches the
  liquidity guard. A shared per-block budget also limits the combined relief
  of reductions and renewals plus deferred Jubilee burns to 10% of a
  pre-outflow net-cash snapshot. Do not clamp the quote, sell basket positions
  inside the burn, or manufacture BUCK. An oversized reduction is retried in
  smaller pieces while the wheel can rebalance and replenish liquidity; a new
  block alone does not ensure that a wheel tick has occurred.
- At 50 years of average issuance age, the owner can release the NFT and lien
  without paying an illiquid Jubilee shortfall; the fund assumes the unburned
  issuance and burns it later. The original insurer's nominal, fee-carrying
  refund remains a separate claim if that pool is illiquid. Do not subject
  lien release to the ordinary 10% cash cap or erase deferred principal.
- Insurance deposits are full-value BUCK sent to the relevant insurer's pool;
  refunds are nominal BUCK from that same pool with its average carried fee.
  In-place renewal requires owner and insurer consent and cannot worsen an
  existing K shortfall.
- Retain identity, proof, receipt-fragment, allowance and event behavior.
  Preserve the low-K basket exit/solvency checks; outside holders buying
  discounted BUCK and retiring obligations supply the corrective demand.
  Never make an exit pass by inventing relief, proceeds or capacity.
- The existing basket's wheel, valuation and position interfaces are the
  starting point for Jubilee's mark and investment/withdrawal work. Reuse or
  extend those components; do not build a second basket, price engine or
  independent upkeep system merely to integrate the fund. Relief uses the
  last valid mark published by that wheel. At the start of a burn, invoke a
  valuation-only wheel entry to refresh it without executing trades or
  rebalancing under the token lock. Guard price and position consistency;
  liquid fund cash is read separately at settlement.

* 1. Next milestone: Buck-compatible token API

- [ ] Restore =mint(uint256)= and =mint(uint256,uint256[])= as atomic token
  entry points; preserve explicit NFT order, cheapest-premium-first default,
  =uint256.max= issuance and the established =Minted= event's meaning.
  Translate to actual per-NFT issuance, insurer deposit and spendable balance;
  validate owner, identity, K, claim freshness, pool, account bounds and
  premium reserve before any effects. Capture the pre-mint reserve once for a
  multi-NFT call so BUCK issued by one slice cannot fund another slice's gate.
  Verify the actual post-call =balanceOf= increase, in the caller's units, is
  at least the requested amount, or revert. This includes the premium and
  rounding and applies after an existing K shortfall as well.
- [ ] Restore =burn(uint256)= and =burn(uint256,uint256[])= as atomic token
  entry points, retaining explicit NFT order and most-expensive-first default.
  Map the requested actual =balanceOf= decrease to the correct per-NFT
  principal, coverage, marked relief and nominal insurer refund. Check the
  actual result after rounding; if it is less than requested or any required
  relief/refund is unavailable, revert the whole call. Never assume the
  requested balance decrease equals principal. Keep a direct, bounded
  per-facility interface for precise callers and investigate a maximum
  principal/coverage bound for callers concerned about over-retirement.
- [ ] Permit an *approved neutral Carrying service* to own and use a facility.
  Design and enforce the service authorization before enabling this path,
  including safe delisting and access to settlement; a registry identity
  binding alone is not an allowlist. Preserve its gross nominal balance and
  its actual net value, pooled carried fee, full-value premium payment and
  proportionate nominal insurer refund. A fresh premium must not be diluted by
  the owner's old pooled fees: assess allocating the freshly issued premium
  directly to its insurer without bypassing identity or event requirements.
  Use full net economic value for the funding reserve; verify this choice and
  rounding with independent Carrying-owner vectors before implementation.
- [ ] Coordinate one-call burn with the external fund without changing the
  authenticated owner or weakening locks. Refactor the current separate
  authorize/fund-callback path rather than using =this.issue= or a forwarding
  contract that changes =msg.sender=. Guard the entire transition, insurer
  preparation, claims, fund, NFT and token updates against reentrancy; a
  failure at any point must leave every participant unchanged.
- [ ] At burn preflight, request the wheel's valuation-only refresh while
  preserving Buck's non-reentrancy. This must not execute an ordinary wheel
  work step, transfer BUCK, redeem the fund's position, perform swaps, or pay
  a keeper reward through the token. Ensure the mark used for relief is the
  one just published for the actual fund position and remains valid after any
  insurer callbacks; reorder or revalidate if a callback can change it.
  The current legacy Buck and BuckNet do not call a wheel on burn today.
- [ ] Reinstate =quoteMint= and =quoteBurn= with their old argument and return
  shapes and execution-consistent rounding. Add a richer quote for per-NFT
  principal, coverage, refund, actual net relief, owner contribution and
  available liquidity; distinguish a quote from a guaranteed execution.
  Specify zero, duplicate IDs, bounded work and maximum amounts with tests.
  The burn quote should expose whether the requested visible balance change
  fits the shared block budget and the operation-level liquidity limit, and
  help callers choose a feasible smaller request without silently clipping it.
- [X] Restore =creditLimit=, =mintsBacked= and =mintsPrincipal= with units and
  per-facility constraints documented. A two-facility test covers inactive
  coverage, a premium deposit, a K change and partial settlement.
- [ ] Continue exposing distinct wallet, principal, age, fund nominal/net and
  actual relief views. Replace consumers of signed balances rather than
  inventing a signed value that hides simultaneous wallet cash and debt.
- [ ] Preserve the plain and proof-bearing =approve=, =transfer=,
  =transferFrom=, =allowance=, identity fragments, public/private identity
  checks and receipt event. Port shared real-registry, real-proof tests for
  all identity combinations, invalid proofs, wrong chain or parties, ordinary
  and Carrying transfers, finite/infinite allowance, zero/self transfers and
  full rollback. Reconcile legacy revert payloads and zero-address approval
  where consumers depend on them; test actual net delivery separately.
- [ ] Preserve bounded =issue= and renewal APIs, owner-selected insurer and
  maximum premium. An old =mint= has no field for a premium ceiling or
  expected pool; document that limitation rather than imply the old
  signature protects the caller against a change before execution. Add a
  bounded batch form if needed, without silently changing the legacy signature.
- [ ] Implement or explicitly retire the old =settleRelief=, demurrage
  sponsorship, fee/relief counters and fund-view aliases according to actual
  callers. Do not add no-op or fabricated compatibility methods. Classify
  =mintFromBasket=/=burnFromBasket= as legacy simulation-hook behavior, and
  keep its distinct detection only until an economically sound replacement is
  demonstrated; do not create unsupported unbacked issuance in the net token.
- [ ] Compare representative old and new ABI signatures, events, result
  units, errors and pre/postconditions; run legacy non-obsolete behavioral
  tests against the new token, not just an ABI diff. Add two-NFT, premium,
  dynamic K, insufficient funds, proof failure, cap/refusal and atomic rollback
  cases. Preserve and update the original =Buck.sol= explanation and
  pre/postcondition documentation; remove descriptions of signed balances or
  implicit draw/repayment only where they are no longer true.
- [ ] Measure linked runtime and creation size, gas and deployment graph.
  BuckNet is already near EIP-170's runtime limit and BuckCredit is closer;
  prefer a linked library when appropriate and verify caller/storage context.
  The old and new constructors both take four addresses but their fourth
  argument denotes an insurer pool and Jubilee fund respectively: make
  deployment/model selection explicit so the wrong graph cannot silently
  initialize.

* 3. One basket wheel for valuation and Jubilee liquidity

- [ ] Use the existing =BuckBasketEquityStorage._snap=, constituent marks,
  =sharePrice/valueOf= and share/basis rules as the starting valuation source.
  Add a bounded valuation-only entry on the existing wheel and have each burn
  ask it to refresh the published Jubilee mark. The entry may write the mark
  and its metadata, but basket and market reads must be static: no arbitrary
  upkeep, swaps, redemption, BUCK transfers or wheel rewards. Ordinary wheel
  ticks can independently invest or replenish liquidity. The prototype calls
  =basket.valueOf= live; the wheel does *not* yet publish this mark. Specify
  and test refresh failure, stale or absent input, position changes, callbacks,
  same-block price moves and price guards. Define post-gain-fee full-value
  BUCK and Carrying-pool adjustments; a mark is not executable liquidity.
- [ ] Integrate fund investment, reserve replenishment, maturity processing
  and deferred burns as bounded work in the existing =WorkWheel=/basket-wheel
  slot and tick mechanism, or a minimal extension of it. Reuse the fund's
  existing one-receipt =deposit/increasePosition/redeemShares= and reserve
  policy; its current defaults are a net cash target of 10% of the capped
  relief benchmark, a one-day half-life on observed excess and a minimum
  investment of 1% of the target. This target is distinct from the 10%
  *payment* cap. The wheel, not a second independent loop, schedules ordinary
  actions. Define authorization, price/slippage bounds, actual net proceeds,
  minimum investment threshold, re-arm after cash or mark changes, cursor
  fairness, gas and incentives. A block advancing does not by itself ensure
  anyone called the wheel.
- [ ] Prototype the smallest integration: designate the existing wheel as the
  fund's investment keeper and add a bounded due/run kind to that wheel.
  Merely changing the keeper is insufficient: =tick(maxWork,maxScan)= has no
  route or price arguments, whereas =investScheduled= and =redeemScheduled=
  currently require caller-supplied minimums and sale routes. Prefer reusing
  the basket's guarded observations and configured policy to derive bounded
  on-chain inputs; compare that with an authenticated, bounded work payload
  before choosing. Never substitute zero minimums or unbounded swaps just to
  make a tick permissionless. Test both input authenticity and execution.
- [ ] Give the fund one persistent basket position: top-ups retain the receipt
  ID; partial redemption preserves remaining shares, basis and ID; full exit
  may clear it. For in-kind output count only verified actual net BUCK after
  bounded conversion; zero/partial proceeds or an underwater/low-K refusal
  must not be reported as a successful complete sale.
- [ ] Port the basket's actual BuckCredit issuance, explicit repayment, K
  checks, wheel operations and equity accounting to the new token. Resolve
  the circular mark: the fund owns basket shares while the leveraged basket
  itself has an age-weighted claim on the fund. Specify which realizable
  relief can enter which valuation and prove no recursive calls, double
  counting, dilution or free value. Test conservative and/or solved marks
  against entry, exit, full and partial claims, losses, singular cases and
  independent numerical references before selecting a policy.
- [ ] Exercise mint and burn before/after wheel work, same-block batches and
  multiple blocks, stale/manipulated/missing marks, partial or zero sales,
  adverse prices, insurer callbacks, low-K exits, reserve refilling, arbitrage
  and rebalancing. Confirm both operation-level and shared block-level
  liquidity limits cannot be evaded across NFTs, accounts, renewals or
  callback paths. No guarantee of a wheel tick or an executable market is
  implied merely by waiting for a later block.
- [ ] Compare the actual Python =BasketWheel= and JS =BasketMachineWheel=
  slot sets, ordering, failure handling and behavior. Consolidate them only
  after both runtimes retain their distinct shock, routing, rebalance and
  failure coverage; keep the existing shared chassis rather than introducing
  a separate Jubilee wheel.

* Selected policies and current implementation limits

The user selected actual =balanceOf= changes for compatible mint and burn,
approved Carrying facility owners, a shared per-block 10% cash budget and the
last published wheel mark. The shared budget is now implemented; the current
prototype still rejects Carrying owners, lacks compatible mint/burn entry
points, reads a guarded live basket mark and does not make the wheel perform
the fund's work. Do not treat the other selected policies as implemented.

For example, if the fund has 100 liquid BUCK, the current guard can permit one
operation paying 10 and another in the same block paying 9. The shared
block budget snapshots the fund's net cash immediately before the block's
first *positive* protected outflow, allows at most 10% of that snapshot across protected
outflows in that block, and still checks each operation against current cash.
Cash received later in the same block would not increase that block's budget;
the next block starts a new snapshot. Reductions, renewals and deferred burns
share the budget, including across NFTs and transactions. Failed operations
must not consume budget; a batched operation cannot reset it.

Preserve the 50-year release exception, explicit owner protection and ordinary
burn rollback. Determine exact freshness/invalidation parameters and how tiny
raw-unit liquidity/dust, stale marks, no wheel callers, empty or distressed
baskets and an insolvent insurer behave without promising liquidity or
blocking matured lien release. The cross-holding analysis remains an
implementation gate.

The block budget bounds how much Jubilee pays under a temporarily inaccurate
mark, not how much an individual borrower could lose from an understated mark.
It also does not guarantee that a stale or manipulated market observation is
correct. Keep a borrower-selectable minimum relief and principal/coverage
bound, guarded inputs, and the explicit published-mark checks. A valuation-only
refresh is bounded separately from ordinary wheel work: a failed burn rolls back
its own refresh, and it is not evidence that arbitrage, a fund sale, or other
liquidity-replenishing work occurred.
