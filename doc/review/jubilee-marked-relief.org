#+TITLE: Jubilee relief from a marked portfolio and a liquid reserve
#+DATE: 2026-10-30
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* Status

This note analyzes the user's proposed no-sale valuation. The Python
=quote_marked_retirement= is a diagnostic; ordinary =JubileeFund.reduce= and
=renew= still use the earlier proportional *sale* implementation. Do not
describe the marked rule as deployed or as validated by a five-year run.

* The numerator and denominator

Let =P= be the principal selected for retirement, =s= the selected facility's
accrued, capped BUCK-seconds, and =T= 50 nominal 365.25-day years in seconds.
Its baseline target is =q = s / T=. Let =S= be the sum of the capped
BUCK-seconds of *all* open facilities plus =T= times unburned Jubilee-assumed
matured principal. The total benchmark is =B = S / T=. Let =C= be the fund's
liquid BUCK *net* of its own carried fee and =L= the net, full-value BUCK mark
of the fund's basket receipt. With =V = C + L=, the natural proportional
allocation is:

#+begin_example
relief = min(P, V * s / S) = min(P, q * V / B),  if S > 0
relief = 0,                                      if S = 0
effective annual relief rate = 2% * V / B         before the principal cap
#+end_example

The =JubileeClaimBook= already computes =S= with bounded maturity batches;
the current branch includes matured deferred fund obligations in it. Use
full-precision multiplication/division and the scaled net =C=; rounding each
facility's target before forming the ratio would lose fractional BUCK. =V=
must not count the Carrying account's accrued fee as spendable value or count
the basket position's value twice. Under the proposed cash guard, a reduction
reverts if the computed net relief exceeds one tenth of =C= immediately before
settlement. An owner-specified minimum relief still applies. The quoted relief
must not be silently clamped to the cap.

This is equivalent to allocating the same share of cash and marked LP value.
If an actual proportional LP sale returns exactly its proportional mark with
no slippage or fees, the earlier sale-based rule gives the same amount. They
differ when the mark and realized proceeds differ. In an unchanged market,
before the principal cap, paying a selected claim reduces both =V= and =B= in
the same proportion, so =V/B= is unchanged for the other claimants.

* Numerical example

Suppose total outstanding issuance is 100 million BUCK, the selected
facility issued 1 million one year ago, and the whole economy's cumulative
benchmark =B= is 10 million BUCK (an average of five BUCK-years per issued
BUCK). The other facilities' history is needed to know this denominator; the
100-million supply alone does not determine it.

| Item | Amount |
|------+--------|
| Selected facility's BUCK-years | 1 million |
| Total benchmark BUCK-years | 500 million |
| Selected fraction =s / S= | 0.2% |
| Selected baseline target | 20,000 BUCK |
| Liquid cash net of fees | 1 million BUCK |
| LP receipt marked value, net of fees | 9 million BUCK |
| Whole fund mark =V= | 10 million BUCK |
| Fund-to-benchmark ratio =V/B= | 1.0 |
| Relief | 20,000 BUCK |
| Liquid limit, 10% of cash | 100,000 BUCK |

At a 10.25 million fund mark the implied annual rate is 2.05% and the relief
is 20,500 BUCK. At 9.5 million, the rate is 1.90% and relief is 19,000 BUCK.
A one-million facility aged 50 years has a one-million baseline target; with
the same 10-million benchmark and mark it represents 10% of the accrued
claims, not 1%. It would hit the 100,000 liquid limit if it sought immediate
payment. The separate matured-lien procedure instead releases the NFT and
records unburned issuance as Jubilee debt; it does not require that payment.

Multiplying the 1% /supply/ share by the facility's one-year/50-year age and
then by the fund's 10-million mark yields 2,000 BUCK, one tenth of the
age-weighted allocation in this example. It would agree only if the benchmark
equalled the entire 100-million supply, i.e. all outstanding principal were
at its 50-year cap. Applying age to the numerator without normalizing against
the other facilities' ages applies age twice in a young economy.

If the 1 million cash figure is *nominal* rather than net and its carried fee
is 0.5%, =C= is 995,000 BUCK, not 1 million; with a 9-million net LP mark,
=V= is 9.995 million. At a continuous 2% annual rate, an unchanged balance
aged six months loses about 0.995%, not 0.5%. A mixed pool can have a different
weighted average age. A stated 4% annual investment return alone is not enough
to determine today's multiplier: position entry dates, cash flows, demurrage,
losses and the current mark all affect =V/B=.

* What the current basket can and cannot establish

=BuckBasketEquity.valueOf(id)= currently returns the fund's share of equity
at the basket's venue mark, without selling the receipt. In the experimental
cash-only net-token mode, equity reads net BUCK held in the basket wallet.
This is a useful candidate input, but it is not yet a demonstrated
executable, net-of-demurrage valuation of the whole receipt:

- The legacy V3 venue can fall back to spot when the TWAP is unavailable. The
  stricter venue requires observation history, but TWAPs can still be stale
  or manipulated and a mark is not a liquidation price.
- V3 position marks use twice the modeled BUCK side of full-range liquidity;
  they do not by themselves prove that the pool's nominal BUCK side is net of
  its Carrying fee. Uncollected fees and execution costs also need explicit
  treatment. Test against actual in-kind redemptions, both token ordering
  directions and aged Carrying pools before accepting this mark as =L=.
- If a future leveraged basket owns credit while Jubilee holds its shares,
  the marked basket claim can depend on Jubilee's marked holdings. The
  experimental net basket disallows that mode; the fixed-point and dilution
  problem is detailed in [[file:basket-jubilee-crossholding.org][the cross-holding note]].
- =valueOf= can revert when venue pricing is unavailable. Define an explicit
  user-authorized fallback or let the operation fail; never invent a price.
  A 10% cash limit bounds one operation but does not by itself prevent repeated
  extraction against a manipulated mark.

Before using the mark for on-chain relief, compare quotes to actual real-pool
withdrawals and conversions with demurrage, liquidity, fees and TWAP history;
specify freshness/manipulation protection and how a keeper restores the reserve.
The Python mark experiment assumes an ideal, BUCK-only strategy and cannot
establish those properties.
