#+TITLE: Jubilee relief from a marked portfolio and a liquid reserve
#+DATE: 2026-10-30
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* Status

This note analyzes the no-sale valuation implemented by the experimental
=JubileeFund.reduce= and =renew= and the Python reference. The prototype is
not deployed, has not been validated by a five-year run, and still requires
the oracle, executable-liquidity and leveraged-basket work described below.

* The numerator and denominator

Let =P= be the principal selected for retirement, =s= the selected facility's
accrued, capped BUCK-seconds, and =T= 50 nominal 365.25-day years in seconds.
Its baseline target is =q = s / T=. Let =S= be the sum of the capped
BUCK-seconds of *all* open facilities plus =T= times unburned Jubilee-assumed
matured principal. The total benchmark is =B = S / T=. Let =C= be the fund's
liquid BUCK *net* of its own carried fee and =L= the net, full-value BUCK mark
of the fund's basket receipt. With =V = C + L=, the natural proportional
allocation is:

#+begin_example
relief = min(P, V * s / S) = min(P, q * V / B),  if S > 0
relief = 0,                                      if S = 0
effective annual relief rate = 2% * V / B         before the principal cap
#+end_example

The =JubileeClaimBook= already computes =S= with bounded maturity batches;
the current branch includes matured deferred fund obligations in it. Use
full-precision multiplication/division and the scaled net =C=; rounding each
facility's target before forming the ratio would lose fractional BUCK. =V=
must not count the Carrying account's accrued fee as spendable value or count
the basket position's value twice. Under the proposed cash guard, a reduction
reverts if the computed net relief exceeds one tenth of =C= immediately before
settlement. An owner-specified minimum relief still applies. The quoted relief
must not be silently clamped to the cap.

This is equivalent to allocating the same share of cash and marked LP value.
If an actual proportional LP sale returns exactly its proportional mark with
no slippage or fees, the earlier sale-based rule gives the same amount. They
differ when the mark and realized proceeds differ. In an unchanged market,
before the principal cap, paying a selected claim reduces both =V= and =B= in
the same proportion, so =V/B= is unchanged for the other claimants.

* Why the global BUCK-seconds counter needs maturity checkpoints

The uncapped outstanding BUCK-seconds can be kept without scanning NFTs.
Between issuance or reduction events, add =current outstanding issuance *
elapsed seconds= to one aggregate. Issuance starts with zero age; when
principal is retired, subtract the age of that principal, which is already
needed for its own relief. Include the issuance shifted to Jubilee until it
is actually burned. This is a running aggregate for the still-outstanding
stock, not the all-time integral that also includes already-retired stock.

The requested *per-facility 50-year cap* needs one further piece of
information: when each facility stops contributing to that aggregate's
growth. For example, issue 100 BUCK at year 0, issue another 100 at year 50,
and look at year 100 without any reductions. The uncapped stock integral is
15,000 BUCK-years, but the sum of the two capped claims is 10,000: both are
at their 50-year cap. A payout based on the uncapped denominator gives either
facility one third of the fund; the capped denominator gives it one half.
The user's owner-at-convenience closure means an old facility can remain
unclosed after its cap; its age must not continue diluting others. The same
applies to Jubilee's assumed matured issuance, which stays in supply but no
longer earns additional relief.

The current claim book is a way to maintain that cap incrementally. It tracks
the next saturation times and lets anyone process due maturities in bounded
batches; a normal aggregate read does not enumerate or consult every
BuckCredit. Issue, reduction and renewal synchronize the one affected
facility. A simpler uncapped global integral is feasible if that different
economic treatment is deliberately chosen, but it does not produce the sum
of separately capped claims when closure or processing is delayed.

* Numerical example

Suppose total outstanding issuance is 100 million BUCK, the selected
facility issued 1 million one year ago, and the whole economy's cumulative
benchmark =B= is 10 million BUCK (an average of five BUCK-years per issued
BUCK). The other facilities' history is needed to know this denominator; the
100-million supply alone does not determine it.

| Item | Amount |
|------+--------|
| Selected facility's BUCK-years | 1 million |
| Total benchmark BUCK-years | 500 million |
| Selected fraction =s / S= | 0.2% |
| Selected baseline target | 20,000 BUCK |
| Liquid cash net of fees | 1 million BUCK |
| LP receipt marked value, net of fees | 9 million BUCK |
| Whole fund mark =V= | 10 million BUCK |
| Fund-to-benchmark ratio =V/B= | 1.0 |
| Relief | 20,000 BUCK |
| Liquid limit, 10% of cash | 100,000 BUCK |

At a 10.25 million fund mark the implied annual rate is 2.05% and the relief
is 20,500 BUCK. At 9.5 million, the rate is 1.90% and relief is 19,000 BUCK.
A one-million facility aged 50 years has a one-million baseline target; with
the same 10-million benchmark and mark it represents 10% of the accrued
claims, not 1%. It would hit the 100,000 liquid limit if it sought immediate
payment. The separate matured-lien procedure instead releases the NFT and
records unburned issuance as Jubilee debt; it does not require that payment.

Multiplying the 1% /supply/ share by the facility's one-year/50-year age and
then by the fund's 10-million mark yields 2,000 BUCK, one tenth of the
age-weighted allocation in this example. It would agree only if the benchmark
equalled the entire 100-million supply, i.e. all outstanding principal were
at its 50-year cap. Applying age to the numerator without normalizing against
the other facilities' ages applies age twice in a young economy.

If the 1 million cash figure is *nominal* rather than net and its carried fee
is 0.5%, =C= is 995,000 BUCK, not 1 million; with a 9-million net LP mark,
=V= is 9.995 million. At a continuous 2% annual rate, an unchanged balance
aged six months loses about 0.995%, not 0.5%. A mixed pool can have a different
weighted average age. A stated 4% annual investment return alone is not enough
to determine today's multiplier: position entry dates, cash flows, demurrage,
losses and the current mark all affect =V/B=.

* When the keeper needs to sell

Issuance and reductions checkpoint accumulated demurrage before changing the
outstanding supply. Between those events the same amounts are visible through
the lazy balance views. At a nominal rate =r=, supply =P= and liquid fund
balance =C=, the instantaneous gross fee is =r P= per year. The net liquid
inflow before investment, relief and other transfers is =r (P - C)=: the
liquid fund's own fee returns to itself. Some of that inflow may come from
BUCK inside the fund's own basket position, in which case it is a move from
the fund's investment mark to its cash, not additional consolidated profit.

When the fund is at its benchmark and facilities retired early have average
age =a= years, an approximate annual relief outflow for =R= BUCK of principal
retired per year is =r a R=, up to the 50-year cap. This simple comparison
suggests that fee cash inflow exceeds relief outflow when =P - C > a R=.
For illustration, with 100 million issued, 1 million liquid, a 2% rate and
5-year-old reductions, the initial cash inflow rate is about 1.98 million per
year; retiring 1 million of principal per year requests about 0.10 million of
relief. Retiring 20 million per year instead requests about 2 million. These
are instantaneous estimates, not a stable-world simulation: supply, ages,
cash, asset marks and repayment behavior change, and matured deferred debt is
paid separately.

The keeper should normally invest the matured portion of excess cash. It can
attempt an independently bounded position sale when cash is below the reserve
target, but low K, thin liquidity or failed conversions can prevent that sale.
The present tests establish these mechanisms, not how often a realistic
economy needs to invoke them. No running keeper or five-year =BuckNet= economy
has yet been integrated.

* Insurer callback ordering

An original insurer pool may need to restore its nominal deposit before a
reduction or renewal. That preparation is an external callback, so it can
change markets or other relevant state while the fund's reentrancy lock is
held. Previously the fund quoted the basket before calling the pool; a
controlled callback then changed the basket value and the stale amount was
still paid. Both paths now perform preparation after initial authorization
checks and before taking the marked quote. Renewal rechecks its owner/insurer
consent and principal snapshot after the callback. The token checks the owner,
minimum, liquidity cap and K constraints at settlement. A callback loss that
violates the owner's minimum reverts the callback and settlement together.

The test basket's controllable loss models an external market change; it is
not itself an open withdrawal path in the real basket. The real-basket oracle,
callback and market assumptions still require a broader adversarial audit.

* What the current basket can and cannot establish

=BuckBasketEquity.valueOf(id)= currently returns the fund's share of equity
at the basket's venue mark, without selling the receipt. In the experimental
cash-only net-token mode, equity reads net BUCK held in the basket wallet.
This is a useful candidate input, but it is not yet a demonstrated
executable, net-of-demurrage valuation of the whole receipt:

- The legacy V3 venue can fall back to spot when the TWAP is unavailable. The
  stricter venue requires observation history, but TWAPs can still be stale
  or manipulated and a mark is not a liquidation price.
- V3 position marks use twice the modeled BUCK side of full-range liquidity.
  The experimental net mode now converts pool quotes and position marks from
  nominal to full-value BUCK using the pool's exact net/nominal balance ratio.
  One real-pool idle-age test checks that correction. The fund deducts the
  receipt's unrealized 25% gain fee. Uncollected fees, finite-range
  approximation and execution costs still need explicit treatment; more
  actual in-kind redemption and token-ordering tests are required before
  treating the mark as an executable price.
- If a future leveraged basket owns credit while Jubilee holds its shares,
  the marked basket claim can depend on Jubilee's marked holdings. The
  experimental net basket disallows that mode; the fixed-point and dilution
  problem is detailed in [[file:basket-jubilee-crossholding.org][the cross-holding note]].
- The fund requires active liquidity and full TWAP history for every pool
  contributing assets; absent a price, the operation reverts instead of using
  the venue's spot fallback. It also requires spot and TWAP to be within the
  basket's configured nonzero deviation band (at most 10%). A 10% cash limit
  bounds one operation but does not by itself prevent repeated extraction
  against a manipulated mark.

Before deploying marked on-chain relief, broaden the comparison of quotes to
actual real-pool withdrawals and conversions, and audit manipulation,
liquidity, fees and how a keeper restores the reserve. The Python mark
experiment assumes an ideal, BUCK-only strategy and cannot establish those
properties.

** Real-pool shock observation

The contract test =JubileeEquityIntegrationTest= now compares the marked value
of 20% of the fund's receipt with the actual BUCK received by separately
unwinding and converting those shares through a real V3 pool. The pool has
ten years of carried demurrage, and the fund has a valid TWAP history. With
BUCK as token0, the controlled example gives:

| Market state | Receipt mark for the selected shares | Net BUCK from sale |
|--------------+--------------------------------------+--------------------|
| No price shock | 6,247.781511 | 6,216.244497 |
| Immediately after a large TOKEN sale | 6,247.781511 | 3,842.253096 |

The result is similar with the opposite token ordering. In the shock case the
existing historical TWAP has not yet moved, even though the spot market and
executable proceeds have. Before the guard was added, commit =b7f99bc1= also
demonstrated an authorized reduction paying that old mark from the liquid
reserve. The current test preserves the separate sale comparison and confirms
that reductions and renewals are refused while the prices diverge. After the
600-second TWAP window catches up, the reduction can be retried; small moves
inside the configured band remain permitted. Both token orders and price
directions are covered. Another dislocation at 50 years blocks an ordinary
quote but leaves mature lien release and its deferred Jubilee obligation
available in both token orders.

The interim band prevents that particular stale-price payout, but the 10% cash
limit caps an individual payout, not repeated payouts. A sustained manipulated
price can move both spot and TWAP together; a thin pool can also have a poor
executable price inside the band. Conversely, a legitimate or adversarial
large spot move can delay ordinary reductions and renewals until the prices
reconverge. Matured lien release remains independent of the mark. The exact
loss or delay in a live market is not established by this isolated pool or
single swap; liquidity, external prices, execution, slippage and other
participants matter. An independent price policy, manipulation costs and
executable-value protections remain deployment prerequisites.
