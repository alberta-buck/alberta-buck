#+TITLE: Carrying and ordinary account transitions
#+DATE: 2026-10-29
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* The boundary

The registry permits the original binder of a contract identity to change its
Carrying flag until an identity-bound approval freezes it. The net ledger
caches its balance representation. Simply changing the registry flag leaves
that representation stale: subsequent transfers revert or report the wrong
kind of balance. Silently reinterpreting nominal BUCK as full-value BUCK would
be worse, because already-paid demurrage could become spendable again.

The clean-genesis prototype opts the registered Buck into a registry
callback before its first issuance or transfer. A permitted flag change
then checkpoints the account and converts the representation atomically before
the registry commits the new flag. Legacy tokens do not opt in and retain the
existing registry behavior. The binder and approval-freeze permissions are
unchanged. The hook must reject an operation completely if it cannot preserve
its invariants; a failed callback must not leave the registry flag changed.

* Conversion

Both representations keep the same fractional *net* BUCK. On ordinary-to-
Carrying conversion the current net held and issued-unspent parts become the
initial Carrying nominal total; subsequent demurrage is carried proportionally.
On Carrying-to-ordinary conversion the old nominal fee history is discarded
and only current net BUCK remains spendable. Those fees had already accrued to
Jubilee and are not credited again. The held and unspent parts remain separate
across both transitions. A Carrying outflow transfers a proportional share of
the combined net value and consumes held before unspent.

For example, an ordinary account holding 100 BUCK for one year has about
98.019867 net BUCK. Switching it to Carrying starts with that same net and
nominal value. After another year, its nominal value is unchanged while its
net value has declined to about 96.078944. Switching back reports the latter
as ordinary spendable value; no new fund payment is created at the switch.

The prototype currently requires an ordinary owner to issue, reduce and renew a
facility. A switch *to* Carrying while that account owns an NFT with
outstanding principal is therefore rejected rather than trapping its debt.
Checking the owner's NFTs on this infrequent change is potentially expensive
for an owner with many positions; it is not done on every transfer. The
Jubilee fund and an insurer's registered premium pool remain Carrying so their
funding and nominal refund obligations cannot silently change posture. A new
pool may be registered for future policies; old policies retain their pool.
Whether a future contract should support Carrying facility owners or release a
fully settled insurer pool is a separate extension.

* Validation

Test repeated round trips, aged and mixed balances, a transferred Carrying
fee, held versus unspent parts, already-accrued Jubilee value, zero balances,
unauthorized changes, frozen identities, open credit, fund/pool protection,
and rollback on callback failure. Net value and fund cash must not jump at a
mode change. Run legacy registry and token tests as well as the new token tests.
