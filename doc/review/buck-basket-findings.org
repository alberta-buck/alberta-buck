* What the completed runs establish

The active basket earned positive marked returns on all 24 short three-phase
cases. On historical commodity anchors, returns depended on the daily path,
the opening shareholder base, the horizon and the currency in which an
investor measured wealth. Actual wallet exits give a different result from
marked returns on an outstanding receipt. The comparisons below use the
current household simulator and EVM contracts.

The active strategy lets relative TOKEN departures develop. Once a weight
gap persists and the outward movement slows, the available discretionary
trade can grow. Its raw request still competes with whole-book purchases
or sales and faces credit, size and execution limits. Strict maintenance
retains normal issuance, parity response, deposits, withdrawals, arbitrage
and safeguards. Comparing the two worlds on the same price tape and
household seed measures the contribution of enabling discretionary upkeep,
including its later effects on holdings and market prices.

* How production handles a relative-price move

The basket provides TOKEN/BUCK liquidity, adjusts how much TOKEN it holds
against BUCK parity, and optionally changes the mix of TOKENs when a
relative price move slows. These actions can occur in the same response.
A rule that only changes discretionary timing can therefore change far fewer
orders than the total number of basket transactions suggests.

| Decision | Current household-world policy |
|----------+--------------------------------|
| Hold and place inventory | A response pairs idle TOKEN with fresh BUCK in its own TOKEN/BUCK pool, subject to credit, depth and a minimum amount. Daily work refreshes the credit mark. Position sync collects fees. |
| Set exposure | Let X be the TOKEN book, N the lien less net wallet BUCK and the marked BUCK side of LPs, and E = max(0, X - N) at the sizing marks. The TOKEN target is E plus desired N. A fresh reading sets desired N/E to -5 times (basket cost in net BUCK minus one). A cheap BUCK implies a sale of TOKENs and retention of BUCK. A dear BUCK implies issuance to buy TOKENs. At parity the desired N is zero and the TOKEN target is E. A 10 bp parity move changes the desired exposure by 0.5% of equity before constraints. With a stale sample the book keeps its existing net exposure inside the ceiling. |
| Bound exposure | The normal net-issuance ceiling is E times 0.9K/(1-0.9K). The hard collateral relationship at the LOW mark is approximately N at most K times the TOKEN value, subject to facility activation, face and other issuance limits. Unpaid Jubilee relief can enter the share mark and is excluded from this collateral. Normal exposure follows the parity aim inside the ceiling. If net issuance exceeds the ceiling, the plan calls for proportional sales before discretionary sizing. |
| Set the mix | Start with published TOKEN weights. Multiply each weight by the square root of its opening price over its current TWAP and normalize across the book, then clip each target to ±15% of its declared allocation. A common move in all TOKEN/BUCK prices cancels in the normalized ratios. The safeguard applies when a holding falls more than 30% of its declared allocation away from it and asks for the whole gap to the working target, subject to execution limits. |
| Decide when a gap qualifies | The gap is a share of the whole book. Default slack is 0.25 percentage points and patience is two percentage-point days. A steady one-point gap takes about 2.67 days beyond its initial observation to accumulate that much urgency. Time accrual is capped at one day per observation gap, drains below slack and restarts after a side change. |
| Decide how fast to work it | A prospective seller must remain roughly 1% dear relative to the buyer and their accepted relative reference in the filtered reading and each live route. Both BUCK and USDC routes must be ready. The policy measures the change in filtered velocity on its own timescale. A slowing outward move releases part of the gap, confirmed closing adds to the rate, and a quiet separated pair has a small braking allowance. Estimated observation error and the basket's own recent impact temper the release. A holding averages directional pair rates against peers using the declared peer weights. It can sell or buy without an equal leg in the same response. |
| Apply the pace and limits | Responses are spaced at least 30 minutes when work succeeds. One-day half-life is the fastest ordinary exponential release. Unspent release can accrue between fills, and execution clears the constituent's allowance. Aggregate book-size drift beyond 2% can call for the fastest pace without a pair signal. The plan takes the greater of that work and the constituent request, then applies per-step limits of 5% of current holdings or target and 1% of pool BUCK depth, credit constraints, and a minimum of two grains. The execution uses available funding and live venue protections. |
| Repair thin Carrying balances | When placement and normal trading do no work, the responder can refresh an eligible LP slice, burning it and re-pairing the TOKEN with freshly issued BUCK. The default refresh floor and target are 99% and 99.5% net/gross. |
| Sample parity | The price book uses the published commodity quantities, a ten-minute pool TWAP and a 5% spot/TWAP guard. It can use guarded USDC routes to improve the net BUCK bid. It attempts a sample at 30-minute intervals and treats a valid observation as fresh for an hour. Bid marks are marginal quotes. |

* Why historical prices and three-phase prices differ

The three-phase generator gives each TOKEN an evenly offset sinusoid
with 12% amplitude and a 30-day period. The historical sources provide
monthly wage, construction, food, energy and gold observations. The
standard daily bridge uses the next monthly endpoint to construct
intervening prices. The carry-forward sensitivity test releases each
monthly observation after a fixed 61-day lag. Actual daily paths and
first-publication vintages are unavailable for this five-series basket.

Over 22 complete 15-day windows beginning at close 29, the typical
absolute TOKEN move relative to its four peers was 22.354 log-percentage
points for three-phase, 2.545 for history and 2.693 for reverting history.
Three-phase reversed in 105 of 105 adjacent TOKEN/window comparisons,
history in 68 and reverting history in 72. Fifteen days is half the
synthetic period. This measures the size and regularity of the opportunity.
Fill size, timing, costs and remaining inventory determine the profit.

* When the active policy earns its keep

Active upkeep can sell a relatively rich TOKEN and buy a cheap one as the
spread starts to close. Repeated reversals let both sides of the adjustment
pay. A lasting change in relative value can leave the basket holding more of
the permanently cheaper TOKEN. An allocation gap measures how far the book
is from its target. A slowing relative price measures how quickly it is
still moving away. Both can appear during a permanent repricing. Stronger
target tilt increases the inventory exposed to either outcome. Parity,
leverage, LP marks and shareholder flows can also change as the paired
worlds evolve.

** What the completed sizing sweeps changed

The 36-close signal experiment completed 280 arms across five price
regimes, two noise levels and two household seeds. Production's BUCK share
return beat strict maintenance in 11 of the 20 pairs, tied in four and
lost in five. Three-phase won all four (+0.953 to +2.424 pp), as did the
combined shock (+0.196 to +1.045 pp). Mixed ranged from -0.556 to +0.971 pp,
history from -0.380 to 0.000 pp, and reverting history from -0.597 to
+0.432 pp. At zero noise both household seeds encounter the same price tape.

All three candidate laws size the rate on an eligible allocation gap.
Velocity uses relative closure beyond venue uncertainty and estimated own
impact, with full strength at 0.5% closing speed per day. Excursion uses
the current allocation gap, with full strength at 2% of the book. It can
act before a turn. The product multiplies both strengths. The sweep crossed
a common 0.25% gap slack with standard or early eligibility (0.020 or
0.005 share-days) and a one- or three-day half-life at full strength. The
candidate arms also gate accrued discretionary allowance at a zero current
rate. Production can release part of the gap during deceleration and can
apply banked allowance under its ordinary rules.

Velocity and the product each had only four positive decision-time rates
across 80 arms. Neither changed an order, and every share return matched
maintenance. Excursion changed 5,842 TOKEN plans across 80 arms, with 46
wins, five ties and 29 losses. It beat maintenance in 16 of 16 three-phase
arms and three of 16 historical arms. On the four combined-shock pairs,
standard patience with the three-day half-life gained 0.510–0.987 pp over
maintenance. The one-day version ranged from -0.280 to +1.260 pp. On the
730-close pinned historical tape, production, excursion and maintenance
returned +0.831%, +0.768% and +0.258% in BUCK. They changed 54, 83 and zero
plans respectively. Velocity and the product again matched maintenance.
Production's passive TOKEN index gained +2.723% in that world.

The braking experiment completed all 36 arms of its six-group preset. It
crossed 20 and 100 bp/day outward speed at half release with rates of one
and four per day before the common cap, preserving the other production
gates. All 12 three-phase candidate arms beat maintenance. The 12 historical
candidate arms produced one win, eight ties and three losses. Within the two
noisy historical groups, the 20-bp, four-per-day version averaged +0.467 pp
against maintenance while the other three settings averaged -0.115 to
-0.032 pp. Narrow eligibility can explain an inactive law, and increased
eligibility or more fills alone can leave returns worse. The small braking
preset supports testing on withheld prices and household seeds before
choosing a setting.

* Funding, stability and withdrawals


** Fresh issuance and the cost of liquidity

A funded wheel purchase reserves issuance room before trading and pays
the authenticated pool directly from its facility for the amount the
callback actually needs. Pairing credits the basket's BUCK side of the LP
and raises gross principal, which is why outstanding principal, spendable
net wallet BUCK, net issuance and pool depth belong in separate ledgers.
An idle wallet near zero avoids carry on a standing buffer. Carrying pool
balances still lose net value relative to nominal face. Refresh can restore
some pooled BUCK liquidity by replacing a slice with fresh BUCK, using
facility room and gas. Its value to the basket depends on LP ownership,
fees, carrying cost and the price at which the released BUCK can be used.
The 730-day comparisons changed the ranking of the zero-wallet and
flow-reserve arms when refresh was switched off, and the tighter 99.5%/
99.7% band with zero reserve lost 0.815% on that path. Those comparisons
support keeping fresh on-demand funding as the baseline and measuring
a buffer and a refresh change separately. Sources:
[[file:../../src/basket/BuckBasketUniswapV3.sol::issueToPool][pool callback]],
[[file:../../src/basket/BuckBasketEquityStorage.sol::function _fundCash][funding and credit]],
[[file:../../src/basket/BasketMarketOps.sol::function refreshPosition][LP refresh]]
and [[file:bucknet-economy-implementation.org::The earlier policy at][retained refresh comparison]].

A funded flash cycle can draw against available facility room, trade
through configured routes and repay atomically. The strategy checks the
wallet and its own LP positions at frozen marks around a cycle. The
withdrawal comparator's 1,320 authenticated cycles recorded 213,630.58
BUCK of frozen-mark gain, comprising -18,306.18 cash and +231,936.76
owned LP value. The included LP fees, frozen-mark gains and share NAV
measure overlapping economics. Count fees once, and value the resulting
inventory at executable depth for an exit. Keeper gas belongs to the actual
payer even when the portfolio passes a gain guard. Sources:

** Parity, correlated shocks and feedback

A relative commodity signal and a BUCK-wide demand signal can have the
same short-run effect on a direct pool. A broad BUCK premium should push
basket exposure toward issuance under the existing sign convention. A
discount should move exposure toward holding BUCK. The signal enters
alongside K, which changes the hard room, and a separately capitalized
desk with its own outer bands, persistence and budget. LP swaps and share
flows can also move the prices the next controller reads. A common-time
quote and a well-behaved response to one temporary markdown give little
information about joint behavior when credit, liquidity and demand move
together. On a low-K day, the hard room left above the normal ceiling can
be small in equity terms even with all the caps working. A sharp TOKEN
markdown and a K fall can reduce room before exposure catches up.
Step limits and reasonable impact constraints can extend the period spent
over the soft ceiling. Source:
[[file:../../src/basket/BasketDesk.sol::function _netOperation][desk]],
[[file:../../src/basket/BasketUpkeepFacet.sol::function _plan][book response]]
and [[file:bucknet-economy-implementation.org::*Next experiments in priority order][recorded parity and K questions]].

A permanent relative markdown produces the same initial price and gap as
its temporary counterpart. A slowing or quiet venue can grant release even
while the old relative anchor overstates the value of accumulated TOKEN.
The lab's two-step repricing demonstrates the loss can grow with tilt.
A genuine repricing of the underlying mandate requires a governed update
with documented evidence. Sizing can respond earlier by reducing only
discretionary adds to an established adverse leg and preserving the
declared weights, mandatory safeguards and settlement. A corroboration
rule can also decline new discretionary risk while a route is stale,
shallow or disagrees with independent observations. Track how much of a
temporary rebound such a rule gives away. Source:

** Exit value, order of redemptions and keeper availability

A normal partial BUCK exit uses cash and eligible credit first, preserving
LP when the facility can fund the payout. A remaining cash redemption
can unwind and sell proportional LP/TOKEN inventory subject to execution
and post-exit credit conditions. It can leave a receipt balance when the
residue cannot be paid. In-kind exit delivers TOKEN while accounting for
the share's liability and available residual cash. Quotes at LOW marks
and transaction minimums constrain settlement. A payout that uses spare
credit leaves less room for later holders, and an asset sale changes
remaining depth and marks. A contract test pins a near-zero-K failure in a
fully paired fixture where the BUCK marked in an LP exceeds the amount
burning it actually delivers for retirement. Reproduce the exact fixture
with current deployment flags and thin or stale markets before concluding
how widely the failure applies. Source:
[[file:../../src/basket/BasketExitFacet.sol::function exitShares][exit path]],
[[file:../../src/basket/BasketMarketOps.sol::function exitAssets][cash and in-kind execution]]
and [[file:../../test/basket/BuckCompoundBasketExit.t.sol::function test_nearZeroKExitOfAFullyPairedBasketIsShortByTheMarkedHeldBuck][recorded low-K failure]].

Keeper performance is part of the availability claim. The observer samples
one set of routes and considers every pair. Route reads grow with the
number of constituent/venue combinations. Pair work grows quadratically

* Existing agents and the demand for cheap BUCK

Debtors pledge assets, draw credit and can sell BUCK for USDC. A sale
can depress the BUCK exchange rate. Issued principal, net sell flow,
and an increase in the BUCK cost of the commodity mandate are three
different measurements. In the canonical configuration, six debtor and
six saver arrivals per year are base rates. Investor arrivals, spending,
credit use and price-sensitive entry determine the realized balance of
flows. Thirty base investor arrivals per year is the existing policy's
central calibration; six is a useful low-demand stress setting.

Two sources of countervailing demand already operate. Households with
liabilities and spare cash can purchase BUCK to retire principal when the
discount covers their threshold, fees and impact. The default individual
threshold spans 0.5–3%. Households also build BUCK working balances for
future spending. Their spending share increases with adoption, and the
working balance rises when BUCK is cheap, subject to a cap. Thin liquidity,
cash needs and limited disposable income can prevent both responses.

Use these mechanisms before introducing another agent. An employer with a
contractual BUCK payroll would add a different cash-flow obligation with a
known amount and settlement date. Household working cash approximates
advance purchasing demand. It does not reproduce an employer paying a
worker who then spends the received BUCK. A separate payroll agent is worth
investigating if the response to scheduled obligations becomes the question
or if observed bill and retirement purchases cannot explain the imbalance.

Judge investor performance first in cumulative BUCK per opening share and
BUCK proceeds versus basis for closed receipts. Also report drawdown,
time to redeem, cash versus in-kind payouts, and remaining-holder equity.
Use USDC as a second numeraire. Its return also depends on the change in
the BUCK/USDC bid. These worlds have no independent M2 series or rule that
fixes BUCK against M2. A BUCK gain therefore does not imply any particular
USDC or purchasing-power return.

** Separation, urgency and outward motion

Today urgency adds the allocation gap beyond slack times elapsed
time. Pair separation enters later as a pass/fail check against
accepted relative history. A ratio 15% beyond its reference uses the
same motion rule as one just beyond the 1% floor. Positive outward
velocity and acceleration can reduce the pair rate to zero even
when the spread is wide. Braking can release some of the gap while
the ratio is still moving outward.

The frozen CNST trace distinguishes earlier eligibility from the
ability to change a trade. Both unfunded close-342 maintenance buys

** High-resolution raw-price study

=doc/review/urgency-trial.py= at =b7560339^= mirrored the gap
accumulator while driving the contracts. The Python Kalman experiment
in =pair_observer.py= tracked motion in a different model. The raw-price
runner =alberta_buck.sim.pair_price_study= uses the current alpha-beta
interval observer, pair rate and gap accumulator. It retains the
current target formula and tests tilt zero as well as the default
half tilt. Tilt deliberately moves the target against relative price.
With full-range liquidity, half tilt tends to increase a gap that already
grows as the LP position reprices. It changes the desired exposure
independently of the quality of a turning-point signal.

The runner takes the common monthly USD anchors for LABR_US, CNST,
FOOD_US, NRGC and XAU from the quote engine, or a CSV with
=timestamp,LABR,CNST,FOOD,NRGC,PAXG=. It uses the imported timestamps
when they are already at block resolution. Across missing intervals,
it interpolates in log price and can overlay independent seeded
random walks pinned to both endpoints. The generated points between
monthly anchors are scenarios. The 0, 50 and 150 bp noise settings
specify pre-pinning daily log volatility, scaled by the square root
of elapsed time. An optional causal 30-day EWMA of absolute hourly
relative moves widens each pair's empirical rate allowance.

The normal run samples every 12 seconds. The same price path can be
replayed with wheel calls after 12 seconds, a minute, half an hour
and four hours in rotation. Each observation averages the interval's
held quotes. The filter uses the difference between interval
midpoints. Urgency and gap-release allowance use elapsed days and
the one-day accrual cap. The observer starts a fresh warmup after a
gap greater than one day. The =--previous-gap= arm tests crediting
the last observed allocation gap over the elapsed interval and
clearing it on a side change. The default follows the contract and
credits the newly observed gap. After a late observation, these rules
can disagree on how long the new gap has existed.

The outward experiment adds a small release, capped at 10% of the
ordinary maximum rate. It tapers to zero when the projected outward
move from the upper rate bound and positive acceleration over one
response time consumes the permitted fraction of excess separation.
The acceleration penalty relative to the available separation falls
as separation grows. The absolute acceleration required to stop the
early release rises. Existing braking and closing remain available.
This is a candidate sizing rule.

updates urgency through historical side changes. It uses the

*** Separation-dependent braking

The candidate replaces the earlier drift-budget rule with an
explicit threshold for signed rate change, in units of the pair's
empirical allowance. Just beyond the price-separation floor,
the outward velocity must fall by about one allowance over the
observer response time. As the separation grows, the requirement
falls linearly to 0.05 allowance in the mild-braking arm. An
exploratory signed threshold of -0.25 allowance lets only weak
outward acceleration through once the separation is wide. The
release grows above the threshold and remains subject to normal
eligibility, live price separation and caps. This candidate
applies the threshold to the closing release as well, including a
pair already converging.

Two versions use either the ordered price ratio versus its
accepted reference, or the ordered held-weight ratio versus its
target-weight ratio, to ease the threshold. The weight version
requires the inventory imbalance to agree with the trade direction.
Both versions still require price separation. The test fixture
passes the same mild braking at wide separation that it rejects
near the reference. Clear inward acceleration passes both.

The one-year sweep used two widths (5 and 15 log-percent),
the mild and signed thresholds, both tilt and patience settings,
and two realizations of each nonzero noise level. With six hours

*** Inventory changes, small stalls and slow references

The wheel's completion memo already includes basket BUCK and TOKEN
balances and pool spot and liquidity. A change invalidates the idle
memo. The response still requires a caller and is subject to its
minimum interval, 30 minutes in the study. At a response the facet
observes pairs, places idle holdings, re-marks the book and records the
gaps and rates from the resulting plan. A holding change within the
interval can therefore await a later response even if the wheel now
rechecks the slot. A useful experiment is an inventory-triggered
observation that captures current holdings, marks and decision inputs
without bringing forward trade execution. Check deposit, exit,
arbitrage credit and same-block repeats. Same-timestamp captures must
not add urgency or count as fresh rate evidence.

The small-stall arm lowers the live and filtered spread floor to 35
or 50 bp and offers a small release when outward rate and acceleration
are within the observer allowance. It retains the ordinary braking
and closure releases. On repeated 0.5% price divergences with a
16-day plateau and subsequent reversal, no arm made an eligible
decision at 25 bp allocation slack. A 0.5% price ratio move is only
about a 4.5 bp CNST allocation gap from full-range mark elasticity at
declared weights, before any change in the targets. At 5 bp slack
and 25 bp-days of patience, the half-tilt arm with a 35 bp spread
floor made at most two fills at the ordinary grain and lost a small
amount after fees. Reducing the grain from 2 to 0.25 bp made at most
six fills. The best gain at 5 bp per leg was below 0.0001 pp over the
synthetic year. At 30 bp per leg no candidate beat maintenance on
that tape. The study excludes gas and impact. Keep a sustained
separation control when testing a longer phase of falling outward
acceleration. The stall arm tests current acceleration and does
not estimate its rate of change.

The long-run hypothesis is error correction around a common nominal
trend. One parsimonious model is a currency inflation trend with a
different response lag per commodity, a slow commodity-specific real
price baseline, and a temporary residual. A steady 3% annual trend
with a two-month versus six-month lag produces about a 1% difference
in price levels after calibration. It is a change in the inflation
trend that can produce an expected temporary catch-up. A persistent
reduction in real production cost can move the slow baseline.
Relative prices alone cannot identify the cause or the permanence.
A lag-informed reference would first remove the lag already
expected from the measured divergence. Any temporary target tilt
should depend on expected catch-up beyond that reference and the
cost of acting. A mandate change in long-run weights is a separate
decision.

=commodity-inflation.py --lag-forecast= fits one lag from zero to
12 months using data before 2000 and predicts six-month nominal
price changes on data from 2000 onward. It assumes a one-month CPI
release delay and projects the trailing 12-month CPI rate beyond
the observed months. This is a pilot on the vendored CPI history,
with overlapping forecast windows and without historical vintages.
Food's fitted five-month lag reduced forecast RMSE from 2.94% to
2.85%. Construction improved from 5.23% to 5.20%. Labour tied, while
the other five series worsened slightly. That supports keeping the
lag term in off-chain research and comparing it with an unchanged
reference before tying targets to it.

*** Which sensors matter on the historical proxies?

The Python ablation keeps maintenance, tilt, costs and caps matched
within each comparison. The tested alternatives are a one-point
allocation band, elapsed-time gap persistence by itself, and
persistence plus one observed turn. The turn compares consecutive
10-minute basket-relative price marks at actual wheel calls. After
the same 1% relative-reference separation, it requires the aligned
pair to be closing by more than the fixed rate floor. Its requested
rate is proportional to the allocation gap up to the existing
excursion cap. It uses no fitted acceleration or volatility for
the trade decision. The common simulator still applies the opening

**** Switching the signal in a contract-backed world

The Solidity wheel uses one upkeep facet and a selectable pair policy.
The choices are =motion= (the incumbent, default), =gap= (persistent
allocation gap, sized by excursion), and =turn= (the same gap rule with
one relative-price turn). In =turn=, the policy compares two consecutive
wheel observations of the basket's TWAP window on each ready quote
route. The pair must still be on the intended side of its relative
reference on both the cached marks and the live routes. The observed
closure must clear a rate floor times elapsed seconds and any remaining
own-impact allowance. The two simpler modes scale release up to the
shared maximum at a two-point allocation gap. The floor, depth, book
and issuance limits remain in the same upkeep path.

Savings exposes the mode in *World settings* when starting a new world.
For a matched Python run, vary only =machine.rebalance_mode=. For example,
the following uses the same seed, history, tilt and execution cadence
for all modes. Run it in the development environment with compiled
artifacts.

#+begin_src sh
for mode in motion gap turn; do
    python -m alberta_buck.sim --experiment alberta_buck/sim/experiments/economy.toml \
        --days 120 --set scenario.seed=41404 --set scenario.prices=history \
        --set machine.tilt=0 --set "machine.rebalance_mode=$mode" \
        --out "build/research-results/rebalance-$mode.json" --run-id "rebalance-$mode"
done
#+end_src

=PairRebalancePolicy.setSignalMode= also lets basket governance switch a
deployed world using enum values 0, 1 and 2. It preserves existing
allocation urgency, clears unused discretionary allowance and resets
the signal baseline. Only the basket's responses set the two turn marks.
Use activation-time selection for matched comparisons.

The four-close historical smoke used seed 41404 and zero tilt. All
three modes completed with identical price rows, opening equity,
closing share value and 26 confirmed arbitrage cycles. The WebEVM
smoke booted with =turn= and switched to =gap= as governance. The
Solidity fixtures also exercised a confirmed turn, a route disagreement,
a live crossing and the clearing of allowance on a switch.

Measure the held share and directly held BUCK at the same starting
capital and closing mark. Attribute maintenance, pair-driven fills,
pool fees, flash-arbitrage receipts and charged demurrage separately.
Report reserve and relief effects against the underlying exposures so
the larger TOKEN/BUCK book can be compared with the cost of carrying
BUCK directly.

*** Passive range execution and redemptions

The treasury seeder already places a funded one-sided V3 range
from the live BUCK/USDC price toward parity and lets corrective
arbitrage convert the inventory. For a TOKEN/BUCK pool, price here
means BUCK per TOKEN. A TOKEN-funded range above spot sells TOKEN
for BUCK as the price rises. This can realize a rebound and
stock a redemption buffer. A BUCK-funded range below spot buys
TOKEN as the price falls. An above-spot range needs TOKEN
inventory even when the ultimate allocation objective is to buy
that TOKEN at a cheaper price.

Beyond a filled sell range, a continuing rise lets the basket offer
another bounded TOKEN tranche farther out. The first range's
BUCK proceeds can stay available for redemption. New sell ranges
require TOKEN still available beyond the target and retained
liquidity. Recrossing an open range can undo conversions until
liquidity is withdrawn. The buy-side ladder draws down a cash
budget as a falling price continues. A permanent repricing can
leave it with a larger position, just as with active purchases.
Completed ranges, partially filled ranges, and the inventory
left out of the range all enter the same final book mark.

Use the same target, inventory budget and price signal when
comparing an immediate swap, a single range and a bounded ladder.
Measure BUCK ready by the redemption deadline, net fees, capture
price, partial fills, recross, mint and burn gas, and terminal
inventory against maintenance. The existing synchronous payout
remains backed by liquid BUCK or the established immediate unwind.
Price-only simulations cannot estimate a range's fee share or
fill timing from an interpolated external price alone.

** CNST reversal on a short historical slice

The matched replay used the five historical columns from closes 335
through 346, with five warmup closes, seed 41404, one starting debtor,
one saver, six investors and zero scheduled arrival rates. The price
anchors and household stocks were established at the start of the
slice. It is a fresh-world reproduction of the price move.

In the world with zero extra Jubilee funding, there was no non-Jubilee
receipt flow on source day 342 and Jubilee minted about 1.9 bp of
prior-close supply. The first three CNST decisions reached 114–120
bp-days of urgency. The fourth reached 200 bp-days with a 246 bp
underweight allocation, but the directional rate was zero. A confirmed
23,063 BUCK purchase on that response was already in the maintenance
plan. The price gap persisted long enough to observe both gates.
On the external tape, CNST had fallen 11.25–15.32 log-percent against
each peer from the slice opening by that close, and the spread had
been widening. The external separation does not establish readiness
or live spread on either on-chain quote route.

On source day 343 the active plan called for confirmed CNST buys
worth 15,564 and 14,808 BUCK. The same-state plans with the pair rate
disabled called for zero on those two decisions. Two other positive
asks that day, 681 and 831 BUCK, were below the minimum trade size
of about 2,418 BUCK (two grains). This sequence exposes three limits separately.
Urgency delayed eligibility, the directional rate still held when
urgency first qualified, and minimum size absorbed small asks.

From matched opening equity of 6.06M BUCK, the active share mark
gained 0.363% and maintenance gained 0.348% over treatment, a
0.014 percentage-point difference.

The 2M investment opened treatment at 8.80M BUCK in both arms, 45%
above the unfunded pair. Non-Jubilee receipt flow was again zero on
source day 342. The largest CNST decision gap was -291 bp. One
decision missed urgency and three eligible decisions had zero
directional rate. The first changed CNST plan with a confirmed swap
again came on day 343, for 15,187 BUCK, with a second on day 344 for
9,392 BUCK. The active share mark gained 0.049% and maintenance lost
0.024%, a 0.073 percentage-point difference. The additional capital
increased the measured gap and changed the return. It left the
directional delay in place on this tape and seed.

Replay the historical full state and log per-peer readiness,
separation and motion to explain the zero directional rate. This
short slice uses new opening state and price anchors and the two
funding cases share one price path. The matched mature-scale and
controlled-flow experiments remain open.

* Recommendations for performance and stability

1. Finish the funded, matched comparisons before changing the contract.
   Rank alternatives on investor BUCK return, matched receipt payouts and
   drawdown. Compare active with maintenance at each funding level before
   assigning a gain to the signal. Examine the worst seed alongside the
   median, and compare at the last common observed commodity anchor.
2. Determine whether realized buybacks and working-money purchases absorb
   issuer sells. Compare accepted purchases, principal retired, working
   balances, net market flow, depth and duration of BUCK discounts. If
   additional demand increases ROI, check whether it came from profitable
   TOKEN trades, improved parity or a transfer of value from another cohort.
3. Preserve the existing braking rule as the reference. If the longer runs
   support tuning, change one of target tilt, activation threshold or
   release speed at a time. Check both a temporary markdown and a permanent
   repricing with the same first leg. Count eligible gaps, changes to
   executable plans and fills. Increasing signal frequency can add churn
   or leave the final orders identical.
4. Keep available-credit-first redemption and near-zero idle cash as the
   operating baseline. Test a small buffer only against measured savings in
   mint transactions, carry and keeper gas. Cross clustered withdrawals
   with a lower credit limit and thinner venues. Verify paid BUCK, time to
   proceeds, depth left for the next holder and time to recover parity.
5. Test historical candidates on independently observed prices where
   possible, otherwise disclose the interpolation and release assumptions.
   Select a change using several held-out windows and household seeds.
   A candidate needs positive incremental BUCK returns after costs and
   acceptable downside and liquidity under persistent shocks. USDC marks
   and realized exit proceeds remain useful independent checks.
