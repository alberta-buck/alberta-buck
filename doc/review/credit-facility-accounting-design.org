#+TITLE: Buck, credit facilities, Jubilee, and insurer pools
#+DATE: 2026-10-29
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:3

* Status

This is the proposed accounting boundary and an executable Python reference,
not a description of the deployed Solidity contracts. It supersedes the
signed-account proposal in [[file:jubilee-single-position-design.org][the earlier single-position design]] wherever that
design treats unused activated credit as unissued or assigns relief to a
negative account balance. Its basket top-up, callback, and O(1) fund-checkpoint
analysis remain useful subject to the corrections here. The economic formulas
and commands are also explained in [[file:../../alberta-buck-demurrage.org][Demurrage and the Jubilee Fund]].

* The accounting boundary

| Party | Authoritative responsibility |
|-------+------------------------------|
| Buck | Owns transferable BUCK: ordinary holdings, issued-but-unspent BUCK, their demurrage, Carrying fee history, ERC-20 transfers, and aggregate issuance. |
| Each BuckCredit NFT | Owns its insured coverage, insurer and pool, outstanding issued principal, and the time integral of that principal. Relief belongs to the NFT presented for reduction. |
| Jubilee fund | Owns the demurrage proceeds and one adjustable basket position; liquidates a proportion, computes payable relief from available net BUCK, and pays full-value BUCK. |
| Each insurer's pool | Receives that insurer's premium deposits and supplies the corresponding nominal refunds, with the pool's average Carrying fee history. |

For an ordinary account, let H be its received/held net BUCK and U its net
issued-but-unspent BUCK. The account may have H, U, and outstanding liabilities
at the same time. Its ERC-20 =balanceOf= reports the *full spendable balance*
H + U; separate views expose H and U without subtracting its NFT liabilities.
Spending U transfers BUCK already issued: it neither mints again nor reduces an
NFT. Receiving H does not automatically retire any NFT. The user explicitly
selects facilities for repayment. These views do not include merely potential,
not-yet-issued capacity.

A Carrying service reports its nominal transferable balance in =balanceOf=.
An ordinary recipient receives the corresponding net value after the service's
proportionate carried fee; a Carrying recipient retains both nominal BUCK and
the fee history. Thus =balanceOf= remains the complete amount the account can
transfer, but a nominal Carrying transfer does not promise an ordinary recipient
that same amount of net value. Interfaces needing full-value payment must say so
and gross up the nominal transfer. Wallets, AMM reserves, allowances, events,
and routing quotes need to use the appropriate units consistently.
The reference restricts facility owners to non-Carrying accounts, like the
current basket's credit-holding account. It rejects making an account
Carrying while it has issued credit. Whether to support a Carrying facility
owner requires defining the nominal and net views of its unspent BUCK and
full-value premium/repayment paths; it must not silently omit that unspent
amount from =balanceOf=.

When a facility issues 1,000,000 BUCK into U with no premium or other flows,
H is zero, U and =balanceOf= are 1,000,000, the facility's outstanding
principal is 1,000,000, and total issued increases by 1,000,000 immediately.
U starts paying demurrage immediately. Its initial demurrage base is 1,000,000
BUCK; this base decreases as fees are attributed to Jubilee. At nominal 2%
continuously compounded, the reference produces:

| Time | H | U and balanceOf | Jubilee net | Facility target relief |
|------+---+-----------------+-------------+------------------------|
| issue | 0 |      1,000,000 |           0 |                      0 |
| 1 year | 0 | 980,198.673307 | 19,801.326693 |             20,000.000000 |
| 50 years | 0 | 367,879.441171 | 632,120.558829 |          1,000,000.000000 |

At one year, gross demurrage is 20,000: 19,801.326693 moved from U to the
fund and 198.673307 was paid by the fund to itself. The latter is already in
the fund, not newly created cash. The target is not a guaranteed fund payment;
the actual payment is computed after liquidation and is limited by available
net fund BUCK and the principal reduced. With no other assets or transactions,
the fund and U together can extinguish the whole outstanding million.

* Evidence from the existing contracts

A temporary Foundry comparison deployed copies of =master= and the feature
branch, activated one 1,000,000-BUCK facility at K = 1 and zero premium, and
waited one year. Values below are BUCK, ignoring one raw-unit rounding in the
master NFT quote:

| Contract version | balanceOf | Account fee | NFT/account target relief | Jubilee net |
|------------------+-----------+-------------+---------------------------+-------------|
| master | 1,000,000 | 0 | NFT: about 20,000 | 0 |
| feature branch | 1,000,000 | 0 | account: 0 | 0 |
| proposed reference | 980,198.673307 | 19,801.326693 | NFT: 20,000 | 19,801.326693 |

Both versions expose unspent activated capacity in =balanceOf=, but neither
puts the untouched amount into outstanding BUCK supply or charges it
account-level demurrage. Master stores coverage-seconds on each NFT; it ages
activated coverage, which is not necessarily equal to BUCK issued after K,
valuation changes, or premium funding. The branch instead ages an aggregate
negative account balance. Neither reconstructs a separate actual principal and
age for each NFT. Incoming BUCK in the branch can also reduce that account
lien automatically, which does not match an owner's choice of facility.

The older pre-signed implementation at =b1af78ff= explicitly created BUCK
at activation and is informative about issuance, but its old balance/limit
ratchet and gross-linear demurrage are not this design. Reverting wholesale to
it or to master would also lose subsequent K and accounting fixes. Preserve
useful tests and fixes while changing the accounting boundary.

* Candidate core and operations

** Buck

Store sufficient nonnegative net state to account for H and U independently,
plus Carrying nominal/fee state where required. Use one authoritative issuance
counter and checkpoint the fund before that counter or direct fund cash changes.
An account's balance need not encode its debt or its age in a sign. An
implementation may pack fields, but must prove precision, saturation, bounds,
and the cost of long-idle accounts before choosing widths. Global issuance and
fund flow counters should use 256-bit amounts; token account units and these
counters need not use the same representation.

Useful views and actions, with names illustrative rather than ABI commitments:

#+begin_example
balanceOf(owner)                   -> H + U, or nominal Carrying balance
heldNetOf(owner), unspentNetOf(owner), carriedFeeOf(owner)
totalIssued()                      -> sum of outstanding NFT principal
issue(facility, amount)            -> add issued amount to owner's U
transfer / transferFrom            -> move spendable BUCK; U is already issued
reduce(facility, principal, ...)   -> select this NFT and settle its reduction
#+end_example

The issue authorization needs the existing insurer/owner, appraisal, K,
premium-funding and identity gates. Premium payment is a real full-value BUCK
transfer from available account funds to the insurer's pool; it is not a
second mint hidden in =balanceOf=. All new issuance, including any amount
needed to fund a premium, must be attributed to selected NFT principal. An
amount already issued remains spendable and incurs demurrage even if a later K
change reduces capacity for new issuance. The precise per-facility allocation
of K and face value requires an implementation decision, especially where
coverage, depreciation, and insurance charges differ from issued BUCK.

For ordinary senders the reference spends H first, then U. That ordering is an
implementation candidate, not a separate liability repayment. A standard
transfer, including =transferFrom=, must have the same spendable balance and
spend order. A facility's ownership and principal cannot be inferred from a
particular transferable BUCK after it leaves U.

** BuckCredit

For each NFT keep outstanding principal P and its time integral
S = integral(P dt), plus insured coverage, valuation, insurer, premium terms,
and the original premium pool/principal. Gross issued BUCK-seconds remain
relevant to system-wide demurrage, including after 50 years. The NFT's
*claimable* seconds are capped at P*50 years before principal changes, so
unused age past the cap cannot give later issuance immediate relief. For a
reduction x, the reference assigns the proportional claimable seconds S*x/P
and caps the baseline relief r*S*x/P at x. Different NFTs owned by the same
account retain different ages.

The present reference uses one proportional average age within an NFT. Once
new credit joins an old facility, the aggregate principal resumes accruing
at 2% until the combined claim reaches its cap. For example, after a 100-BUCK
facility has aged 100 years, issuing another 100 gives it an immediate target
of 100, then 104 one year later. Separate issuance-vintage relief would yield
102 after that year and requires a different, more complex accounting model.
This averaging rule must remain visible when evaluating renewal and reductions.

Keep principal and coverage as separate quantities; they need not be equal at
K other than one. Avoid duplicate mirrored coverage mappings if BuckCredit can
be the single source of truth, but preserve the ability to attribute every
issuance and premium to the correct NFT. Exact NFT transfer rules and which
owner can reduce or renew a facility must be specified; transferring an NFT
must not silently transfer or destroy somebody's existing BUCK account.

** Jubilee and the single basket position

Use a separate Carrying Jubilee manager. =BuckBasketEquity.deposit= creates a
receipt, while =increasePosition= adds the same asset to an existing receipt
and returns its ID, credited amount, and added shares. The equity basket now
prices both paths using the amount actually credited to its net BUCK equity;
an aged Carrying transfer is no longer priced at its nominal amount. Under the
legacy token, that equity change can also reflect a change in an outstanding
relief claim; the net-demurrage integration must revalidate this measurement.
TOKEN deposits use the balance actually received. Shares and cost basis use a
pooled average, and partial exits update the receipt's remaining basis and
TOKEN principal. Deposits into a basket with existing shares but zero equity
are rejected: its entry price is undefined, and minting one share per BUCK
would give existing worthless shares a recapitalization windfall. A deliberate
recapitalization policy remains separate work.

=redeemShares= returns the actual BUCK paid, shares retired, and remaining
shares. It returns the same ID while shares remain, or zero when the emptied
receipt is burned. Zero shares reverts; it cannot trigger legacy =redeem(id,
bp)=, where =bp == 0= means full redemption. In-kind TOKEN transfers are
reported by the existing =PaidInKind= events; a recipient must measure its
own net receipts if a TOKEN charges a transfer fee. An equity-less exit still
reverts =Underwater=;
the manager must treat failed or partial sales as such, not invent proceeds
or remove unretired shares. The older [[file:jubilee-single-position-design.org][single-position design]] records the
pre-fix probe that motivated the net-deposit correction.

Reduction should be coordinated outside a locked Buck call, since the basket
calls Buck and Buck has a reentrancy lock. Authorize the real holder, preserve
identity and allowance checks, redeem the selected proportion of the fund's
position, and then compute relief using actual net cash. The current reference
defaults to selected baseline relief divided by the sum of outstanding capped
baseline relief; a zero benchmark requests zero redemption. Its original
10% reserve-signal payout is only a comparison and has a demonstrated
first-mover problem, described below. Proportional cash-plus-proceeds payout
is the chosen reference default and intended contract behavior. It is not a
guaranteed 2% annual return, and its contract implementation still needs
fixed-point, liquidity, and adversarial validation.
Zero proceeds or zero payable relief are valid. The owner supplies the rest of
the reduction; a failed owner payment or failed transaction must roll back all
steps. The fund grosses up its Carrying transfer to deliver the selected full
net relief. It must not count carried demurrage as fresh income twice.

A basket redemption can be illiquid, return TOKEN, leave shares outstanding,
or revert. The design must specify the treatment of actual proceeds and any
partial result; it cannot assume a quoted mark is immediately available BUCK.
It must also retain the basket's low-K exit and solvency checks. Household
incentives to buy discounted BUCK and retire liabilities are a separate
stability problem.

*** A simpler payout signal and a concrete ordering failure

The reserve-signal prototype treats all post-sale cash divided by 10% of the
baseline benchmark as its ROI multiplier. That treats intentionally undeposited
cash, and the sale itself, as investment gain. For two equal 100-BUCK
facilities after ten idle years, the fund contains 36.253849 net BUCK and each
facility's baseline target is 20. The prototype pays the first reduction the
entire 36.253849; the second receives zero and its owner is 18.126925 BUCK
short of closing. This is a defect in that policy, not evidence of a missing
source of BUCK. Which facility happens to close first changes the outcome.

The chosen rule removes the reserve ratio from payout. Let b be the selected
facility's baseline relief, B the sum of all capped baselines, C the fund's
net cash just before sale, f = b/B, and Z the actual net BUCK proceeds of
selling fraction f of the basket position. If b and B are nonzero:

#+begin_example
payable relief = min(principal being reduced, f * C + Z)
effective factor = payable relief / b
#+end_example

If b is zero the relief and default sale fraction are zero. The sale proceeds
are already included in available fund cash; do not count them twice. With the
two idle facilities, f = 1/2 and Z = 0 for the first reduction: each receives
18.126925 and can close. If the realized fund portfolio is 2.5% above its
benchmark and sales are proportional at that mark, this computes a 1.025
factor, corresponding to 2.05% instead of the baseline 2%. A zero-value sale
contributes zero; cash and actual proceeds still bound the payment. This is
one simple way to realize returns gradually as positions are withdrawn, with
no separate EMA state. Price impact, partial exits, different facility ages,
rounding, capped claims, and adversarial ordering still need testing.

The administrative 10% target independently controls cash retained for
operations and gradual investment of surplus. The isolated =JubileeFund=
prototype implements the schedule below, but it is not deployed or yet
validated with a leveraged basket. A reserve target is a liquidity control;
cash sitting above that target is not by itself proof of portfolio return.

Keep a last-checkpoint time, the last
observed excess cash E = max(0, C - 0.1*B), and an uninvested deployment
budget P. For an interval dt in which the observed excess was unchanged, let
P += (E - P)*(1 - 2^(-dt/day)). Checkpoint the old E before recognizing a
cash or benchmark change; then cap P at the new excess. If P can fund at least
1% of the current reserve target, deposit it into the same receipt, subtract
the actual net outflow from P, and record the resulting excess. In a constant interval this
deploys half the outstanding gap in a day and accumulates sub-threshold
budgets rather than forgetting them. Increases seen only at a later lazy
checkpoint are not treated as if they were present throughout the interval.
This is a discrete approximation while cash and targets evolve. An unobserved
decrease and subsequent increase can mature the budget faster than continuous
observation would, though current excess always caps spending. A mint
checkpoints without trading while Buck is locked; an authorized keeper must
execute a bounded investment separately. Governance can explicitly override
the schedule. More varied keeper timing and market simulations remain needed.

The isolated token's =fundFlows()= reports full-width cumulative gross
demurrage, net demurrage received from outside the fund, net BUCK transfers
into and out of the fund, and net relief extinguished. Each count consists of
256-bit whole raw BUCK units and a separate fractional remainder with a =1e36=
denominator. The views include demurrage accrued since the last checkpoint.
The net cash reconciliation is:

#+begin_example
net fund cash = net demurrage + transfers in - transfers out - relief
#+end_example

Gross demurrage also includes demurrage on BUCK already in the Carrying fund;
it must not be added a second time to the fund's net cash. The fund separately
reports =investmentFlows()=: cumulative actual net BUCK invested in its
managed position and received from sales, including actual BUCK from bounded
conversions of new in-kind TOKEN. Historical unconverted TOKEN is excluded.
These two cumulative flows alone do not
measure profit: the unsold position still has value. Counters have full
256-bit whole-unit range, but still revert if that range is eventually
exhausted; instantaneous scaled issuance has a separate lower bound.

** Renewal in place

An owner and authorized insurer can agree an appraisal, new coverage, premium
rate and optional additional issuance. An atomic renewal checkpoints this NFT,
redeems the required Jubilee share, applies the actual relief to that same
principal, updates the coverage and issues any agreed additional U in one
transaction. No step releases old coverage before the replacement is effective.
The reference enforces that the final =max(0, principal - limit)= cannot exceed
an existing shortfall; it neither recalls issuance after a K cut nor permits a
renewal that worsens it. Exact K allocation and authorization still need design.

The Python prototype explores returning the old nominal insurance principal
and paying a freshly quoted full-value deposit on renewal. It supports an
insurer change while refunding only from the recorded old pool. That is one
possible settlement convention; retaining a same-insurer deposit and settling
only an agreed difference may be cheaper and needs separate specification.
The isolated Solidity prototype implements the same-insurer form with
independent owner and insurer consent; an insurer change is not implemented.
The reference does not perform actuarial pricing, signatures, or on-chain
callbacks. It resets the renewed facility's issuance-age checkpoint after
crystallizing actual relief: an unpaid baseline is not a guaranteed extra
claim.

** Insurer premium pools

A 10 BUCK deposit transfers *10 full-value BUCK* to the facility's upstream
insurer's Carrying pool. A full release returns *10 nominal BUCK* from the
same pool, with its proportional average carried demurrage. For example, if
the pool has 100 nominal and 20 carried fee, its 10 nominal refund has 8 net
BUCK and 2 carried fee. Partial releases return a proportional nominal
principal. The refund is not grossed up like Jubilee relief.

Record the actual pool against the NFT, so an insurer's later pool-registry
change cannot redirect an existing deposit. A negotiated insurer change must
settle or transfer the old obligation atomically; no other insurer's pool
implicitly covers it. An insurer can hold, invest and redeem its own assets,
including at the last instant, provided it can deliver the nominal obligation
and associated pool-average fee history. The reference models a simple
Carrying investment account that can be moved back before refund; actual
basket positions, returns, liquidity and insolvent-insurer policy remain to be
specified. A strategy that has lost its nominal assets may be unable to pay;
the current reference fails the release atomically instead of inventing funds
or consuming another insurer's deposit.

* Global checkpoint and open decisions

For no intervening issuance change or external fund flow, let I be total
outstanding issuance, F the fund's liquid *net* BUCK, and D = exp(-r*dt).
Conservation includes ordinary H, issued U, pool and strategy BUCK, and F.
The fund can checkpoint without enumerating holders:

#+begin_example
F_new = F + (I - F) * (1 - D)
gross demurrage increment = r * I * dt
fund self-fee increment = gross increment - (F_new - F)
#+end_example

Checkpoint before I or fund cash changes and verify every transfer path,
including unspent issuance, Carrying fee transfer, baskets and insurers. The
basket receipt is a claim on underlying assets: do not add both the receipt's
mark and the underlying BUCK to token supply. Carrying =balanceOf= values are
nominal and cannot simply be summed to compute economic net supply.

The reference sums =min(P_i, r*S_i)= across NFTs for its redemption benchmark.
A global BUCK-seconds counter alone can track the *uncapped* sum in O(1), but
not generally the sum of separately capped claims after 50 years. The standalone
=JubileeClaims= library now tracks the exact sum of capped claimable
BUCK-seconds: its slope is the total principal of unsaturated positions, and a
min-heap records each facility's next saturation. An update is O(log N). The
chosen and total BUCK-seconds form the payout ratio before rounding to external
BUCK units; this avoids separately rounding every NFT's fractional claim.

Maturities are processed in bounded, permissionless batches. Exact reads and
position changes revert while a maturity backlog remains, rather than using an
overstated denominator. A keeper or any caller must clear the backlog before
those operations can proceed; the liveness and gas tradeoff remains. In a
64-facility simultaneous-maturity test, a batch
of eight cost at most 328,483 gas in this environment; larger and adversarial
workloads still need measurement. The isolated token constructs a separate
=JubileeClaimBook= around the library; only the token may synchronize a
facility, the book reads NFT state directly, and processing is permissionless.
The fund uses this benchmark through the token's views.

Relief above the nominal target can also make gross accrual minus actual
payments negative, so cumulative full-width inflow and outflow counters need
guarded arithmetic. The target reserve's responsiveness, sale frequency,
minimum transaction size, ordering effects, and possible deliberate gaming
also require simulation.

Existing delegated demurrage is a separate user-facing feature, not needed by
this reference. Removing it could simplify Buck but requires a decision about
lost functionality; retaining it requires a net-balance conservation design.
Likewise, retire older baskets or agents only when their distinct checks are
covered. This work does not alter the low-K exit rule.

* Implementation and validation

1. Derive fixed-point net decay and Carrying allocation independently, including
   dust, mixed-age transfers, up to and beyond 50 years, touch-frequency
   independence, overflow and rounding. Migrate old state explicitly or start
   a clean genesis: a raw-linear checkpoint cannot reconstruct net history.
2. Implement and test basket same-receipt top-up and precise reduction with
   actual net deposits, basis and share changes, callbacks, fees, zero value,
   in-kind/partial exits and existing solvency checks.
3. Implement per-facility issuance/age, separate held and unspent balance,
   =balanceOf= and ERC-20 parity, NFT ownership/authorization, K changes,
   Jubilee pre-redemption and grossed-up payment, insurer-pool refunds and
   atomic renewal. Test zero payout, no cross-insurer subsidy and rollback.
4. Update the Rust mirror, Python/JavaScript integrations and sandboxes, then
   rerun contract tests and five-year economy simulations with freshly
   generated reports. Existing simulations and Solidity tests exercise the
   old accounting and are not validation of this proposed design.
