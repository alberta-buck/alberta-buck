* Decisions and invariants

- [X] Use the selected share of net cash plus the marked basket position,
  capped by principal, as the default relief. Pay only from existing cash and
  revert if the payout exceeds 10% of that net cash. The old proportional-sale
  and reserve-ratio formulas remain reference comparisons.
- [X] Carrying services use nominal =balanceOf=, transfers, allowances and
  events. Carried fees affect their net value and pass proportionally on
  transfer; ordinary accounts report net spendable value. Services are meant
  for BUCK-neutral flows, and their users must understand pooled fees.
- [ ] Enforce the intended allowlist of BUCK-neutral service contracts and
  verify ordinary/Carrying, pool/recipient, insurer, Notes and AMM behavior.
  Current registry binding is not an independent neutral-service allowlist.
- [X] An ordinary account's held and issued-but-unspent net BUCK are
  simultaneously spendable. New, not-yet-issued capacity is separate.
  Transfers do not retire any facility implicitly.
- [X] A facility's outstanding issued principal and integral of BUCK-seconds
  belong to the selected BuckCredit NFT, not to an account-sign state. Issued
  but unspent BUCK start incurring demurrage immediately. Early actual relief
  is not an unconditional promise of the 2% target; at maturity the fund
  assumes any unburned principal when the lien is released.
- [X] Jubilee is a separate Carrying fund and holds at most one adjustable
  basket receipt. The keeper sells separately when cash is below target;
  reductions and renewals do not sell. Gross up the nominal Carrying outflow
  to deliver full net relief. A partial or in-kind keeper sale counts actual
  net BUCK, preserves unsold shares and never bypasses low-K exits.
- [X] Each insurer receives its own facilities' *full-value* premium deposits;
  a release returns the agreed *nominal* deposit with the pool's average
  carried demurrage from the same recorded pool. No cross-insurer subsidy.
- [X] Require the holder's maximum acceptable premium on each issuance and
  revert atomically if current terms or a changed mark exceed it. The
  [[file:bucknet-economy-implementation.org][economy checklist]] tracks matching clients, artifacts and snapshot compatibility.
- [X] Renewal checkpoints and settles only the selected facility, updates
  insured value, rate and any new issuance atomically, and must not temporarily
  deactivate coverage or worsen a pre-existing K shortfall.
- [ ] Finalize and verify exact rounding, 50-year cap, per-facility partial
  retirement, total issuance, fee transfer/settlement, and migration invariants
  against independent high-precision references. An isolated contract
  prototype exists; full cross-implementation proof remains open.
- [X] Prototype an exact aggregate benchmark for separately capped NFT
  claimable seconds without enumerating all owners on each operation.
- [ ] Integrate and stress-test bounded maturity processing, keeper liveness,
  per-NFT atomic synchronization, and final payout rounding; the benchmark

* Milestones

** New: separate Jubilee reserve rebalancing from credit settlement

The desired design makes Jubilee redemption a separate keeper transaction.
A normal reduction or renewal must not redeem a basket position, and its
actual net relief must not exceed 10% of Jubilee's net liquid BUCK immediately
before settlement. Revert, rather than silently reduce relief, when this limit
is exceeded. Preserve owner authorization and minimum relief, Carrying gross-up,
the single basket receipt, in-kind slippage bounds and low-K basket safeguards.

The user also requires that a 50-year-old facility can release its lien and
refund its premium from its own insurer pool, even when its owner has spent
all issued BUCK. A strict per-transaction liquidity limit alone cannot ensure
that outcome: the facility may be large relative to the fund, the basket can
be illiquid or lose value, and a final raw-unit remainder cannot always be
split. Insurer refunds can independently be illiquid. The owner cannot be
assumed to hold the BUCK issued by the facility. The user chose to release
the matured lien and record any unburned issuance as an explicit Jubilee
obligation, retaining it in supply until the fund burns it. Maturity is when
the facility's pooled average issuance age reaches 50 years; individual
top-ups do not each require a separately aged tranche. An unpaid insurer
refund remains a claim on the original pool and can be retried.

* Open implementation constraints

The fixed 10% limit can prevent a final tiny deferred BUCK burn if liquid net
cash is smaller than ten raw BUCK units. The former owner's lien is already
released, but that dust remains in supply as a fund obligation. Keeper sales
may fail under low K or insufficient market liquidity. The lien does not need
a basket sale, but maturity processing can require bounded public batches.
An insolvent insurer cannot physically return its nominal deposit immediately:
the original-pool refund survives as a separate claim and can be retried.
These are not guarantees of an available liquid market or a solvent insurer.

The new =BuckCredit.setBuck= assignment is reserved for the account or factory
that deployed that credit contract. A factory must provide its own authorized
initialization path. This protects *new* deployments; it cannot repair an
already deployed immutable credit contract. The legacy =Deploy.s.sol= now
wires and verifies both the credit and registry links, but still uses legacy
Buck and stub verifiers. =make nix-deploy-dry-run= simulates that script in a
local EVM without broadcasting; it is not a deployment recipe for BuckNet.

On 2026-10-29, installing the isolated worktree's locked =core/js=
dependencies reported npm audit findings. A separate =npm audit --omit=dev
--json= from =core/js= reported 54 affected packages: 37 high, three moderate
and 14 low, including the direct =tevm= dependency and transitive packages.
Package counts are not distinct exploitable vulnerabilities, and inclusion in
the dependency tree does not establish reachability in the browser bundle or
server. Review the advisories, deployed bundle and upgrade paths before
release; do not blindly apply a lockfile-wide fix without testing. The ignored
local =build/core-js-audit-production.json= contains this point-in-time report;
rerun the command for current results.

The Python model uses floating-point continuous decay and an idealized BUCK
strategy. Its tests are not proof of EVM fixed-point accuracy, market liquidity,
or security. Existing Buck/basket integration depends on signed account APIs,
Jubilee views and per-account settlement; update consumers as part of the change,
not by stubbing old behavior. The current equity basket's own credit must be
handled without recursive settlement through a locked Buck call.

The basket currently measures BUCK contributions by the change in the legacy
account's net equity, which can include changes in accrued legacy relief. It
reverts an equity-less exit, so a future manager must handle a failed sale as
zero proceeds without pretending its shares were retired. New capital cannot
enter a live zero-equity basket until a recapitalization policy is specified.

The fixed-point primitive uses a 36-decimal per-second decay factor and splits
intervals into 1,000-year chunks, so a very small factor is applied before it
is rounded to zero. An interval of at least 9,000 years leaves less than one
unit even for =uint256.max=. Frequent checkpoints require fractional raw-unit
state; integer-only account checkpoints would still lose dust. Its fund
function assumes issuance and net fund cash use the same unit and that every
economic BUCK is accounted for exactly once. The Carrying gross-up uses a
pro-rata floored fee; it does not establish parity with the old BUCK-seconds
representation. Scaled account bounds, per-NFT capped aggregate relief and
actual-token transitions still require full-system testing.
The new NFT book is intentionally not authoritative for legacy Buck activity.
The experimental token and fund call the per-facility hooks atomically, but
neither legacy state migration nor production deployment is implemented.
=BuckCredit= is near the EIP-170 size limit, so further logic needs size
checks or extraction instead of assuming it will fit.

=BuckNet= is a separate clean-genesis integration prototype, not a replacement
for =Buck.sol=. It issues actual unspent BUCK against each activated NFT,
keeps insurer deposits pinned to their original pool, and lets only the
designated fund execute an exact, owner-authorized reduction. =JubileeFund=
implements the marked proportional coordinator, tested with a mock basket and
the cash-only real equity basket, and calls an insurer pool's liquidity
preparation before entering locked settlement. Pool withdrawals require the pool's
allowance; real insurer pool implementations still need integration. The real equity
basket's opt-in net mode holds and invests actual BUCK without a credit
facility; it rejects opening one. This avoids incorrectly treating incoming
BUCK as debt retirement or using unissued credit as a spendable balance. It
does not establish leveraged basket operation or low-K exit behavior under
the new token. One real-AMM in-kind exit is tested in the cash-only mode; more
market and multi-asset cases remain. Its mode cannot be enabled
after shares or credit exist. A separate keeper sale converts newly received
in-kind TOKEN to net BUCK using bounded routes or reverts the sale; ordinary
credit operations do not invoke it. Old in-kind inventory still needs a
disposition policy. Marked relief deducts the basket gain fee and requires
liquidity and a complete TWAP history for each contributing pool; this does
not prove the mark is manipulation-resistant or executable. The reserve
controller defaults to 10%
of capped target relief, accumulates a budget with a one-day half-life and
requires a transaction worth at least 1% of that target. A configured keeper
or governance performs scheduled trades with caller-supplied slippage bounds;
governance also has an explicit manual override. A keeper must be trusted to
set appropriate bounds, or a future production deployment must enforce a price
policy. Issuance checkpoints the budget without trading inside Buck's lock;
keepers must still execute investments. Newly observed surplus is not
backdated, and the budget is capped by current excess. This is a discrete
observation policy, not an exact continuous integral: an unobserved decrease
and later increase in excess can mature budget earlier than a continuously
observed controller. Fund actions checkpoint on both sides of their changes.
The prototype has no insurer-change renewal, production deployment
integration, or supported migration. Its internal fractional
retirement can require integral ERC-20 burn events to be rounded; the exact
split is recorded in =FacilityReduced= and still needs indexer review.

The token creates an immutable =JubileeClaimBook= that alone maintains its
aggregate capped benchmark. Only the token may synchronize an NFT, and the
book reads principal and claim age directly from BuckCredit. Anyone can
process due maturities either through the token or the book. The claim
benchmark includes deferred matured fund obligations, retains BUCK-seconds
precision and rounds the selected
relief only at settlement; it is not the sum of individually rounded raw-unit
claims. Matured entries must be processed in bounded transactions before an
exact view or change; otherwise they revert. Caller incentives, front-running,
large-population gas, and coordination with a real
fund keeper all need explicit integration tests. A quiet or adversarially large
backlog can delay settlement until keepers process it; no assumption that all
old facilities can be swept in one block is made.

The standalone ledger stores fractional net values at =1e36= units per BUCK
raw unit. It separately tracks the fund's gross Carrying amount and net value,
and checkpoints total issuance before fund flows and principal changes. The
scaled aggregate must fit in =uint256=. Cumulative audit counters instead use
full-width whole raw units plus fractional remainders. In the isolated token,
the identity registry synchronizes permitted mode changes before committing
its flag. Outstanding facility owners remain ordinary, and the Jubilee fund
and registered insurer pools remain Carrying. The NFT scan during a mode
change is proportional to that owner's number of credit NFTs. Production
integration and migration must preserve these boundaries.

Rounding exposes a real dust case: after a tiny issuance ages, the debtor and
the fund can each own less than one external raw unit even though together
they can retire one. =retireWithFund= settles their fractional net amounts
atomically and consumes the fund's proportional nominal history; separate
ordinary transfers cannot express that operation. The integrated token must
define detailed fractional-settlement events alongside ERC-20 burn events,
verify which net relief the policy authorized, and test indexer and allowance
behavior. The library is internal and performs no identity, insurer, K, NFT,
or reentrancy checks on behalf of the token.
