A2 key split: the mint binding and the spend proof name different issuers
==========================================================================

Follow-on to doc/review/identity-accumulator-review-grok.txt.
Date: 2026-09-21. Branch: feature/identity-accumulator.

This note records one concrete failure of the A2 anti-framing claim, the
mitigations that actually close it, and an information-flow account of A1,
A2, and B1. The flow is the tool for the failure. A proof is only a tie
between two facts when the same party cannot satisfy one fact with one
value and the other fact with another. Writing down, at each step, who can
generate, intercept, recover, satisfy, or duplicate each artifact is how
that question gets answered. The A2 split is the case where two proofs
about one ciphertext were assumed to share a key, and do not.


1. The failure
--------------

issuer_reenc proves that an A2 ciphertext eIss encrypts the minter's
registered identity under a receiving key, and it hides that key inside

    Q = pk_mint + beta*H

with H the known-log generator of issuer_reenc. deposit_fold_a2 proves
that the spender's own receiving secret k opens that same ciphertext to a
registered identity. Nothing in either proof, in Notes.mint, in
Notes.spendCoupledA2, or in verify_receipt requires those two keys to be
the same point.

An ElGamal ciphertext does not bind a plaintext to one key. For one
randomness r and two registered identity points M_A and M_B,

    pk_mint = pk_real + r^{-1}*(M_B - M_A)
    eIss    = (r*G, M_A + r*pk_mint)
            = (r*G, M_B + r*pk_real)

pk_real = k*G is the recipient's mailbox, which the minter is given in
order to address the note. The minter does not need k. Decrypting under
the hidden mint key returns M_A. Decrypting under the recipient's k
returns M_B.

Ran against the shipped verifiers, with M_A the plaintext of the minter's
registered account credential, M_B a second registered identity with its
own salted leaf, and the recipient's k in a receiving leaf:

    issuer_reenc_verify against the minter's credential     accepts
        the mint proof's statement is "encrypts M_A"
    the same proof against M_B's credential                 rejects
    pk_mint == pk_real                                      false
    deposit_fold_check for the recipient's k                accepts
    deposit_fold_a2_witness, relation 5 on M_B's leaf       builds
    the same witness pointed at M_A's leaf                  refused
    verify_receipt against the minter's credential          VALID
        and the issuer it names is M_B, not M_A

So one ciphertext passes both proofs and names two different registered
issuers. The receipt checker runs both checks and still returns VALID,
because it never asks whether the point the verifiable decryption names
is the plaintext the binding opened.

What this is. The minter picks any registered identity M_B whose naming
salt the spender will be able to use, twists the encryption key by
r^{-1}*(M_B - M_A), and ships that salt as salt_iss. The recipient does
the honest thing: decrypts with k, rebuilds the path, proves the fold.
The spend succeeds. The receipt names M_B. The on-chain mint binding is
a true statement about M_A and a key nobody is later required to use.

The salt is the only extra ingredient. The minter always has it for a
second identity of their own, so a sock-puppet issuer is enough. Naming
an unrelated victim needs that victim's naming salt as well. The key
twist itself needs only public points: M_A, M_B, and pk_real.

What this is not. It is not a break of issuer_reenc in isolation. That
sigma still extracts the minter's own credential plaintext and a key.
It is not an A1 break. A1's fold proves the opened plaintext is
m_rec*G, the same scalar as the account and the receiving leaf, so a
twisted key fails the circuit instead of naming someone else. It is not
a B1 break. B1's issuer is the public Schnorr identity recorded against
the commitment, and the depositor encrypts to that issuer's registered
account key, which is not a hidden choice.

The comment on Notes.mint understates the hole it already names. It says
a colluding pair can encrypt eIss under a throwaway key and leave the
issuer un-nameable while the note stays spendable, and that relation 5
was the closure. Relation 5 closes the un-nameable case. It does not
close this one. The note stays spendable and the receipt names a
registered issuer who is not the minter, which is a framing, not a
silence.

issuer_reenc.py lines 55-59 say the receipt closes the same gap by
requiring that the named point equal C - T. That check is not in
verify_receipt or in the unilateral receipt. It would also be the wrong
equation: T is r'*pk + gamma*G, so C - T = M - gamma*G, not M.


2. Mitigations
--------------

The chain has to reject the twisted ciphertext. A check the recipient
may skip does not meet the collusion rule (an accepted spend must name
the minter, not a minter-chosen substitute). The equality cannot be
carried by issuer_reenc's H. That generator has a public discrete log,
so a prover who wants Q to open to a different key subtracts the
difference from beta and the sigma-side equation still holds. A
cross-proof tie needs a commitment whose blind cannot be shifted.

The sound fix is a salted hash of the encryption key, committed in the
note and in the mint binding, and opened to k*G inside the fold.

    pkHash = Poseidon(pk.x, pk.y, salt_pk)

Mint. issuer_reenc gains pkHash as a public value and proves the key
hidden in Q is the preimage. The A2 mint circuit commits that same
pkHash in the leaf, the way it already commits eIss, and the contract
feeds the binding's pkHash into the mint verifier the way it already
feeds eIss. salt_pk is fresh per note and goes to the recipient with
salt_iss. It is not published. Two notes to the same mailbox hash
differently, so the chain learns a value it cannot link.

Spend. deposit_fold_a2 proves pk = k*G and Poseidon(pk, salt_pk) =
pkHash, with pkHash inside the idHash the note already opens. The
twisted key has a different hash. The spender cannot swap salt_pk,
because the hash is inside the committed note. One extra fixed-base
multiplication is about 472k constraints. The A2 fold is 3,341,261
against a pot22 ceiling of 4,194,304, so one more fits and two do not.
This number is arithmetic on the recorded sizes, not a recompile.

Receipt. The same opening is checked off chain: Q - beta*H equals the
pk_recv the verifiable decryption uses, and the named issuer point is
the plaintext of the credential the binding was verified against.
verify_receipt today verifies the binding against the minter account's
credential and the decryption against a separately supplied point, and
accepts when those points differ. beta and gamma may travel in the
receipt. A receipt already names both parties, and pk_recv is not a
decryption key. They must not be published at mint or at spend.

What the other candidates do.

Off-chain check only. Ship beta and gamma, and have the wallet refuse
a note whose Q does not open to its own pk_recv. This stops an honest
recipient from being fooled. A colluding recipient skips it, the fold
still accepts, and the receipt can still be built. Not sufficient.

Publish pk_recv at mint. Sound, and smaller: the binding's key becomes
a public input and the fold proves k*G equals it. It also links every
mint addressed to that key. That is acceptable for a key used with one
payer, and it is the wrong privacy trade for one lifelong mailbox.
Open question 2 (one key, or one per payer) decides whether this
candidate is even available.

Re-verify the whole issuer_reenc transcript inside the fold, as a
private witness, and bind its beta to k*G. Sound, and it does not
publish the key. It is several variable-base multiplications inside a
circuit that has room for one fixed-base multiplication. It does not
fit the pot22 parameters the fold was built to.

Leave the tie to the receipt. The chain would keep accepting a spend
that names the substitute. The collusion rule is a spend-time rule.


3. How to read the flows
------------------------

Each artifact below is marked with the five questions that decide
whether a proof is a tie:

    G   who can generate it
    I   who can intercept it (see it without being a party to it)
    R   who can recover a hidden value from it
    S   who can satisfy a proof about it (produce a witness the
        verifier accepts)
    D   who can duplicate or replay it (use a copy in place of the
        original, or satisfy the same statement for a different subject)

"The chain" means an observer of the transaction and its events. "The
channel" means whoever carries the off-chain note from issuer to
recipient. The channel is chosen by the issuer and is not the chain.

A fact only binds two parties when the person who can satisfy the
proof is the person the statement is about, and a copied artifact
fails in a new context. The A2 split is a G/S failure: the minter
generates a ciphertext the mint proof accepts for M_A, and the
recipient's honest k satisfies the spend proof for M_B.


4. A2, private issuer, addressed
--------------------------------

4.1 Issuance

Identity M_A = m_A*G, and the account credential E_reg = (rG, M_A + r*pk_iss).
    G  the minter, from the certified record and a fresh account key
    I  pk_iss and E_reg are on chain; m_A is not
    R  sk_iss opens E_reg to M_A. A counterparty who has been shown m_A
       on a receipt already has it. sk_iss stays in the wallet
    S  issuer_reenc is satisfied by (sk_iss, r', pk_mint, beta, gamma).
       The minter chooses pk_mint. Nothing checks it against Bob's key
    D  the credential is bound to the account address. A second account
       of the same identity is a different (sk, E_reg)

Recipient mailbox pk_real = k*G.
    G  the recipient, from the wallet seed. Not from m_rec
    I  the minter is told pk_real on the addressing channel. The chain
       is not
    R  pk_real does not yield k. Trial decryption under m_rec fails
    S  the fold is satisfied only by this k, together with the
       recipient's salt and account key
    D  publishing pk_real does not let anyone else read. Proving the
       binding in the clear (prove_receiving_binding) does: it returns
       k itself

Note randomness r', r_note, rho. Chosen by the minter.
    G  the minter
    I  whoever receives the off-chain payload. r' and r_note are not
       in the serialized notePayload the end-to-end fixture writes;
       they stay in the generator's memory. A real delivery has to
       carry them or the recipient cannot build the fold
    R  from eIss alone, r' is a discrete log
    S  the fold witness uses r' as the opening of eIss.R = r'*G
    D  fresh per note. Reuse across notes is a privacy break, not a
       soundness break

eIss = (r'*G, M + r'*pk), the ciphertext at issue.
    G  the minter. Honest: pk = pk_real and M = M_A. Attack: pk =
       pk_mint as in section 1, and the same bytes are also an
       encryption of M_B under pk_real
    I  the chain. A2 mint publishes eIss as a public input of the mint
       SNARK and of verifyIssuerReenc, tied to the committed leaf
    R  k opens it to M_B under the attack, and to M_A under the honest
       key. m_A opens it to neither, unless the minter chose the
       identity point as the key, which is the bug the receiving key
       removed
    S  mint proof: the minter, for M_A, under whatever pk they put in Q.
       Spend proof: the recipient, for whatever point k yields, provided
       that point has a leaf whose salt they were given
    D  the ciphertext can be copied. The mint SNARK binds these exact
       bytes into the leaf. A different eIss is a different note

issuer_reenc transcript, including Q, U, T.
    G  the minter
    I  the chain, in full. Q hides pk. T hides M, because of gamma.
       Hiding is perfect for uniform beta and gamma only while nobody
       is given those blinds
    R  beta opens Q to pk_mint. gamma opens T to r'*pk_mint, and then
       C - r'*pk_mint is M_A. The recipient is not given beta or gamma
       today, so they cannot test pk_mint against pk_real
    S  the on-chain verifier, against msg.sender's credential. The
       statement is "encrypts that credential's plaintext under the key
       in Q". It is silent on whether that key is pk_real
    D  the transcript is bound to (issuer address, chain id, E_reg,
       eIss). It will not verify against M_B's credential. It does not
       need to: the spend never consults it

salt_iss, the naming-association salt, and salt_pk if the mitigation
in section 2 is adopted.
    G  the holder of the identity whose leaf it opens. For an honest
       note, the minter. For the attack, whoever owns M_B's leaf; the
       minter, when M_B is their sock
    I  the recipient, because the payload must carry it. Not the chain
    R  the salt plus the published tree locates the leaf. It does not
       reveal k, and it must not be the salt of the issuer's receiving
       leaf
    S  relation 5 is satisfied by anyone who has this salt and a
       ciphertext their own k opens to that leaf's point. That is the
       whole of the attacker's extra power
    D  a salt from a different association does not open this leaf.
       The fold refuses M_A's salt on a decryption that landed on M_B

idHash = Poseidon(eNote, eIss), cm = Poseidon(A2, v, rho, idHash, pred).
    G  the minter
    I  cm is on chain. idHash, eNote, v, rho are not
    R  preimage resistance. The chain cannot open cm
    S  the mint SNARK, by the minter. The fold, by the recipient, who
       recomputes idHash from the payload
    D  the leaf-tie forces the binding's eIss to be the eIss inside
       this idHash. It does not force the key inside that eIss

4.2 Transmission

The channel carries, or the recipient cannot spend and cannot check:
the opening (flavor, v, rho, idHash, predicate), eNote, eIss, r_note,
r', salt_iss, and the issuer_reenc transcript if a receipt is wanted.
    G  the minter
    I  the channel, and anyone the channel leaks to
    R  k, which the channel does not get, opens eNote to v*G and eIss
       to whichever point the key encrypts. The channel cannot decrypt.
       The channel also cannot tell that the key is wrong
    S  possession of the payload does not satisfy the fold. The fold
       also needs k, the recipient's salt, and the account key
    D  a copied payload spends once, by the first party who holds k
       and the account. A copied payload without k spends never

The recipient can detect the attack only if they are given an opening
of Q (beta) or a committed pkHash. They are given neither today. Honest
software decrypts, sees a registered identity, and proceeds.

4.3 Spend

Private witness of deposit_fold_a2:
k, m_rec, sk_dep, salt_rec, r', salt_iss, the two Merkle paths, eNote,
eIss.
    G  k, m_rec, sk_dep, salt_rec: the recipient, from the seed and the
       account. r', salt_iss, eNote, eIss: the minter, via the channel.
       Paths: anyone, from the published tree, once they know the leaf
    I  the witness is not published. The proof is
    R  the proof is Groth16. It does not yield the witness
    S  the recipient. Also anyone the recipient has given k, which is
       what the cleartext binding proof does. A thief who stole k but
       not the account, or the account but not k, fails relation 2 or
       relation 3. A thief who stole the payload but not k fails
       relation 1
    D  public inputs are the nullifier, the identity root, eEnc, and
       the depositor's key and credential as read from the registry.
       A second depositor cannot replay it. The same depositor cannot
       replay it after the nullifier is marked. eEnc is a fresh
       re-encryption, so the proof does not reveal the mint's eIss

What the spend statement actually says, and what it does not:
    k opens eIss to M*, and M* is on a salted leaf.
    It does not say M* is the plaintext of the minter's credential.
    It does not say k*G is the key in Q.
    The minter can satisfy the first by choosing the ciphertext, and
    the recipient satisfies the second honestly. That is section 1.

SpentCoupledA2 logs dc.P_I. The fold does not read dc. The logged point
is whatever the caller supplied. It is not a commitment to M*.

4.4 Receipt

make_receipt decrypts eIss under k and names the result. verify_receipt
then checks issuer_reenc against the minter account's credential, and
checks the verifiable decryption against the decrypted point, and
checks both points for unsalted membership.
    G  the recipient, from k and the payload
    I  a receipt is shown to a named auditor. It names both parties by
       design
    R  the auditor sees M* as k opened it, which is M_B under the attack,
       and sees that the minter's account produced some binding. The
       auditor is not shown that the binding's plaintext is M_A
    S  the recipient. The minter cannot produce the verifiable
       decryption without k
    D  the checks do not include "M* equals the credential plaintext",
       so a receipt that names M_B verifies against M_A's account.
       That is the same split, one layer up

The fix in the receipt is the opening of Q to the same pk the
decryption uses, and a comparison of the two plaintexts. It is needed
so a receipt cannot launder the split. It is not a substitute for the
check in the fold: a colluding recipient will emit whatever receipt
they want, and the chain is what must refuse the spend.


5. A1, public issuer, addressed
-------------------------------

5.1 Issuance

The issuer is msg.sender, a registered public identity, and a Schnorr
over keccak(cms). The chain records publicIssuerOfCommitment[cm] =
msg.sender. There is no hidden issuer plaintext.
    G  the minter
    I  the chain: the address, the Schnorr, the commitment. M_iss sits
       inside idHash, which sits inside cm, and also in the public
       identity record of msg.sender
    R  the address is the issuer. No decryption
    S  verifyIssuerSchnorr, by the holder of the issuer account key
    D  the signature is over this batch's cms. It will not verify for
       another batch. A later issuer cannot overwrite the stored address

eNote = (r_n*G, v*G + r_n*pk_real), eRec = (r'*G, M_rec + r'*pk_real).
idHash = Poseidon(eNote, m_iss, sigma). eRec is not inside idHash.
    G  the minter, who is given M_rec and pk_real
    I  neither ciphertext is a mint public input. Both travel off chain
    R  k opens eRec to M_rec and eNote to v*G. m_rec opens neither
    S  the A1 fold requires the opened plaintext to be m_rec*G, the same
       scalar as the account credential and the receiving leaf, and
       requires eNote = (v + r_n*k)*G with v public. A twisted key makes
       k open eRec to a point that is not m_rec*G, and the witness
       builder refuses. There is no second issuer for the twist to name
    D  eRec can be replaced by a fresh encryption of the same M_rec under
       the same k (the spend's eEnc). It cannot be replaced by an
       encryption under a different key

5.2 Transmission

Opening, eNote, eRec, r_note, r'. No issuer salt: A1 has no relation 5.
    G  the minter
    I  the channel
    R  the channel cannot decrypt without k
    S  the channel cannot spend
    D  same as A2. One spend, by the holder of k and the account

5.3 Spend

deposit_fold_a1. Same four relations as A2, no issuer leaf. v is public
and pins eNote.
    G  the recipient
    I  the chain sees the nullifier, the face, the recipient address,
       eEnc, and the proof. It does not see M_rec or pk_real
    R  the proof does not yield k
    S  the recipient. The issuer, holding M_rec and pk_real but not k
       or the account, cannot
    D  bound to the nullifier and to the depositor's registered account,
       as in A2

The A1 receipt decrypts eRec under k to M_rec and names the public
issuer from the Schnorr identity. There is no hidden second plaintext,
so the section 1 split has nowhere to land. The receipt still does not
carry a proof that pk_real is the registered mailbox of M_rec. That is
the earlier gap (the binding evidence discloses k, and no hiding proof
exists), and it is independent of this flaw.


6. B1, public issuer, bearer
----------------------------

6.1 Issuance

Same public-issuer mint as A1. idHash = Poseidon(m_iss, sigma). No
recipient, no eIss, no receiving key. The bearer secret is rho, chosen
by the minter and committed inside cm.
    G  the minter, including rho
    I  the chain sees the issuer address and cm, not rho
    R  rho is not recoverable from cm
    S  whoever is later handed rho, and the minter, who kept it
    D  the issuer address is stored against this exact cm. spendCoupledB1
       requires publicIssuerOfCommitment[cm] == the issuer argument. A
       second issuer cannot be substituted at spend

6.2 Transmission

The opening, including rho, passes hand to hand. Whoever holds it can
race the spend. That is the bearer predicate, not a flaw in the binding.
The issuer already holds rho. A photograph of an unsealed note is a copy.
    G  the minter
    I  anyone who sees the note
    R  nothing hidden beyond rho, and rho is the note
    S  the first spender who also has a registered identity
    D  two copies spend once. The nullifier is a function of rho and
       idHash

6.3 Spend

The depositor encrypts M_dep under the issuer's registered account key
pk_iss, which is on chain and is not chosen by the depositor for the
purpose of naming someone else.
    G  the depositor, who knows M_dep, r, and pk_iss. They do not know
       sk_iss, so r*pk_iss stays variable-base. Giving them a form that
       factored through G would give them sk_iss
    I  the chain sees eDepForIss and the issuer address
    R  sk_iss, held by the issuer, opens it to M_dep. A certified record
       that discloses m_iss does not. m_dep does not
    S  verifyDepositorBinding is one sigma on one secret m_dep: the
       account credential and the ciphertext and P_dep = M_dep + b*H.
       The shared response is a real tie. identity_membership_b1 then
       proves T = b*H_PEDERSEN, the addition precondition, and the salted
       leaf. H_PEDERSEN has no known log, so the blind cannot be shifted
       onto a different registered identity
    D  the membership proof is bound to the sigma's P_dep because the
       contract passes that point in. The spend is bound to the mint by
       the stored issuer address. A proof built for a different issuer
       key fails the sigma

The B1 receipt is the issuer's. They decrypt with sk_iss and produce a
verifiable decryption under pk_iss. A substituted ciphertext fails the
sigma, so the depositor cannot make the issuer recover a different
identity from the one the proof certified.

B1 has no hidden second key on the issuer side. The issuer key is the
account key the registry stores for the address the mint recorded. That
is why the section 1 construction does not transfer.


7. What the flows change about the open questions
--------------------------------------------------

The five questions in the previous note stand. Two of them get sharper.

One receiving key, or one per payer. Publishing pk_recv at mint is a
sound and cheap closure of section 1, and it links every mint addressed
to that key. A per-payer key makes that link the pair who already share
a channel. A lifelong key makes it a public index of one recipient's
incoming A2 notes. The salted-hash closure keeps a lifelong key
unlinkable, at the cost of one fixed-base multiplication and a wider
note commitment. The choice is this trade, not a free preference.

The receipt's job. A receipt that names M* without proving M* is the
plaintext of the binding is not evidence of who paid. The opening of Q
belongs in the receipt. It does not belong on chain. And the spend
proof has to enforce the same equality, or a colluding recipient omits
the opening and the chain still pays.

The security argument is now blocked on this, not only on the missing
rewrite of Theorems 7 and 8. A theorem that says an accepted A2 spend
names the minter is false of the code as it stands. The statement to
prove, after the salted-hash tie, is: the key the fold opens to k*G is
the key issuer_reenc committed, so the point k decrypts to is the
plaintext of the minter's credential.
