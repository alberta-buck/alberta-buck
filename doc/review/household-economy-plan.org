* What this is for

The simulations exist to show that the BUCK holds equilibrium -- K inside its range, the basket's
price in BUCKs near one -- while credit issuance and the demand for BUCKs grow at different rates,
and then while natural and deliberate disturbances hit it. Earlier worlds answered this with casts
that grew by accretion: big synthetic borrowers, finite cohorts, several overlapping savers and
depositors, and defenders added one at a time. Their failures were often failures of the cast.

This plan replaces them with one economy built from three kinds of household, a few agents that
only keep markets connected, and the basket machine itself. Disturbances come afterwards, as arms
on that economy. Preserving earlier runs is not a goal; reproducing earlier results is not required.

This document is the plan of record for branch =feature/household-economy=. Its ledger, at the end,
records what has been done. A session resuming the work reads "Decisions", "Order of work" and the
ledger, then carries on.

* Decisions

Taken 2026-10-17, by the session that wrote this plan, on Perry's instruction to proceed with its
recommendations.

1. *The baseline cast is households plus plumbing plus the machine.* No undertakings desk, no
   excursion arbitrageurs, no synthetic borrowers, no old savers or depositors. Equilibrium must
   come from the households, K and the basket machine (its wheel and its desk). Defenders return
   later as optional arms.
2. *Arrivals are drawn by the price gap, on a straight line.* Supply roles arrive faster while the
   BUCK is dear, the investor role faster while it is cheap: rate x (1 + 200 x gap), at most 8x,
   the gap being a 30-day average. See "Arrivals". (First 50 and 4x; raised the same day after
   400-day centres needed a 1--3% standing gap to draw enough investors.)
3. *The basket machine (=equity-machine=) is the only basket in routine runs.* The older upkeep and
   the pro-rata basket are not part of the grid.
4. *Investors hold BUCKs.* That is the demand for BUCKs the grid varies: an investor buys BUCKs
   with the dollars it saves and keeps them as money; while the BUCK is dear by more than its
   premium it converts them into basket shares instead. (Revised the same day: the first version
   deposited every BUCK in the basket, which spends deposited BUCKs on commodities, so it added
   no demand for BUCKs at all; see the ledger.) Debtors and savers invest their own surplus
   through a TOKEN, as they do now.
5. *Worlds start young and grow.* No opening placed outside the market; the opening population is
   small and refinances through the market like everyone after it.
6. *The household's refinancing path is the only one.* The older debtor classes are retired, not
   reconciled with it.
7. *The centre is balanced and sized.* (Added the same day, from the 400-day centres in the
   ledger.) At base rates the investors bring about as many dollars a year as the debtors bring
   BUCKs to sell; the BUCK/USDC pool is $10M, sized to an economy of a few million dollars a year
   rather than $50M; and credit savers keep 30% of their K-scaled limit drawn and invested
   (=carry_utilization= 0.3), which is the flow K moves: a higher K, more carried supply; a lower
   K, repayment from the basket.
8. *Issuers come faster as K rises; households repay only by selling.* (Perry, 2026-10-18.) A K near
   1 means unlimited BUCKs from any insurable asset, so no rational actor stays away: debtors and
   savers arrive (1 - 0.75) / (1 - K) times as fast as at K 0.75 (5x at 0.95), on top of the
   dear-side gap term; this stands in for recursive collateral. Escaping interest-bearing USD
   mortgages is so desirable that the BUCK system, not the households, must rate-limit it. A debtor
   or saver sells its insured asset at 10% a year: the sale pays any mortgage, its basket shares and
   then dollars buy back the whole lien, its coverage is released and it leaves. It repays BUCKs at no
   other time; spare income goes into the basket (the =basket= surplus policy). The savers' 30%
   carrying share stays; its repayment when K falls is removed.
9. *Saving in BUCK deposits, paydown at a discount, money held to pay with.* (Perry, 2026-10-18.)
   Idle money goes into the basket as BUCK deposits (no TOKEN step): spare dollars buy BUCKs that are
   deposited, and BUCKs borrowed at a premium go in directly. While the BUCK trades at a discount d,
   a household with a lien pays down 0.01 x (2^(d / 0.005) - 1) of it each decision (an urgency that
   compounds with the discount), its basket shares first, then spare dollars. Every household that
   pays in BUCKs keeps a month of its spending in a BUCK wallet apart from its lien, topped up each
   payday and more while the BUCK is cheap (1 + 200 x discount, at most 8x); the share of spending
   paid in BUCKs ramps from 10% to all of it over a household's first year. The grid's demand axis
   now scales that ramp (3 years, 1 year, 4 months) instead of the investors' arrival rate. This is
   the economy's demand for liquidity; K should settle where issuance meets it.

* The agents

** Roles

One class family, three roles. Every member has its own keyed draws (=household_policy.uniform=),
its own accounts and its own actual and counterfactual books.

| Role | Class (Python and JS) | Brings | Does |
|------+-----------------------+--------+------|
| debtor | =CreditHouseholdAgent= | an insured home and an external mortgage | refinances on carrying cost; afterwards retires at a discount or invests |
| saver | =CreditSaverAgent= | an insured asset, no mortgage | issues when the BUCK is dear and invests; retires when cheap |
| investor | =HouseholdInvestorAgent= (new) | dollars and a yearly saving | buys BUCKs and deposits them in the basket; withdraws at the end of a horizon |

The investor, in detail:

- Settings (new keys in =DEFAULTS=, both languages): =invest_cash_k= [10, 100] (opening savings,
  $k), =invest_income_k= [2, 20] (yearly saving, $k), =hold_years= [3, 15] (horizon). Validation:
  non-negative, =hold_years= positive.
- No insured asset: no =setCreditIssuer=, no credits, face 0, no premium, no mortgage, no cash
  buffer. Its income is already its saving: =living_frac= does not apply.
- Each decision (=decision_days= [5, 20], phased; =investor_action=): unless the BUCK is dear by
  more than its =issue_premium= after the fee, it buys BUCKs with all its cash, at most
  =max_impact_bp= of the BUCK/USDC pool's dollars per decision, and holds them. While the BUCK is
  that dear it buys none and converts the BUCKs it holds into basket shares with
  =depositToken(BUCK, amount, 0)=. When the basket's price guard refuses (=Slippage()=, selector
  =0x7dd37f70=), it offers half, up to four times; what is still refused stays in its account for
  the next decision. A guard refusal is counted as a refusal, not an error.
- At =arrive_day + 365 x hold_years= it exits: redeems every receipt, sells what comes back (BUCKs
  through BUCK/USDC, any TOKEN through its USDC pool), impact-capped per decision, until nothing is
  left; then it stops.
- Books: actual = dollars + BUCKs held x spot + basket shares x share price x spot + anything
  pending. The counterfactual keeps every dollar in dollars.

** The population coordinator

=HouseholdPopulationAgent= stops being expanded and discarded before the run. It stays in the cast,
owns its members, and at the start of each day decides who arrives.

Settings (=[agents.HouseholdPopulationAgent]=):

| Key | Default | Meaning |
|-----+---------+---------|
| =initial_debtors=, =initial_savers=, =initial_investors= | 5, 5, 0 | members present on day 0 |
| =debtor_rate=, =saver_rate=, =investor_rate= | 3, 3, 0 | arrivals per year at the base pace |
| =growth= | ="steady"= | base pace over time: =steady= 1; =rising= 0.25 to 1.75; =falling= 1.75 to 0.25 (straight lines; each averages 1) |
| =gain= | 50 | arrival multiplier per unit of price gap |
| =gain_cap= | 4 | the most the multiplier reaches |
| =signal_halflife_days= | 30 | the gap's averaging |
| =settle_days= | 7 | days before the gap counts (the opening transient) |
| =max_members= | 2000 | a safety bound; exceeding it raises |
| =population= | 0 | the population's name, which keys its arrival draws |

The older keys stay valid so the savings sandbox and its public choices keep working: =initial=,
=arrival_rate= and =saver_share=, when no role key is given, become =initial_savers = round(initial
x saver_share)=, =initial_debtors= the rest, and the two rates in the same proportion. The role of
each member is then fixed by the split, not by a draw.

** Arrivals

Each day, before any agent acts:

1. Read the basket's price in BUCKs, =basketValueInBuck= (bvib). From =settle_days= on, update an
   exponential average with half-life =signal_halflife_days= (it starts at 1). The gap is
   =1 - average=: positive when the BUCK is dear, negative when cheap.
2. For each role, multiplier m = min(=gain_cap=, 1 + =gain= x max(0, s x gap)): debtors and savers
   use the dear side (s = +1), investors the cheap side (s = -1). Before =settle_days=, m = 1.
3. Add rate x growth(day) x m / 365.25 to the role's cumulative intensity.
4. The role's k-th arrival happens on the first day its cumulative intensity reaches the sum of its
   first k unit exponentials, E_k = -ln(1 - uniform(seed, population, "<role>/arrival/<k>")).
   Several may arrive in one day.

With =gain= = 0 the same draws give an ordinary Poisson stream at the base pace, so a responsive arm
and its open-loop twin differ only by the response. The rule lives in =household_policy.py= as a
pure step function (state, day, bvib, settings) -> (state, arrivals), mirrored in
=household-policy.js= and checked by a shared vector: a scripted bvib series and its arrival days.

Members are named =<population>/<role>/<k>=; initial members take the first k of each role and
arrive on the coordinator's first morning. A member arriving on day d has =arrive_day= = d; its
income, interest and decisions start then.

|------+--------|

=python -m alberta_buck.sim.economy= runs it:

- =plan --campaign NAME [--cells centre|grid|all]= lists the cells.
- =run --campaign NAME [--cells ...] [--jobs 8]= copies the committed tree into
  =build/sim/economy/NAME/snapshot= (=git archive HEAD=), records =source-manifest.json=, and runs
  each cell from the snapshot (=PYTHONPATH= and =ALBERTA_BUCK_REPO= set to it) into
  =runs/<cell>.json= with a log, writing =.partial= until complete. The loop writes a completed
  run's PDF beside it.
- =report --campaign NAME= reads completed vectors only and writes =index.html= with the acceptance
  table.

Cells are named =s<supply>-d<demand>-<prices>-<seed>[-<variant>]=, with =lo=, =mid= and =hi= for
the factors 1/3, 1 and 3 and the variants =open= (=gain= 0), =rising= and =falling=; for example
=slo-dhi-history-41404=. Before the runs fan out, each seed runs two days alone to warm the shared
identity cache.

** Acceptance

Measured from day 365 to the end:

- mean |bvib - 1| below 1%, and its 99th percentile below 5%;
- K inside [kmin + 0.05, kmax - 0.05] on at least 95% of days, and never at a rail (within 0.005
  of kmin or kmax) for more than 30 days in a row;
- no household errors (guard refusals are counted apart), no exhausted arrivals.

The index also shows K at the end, supply, BUCKs drawn by role, investors' deposits, arrivals by
role and the average multipliers. A cell that fails is a finding, not a bug, until its cause is
understood.
