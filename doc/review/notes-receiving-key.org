* 1 Purpose and status
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: purpose
:END:

This is the architecture for review finding R5: addressed Notes are encrypted
to the recipient's identity point, so the identity scalar is the decryption
key, and every party that legitimately holds it can read that recipient's
note traffic. The finding was deferred behind the accumulator; this document
records the approach, the prototype that establishes it, and what it costs.

Status: implemented across four backends, with all three spend gates built,
set up under dev entropy, and verifying on chain; =Notes.sol= verifies the
folded gate and nothing else, and the superseded verifiers are deleted rather
than deprecated.  Receipts name by evidence, never by a derivation anyone can
repeat, and A2's naming is non-frameable: the mint binding's =T= is committed
in =idHash= and the fold ties it to the spender's own key ([[#split][section
4.6]], closed 2026-09-22).  The reading plan ([[#reading-plan]]) has what
remains before the merge.

Implemented on 2026-09-20, with =alberta_buck/test/test_receiving_key.py=
carrying twenty-six conformance tests:

- =wallet/recvkey.py=: the receiving key, derived from wallet seed material
  and not from the identity, with rotation indexed by the same counter the
  accumulator's salt uses; and the holder-produced binding evidence of
  [[#architecture][section 3.5]].
- =registry/tree.py=: =receiving_leaf=, the third leaf function, committing
  =(M, pk_recv, salt)=; and =insert_receiving=, the admission.
- =wallet/deposit_fold.py=: the folded gate of [[#fold][section 3.3a]] -- one
  witness, four relations, the payload theft refused on relation (3) -- as
  the reference the circuit is checked against.

Still to come, and deliberately together: the A1 and A2 payload
substitution, =make_receipt=, the circuits, and the one regeneration they
share ([[#sequencing]]).

The prototypes remain as the standalone argument:
=scripts/review/receiving_key_proto.py= (eleven checks),
=deposit_gate_split.py= (nine) and =deposit_gate_folded.py= (three).

Dependency: the salted accumulator leaf of
[[file:accumulator-spec.org][the accumulator specification]], because the
binding between a receiving key and an identity must be hidden, and that leaf
is where it hides.

* 2 Why the current design cannot be patched
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: contradiction
:END:

The system's read/write separation makes the identity scalar a *read
capability*, disclosed to every counterparty by design: Bob learns Alice's
scalar when she pays him. The note layer then used that same scalar as a
*decryption key*. Those two roles are incompatible. A value handed to
everyone you transact with cannot also be what decrypts your future traffic,
because disclosure is then retroactive and prospective at once.

No treatment of the ciphertext repairs this. If the identity scalar is the
private key, whoever holds it decrypts, and the design discloses it on
purpose. The key has to move off the identity.

** 2.1 The precise defect, which is sharper than the general one

An A1 note encrypts the recipient's identity point *under the recipient's
identity point*:

#+begin_example
eRec = (r'G, M_rec + r'*M_rec) = (r'G, m_rec*(G + R))
#+end_example

That is not a Diffie-Hellman instance at all. The message and the key share
one secret, so the usual hardness collapses: a candidate scalar is tested
with a single multiplication, =C == m_i*(G + R)=, needing no proof, no key
and no issuer secret. The prototype's first check does exactly that and
identifies the recipient out of nine candidates.

Encrypt the same plaintext under a key independent of the identity and the
residual test is plaintext-checking under an independent key, which is a
decisional Diffie-Hellman decision and infeasible. That is the same lock the
registration layer already relies on.

* 3 The architecture
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: architecture
:END:

** 3.1 Notes are addressed to a receiving key

The recipient holds an independent receiving secret =k= with =pk_recv = k*G=.
A payer addresses a note by encrypting under =pk_recv= instead of under
=M_rec=. The plaintexts do not change:

#+begin_example
A1   eNote = (r_n*G, v*G      + r_n*pk_recv)     was r_n*M_rec
     eRec  = (r'*G,  M_rec    + r'*pk_recv)      was r'*M_rec
A2   eNote = (r_n*G, v*G      + r_n*pk_recv)
     eIss  = (r'*G,  M_iss    + r'*pk_recv)
#+end_example

Unilateral addressing survives: the payer needs =pk_recv=, learned out of
band through the same channel that carries =M_rec= today, and needs nothing
from the recipient at payment time.

** 3.2 The binding is inside the accumulator leaf, not published

A note addressed to a key of the payer's choosing would break mutual
decryptability and the receipt, so the receiving key must be bound to the
identity. That binding =MUST NOT= be public: a spend proving against a public
binding would reveal the recipient's registered key and deanonymise them to
everyone, which is worse than the problem being solved.

The leaf therefore commits both -- and it turns out to need committing
*twice*, because the fact has two consumers that hold different things:

#+begin_example
receiving_leaf(m_rec, k, salt)       = Poseidon(TAG_R, m_rec, k, salt)
mailbox_leaf(M_rec, pk_recv, salt')  = Poseidon(TAG_M, M.x, M.y, pk.x, pk.y, salt')
#+end_example

The SPEND proves the association in zero knowledge and the prover holds the
scalars, so its leaf commits them: committing the points there would oblige
the circuit to re-derive both, at 471,896 constraints apiece
([[#fold-built][why scalars]]). A PAYER must check the association BEFORE
paying and holds no secret at all -- only the two points, which it needs
anyway -- so for that consumer a scalar leaf is unopenable, and its leaf
commits the points. Distinct associations carry distinct salts, so the two do
not link.

Neither leaf is public: both are salted, and the prototype confirms the
scalar one stays unscannable against a party holding every certified
identity, the whole published subtree, and =pk_recv= itself.

** 3.3 The note-binding relation keeps its shape

The note-binding circuits avoid variable-base scalar multiplication by
witnessing the product of the randomness and the recipient's scalar and
computing it as a fixed-base multiple of =G=. Under the substitution the
witness becomes =r*k= instead of =r*m_rec= and the shape is identical:

#+begin_example
R = r*G           C = M + (r*k)*G
#+end_example

For that relation this is a substitution of which scalar the recipient proves
knowledge of, not a redesign, and the prototype checks it for both the
identity and the value ciphertext. Constraint counts should be close to
unchanged, which =MUST= be measured rather than assumed.

** 3.3a The deposit gate does NOT keep its shape, and must be folded
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: fold
:END:

=deposit_couple_prove= uses one witness =m_rec= for two jobs: it decrypts the
note's ciphertext, and it is the identity inside the deposit account's
registered credential. The Fiat-Shamir nonce shared across those two relations
is not an implementation detail. It /is/ the statement "the account cashing
this note belongs to the person the note was addressed to".

Separating the two jobs deletes that statement. =deposit_gate_split.py=
demonstrates the consequence: a thief holding a stolen payload proves the
reading half with the stolen =k= and the identity half with its /own/
registered identity, both halves are true, and nothing says they belong
together. The note becomes spendable by the wrong person. The sigma also
accepts any receiving key at all, because "P commits whatever this scalar
decrypts to" is satisfied by every scalar.

This is finding 5 in a new place: an equality inferred from two proofs that
share a public point. The remedy is the same. One witness, one circuit, and
the tie stated rather than assumed:

#+begin_example
(1) k decrypts the note ciphertext to the point committed in P
(2) the account credential decrypts under sk to the identity M
(3) a registered leaf commits the pair (M, k*G) under the holder's salt
(4) that leaf's path folds to a posted root
#+end_example

Relation (3) is the step that used to be free, because the two scalars were
the same value. =deposit_gate_folded.py= builds this witness, refuses the
thief on relation (3), and shows a rotated receiving key spending under an
unlinkable leaf.

*Consequence for sequencing.* The receiving-key change and the finding-5 fold
are the same piece of circuit work, not two adjacent ones. Both replace a
public-point equality with a shared private witness, both need the
accumulator leaf, and both land in one trusted setup together with the
aggregator depth raise.

** 3.4 Rotation and succession

A new receiving key is a new leaf with a fresh salt and the old leaf cleared:
the re-association mechanism the accumulator already specifies. Two leaves for
one identity with different keys do not link.

** 3.5 The payer's assurance is a holder-produced proof

For a receipt to name a person, the payer needs the identity, which it learns
bilaterally as today, and assurance that =pk_recv= belongs to it. That
assurance is holder-produced and the receipt captures it at payment time --
but it is a *hash and a path*, not a proof, and the difference matters.

Against the scalar leaf the payer had three ways forward and all three were
worse: take =k= from the holder, which hands over the mailbox in both
directions in time; take a Groth16 proof of the leaf's preimage, which costs
a fixed-base multiplication, a ceremony and a pairing verifier inside every
receipt checker; or go without, and address notes to unverified keys. The
mailbox leaf of [[#architecture][3.2]] costs one Poseidon and one path, and
=verify_receiving_binding= needs no secret at all.

Which side needs it, and which does not, is worth stating because it is not
symmetric. A RECIPIENT-generated receipt does not: its verifiable decryption
under =pk_recv= plus its own self-naming already tie the receipt to the party
controlling that Identity's account. An ISSUER-generated one does: the issuer
cannot open the note's ciphertexts at all -- that capability was the defect --
so without the binding it names the MAILBOX it paid and the receipt is stamped
=UNVERIFIED RECIPIENT=.

** 3.6 B1 needs no change in kind

B1 encrypts its depositor evidence under the issuer's *registered account
key* (=elgamal_encrypt(M_dep, pk_iss, r)=), not under an identity scalar, so
knowing a certified record does not decrypt it. B1 was right all along and
A1 and A2 are the deviation. A design whose odd case turns out to be the
correct one usually already contains its own answer.

** 4.1 Why the payload substitution cannot land alone
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sequencing
:END:

Surveyed against the source on 2026-09-20, after the architecture's own
surface was implemented. Two facts constrain the order, and neither was
visible from the design.

*The mint is ported four times, and the vectors are asserted current.* The
A1 payload is built in Rust as well as Python --
=core/rust/buck-wallet/src/flows.rs= computes
=elgamal_encrypt(m_rec_pt, m_rec_pt, r_prime)= for =eRec=, with both
bindings and =core/js/src/wallet-core.js= on top of it. And
=test_wallet_vectors_current= asserts that freshly built vectors equal
=core/vectors/wallet-kernel-vectors.json= byte for byte. So changing the
Python mint alone does not leave a passing suite with a documented gap: it
leaves either a stale committed vector or a Rust kernel computing a
different note. The substitution is a four-backend change with one
regeneration, which is the shape every identity change in this repository
has.

That regeneration is the expensive one, and the plan already records paying
for it twice as the mistake to avoid: the Notes end-to-end fixtures embed an
identity root, so regenerating them runs the note-binding prover over a
2.4M-constraint circuit. The aggregator depth raise, the finding-5 repairs,
the folded gate and this substitution all force it. They go together or they
go three times. How much of that is a /trusted setup/ rather than a re-prove
turned out to be less than assumed; see [[#circuit-cost]].

*A1 gains an argument; A2 gains none.* The earlier reading of "four call
sites, the encryption key argument and nothing else" holds for A2 and not
for A1, and the difference is worth stating because it sets which signatures
move:

| Function | Change | Why |
|----------+--------+-----|
| =mint_unilateral_a2= | rename only | =M_rec= appears three times and every one is the key role: =eNote=, =eIss=, and the =issuer_reenc= anti-framing slot. Nothing else reads it, so the parameter becomes =pk_recv= and no signature moves. |
| =mint_unilateral_a1= | one new argument | =eRec= encrypts =M_rec= /under/ =M_rec=: the identity is the plaintext as well as the key. Only the key becomes =pk_recv=, so the mint needs both points. |
| =make_receipt=, =make_receipt_a1= | signature changes | each derives the recipient's identity from the secret it decrypts with, and those are now two values. |
| =verify_receipt=, =verify_receipt_a1= | additive | the verifiable decryption is checked under =pk_recv=, so the receipt carries it, plus the binding of [[#architecture][3.5]]. |

Carrying =pk_recv= in a plaintext receipt costs nothing: the receipt already
names both parties, and the key alone decides no addressing, which is the
=receiving_key_proto= check that hands the adversary =pk_recv= and watches it
find nothing.

** 4.2 What the circuits actually cost, measured
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: circuit-cost
:END:

Measured on 2026-09-20 against the deployed build, because the plan had
assumed the note-binding substitution forces a rebuild and it does not.

*A2's note-binding circuit does not change at all.* It never multiplies by
the recipient's scalar at a variable base -- it witnesses the products
=sm = s*k=, =rm = r*k= and checks them fixed-base -- so substituting the
receiving secret for the identity scalar renames a private input and leaves
the arithmetic alone. Compiled both ways, the =r1cs= is byte-identical, and
identical to the one the deployed proving key was generated from:

#+begin_example
750a1f46...eca9b   circuits/note_binding.circom, before the rename
750a1f46...eca9b   circuits/note_binding.circom, after
750a1f46...eca9b   build/snark/note_binding/note_binding.r1cs (deployed)
#+end_example

So the 3.3 GB proving key, the verification key and
=NoteBindingVerifier.sol= all survive untouched. A receiving-key witness
proves against them in 5.2 seconds and verifies: =WITNESS IS CORRECT= over
2,416,748 constraints, then =OK!=. No trusted setup, no verifier
redeployment, no ceremony.

One wrinkle worth recording, because it looks like a contradiction and is
not: the =circom= C++ witness calculator embeds input signal /names/, so it
refuses a renamed input with =Signal not found= even though the =r1cs= is
identical. Recompiling the calculator is minutes; it is not a setup.

*A1's does change, and needs a fresh setup.* A1 carries both facts in one
ciphertext, so its circuit needs both scalars: the identity as a /base
multiple/ (=M_rec = m_rec*G= in the =eEnc= and =P_I= relations) and the
receiving secret as a /product factor/ (=u = v + rn*k=, =tm = t*k=). That
adds a private input, so the =r1cs= differs and the proving key must be
regenerated:

| Circuit | =r1cs= | Setup |
|---------+--------+-------|
| =note_binding.circom= (A2) | byte-identical | none |
| =note_binding_a1.circom= | =371041e7= vs deployed =02f70e06=, 2,859,018 constraints | required |

The same asymmetry as the mint, from the same cause: A1 names and keys in
one object, A2 only keys. Where that asymmetry appears, A1 pays.

In the end neither setup was needed and both circuits are retired: the fold
absorbed the note tie as one of its relations, so =note_binding.circom= and
=note_binding_a1.circom= left the tree with the rest of the superseded surface
([[#reading-plan][landing 1]]). The measurement above is kept because it is
what established that the substitution was cheap and the /gate/ was the
expensive part -- which is why the fold was built rather than the substitution
shipped alone.

*What this cost in the end.* Nothing, for A2's note binding -- and the A1
sibling was never set up, because the fold absorbed it. The end-to-end world
regenerates through the gates themselves, and the generator now builds its
witness from the serialized payload, which turned a fixture that proved a
spend nobody could perform into one that proves the delivery sufficient.

** 4.3 The folded gate, built and measured
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: fold-built
:END:

=circuits/deposit_fold_a1.circom=, with
=alberta_buck/wallet/deposit_fold.py= as its clear-text reference and
=scripts/snark/gen_deposit_fold_a1_input.py= as its witness builder.

*The three finding-5 defects are absent by construction rather than patched.*
That is the fold's dividend, and it is worth stating precisely because the
plan had them down as three separate repairs:

| Defect | How folding removes it |
|--------+------------------------|
| The witnessed tie point =T= | =P_I= existed only so a sigma and a membership SNARK could share a hidden value. One proof needs no shared point, so there is no =P_I=, no blind, and no =ScalarMulH= |
| Unchecked 64-bit limbs | Every witnessed limb array is consumed by =ScalarMulG=, which range-checks each limb with =Num2Bits(64)=. The intermediate products are native signals, so no limb of theirs exists to alias |
| Incomplete addition | Sums of points become sums of SCALARS before multiplication -- =eEnc.C = (m_rec + t*k)*G= rather than =m_rec*G + (t*k)*G= -- so the circuit performs no curve addition at all |

*The constraint budget forced a better leaf.* Built over point coordinates
the gate is 4,247,066 constraints, against a =pot22= ceiling of 4,194,304 --
a miss of 52,762, or 1.3%. The two fixed-base multiplications that re-derive
=M_rec= and =pk_recv= from scalars the prover already holds cost 943,792 of
that, 22% of the circuit, purely to reach the leaf. Committing the scalars
instead brings it to *3,303,970*, inside the ceiling with 890,334 to spare --
enough for the aggregator depth raise, which costs about 2,400.

That change is in the specification as v0.7, and it binds more tightly than
what it replaced: the circuit now hashes the very signals its other relations
consume, so the identity in the credential relation and the identity in the
leaf are the /same signal/ rather than two values joined by a derivation
whose output limbs the gadget does not range-check.

| Circuit | Constraints | Setup |
|---------+-------------+-------|
| =note_binding.circom= (A2) | 2,416,748 | none -- r1cs unchanged |
| =note_binding_a1.circom= | 2,859,018 | required |
| =deposit_fold_a1.circom= | 3,303,970 | required, fits =pot22= |
| =deposit_fold_a2.circom= | 3,336,421 | required, fits =pot22= |

*Validated at the witness level, which needs no ceremony.* Two independently
written implementations of the same relations -- the circom and the Python
reference -- agree on an honest witness, for both gates:
=WITNESS IS CORRECT= across all 3,303,970 constraints for A1, and across all
3,336,421 for A2.

And the relations are enforced /in the circuit/, not merely in the reference.
Tampering with one private signal of an otherwise honest witness makes the
C++ calculator abort at a named constraint:

| Tamper | Refused at | Which relation |
|--------+------------+----------------|
| =salt= alone | =identityRoot === mp.root= | (4): the leaf moved, so the path stops folding to the posted root |
| =m_rec= alone | =wV.out === mV.out + tk= | (1): the Identity IS the point =k= decrypts the note to |

The second is the fold's argument made concrete. One =m_rec= signal carries
the decryption relation, the credential relation and the leaf, so a single
tamper breaks all three at once. That is what "state the tie instead of
inferring it" buys: there is no seam between the two secrets for a thief to
work in, because there are no longer two proofs to put a seam between.

*The A2 sibling is written too*, =circuits/deposit_fold_a2.circom=, and its
extra relation is cheap: 3,336,421 constraints against A1's 3,303,970, under
1% more. Both carry seven fixed-base multiplications; A2's fifth relation, its
two curve additions and its limb range checks are all small beside one of
those. Where A1 avoids curve addition by construction, A2 cannot -- its
plaintext is an arbitrary point -- so it instead ENFORCES the incomplete
gadget's precondition, asserting at each addition that the two x-coordinates
differ, which excludes the doubling and the negation case together. Comparing
them reduced mod the native field is sufficient, since equal true coordinates
would be congruent.

*What the gates have.* A dev-entropy setup each, a generated Solidity
verifier, an adapter that derives every public input on chain, and the
=Notes.sol= call path -- the only addressed call path there is. What they do
not have is a ceremony.

** 4.4 Why only A2 ships issuer witness material
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: payload
:END:

Settled 2026-09-21. The rule turns out to be one line, and it decides all
three flavours at once:

#+begin_quote
A note ships witness material about a party *other than the prover* exactly
when its spend proof must assert something about that party. Only A2 must.
#+end_quote

| Flavour | Who must be proven registered | Who makes the proof | Witness shipped |
|---------+-------------------------------+---------------------+-----------------|
| A1 | the recipient | the recipient | none -- it holds its own salt |
| A2 | the recipient /and the issuer/ | the recipient | the issuer's salt |
| B1 | the depositor | the depositor | none -- it holds its own salt |

A1 and B1 prove membership of the prover's *own* Identity, so the prover has
its own salt and nothing needs to travel. A2 is the exception because its
ciphertext decrypts to the ISSUER's Identity, and the recipient must show that
point is registered -- otherwise a colluding issuer keys the note to a
throwaway and leaves the recipient holding garbage no receipt can name. That
is a claim about a third party, so the third party must supply the witness.

*What travels, and what does not.* Only the salt. Paths go stale as the
subtree grows; salts do not. The recipient rebuilds the path from the
published subtree at spend time, which is what publishing private subtrees in
full was for ([[file:accumulator-spec.org::#publication][specification section
9]]).

*Which salt.* Not the issuer's receiving-leaf salt -- that leaf commits the
issuer's mailbox key, and opening it would hand every recipient the issuer's
reading key. It is the salt of a SECOND association of the same Identity, an
ordinary =identity_leaf_salted=. Distinct associations carry distinct salts
([[file:accumulator-spec.org::#renewal][section 8.3]]), so the one that names
the issuer says nothing about the one that reads its mail. The mechanism
already existed; A2 is simply its first consumer.

*Where the hiding generator survives, and where it does not.* Neither fold
instantiates =ScalarMulH=: folding removed the shared hidden point that
needed a blind. So =H= remains in exactly two places, and they have opposite
requirements. Inside =issuer_reenc= it blinds within ONE sigma, where the
extractor recovers both openings and a known discrete log costs nothing --
which is what that module's source comment says, and for that use it is
right. Across B1's sigma and its membership proof it carries an equality
between two proofs, and there a known log is fatal. So the hash-to-curve
generator is needed for B1's =P_dep= and nothing else; the A2 mint binding
can keep the generator it has.

*Would A1 or B1 gain from shipping more issuer data?* No, and in both
directions the answer is instructive.

- *It cannot reduce the proving cost.* Every fixed-base multiplication in
  either fold pins one of the /prover's own/ secrets -- its randomness, its
  receiving key, its account key, its identity. Public data about a
  counterparty cannot discharge a constraint whose job is to pin a witness the
  counterparty does not hold.
- *For A1 it would add nothing and cost staleness.* A1's issuer is public by
  the flavour's definition, so it belongs in a public subtree with an unsalted
  leaf, and any party that knows the issuer's Identity computes that leaf
  itself. A shipped path would only go stale.
- *For B1 it would be a privacy loss for no gain.* The fact B1 needs -- the
  depositor is registered -- travels as a /proof/ in the spend transaction,
  which the issuer reads off chain and verifies with no witness at all. That
  is already the cheapest possible form. Shipping the depositor's salt instead
  would let the issuer locate and watch the depositor's leaf, which is strictly
  worse.
- *And B1 cannot be helped by issuer key material at all*, for a reason worth
  recording because it looks like an optimisation waiting to happen. B1's
  depositor forms =E_dep_for_iss = (rG, M_dep + r*pk_iss)=. To factor
  =r*pk_iss= through =G= -- which is what makes A1 and A2 cheap -- the
  depositor would need =sk_iss=. Any construction that gives it that hands the
  depositor the key which is supposed to make the issuer the only reader. B1's
  one variable-base product is irreducible, and that is a third reason not to
  fold B1.

So the three payloads are each minimal for their own privacy and proving
requirements, and the differences are not incidental: they track exactly which
party is private and which party is proving.

** 4.5 What each flavour must prove, side by side
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: flavours
:END:

The three flavours are not three variations on one gate. They differ along
axes that turn out to be mostly consequences of two root facts, and this
table is the compressed form of everything sections 3 and 4 establish.

#+ATTR_LATEX: :font \scriptsize
| | A1 addressed, public issuer | A2 addressed, private issuer | B1 bearer, public issuer |
|---+-----------------------------+------------------------------+--------------------------|
| Spend authority | the Identity named at mint | the Identity named at mint | knowledge of \( \rho \) |
| Whose secret opens the payload | recipient's \( k \) | recipient's \( k \) | *issuer's* \( sk_{iss} \) |
| Who produces the naming evidence | recipient | recipient | *issuer*, off chain |
| Two facts, how many secrets | *two* (\( m_{rec}, k \)) | *two* (\( m_{rec}, k \)) | *one* (\( m_{dep} \)) |
| Tie mechanism | leaf relation, in-circuit | leaf relation, in-circuit | shared FS nonce, sound once \( \log_G H \) unknown |
| Note-to-spend tie needed | yes | yes | *no* -- evidence is fresh at spend |
| Decrypted plaintext | \( M_{rec} = m_{rec}G \) | \( M_I \), arbitrary point | \( M_{dep} = m_{dep}G \) |
| Curve addition needed | *no* -- scalar sums suffice | *yes* | no |
| Encryption product factors through \( G \) | yes (\( r_n k \)) | yes (\( r' k \)) | *no* -- would need \( sk_{iss} \) |
| Whose membership is proven | the recipient's own | *the issuer's* | the depositor's own |
| Issuer witness shipped | none | the issuer's salt | none |
| Gate | folded circuit | folded circuit, +1 relation | sigma + membership circuit |
| Constraints (depth 20) | 3,306,390 | 3,341,261 | 491,764 |

*The four asymmetries, and where each comes from.*

1. *One secret or two.* A1 and A2 must prove two facts resting on two
   DIFFERENT secrets -- the Identity scalar and the receiving key -- so no
   shared nonce ties them and no choice of generator repairs it. The tie has
   to become a relation, which is why they fold. B1's two facts rest on ONE
   secret, so its sigma's shared nonce is a genuine tie and it needs no fold.
   This is the deep one, and it decides the shape of each gate.
2. *Whether the product factors through \( G \).* A1 and A2 are proven by the
   RECIPIENT, which holds both the randomness and the receiving secret, so
   every ElGamal product collapses to a multiple of \( G \) and stays
   fixed-base. B1's depositor encrypts TO the issuer: it holds \( r \) but not
   \( sk_{iss} \), so \( r \cdot pk_{iss} \) is irreducibly variable-base.
   Any construction that made it fixed-base would hand the depositor the key
   that makes the issuer the only reader. A third reason not to fold B1.
3. *Whether the plaintext is a known multiple of \( G \).* A1 decrypts to the
   recipient's own Identity, so its point sums fold into scalar sums and it
   performs no curve addition at all -- which is how it escapes finding 5's
   incomplete-addition defect by construction. A2 decrypts to an arbitrary
   point and cannot, so it must ENFORCE the gadget's precondition instead.
4. *Whose membership is proven.* A1 and B1 prove the prover's own, so the
   prover holds its own salt. A2 alone proves a third party's, which is the
   whole of [[#payload][the payload rule]].

*What the three payloads therefore carry.* Each is minimal for its own
privacy and proving requirements, and the differences are not incidental:
they track exactly which party is private and which party is proving. A1
carries its issuer in the clear because that issuer is public; A2 carries a
salt because its issuer is private and must still be provably registered; B1
carries neither, because the fact it needs travels as a proof the issuer
verifies with no witness at all.

** 4.6 The A2 key split, and why the tie must be committed
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: split
:END:

Found and confirmed 2026-09-21, by reproduction against the shipped verifiers.
It is the one place where the payload rule of [[#payload][4.4]] was satisfied
and the architecture still admitted a framing.

*The failure.* An ElGamal ciphertext does not bind its plaintext to one key.
For one randomness \( r \) and two registered Identities \( M_A, M_B \), a
minter holding the recipient's mailbox key \( pk_{real} = kG \) sets

#+begin_example
pk_mint = pk_real + r^{-1}*(M_B - M_A)
eIss    = (r*G, M_A + r*pk_mint)
        = (r*G, M_B + r*pk_real)
#+end_example

The same bytes are an encryption of \( M_A \) under the key the mint binding
hides in \( Q \), and of \( M_B \) under the key the recipient actually holds.
=issuer_reenc= is a true statement about the minter's own credential. The fold
is a true statement about a registered Identity. Neither says the two keys are
the same point, and nothing in =Notes.mint=, =spendCoupledA2= or
=verify_receipt= asks:

| Check | Result |
|-------+--------|
| =pk_mint == pk_real= | false |
| =issuer_reenc_verify= against the minter's credential | accepts |
| the same proof against \( M_B \)'s credential | rejects |
| \( k \) decrypts =eIss= to \( M_B \) | yes |
| =deposit_fold_check= for the recipient's \( k \) | accepts |
| =deposit_fold_a2_witness=, relation (5) on \( M_B \)'s leaf | builds |
| the same witness aimed at \( M_A \)'s leaf | refused |

*Why it is worse than a collusion.* The recipient does the honest thing --
decrypts, sees a registered Identity, rebuilds the path, proves the fold -- and
the receipt names \( M_B \). An honest party produces the false receipt, and
nothing it is given lets it notice: \( \beta \) and \( \gamma \), the blinds
that would open \( Q \) and \( T \), never travel. The minter needs only a
sock-puppet Identity of its own, whose naming salt it already has.

Relation (5) is not the hole. It closes the /un-nameable/ case -- a note keyed
to a throwaway point. This is the /misnamed/ case, and registration does not
help: =IdentityRegistry.register= deliberately refuses a caller-supplied leaf,
so a credential's plaintext is not tied to any accumulator leaf. The tie says
WHOSE Identity; relation (5) says REGISTERED. Both are needed.

*One promise already in the source, wrong three ways.* =issuer_reenc.py= says
the gap is closed in the receipt by requiring \( M_{named} = C_i - T \). That
check is not implemented; it is in the wrong layer, because an accepted spend
must name the minter whether or not a receipt is ever built; and the equation
is wrong -- \( T = r'pk + \gamma G \), so \( C_i - T = M - \gamma G \). A
receipt verifier cannot compute the binding's plaintext at all unless
\( \gamma \) travels.

*The fact that decides every candidate fix.* At spend the chain cannot look
anything up. =cm= and =idHash= are hidden inside the spend proof and the
nullifier reveals nothing, so the ONLY channel from mint to spend is =idHash=.
A tie the chain enforces at spend must therefore be committed in =idHash= --
which means =mint_batch_a2.circom='s =Poseidon(8)(eNote, eIss)= changes, and
with it six generated A2 verifiers. That is true of every candidate, and it
corrects the cost accounting: publishing \( pk_{recv} \) at mint is not the
cheap option, because binding it to the spend needs the same widening and it
links every mint to one mailbox as well.

*The closure, decided 2026-09-21: one lifelong mailbox, so commit \( T \).*
Blind the mint binding's \( T \) on a generator with no known logarithm and
have the fold open it:

- =issuer_reenc= L3/L5 replace \( \gamma G \) with \( \gamma H_{PEDERSEN} \).
  A generator swap in the sigma and in its Solidity mirror -- no new witness,
  no new public input, and =IdentityRegistry= already carries =H_PED_X/Y=.
  Blinding on \( G \) cannot be repaired by rearrangement: in a prime-order
  group a known-log blind binds nothing at all.
- =idHash_a2= commits \( T \).
- =deposit_fold_a2= adds =ScalarMulHP(gamma)= and one guarded addition,
  checking \( T = rm \cdot G + \gamma H_{PED} \). Since \( rm = r'k \) is
  already enforced natively against the \( k \) in the leaf, this forces
  \( r'(pk_Q - pk_{recv}) = (\gamma - \gamma_m) H_{PED} \), so an attacker
  needs \( M_B - M_A = \delta H_{PED} \) for a \( \delta \) it knows: a
  discrete log. The same assumption B1's \( P_{dep} \) already rests on.
- Cost: *3,341,261 + 471,896 = 3,813,157*, inside =pot22= with 381,147 to
  spare. Six small A2 mint setups (~6K R1CS per leaf), one 3.8M fold setup.
  As built, with the leaf tags and the ten-word =idHash=: 3,827,527.
- =verify_receipt= then checks \( M_{named} = C_i - T + \gamma H_{PED} \),
  which is the corrected form of the promise above. \( \gamma \) travels in the
  payload and in the receipt, never on chain.

A =pkHash= commitment was considered and is not constructible on the mint side:
a sigma cannot prove a Poseidon preimage, and the mint cannot move into a SNARK
because both \( r' pk_{recv} \) and \( sk_{iss} R_{reg} \) are variable-base --
which is why that binding is a sigma in the first place. A mint-time closure
(the recipient ships a hiding mailbox commitment and the sigma ties \( Q \) to
it) is sound and touches no circuit the gate already has, but it fixes one
commitment per payer, so the chain could group a payer's notes to one
recipient. With a lifelong mailbox that is the wrong trade; it would be the
right one under a key per payer, which is why [[#open][open question 2]] and
this closure are ONE decision rather than two.

* 5 What it costs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: cost
:END:

*Recoverability changes from the certified record to the wallet seed.* Today,
losing a wallet is not losing notes, because the identity scalar can be
recomputed from the record and the identity scalar is the key. That property
and the harvesting vulnerability are one fact seen twice: a decryption key
recoverable from a disclosed record is a decryption key everyone holding the
record already has. One cannot be kept without the other, and this
specification chooses privacy.

The mitigation available is the guardian mechanism the papers already
contemplate: wrap a copy of the note payload to a guardian key, or to a
threshold of them, which restores recovery without restoring universal
readability because a guardian key is not the identity.

*What is not lost:* unilateral addressing, the receipt and mutual
decryptability results, membership proofs, and the circuits' structure.

  field-element tag, =keccak(tag) mod F_R=.  These are baked into circuits, so
  they move in step C.

Traps worth naming, because each cost a run or an hour:

*Design.*

- The coupling sigma's shared nonce is load-bearing.  It looks like an
  optimisation.  It is only a genuine tie when both jobs use the SAME secret,
  which is true for B1 and false for A1 and A2 ([[#flavours]]).
- =IdentityTree= in =wallet/unilateral_a2.py= subclasses the registry tree
  and overrides =contains= to take a /point/, not a leaf.  Passing a leaf
  fails obscurely.

*Circuits and setups.*

- *Depth is baked into the r1cs, so it is baked into the ceremony.*  Build at
  the target depth or pay for the setup twice.  This was written into the A1
  fold's own trailer and then walked past, costing B1 a redundant setup.
- *=--O2= is required, not an optimisation.*  Without it circom leaves the
  linear constraints in -- B1 goes 491,764 to over 1.2M and snarkjs refuses
  outright.  It must also match the flags the witness was validated under.
- *The folds need =NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=16384=.*  Without it
  snarkjs dies on the ~870 MB r1cs AND LEAVES A 700-BYTE ZKEY that the
  contribute step would accept.  The setup script now refuses a phase-2 key
  under 100 MB.
- *Never edit a shell script while a run of it is in flight.*  Bash reads
  lazily by byte offset and can resume mid-line.
- *=snarkjs r1cs info= OOMs on a large r1cs.*  Parse the header instead:
  section type 1 holds =fieldSize=, the prime, then =nWires=, =nPubOut=,
  =nPubIn=, =nPrvIn= as u32, =nLabels= as u64, =nConstraints= as u32.
- *A =ScalarMulG= costs about 471,896 constraints.*  It is the only real lever
  on circuit size; hashes and Merkle paths are noise beside it.
- The BN254 modulus helpers are =BN254_MOD_Q0..Q3=; the curve-parameter
  helpers live in =bn254_g_scalarmul.circom=, and the =H_PEDERSEN= gadget
  carries its own under a =BN254HP_= prefix so both can be included.
- =build/= is gitignored.  The 4 GB of proving keys are local-only; the
  durable artifacts are the committed verifiers and vectors.
- *An attack the builder refuses has not been shown refused by the circuit.*
  The Python witness builder asserts every relation first, so a missing
  constraint is invisible through it.  =--split-raw= steps around the
  builder's check and must fail in the witness calculator; do the same for
  any new relation.

*Vectors.*

- *Rebuild the kernel before emitting =identity.json=.*  =emit-vectors=
  dispatches receipt building to whichever kernel is installed, so a stale
  =.so= writes the old code's receipts under the new code's hashes.  Run
  =make nix-core-build= first, then confirm =BUCK_IDENTITY_BACKEND=py= emits
  the same bytes.
- *Some Forge vectors have no make target.*  =scripts/gen_b1_binding_vectors.py=
  and =python -m alberta_buck.registry.vectors= write =test/vectors/b1_binding.json=
  and =test/vectors/registry/=; a generator or leaf change needs both rerun.
- *One more argument to =MintVerifierA2Adapter.verifyMint= overflowed the
  stack.*  The public-signal assembly moved to a helper taking the four
  scalars as one =uint256[4]=.

* 9 Open questions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: open
:END:

1. *Trial decryption cost.* The recipient scans candidate notes by trial
   decryption, as it does today with the identity scalar. Unchanged in order,
   but worth measuring at realistic note volumes.
2. *One receiving key or one per payer.* DECIDED 2026-09-21: one lifelong
   mailbox. It is simplest, and because the key is used as an encryption key
   rather than published per note, it does not link a recipient's notes under
   DDH. A key per counterparty would bound the damage of a leaked receiving
   secret, and would also have made the cheaper A2 closure available
   ([[#split][4.6]]); choosing one key is what makes the committed \( T \)
   tie the required closure rather than one option among several. Stealth
   addressing, which derives a fresh key per note, adds little here for its
   complexity, since the address is exchanged bilaterally rather than
   published. What the decision does leave standing is question 3: a leaked
   mailbox key is a lifelong read, so guardian wrapping and rotation hygiene
   carry more weight than they would have under per-payer keys.
3. *Guardian wrapping.* Whether recovery wrapping is part of the note payload
   or a wallet-side convention, and who the guardians are.
4. *The A1 value ciphertext.* =eNote= encrypts the value under the recipient
   key. Whether the face should remain derivable by the recipient alone, given
   the spend publishes it anyway, is a simplification worth considering while
   the payload is being changed.
5. *A written security argument*, which this document does not contain and
   which the architecture owes in the form Part I of
   =alberta-buck-proofs.org= owes for the registration presentation.
