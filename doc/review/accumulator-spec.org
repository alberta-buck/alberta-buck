* 1 Scope, status and conformance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scope
:END:

This is phase 1 of [[file:accumulator-plan.org][the accumulator plan]]: the
specification of how a certified identity enters the accumulator that Notes
membership proofs and credit issuance are checked against, and how a party
holding an identity establishes facts about it, without handing anyone a
test for an identity scalar.

Status: written before implementation, unreviewed. v0.2 resolved the five
design questions v0.1 left open. v0.3 sets the parameters from the expected
posting cadences, and adds delegated authority, which the insurer band
ladder made unavoidable. Both are in [[#decisions]]. v0.4 adds deployment
and governance ([[#governance]]): one root authority per deployment, on-chain
enrollment, an accountable rather than trustless aggregator, which rules
bind a delegate and which do not, and the line between the consistency the
protocol enforces and the accuracy it cannot. v0.5 settles the last three
items: scope without an asset taxonomy ([[#scope]]), the retention window,
and the aggregator's publication format. v0.6 adds the third leaf function,
which binds an identity to the receiving key its Notes are addressed to
([[#leaves]]), and the two conformance tests that hold it in place; the
receiving key itself is specified in
[[file:notes-receiving-key.org][its own document]], and phase 2 of the plan
implements both. v0.7 makes that leaf commit the two SCALARS rather than
the two points, which the folded spend gate's constraint budget forced and
which turns out to bind more tightly ([[#leaves]]). v0.8 records the second
association an A2 issuer keeps so it can be named without disclosing its
mailbox ([[#renewal]]). =MUST=, =MUST NOT=,
=SHOULD= and =MAY= are normative. Constants are given as the names already
in the source, with their current values, so that a conformance test can
compare rather than guess.

It specifies the accumulator only. The A' registration presentation, the
issuer key format, =register=, =bindContract= and the approve handshake are
unchanged and out of scope; see Part I of =alberta-buck-proofs.org=.

** 1.1 Decisions this specification takes

Listed for a reviewer who wants the choices without the prose.

1. Three leaf functions, not one. The existing unsalted leaf is retained
   for trees whose membership is advertised; a salted leaf is added for
   trees whose membership is private; and a salted leaf over the pair
   =(identity, receiving key)= is added for identity registries, whose
   members address Notes ([[#leaves]]).
2. Leaf hiding rests on Poseidon modelled as a random oracle, which the
   system already assumes for the Notes nullifier, so the accumulator adds
   no assumption. No cheaper construction gives an information-theoretically
   hidden leaf, because a Merkle tree over field elements makes leaf hiding
   a hash property whatever commits underneath it ([[#security]]).
3. The salt is derived from a holder secret, never from the identity, so an
   authority that learns one salt cannot derive another ([[#salt]]).
4. Private subtrees are published in full. Their leaves are hiding
   commitments, so publication leaks nothing and holders compute their own
   witnesses. No witness service is required ([[#publication]]).
5. Each posted root is stored with its timestamp, and each consumer
   enforces its own maximum age. A count of postings is not a bound in
   time, and the same number cannot serve both in-flight tolerance and
   revocation urgency ([[#root]]).
6. Admission stays off =register= permanently ([[#admission]]).
7. Validity is membership in a registry's live-set subtree, whose freshness
   comes from the root's timestamp. There is no epoch dimension in the
   trees, and the identity scalar is stable for life ([[#renewal]]).
8. The insurer envelope is a cached periodic attestation, verified by a
   Poseidon Merkle path, not a per-credit proof, and carries only what a
   consumer gates on ([[#insurer]]).
9. An attesting authority retains the current association only, so it
   cannot reconstruct a holder's change history ([[#renewal]]).

* 2 Objects and notation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: objects
:END:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| =m= | identity scalar, =identity_scalar(canonical_identity)=, stable for the holder's life |
| =M= | identity point, =m*G= on BN254 =G1= |
| =F_R= | BN254 scalar field, the field Poseidon operates in |
| =salt= | a per identity, per tree, per association blinding value in =[1, F_R)= |
| =leaf= | a tree leaf, an element of =[0, F_R)=; =EMPTY_LEAF= is =0= |
| subtree | one authority's own tree |
| aggregator | the tree whose leaves are subtree roots and whose root is posted on chain |
| =identityRoot= | the most recently posted aggregator root |
| root record | a posted root together with the time it was posted |

Depths, which differ deliberately and are already named in
=alberta_buck/registry/tree.py=:

| Name | Value | Constraint |
|------+-------+------------|
| =AGGREGATOR_DEPTH= | 20, raised from 10 | =MUST= equal Solidity =IDENTITY_TREE_DEPTH= and both membership circuits' main depth |
| =KYC_SUBTREE_DEPTH= | 12 | capacity of one registry organization; changing it changes subtree roots and the committed kernel vectors |
| =FEATURE_SUBTREE_DEPTH= | 10 | capacity of one attribute authority |

=AGGREGATOR_DEPTH= is raised from 10 to 20, which is 1,048,576 authorities
rather than 1,024. The design admits clubs, community boards, congregations
and individuals as attribute authorities, and delegated sub-regulators as
insurance authorities ([[#delegation]]), so authorities are a population
rather than a roster. Ten extra levels cost ten Poseidon-2 hashes in the
membership circuit, which is on the order of two thousand constraints
against the millions the note-binding circuits already carry. The change
=MUST= be made in the same trusted setup as the salted leaf and the
finding-5 repairs, because all three touch the same circuits.

* 3 Leaf functions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: leaves
:END:

Three functions. Implementations =MUST= provide all three and =MUST NOT=
change the first two.

#+begin_example
identity_leaf(M)              = Poseidon([M.x mod F_R, M.y mod F_R])
identity_leaf_salted(M, salt) = Poseidon([M.x mod F_R, M.y mod F_R, salt])
receiving_leaf(m, k, salt)    = Poseidon([m, k, salt])
#+end_example

- =identity_leaf= is the function in the source today. It is retained
  unchanged for public trees ([[#classes]]), so every committed vector that
  records it stays valid.
- =identity_leaf_salted= is new. =salt= =MUST= lie in =[1, F_R)=;
  implementations =MUST= reject =0=, which would make the leaf a
  deterministic function of the identity in a tree declared private.
- =receiving_leaf= is the leaf of an identity-registry subtree, and binds
  two things rather than one: the identity a Note names, and the receiving
  secret =k= whose public key =k*G= a Note is encrypted to
  ([[file:notes-receiving-key.org][the receiving-key architecture]]). All
  three arguments =MUST= lie in =[1, F_R)=. It is a third function rather
  than a widened second one because attribute subtrees bind no receiving key
  and their leaves =MUST NOT= change.

  *It commits the SCALARS where its siblings commit coordinates, and that
  difference is deliberate.* The siblings are computed by authorities that
  hold identity points and nothing else. This leaf exists to be proven in
  zero knowledge by a holder that has the scalars, and BN254's =G1= group
  order equals =F_R=, so a scalar is a field element outright -- no
  reduction, no limbs, no aliasing question about a limb decomposition.
  Committing coordinates instead would oblige the spend circuit to re-derive
  both points from those same scalars, at 471,896 constraints per fixed-base
  multiplication: 943,792 to reach a commitment whose preimages the prover
  already holds, and 22% of the circuit. Measured, not estimated: the gate is
  4,247,066 constraints over coordinates and 3,303,970 over scalars, against
  a =pot22= ceiling of 4,194,304.

  Two properties improve rather than degrade. The circuit hashes the very
  signals its other relations consume, so "the identity in the credential"
  and "the identity in the leaf" are the /same signal/ rather than two values
  joined by a derivation. And it is harder to scan: a payer is GIVEN both the
  identity point and the receiving key, so under a coordinate leaf only the
  salt stood between it and a membership test, where here it would also need
  =k=, which is disclosed to nobody.
- Coordinates are reduced mod =F_R= exactly as today, because circom
  reduces its Poseidon inputs the same way.

*Why the binding is committed and not published.* Notes are keyed to =K=
rather than to =M= because an identity scalar is a read capability the
system discloses to every counterparty, and so cannot also be a decryption
key. A note addressed to a key of the payer's choosing would break mutual
decryptability and the receipt, so =K= =MUST= be bound to =M= -- and that
binding =MUST NOT= be public, because a spend proving against a public
binding would reveal the recipient's registered key and deanonymise them to
everyone. Inside the leaf it is provable in zero knowledge and unscannable
to a party holding every certified identity, the whole published subtree,
and =K= itself, the last because deciding a ciphertext's addressee from the
key alone is a DDH decision.

All three =MUST= be implemented identically in Python, in the Rust
kernel, in both bindings and in the circuits, and pinned by vectors, as
every identity primitive is.

* 4 Tree classes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: classes
:END:

Every subtree =MUST= be declared, at enrollment, as exactly one of:

- *private*: membership is a fact about a person that the person did not
  publish. Leaves =MUST= use =identity_leaf_salted=, or =receiving_leaf=
  where the subtree is an identity registry whose members address Notes.
  Membership =MUST= be proven in zero knowledge.
- *public*: membership is a fact the member advertises, for an institution
  acting in a public role. Leaves =MAY= use =identity_leaf=. Membership
  =MAY= be proven by a plain Merkle path, on chain or off.

Classification is a property of the subtree, not of a leaf, and =MUST NOT=
vary per member within a subtree.

| Subtree | Class | Why |
|---------+-------+-----|
| identity registry, per organization | private | participation is not the holder's announcement |
| age, residence, licence and similar person attributes | private | same |
| insurance regulator, per jurisdiction | public | an insurer advertises standing so counterparties can check it |
| any future public-role authority | public | same, and =MUST= be justified at enrollment |

Rationale: the privacy constraint exists because the issuer and a
counterparty hold the same secret, so no function of the identity alone can
admit the one and exclude the other. Where the member wants the fact
checkable by anyone, that is the intent rather than a leak, and the cheaper
construction is correct.

* 5 The aggregator and the on-chain root
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: root
:END:

The two-level structure in =alberta_buck/registry/merkle_service.py= is
unchanged in shape. Aggregator leaves are subtree roots, which are not
identity-derived, so they take no salt.

A holder proving several facts produces one =FullMembershipProof= per
subtree, each a subtree path plus an aggregator path, all verifying against
one root, as =ComposedMembershipProof= already composes them. Nothing in
the proof reveals which subtrees were walked, because the paths are private
circuit inputs.

*Root records and maximum age.* =IdentityRegistry= =MUST= retain a bounded
ring of root records, each a root together with the block timestamp at
which it was posted. Posting =MUST= emit both. A verifier =MUST= reject the
zero root, and =MUST= accept a proof against a retained root only if that
root's age does not exceed the maximum age its own consumer declares.

The ring size is a storage decision. The bound that matters is the maximum
age, and it =MUST= be declared per consumer rather than once for the
protocol, because the two things a bound does pull in opposite directions:
tolerance for proofs in flight wants a long window, and revocation urgency
wants a short one. A count of postings is not a bound in time at all. With
periodic attestation an authority may post monthly, so thirty postings
could be years.

Consequence to accept deliberately: an authority that stops posting
eventually ages out every proof its members can make. Maximum ages
=SHOULD= therefore be generous wherever revocation is not the point of the
check.

*Storage shape.* A root record =SHOULD= be a mapping from root to the time
it was posted, plus a ring of roots for eviction, so that checking a
proof's root is one lookup and eviction is one deletion.

*Ring size: 256 records.* Sized from the fastest cadence the design
expects. Private records resolve at the resolution of a day, so a registry
posts daily; institutional records weekly; regulators quarterly now and
weekly later. An aggregator batching many authorities may therefore post as
often as hourly, and 256 hourly postings is ten days, which covers the
longest maximum age below. The contract =MUST= refuse a maximum age longer
than the ring can cover at the current posting rate, rather than silently
truncating that consumer's window.

*Maximum ages.* Governance-settable per consumer, with these defaults:

| Consumer | Default | Why |
|----------+---------+-----|
| Notes membership gate | 7 days | the gate asks whether a counterparty is a registered identity, not whether it was revoked this morning; a spend that fails because a registry batched late is worse than a spend by someone revoked three days ago |
| Insurer attestation ([[#insurer]]) | 1 day | the insurer proves against a root when it attests, and the aggregator posts at least daily |
| Anything else | 7 days | until that consumer states a reason to differ |

Credit issuance declares no maximum age, because it reads the cached
envelope and its expiry rather than a root ([[#insurer]]).

*Public subtree roots.* For a public subtree ([[#classes]]) the registry
=MAY= additionally store the subtree's own root, posted by the aggregator
service under the same record shape. A public membership proof then walks
the subtree only, at =FEATURE_SUBTREE_DEPTH= hashes, instead of the subtree
and the aggregator at =FEATURE_SUBTREE_DEPTH= plus =AGGREGATOR_DEPTH=. On
chain that is the difference between ten Poseidon calls and thirty, which
at tens of thousands of gas each is the difference between a few hundred
thousand gas and most of a million. There is no privacy cost, because the
subtree is public by construction. Phase 4 =MUST= measure both.

* 6 Admission
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: admission
:END:

1. =IdentityRegistry.register= and =bindContract= =MUST= continue to refuse
   a nonzero caller-supplied =identityLeaf=, permanently. This is a
   decision, not a stopgap, and the revert string =unchecked identity leaf=
   =MUST= be retained so the existing reproduction keeps asserting it.
2. Admission is performed by the authority that owns the subtree. For a
   private subtree the exchange is:
   1. the holder derives =salt= ([[#salt]]) and sends =(M, salt)= to the
      authority over the channel that already carries the certificate;
   2. the authority verifies it certified =M=, or for an attribute
      authority that =M= satisfies the attribute by whatever evidence it
      requires off chain;
   3. the authority computes =identity_leaf_salted(M, salt)=, inserts it,
      and records the association in its own books;
   4. the authority pushes its new subtree root to the aggregator.
3. The authority =MUST NOT= require a zero-knowledge proof at admission.
   It knows =M= already, so a proof would establish nothing it cannot check
   directly.
4. Insertion =SHOULD= be batched. An authority that inserts one leaf per
   holder action publishes the timing of that action; a batch publishes only
   the batch.

* 7 Salt derivation, storage and recovery
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: salt
:END:

#+begin_example
salt = Poseidon([holder_secret, H(tree_id), association_counter]) mod F_R,
       redrawn if 0
#+end_example

- =holder_secret= is a wallet secret, recoverable from the wallet's seed
  material, and =MUST NOT= leave the wallet.
- =H(tree_id)= is the subtree identifier hashed into =F_R=.
- =association_counter= starts at zero and increments on each
  re-association into the same subtree ([[#renewal]]).

Requirements:

- The salt =MUST NOT= be derivable from =m=, from =M=, or from any value an
  authority holds. An authority that learns one salt therefore learns
  nothing about the holder's other leaves, in its own tree or any other.
- The wallet =MUST= be able to recompute every salt it has used from its
  seed material, so that losing local state does not lose the ability to
  prove membership.
- Distinct subtrees =MUST= yield distinct salts for the same identity.
  Otherwise two authorities' leaves for one identity are equal and link
  across trees.

No key-derivation helper exists in the wallet today; one is part of phase 2.

*Re-salting cadence: never on a timer.* A salt =MUST= change only on an
event the holder takes part in: a re-association ([[#renewal]]), or a
renewal the registry chooses to make interactive. Timer-driven re-salting
would force either the holder to be online on the registry's schedule, or
the authority to choose salts itself, which would break recovery from seed
material and make a dormant holder's ability to prove membership depend on
fetching from its authority. The cost of not re-salting is that a leaf's
tenure is visible in the published tree ([[#renewal]]); that is weak
information about an anonymous member, and it matters only where the
anonymity set is small, which is a condition the earlier review already
names.

* 8 Revocation, renewal and re-association
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: renewal
:END:

** 8.1 Validity is membership

The =epoch= field of the certified record =MUST= be read as the epoch of
first certification and =MUST NOT= be bumped. Nothing validates it today:
=canonical_identity_data= imposes no schema, and only issuer documentation
mentions epochs. The identity scalar is therefore stable for the holder's
life and no committed vector changes.

Validity is membership in the registry's live-set subtree: one subtree per
registry organization, containing exactly the members the registry holds to
be currently certified and in good standing. Freshness is the age of the
root the proof is against ([[#root]]), so "live" means live as of that
posting. A certificate's =expires_at= is advisory to its holder and =MUST
NOT= be a protocol gate.

There is deliberately no epoch dimension in the trees. Renewal is the
registry continuing to include the member in the set it posts; lapse is
clearance. This costs one subtree per registry rather than one per registry
per epoch, which matters because the aggregator is depth
=AGGREGATOR_DEPTH= and so holds 1024 subtrees in total.

A leaf that persists across postings lets an observer see how long that
leaf has been present and correlate its disappearance with a revocation. No
identity is revealed. A registry =MAY= re-salt its live set periodically to
remove even that, at the cost of every member refreshing its witness.

** 8.2 Revocation

Revocation is clearing the leaf to =EMPTY_LEAF= and pushing a fresh subtree
root, which =registry/feature_authority.py= already implements. A revoked
holder can produce no membership proof against any root posted after the
clearance, and the consumer's maximum age bounds how long a proof against
an earlier root remains acceptable. A consumer for which revocation is the
point of the check =SHOULD= declare a short maximum age.

Because private subtrees are published ([[#publication]]), a cleared slot is
visible. It reveals that a member was removed, not which, since the leaf it
replaced was a hiding commitment.

** 8.3 Re-association for mutable facts

Mutable facts, an address or a region, live in external records under the
holder's control and are represented by membership in the subtree for their
current value. On a change, the authority =MUST=:

1. insert =identity_leaf_salted(M, salt')= into the subtree for the new
   value, with =salt'= drawn at an incremented =association_counter=;
2. clear the old leaf;
3. push both subtree roots.

The new and old leaves =MUST NOT= share a salt. With distinct salts they are
unlinkable to any party without the authority's own records, and the
attesting authority =MUST= be named as a party alongside the issuer.

*A second association, deliberately.* A holder =MAY= hold more than one
association of the same identity in one registry: its receiving leaf, whose
preimage includes its mailbox key, and a plain =identity_leaf_salted=
association whose salt it can disclose in order to be NAMED. The two carry
distinct salts, so disclosing the naming one reveals nothing about the
mailbox one. An A2 issuer =MUST= hold such a second association and =MUST=
ship its salt in the note payload, because the recipient has to prove at
spend that the Identity it decrypted is registered, and that is a claim about
a party other than the prover
([[file:notes-receiving-key.org::#payload][why only A2 ships it]]). The salt
alone travels: the recipient rebuilds the path from the published subtree,
since paths go stale as a subtree grows and salts do not.

*Retention.* An authority needs the current association only, the identity,
its salt and its leaf index, in order to clear and re-insert. It does not
need history. An authority =MUST= discard a superseded salt and its
association once the re-association is posted, including from operational
logs and backups, so that it cannot reconstruct a holder's change history.
An authority =MAY= retain a cleared association for up to 90 days, so that
an erroneous revocation can be reversed, and =MUST= discard it after that.
Ninety days covers an administrative appeal and nothing longer justifies
keeping a link that exists only to be undone. This is a retention
obligation on the authority rather than a property the protocol enforces,
and it is auditable by policy review rather than by code. A construction in which the holder proves a re-association in zero
knowledge, so the receiving authority never learns which prior leaf was
cleared, would enforce it cryptographically; it conflicts with admission
needing no proof ([[#admission]]) and is left to a later version.

* 9 Publication and witness updates
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: publication
:END:

- An authority =MUST= publish enough of a subtree for a member to compute
  its own path: the leaf list in insertion order suffices.
- Private subtrees =MAY= be published in full, and =SHOULD= be, because
  their leaves are hiding commitments. No witness service is required, and
  the open question the earlier review raised about witness-service privacy
  does not arise.
- A deployment whose threat model includes a future break of Poseidon =MAY=
  withhold private subtrees and have the authority deliver each member its
  path instead. That restores the witness-delivery party and lets the
  authority observe when a member refreshes, and it is the only way to
  remove the retroactive-scan exposure of [[#security]]. Both modes
  =MUST= be supported by the same proof statement, so the choice is
  operational rather than structural.
- A holder =MUST= refresh its path after any subtree update that changes a
  sibling. Publication makes this self-service.
- Public subtrees =MUST= be published in full, since their whole purpose is
  third-party checking.

* 10 Attribute vocabulary
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: vocabulary
:END:

A predicate is membership in a named subtree. Identifiers are namespaced
strings, already the key type of =CentralMerkleService=:

#+begin_example
kyc:<registry-id>                   private   certified and currently live
feature:age-over-18                 private
feature:region:<code>               private   one subtree per region value
regulator:<jurisdiction>:insurer    public    licensed, in good standing
regulator:<jurisdiction>:insurer:class:<n>   public   may underwrite asset class n
regulator:<jurisdiction>:insurer:face:<band> public   per-contract face band, cumulative
regulator:<jurisdiction>:delegates           public   authorities this regulator has delegated to
regulator:<jurisdiction>:delegates:class:<n> public   the scope a delegate was granted
regulator:<jurisdiction>:delegates:face:<b>  public   the band ceiling a delegate was granted
#+end_example

Rules:

- A predicate =MUST= be expressible as membership. Anything needing a
  comparison over the record, rather than a yes or no, is out of scope for
  this version and =MUST NOT= be smuggled into a leaf.
- Bands =MUST= be coarse and =MUST= be distinct subtrees, never a value
  inside a leaf, so that a proof reveals the band and nothing finer.
- A verifier =MUST= name the subtrees it requires, and =MUST= declare its
  maximum root age ([[#root]]). A proof is meaningless without the
  identifier it is against and the staleness it was accepted under.

* 11 Proof statements
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: proofs
:END:

** 11.1 Private membership, in zero knowledge

Public: =identityRoot=, and the committed identity point the companion
relation opens. Private: =M=, =salt=, the subtree path, the aggregator path.

#+begin_example
leaf   = Poseidon([M.x, M.y, salt])
fold leaf up the subtree path      -> sub_root
fold sub_root up the aggregator path -> identityRoot   (a posted root)
and M is the point the companion relation opens
#+end_example

The final clause is the repair of finding 5 and =MUST= be the same private
identity variable, not an equality inferred from two uses of a public
point. The circuit therefore gains one private input, the salt, and its leaf
hasher becomes Poseidon-3.

** 11.2 Public membership

A plain Merkle path, verifiable on chain through the Poseidon contract the
registry already holds, or off chain by anyone. No circuit, no trusted
setup.

* 12 The insurer envelope and the credit gate
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: insurer
:END:

The insurance regulator is a periodic attestor. Its review of underwriting
quality, portfolio risk, reserves and reinsurance concludes in an envelope,
and the envelope is published as membership in public subtrees
([[#vocabulary]]).

*Attestation.* A new entry point takes the insurer's identity point, the
subtree identifiers it claims, and a Merkle path per claim against a root in
the ring. It verifies the paths, then caches:

| Field | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| standing | attested, with the root index it was proven against |
| asset classes | the set the insurer may underwrite |
| face band | the maximum per-contract face |
| expires at | the end of the attestation period |

*The band ladder.* Bands are powers of ten of face, denominated in BUCK
rather than in a fiat unit so that the check needs no oracle:

| Band | Ceiling, BUCK |
|------+---------------|
| 1 | 10 |
| 2 | 100 |
| 3 | 1,000 |
| 4 | 10,000 |
| 5 | 100,000 |
| 6 | 1,000,000 |
| 7 | 10,000,000 |
| 8 | 100,000,000 |

Bands are cumulative: an insurer attested at band =n= may write any face
below the band =n= ceiling, so the envelope stores one integer rather than
a set, and membership in =face:<n>= implies every lower band. Faces are
compared in =BuckCredit='s own units, which carry six decimals, so band
=n= is a ceiling of =10^n * 10^6= in those units.

The ladder starts at ten BUCK deliberately. An insurer underwriting a
neighbour's bicycle is the same shape of actor as one underwriting a
building, and the design supports individuals, families and communities
underwriting things they understand. Nothing in the gate distinguishes a
person from an institution except the envelope its regulator attested.

*Issuance.* =BuckCredit.createCredit= keeps its signature. Before creating
a credit it =MUST= require that the caller's cached envelope is unexpired,
covers =assetClass=, and admits =faceValue= within its band. The check is a
storage read. The client's =setCreditIssuer= opt-in is unchanged and both
=MUST= hold.

Rationale: the signature has 89 call sites across 38 files. A per-credit
proof would touch all of them and would pay a Merkle verification per
credit for a fact that changes once a review period.

*Enumeration.* The envelope is cached on chain for the gate to read, so it
is public and the insurer population and their bands can be enumerated.
That is intrinsic once insurer authority is on chain, and licensee lists
are public record in most jurisdictions, so standing and asset class add no
disclosure. The commercially sensitive field is the underwriter risk
estimate, which the credit gate does not need: the gate asks whether this
insurer may write this asset class at this face, and premium adequacy is
the insurer's own business. The risk estimate is therefore =NOT= part of
the on-chain envelope, and =MUST NOT= be added unless a consumer is shown
to gate on it. Bands =MUST= stay coarse for the same reason.

** 12.2 Scope, and why there is no asset taxonomy
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: scope
:END:

An asset taxonomy on chain would have to be global, versioned and endlessly
refined: land becomes farmland and city lot, vehicle becomes car and truck
and then Ford and pre-1995 and Mustang, across every class at once. The
specification does not have one, for a reason that also makes the gate
simpler.

*What underwriting actually produces* is not a classification. It is a risk
rating per unit time, a scale in BUCK, and a depreciation model chosen to
represent that specific asset. =BuckCredit= already stores exactly those:
=premiumRate=, =faceValue=, and =depType= with =depRate= and
=depreciationFloor=. The asset's identity is a detail of the insurance
contract, held by the insurer and its client, not a protocol object.

The depreciation model carries more of the asset's nature than a class code
would. A bicycle is linear to a floor over a few years, a building is
declining balance at a low rate, land does not depreciate at all. Bounding
an insurer's permitted models therefore bounds the economic shape of what
it can insure, without naming a single asset kind.

*Two restrictions, one checkable and one attributable.*

1. *The parameter envelope*, fully checkable on chain because every value
   is in the credit itself: the face band ([[#insurer]]), the permitted
   depreciation types, a bound on =depRate=, and a bound on =premiumRate=.
2. *The scope*, a =bytes32= identifier the insurer declares when it creates
   a credit. The chain cannot verify what the asset is, so the scope is the
   insurer's public claim about what it is writing. The gate checks that
   the insurer's envelope covers that scope. A false claim is then evidence
   rather than an inference, and the regulator's remedy is clearance.

*Scope identifiers are names, not codes.* A scope is the hash of a
namespaced string owned by the authority that granted it:

#+begin_example
regulator:<jurisdiction>:scope:asset:vehicle
regulator:<jurisdiction>:scope:asset:vehicle:car:ford:pre-1995
regulator:<jurisdiction>:scope:asset:toy
community-board:<id>:scope:asset:bicycle
#+end_example

This gives refinement for free. A new distinction is a new name, granted by
attesting insurers into the subtree for it; no existing name changes, no
version is bumped, and the contract never parses a hierarchy. Two
regulators' =vehicle= scopes are different identifiers, which is correct,
because they are different judgements.

*Narrower is not broader.* Membership in a fine scope does not imply the
coarse one. An insurer attested only for pre-1995 Fords is a member of that
subtree alone, and a credit declaring the broader vehicle scope fails
because the insurer is not attested for it. A regulator that intends an
insurer to write both attests it into both. The contract therefore needs no
prefix matching, which is what makes names affordable.

*The worked examples.* A child running an enterprise as a class project is
attested into =scope:asset:toy= and =scope:asset:bicycle=, at face band 2,
permitted =LINEAR= only. They may insure a neighbour's bicycle for eighty
BUCK and cannot insure a car, because the car's credit declares a scope
they do not hold, and a building's credit exceeds their band and their
model. A car collector is attested into
=scope:asset:vehicle:car:ford:pre-1995= at a higher band with
=DECLINING_BALANCE= permitted, and cannot write a toy.

*The cached envelope* therefore holds, per insurer: standing, the face
band, a mask of permitted depreciation types, a maximum =depRate=, a
maximum =premiumRate=, an expiry, and the set of scopes it may declare. The
scope set is one storage write per scope at attestation, which is periodic,
and one lookup per credit.

*=createCredit= gains an overload*, not a changed signature. The existing
eight-argument form declares the zero scope, which a regulator grants to a
general insurer and withholds from a scoped one; the nine-argument form
takes the scope explicitly. The existing call sites compile and run
unchanged, needing only the fixture line that grants their test insurer an
envelope. The scope is emitted with =CreditCreated= rather than stored, so
=CreditParams= keeps its three-slot packing.

The existing =uint8 assetClass= keeps its current status, which is an
insurer's own coarse tag with no protocol meaning. The gated field is the
scope.

** 12.1 Delegated authority
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: delegation
:END:

A regulator delegates parts of its remit to sub-regulators: community
boards for local mutual insurance, trade bodies for a class of equipment,
and so on. The same holds outside insurance, where the authority to attest
that an identity belongs to a club, a congregation or a professional body
is the club's, not the state's. Delegation is therefore a first-class
object, and it is *public*: an authority's right to attest is not a secret
of the authority.

A delegate is an ordinary authority with its own subtree. What makes it a
delegate is membership in its parent's public delegates subtree, scoped by
the same machinery insurers are scoped by:

- =regulator:<j>:delegates= holds the delegate authorities' identities;
- =regulator:<j>:delegates:class:<n>= and =:face:<b>= hold the scope each
  delegate was granted.

A verifier decides how much delegation it trusts by naming the subtrees it
requires ([[#vocabulary]]). One that trusts only the state requires
membership in the state regulator's own subtree. One that accepts a
delegate requires membership in the delegate's subtree *and* the delegate's
membership in the parent's delegates subtree, both against the same root.
Both are public proofs, so neither needs a circuit.

Rules:

- A delegate =MUST NOT= attest outside the scope its parent granted. The
  protocol cannot enforce this, since the delegate owns its own subtree; a
  verifier enforces it by checking the scope subtrees, and a parent
  enforces it by clearing the delegate.
- Delegation =MUST NOT= nest beyond the depth a verifier is willing to
  walk. Each level is another public proof the verifier must name and
  check, so a chain is self-limiting rather than bounded by the protocol.
- Revoking a delegate is clearing it from the delegates subtree. Its own
  attestations remain in its own subtree until it or the aggregator stops
  posting them, so a verifier that checks the delegation edge sees the
  revocation immediately and one that does not, never does. That is the
  argument for checking the edge.

This is why =AGGREGATOR_DEPTH= is raised: a roster of a thousand national
authorities is one design, and a population of community authorities is
another.

*Out of scope, by decision.* The assumption of asset pools, contracts and
company assets on an insurer's failure by its upstream reinsurers. This
specification assumes instead that an insurer's soundness includes pools
that will pay the expected rate of underwritten payouts with a buffer, and
that reinsurance triggered by failure, together with removal from the
regulator's subtree, tops those pools up where necessary.

* 13 Security requirements
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: security
:END:

Each is a claim an implementation =MUST= be testable against.

- *A-scan.* No party holding any set of identity scalars, including every
  scalar a registry ever certified, has non-negligible advantage in deciding
  whether a given identity is a member of a private subtree, given the
  subtree in full. Rests on Poseidon's preimage and pseudorandomness under
  a uniform salt.
- *A-bind.* A leaf commits to one identity: finding a second
  =(M', salt')= with the same leaf is a Poseidon collision.
- *A-sound.* An accepting membership proof implies the owning authority
  inserted that leaf, under the collision resistance of Poseidon and the
  soundness of the proof system.
- *A-unlink.* Two leaves for one identity, in different subtrees or in the
  same subtree at different association counters, are unlinkable to a party
  without the authority's records, by the salt's independence.
- *A-fresh.* Salt reuse across associations breaks A-unlink, and salt zero
  breaks A-scan. Both =MUST= be rejected rather than merely avoided.
- *A-noleak.* Nothing a verifier sees on chain is a deterministic function
  of an identity: not the leaf, not the path, not the subtree identifier.
  The one exception is a public subtree, where it is intended.
- *A-scope.* A delegate's attestation carries no more authority than the
  scope its parent granted, for a verifier that checks the delegation edge
  and the scope subtrees. For one that does not, the delegate's own subtree
  is the whole of its authority, which is the verifier's choice and =MUST=
  be stated as such rather than treated as a protocol guarantee.

*Assumption accounting.* Leaf hiding rests on Poseidon as a random oracle.
The Notes nullifier already rests on exactly that, so the accumulator
introduces no assumption the system does not already carry. Nothing here
weakens the A' results, which are independent of the accumulator.

No cheaper construction does better. A Merkle tree over field elements
needs a field element at the leaf, so a Pedersen commitment would have to
be hashed to reach one, and hiding of the leaf is then a hash property
whatever commits underneath it: testing a candidate identity against a
hashed Pedersen point costs the same search over the blinding as testing it
against a salted Poseidon hash. Information-theoretic leaf hiding would
take a different accumulator, not a different commitment.

*Retroactive exposure.* Publication ([[#publication]]) means a future break
of Poseidon preimage resistance would let an adversary scan the archived
private subtrees and recover which identities were in which tree. What that
yields is participation and attributes, never accounts, notes or amounts,
so it is strictly less than the exposure the papers already accept for the
ElGamal layer under a discrete-log break, and it =MUST= be recorded beside
it in the long-horizon section of =alberta-buck-proofs.org=. The withheld
mode of [[#publication]] removes it.

* 15 Conformance tests
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: conformance
:END:

1. *Scan.* Build a private subtree of many identities, publish it in full,
   hand a test every certified scalar, and require zero membership
   decisions better than guessing.
2. *Leaf parity.* =identity_leaf= agrees with every committed vector that
   records it, unchanged. =identity_leaf_salted= agrees across Python, the
   Rust kernel, both bindings and the circuit.
2a. *Receiving-key leaf.* The collapsed design -- an identity point
    encrypted under itself -- is scannable with one scalar multiplication
    per candidate, and keying the same plaintext to =k*G= is not; a party
    holding every certified scalar, the whole published subtree and the
    receiving key itself decides no membership of a =receiving_leaf=; the
    holder's binding proof verifies for its own pair and for no other
    identity; a rotated key yields an unlinkable leaf; each argument
    outside =[1, F_R)= is refused; and =receiving_leaf= agrees across
    Python, the Rust kernel, both bindings and the circuit.
2b. *The folded deposit gate.* A witness exists for the honest spender;
    a payload thief supplying the stolen receiving key with its own
    registered identity and account is refused, and refused specifically
    on the leaf relation; a wrong account key is refused on the credential
    relation; an unregistered account and a stale root are each refused.
    Both halves the thief would have passed =MUST= be shown individually
    true, since that is why the tie cannot be left to inference.
3. *Salt rules.* Zero salt rejected; the same identity in two subtrees
   yields different leaves; a wallet restored from seed material recomputes
   every salt it used.
4. *Revocation.* A cleared member proves nothing against any root posted
   after the clearance, and a proof against an earlier root is refused by a
   consumer whose maximum age has passed and accepted by one whose has not.
5. *Re-association.* An address change yields two leaves that no party
   without the authority's records can link.
6. *Root records.* Each retained root carries the timestamp it was posted
   at; a proof against a root inside a consumer's maximum age verifies and
   one outside it does not; a root evicted from the ring is unusable
   whatever its age; the zero root is rejected.
7. *Admission closed.* =register= and =bindContract= still refuse a nonzero
   caller-supplied leaf, with the existing revert string.
8. *Insurer envelope.* An unattested insurer cannot create a credit; an
   attested one can within its asset classes and face band and cannot
   outside them; an expired envelope fails; the 89 existing call sites
   compile unchanged.
9. *Notes.* The membership path still verifies with the salted leaf on the
   repaired circuit, for A1, A2 and B1.
10. *No epoch dimension.* A registry's live set is one subtree whose
    membership changes by inclusion and clearance, and no identity scalar
    or certified record changes at renewal.
11. *Bands.* An insurer attested at a band writes a face below that band's
    ceiling and is refused one above it; a lower band is implied by a
    higher one; the smallest band admits a face of ten BUCK.
11a. *Scope.* An insurer writes a credit declaring a scope it holds and is
     refused one it does not; a fine scope does not admit a credit
     declaring the coarse one; a permitted depreciation type is accepted
     and an unpermitted one refused; the eight-argument overload declares
     the zero scope and the existing call sites are unchanged.
12. *Delegation.* A verifier requiring only the state regulator refuses a
    delegate's attestation; one requiring the delegation edge accepts it
    while the edge holds and refuses it once the parent clears the
    delegate; a delegate attesting outside its granted scope is refused by
    a verifier that checks the scope subtrees.
13. *Aggregation is auditable.* Recomputing the posted root from the
    published leaf list reproduces it; every leaf carries a valid signature
    from its enrolled authority under an increasing sequence; an omitted
    enrolled authority and a fabricated subtree root are each detected by
    comparing that list against the on-chain enrollment set.
14. *Deployment isolation.* A registration proof made for one registry
    address or chain id verifies in no other, and the same certified person
    in two deployments yields two unrelated identity scalars.

* 16 Decisions taken, and the parameters that remain
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: decisions
:END:

** 16.1 The five questions v0.1 left open

1. *Staleness bound.* Resolved by changing the unit. A count of postings is
   not a bound in time, and one number cannot serve both in-flight tolerance
   and revocation urgency. Root records carry a timestamp and each consumer
   declares a maximum age ([[#root]]).
2. *Epochs in the trees.* Resolved by removing the dimension. One live-set
   subtree per registry, with freshness from the root's timestamp, is
   strictly better than either option v0.1 offered: renewal becomes
   continued inclusion, lapse becomes clearance, and the aggregator's 1024
   slots are spent at the rate of registries rather than registries times
   epochs ([[#renewal]]).
3. *Poseidon against Pedersen.* The question rested on a false premise, now
   corrected in [[#security]]: no commitment gives an
   information-theoretically hidden Merkle leaf. The real choice is
   publication against retroactive exposure, and it is resolved in favour of
   publication, with the withheld mode kept as a supported operational
   variant for a stronger threat model.
4. *The authority's change history.* Resolved with a retention obligation
   rather than acceptance: the current association only, discarded on
   re-association, logs and backups included ([[#renewal]]). The
   zero-knowledge construction that would enforce it is recorded and
   deferred.
5. *Insurer enumeration.* Accepted as intrinsic, since the cached envelope
   is public by necessity and licensee lists are public record. Mitigated by
   dropping the underwriter risk estimate from the envelope and keeping
   bands coarse ([[#insurer]]).

** 16.2 Parameters, set in v0.3

From the expected posting cadences: private records resolve at the
resolution of a day, institutional records weekly, insurance regulators
quarterly now and weekly later.

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|-----------+-------+-------|
| Ring size | 256 records | ten days at an hourly aggregator posting, which covers the longest maximum age |
| Maximum age, Notes membership | 7 days | the gate is a registration check, not a revocation check |
| Maximum age, insurer attestation | 1 day | the aggregator posts at least daily |
| Maximum age, default | 7 days | until a consumer states a reason |
| Re-salting cadence | never on a timer | a timer forces the holder online on the registry's schedule or hands salt choice to the authority, which breaks recovery from seed material |
| Face bands | powers of ten, 1 to 8, cumulative, in BUCK | supports a neighbour underwriting a bicycle and an insurer underwriting a building with one ladder |
| Envelope fields | standing, asset classes, face band, expiry | the underwriter risk estimate is not gated on and stays off chain |
| Retention after clearance | a short correction window, then discard | long enough to reverse an erroneous revocation and no longer |

Two things followed from setting them rather than being parameters:
=AGGREGATOR_DEPTH= rises from 10 to 20 ([[#objects]]), and delegated
authority becomes part of the specification ([[#delegation]]).

** 16.3 What remains open

Nothing of substance. The three items v0.4 listed are settled in v0.5:
the retention window is 90 days ([[#renewal]]), the aggregator publishes
its leaf list as canonical JSON with the document hash emitted beside the
root ([[#aggregator-trust]]), and asset classes need no vocabulary because
the gate checks a declared scope and the credit's own parameters rather
than a taxonomy ([[#scope]]).

What remains is implementation, and the security argument for the whole
accumulator, which has not been written and which phase 6 of
[[file:accumulator-plan.org][the plan]] owes in the same form Part I of
=alberta-buck-proofs.org= owes for A'.

* 17 Deployment, authority and governance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: governance
:END:

** 17.1 One root authority per deployment

A deployment has exactly one root authority. For Alberta Buck that is the
provincial identity authority, which exists because the deployment is
provincial. The protocol treats the root as a single address. Whether that
address is an institution, a multi-signature of several, or a governance
contract is a property of who holds the key, not of the protocol, and this
specification =MUST NOT= be read as claiming the root is decentralized. It
is as decentralized as its holder.

** 17.2 Enrollment is on chain

The root authority enrolls the authorities that may own a subtree, and
=MUST= record on chain, per authority: its identifier, its posting key, its
class (private or public, [[#classes]]), and the scope it may attest.
Eviction =MUST= likewise be on chain.

This is not bureaucracy. An enrollment set that only the aggregator knows
makes the aggregator unaccountable; an on-chain set is what makes omission
provable ([[#aggregator-trust]]).

** 17.3 The aggregator, and what it can do
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: aggregator-trust
:END:

The aggregator combines subtree roots into the root that is posted. It is a
role, appointed by the root authority, and it has exactly two powers worth
naming:

- *Censorship by omission.* Leaving an enrolled authority's latest root out
  of a posting. That authority's members cannot prove membership against
  any fresh root, so their proofs age out and they stop transacting.
- *Forgery by inclusion.* Posting a root built over a subtree root its
  authority never produced, fabricating members of that authority.

Neither is prevented by the structure. Both are made *provable*, which is
the achievable property:

1. An authority =MUST= sign =(sub_root, sequence, posted_at)= with its
   enrolled posting key, and the sequence =MUST= increase.
2. The aggregator =MUST= publish the full list of leaves it combined, each
   with the signature that authorized it, as a document in the canonical
   JSON dialect the receipt envelope already uses: the posting sequence and
   timestamp, then one entry per enrolled authority giving its identifier,
   subtree root, sequence and signature. The posting =MUST= emit the hash
   of that document beside the root, so the aggregator cannot show
   different lists to different readers, and the enrollment record =MUST=
   name where it is published.
3. Enrollment being on chain, anyone can recompute the posted root from
   that list, verify every signature, and check that every enrolled
   authority's latest signed root was included.

A mismatch is then evidence, and the remedy is governance replacing the
aggregator rather than anything the protocol does by itself. The
specification =MUST= state this plainly rather than imply the aggregator is
trustless.

*Alternative considered and rejected.* Each authority posts its own root on
chain, and the registry keeps a root per authority with no aggregator at
all. That removes the trusted role entirely and shortens a membership proof
to the subtree path. It was rejected on two grounds. It costs a transaction
per authority per posting, which does not scale to community authorities
posting daily. More importantly it reveals which authority a member belongs
to, because the verifier must name the root it checks, and authority-hiding
matters most exactly where the anonymity set is smallest, which is a small
community. Aggregation buys that hiding, so the aggregator is retained and
accountability replaces removal.

** 17.4 Delegation rules, and which of them bind

The root authority sets the rules a sub-registry follows, and a
sub-registry sets the rules its own delegates follow ([[#delegation]]).
Rules divide into two kinds, and the specification =MUST= keep them
distinct:

- *Enforceable.* Any rule expressible as membership in a scope subtree:
  the asset classes a delegate may attest, the face band ceiling it may
  grant, and any other predicate of the same shape. A verifier that checks
  the scope subtrees enforces these, and no cooperation from the delegate
  is required.
- *Contractual.* Everything else, such as "attest only members of your own
  community" or "review reserves quarterly". The protocol cannot check
  them, because the delegate owns its own subtree and may insert what it
  likes. The parent's remedy is clearing the delegate; a verifier's remedy
  is declining to accept that delegate at all.

Scope =MUST= live in the parent's subtree, never in the delegate's, so a
delegate cannot widen its own authority.

** 17.5 Many deployments

The same implementation is deployable by any group that wants its own
identity authority and its own money: another province, a nation, or a
community that prefers to define value through the insured assets its own
members are willing to underwrite.

- *Isolation is automatic.* A registration proof already binds the chain
  id and the registry address, so a proof made for one deployment verifies
  in no other. The identity scalar hashes a record that names its issuer,
  so the same person certified by two deployments holds two unrelated
  identities, and there is no cross-deployment linkage to remove because
  none exists.
- *Recognition is policy.* Deployment B accepts deployment A's identities
  only if B's root authority enrolls A's authority, which is the delegation
  mechanism of [[#delegation]] applied at the top. Nothing obliges B to,
  and nothing prevents it.
- *A deployment profile* is what a new deployment =MUST= choose: the root
  authority, the aggregator operator, the depths, the maximum ages per
  consumer, the face-band ladder, the asset-class vocabulary, the attribute
  vocabulary, and the class of every subtree.

** 17.6 What the protocol enforces, and what it does not

The backing of the money is a chain of attestations. A credit's face is an
insurer's claim about an asset; the insurer's authority to make that claim
is a regulator's attestation; the regulator's authority is the root
authority's delegation. The chain is explicit, attributable, and rooted in
one authority per deployment.

The protocol enforces *consistency*:

- an insurer cannot write a credit outside the envelope its regulator
  attested;
- a delegate cannot claim scope its parent did not grant;
- an identity cannot register without a credential it holds;
- no party can test another's identity against public data.

The protocol does not enforce *accuracy*. Nothing in it makes an insurer's
reserves real, a regulator's review diligent, or an attestation honest. A
group whose attestations are accurate ends with a currency backed by assets
that exist. A group whose attestations are not ends with one backed by
claims that do not, and the difference appears as a monetary outcome rather
than as a protocol failure.

That division is deliberate and =MUST NOT= be papered over. The protocol's
job is to make the chain of claims explicit, attributable and internally
consistent, so that the consequences of a group's trustworthiness, or its
absence, fall on the group that chose them.
