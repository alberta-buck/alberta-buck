*Confirmed:* the current registration transcript lets an Identity Issuer
identify a known identity and associate it with a registered account. The
issuer can then follow activity publicly linked to that account. This is not
an algorithm for recovering arbitrary unknown identity records, decrypting
every ciphertext, or reconstructing every hidden Note mint-to-spend edge.

There are several separate problems:

| ID | Remaining problem | Evidence and boundary |
|----+-------------------+-----------------------|
| R1 | Public PS signatures test known identities | Current wallet and Solidity registration reproduced |
| R2 | Registration proof fields independently test known identities | Public fields from mined registrations reproduced; no issuer key needed |
| R3 | Public credential plus disclosed identity authorizes fresh registrations | Current Solidity accepted a synthetic attacker registration |
| R4 | Proposed additive-blinding prototype leaks identity and a reusable credential through its proof | Algebra and executable prototype counterexample; not production code |
| R5 | A1/A2 identity-derived encryption keys are known to the Identity Issuer | Ciphertext equations reproduced; public ABI inspected; no complete Note-spend execution in this review |
| R6 | Issuance logs, policy metadata and accumulator designs can expose additional links | Source-established exposure or conditional design risk, as distinguished below |

No production mitigation is implemented by this document. In particular, the
previous draft's claim that the existing additive-blinding prototype was a
small, verified fix is withdrawn. Blinding the signature pair alone is not
enough: its proof must not reveal the hidden message or remove the blinding.

The intended target remains *certified identity without general surveillance*:
counterparties obtain verifiable evidence of their own relationship, while an
Identity Issuer or past counterparty cannot identify unrelated accounts just
by comparing public transcripts with known identity data.

* Roles, assumptions and public information

** Distinguish the two kinds of issuer

- *Identity Issuer*: the KYC authority signing an identity credential.
- *Identity holder*: the person whose certified record the wallet holds.
- *Account*: an Ethereum address and its account encryption/control key.
- *Counterparty*: a party intentionally given identity data for a relationship.
- *Note issuer*: the payer/minter of a Note, not necessarily the Identity Issuer.
- *Observer*: a reader of chain calldata, events, state and public metadata.

The core attacks require only an honest-but-curious Identity Issuer: it may
issue honest credentials and later inspect the chain. It need not bias keys,
collude with a counterparty, intercept a receipt or compromise a wallet.
A previous counterparty with an exact candidate record can perform the public
tests too. An observer without candidate records has a different capability.

** Notation and assumptions

All scalars are modulo the prime group order =q=. =G= generates BN254 G1;
=G2= denotes the G2 generator. Lower-case =e= is the Fiat--Shamir challenge;
=pairing= denotes the bilinear map. Scalar multiplication is additive notation.

#+begin_example
m = keccak256(canonical_identity_data) mod q
M = m*G
issuer secret = (x, y)
issuer public key = (X, Y) = (x*G2, y*G2)
#+end_example

Candidate tests identify a scalar/record equivalence class, not necessarily a
unique human or issuance event. Distinct records are assumed to have distinct
scalars; normal issuer keys have ~y != 0~; signature first points are nonzero.
Several credentials on the same =m= cannot be distinguished by these tests.
The proof-only attacks below assume ~e != 0~; zero challenges are negligible
under honest Fiat--Shamir generation and do not invalidate the other attacks.

Knowing a high-entropy record is different from guessing an unknown hash.
The Identity Issuer already knows the canonical record and hence =m=; the
hash's preimage resistance is no obstacle to testing that known value.

** Objects and capabilities

| Object | Visibility/capability |
|--------+-----------------------|
| Identity record and =m= | Known to issuer, holder and disclosed-to counterparties |
| ~M = m*G~ | Identity point; known to anyone knowing =m= |
| Credential =sigma= | Initially issued privately; a reusable rerandomization is currently published |
| =sk_a= | Independent account secret, not derived from =m= |
| ~pk_a = sk_a*G~ | Public registered account key |
| ~E_a = (r_a*G, m*G + r_a*pk_a)~ | Public account ciphertext |
| Registration signature and proof | Public transaction calldata, even if not retained in storage |
| Counterparty approval ciphertext | Public ciphertext under the counterparty's independent account key |
| A1/A2 identity-addressed ciphertext | Uses =m_rec*G= as encryption key; =m_rec= is not holder-exclusive |
| Receipt | Off-chain evidence; deliberately reveals designated identities when disclosed |
| Optional holder secret =u= | A possible future certified secret, not a current requirement or settled design |

The intended read/write separation is between disclosed identity information
and a retained authorization capability. A private credential could supply
that capability; a new certified =u= is not the only possible construction.

* What the current protocol establishes

** Issuance and account registration

[[file:../../alberta_buck/wallet/ps.py][PS signing]] issues the single-message credential

#+begin_example
sigma = (h, (x + m*y)*h), h != O
#+end_example

For a new account, the wallet chooses fresh independent nonzero randomness:

#+begin_example
A = t*sigma_1
B = t*sigma_2 = (x + m*y)*A
pk_a = sk_a*G
E_a = (R_a, C_a) = (r_a*G, m*G + r_a*pk_a)
#+end_example

The [[file:../../alberta_buck/wallet/nizk.py][registration prover]] and [[file:../../src/IdentityRegistry.sol][registry verifier]] join these relations:

#+begin_example
(A, B) is a valid issuer signature on m
C_a = m*G + r_a*pk_a
R_a = r_a*G
pk_a = sk_a*G
#+end_example

The last relation now proves control of the advertised account key. The
transcript binds the registrant, chain, registry address and internal domain
=AlbertaBuck/FiatShamir/IdentityRegistry/Register/v2=.

The call publishes =issuer, pk_a, E_a, (A,B), proof=. For ordinary registration
the identified address is =msg.sender=. Credential-based contract binding
uses the target specified by its API, not an assumption that the transaction
sender must be the subject. Operator/adaptor provenance links require separate
interpretation; this review does not attribute every contract to a natural
person simply because a binder appears in calldata.

** Counterparty identity confirmation

Alice encrypts her registered identity for Bob:

#+begin_example
E_A_to_B = (R_b, C_b) = (r_b*G, M_A + r_b*pk_B)
#+end_example

The repaired [[file:../../alberta_buck/wallet/chaum_pedersen.py][approval proof]] establishes with shared witnesses:

#+begin_example
pk_A = sk_A*G
R_b = r_b*G
C_A - sk_A*R_A = C_b - r_b*pk_B
#+end_example

The registry supplies Alice's stored source ciphertext/key. Context includes
registry, parties and chain. Bob decrypts =C_b - sk_B*R_b= and checks that
the privately delivered canonical identity record hashes to the resulting
point. This is a meaningful certified-identity consistency check.

It does not establish issuer-view unlinkability: Alice's public registration
may already identify her account. Nor does it establish that only the
originally enrolled person could register that identity, because R3 remains.

For independently generated account keys, these ciphertexts in isolation
retain the intended ElGamal confidentiality under the relevant G1 DDH
assumption. Knowing =m= does not reveal =sk_A= or =sk_B=. Do not extend that
statement to A1/A2 keys deliberately derived from =m=, or to the privacy of
the complete transcript. Absence of a PS field in an approval dataclass is
not a cryptographic proof of approval-transcript privacy.

** Repairs already present, not solutions to the findings below

The merged PRs added fail-closed proof handling, account-key knowledge,
corrected approval relations, Note flavor/public-issuer binding, and narrowly
audited contract-binding authorization/provenance checks. Nonzero unchecked
caller-supplied identity leaves are rejected. These changes address different
soundness and authorization defects.

They neither hide the registration credential nor change identity-addressed
Note encryption into encryption under holder-exclusive keys. Domain tags
remain useful context separation, not a defense against candidate matching.
No approval nonce is needed to explain or repair the attacks in this document.

* R1: a public signature identifies a known identity
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: r1-signature
:END:

*Status:* reproduced with current wallet verification and public calldata
from accepted local Solidity registrations.

An Identity Issuer retains =(x,y)= and its issued records. For each registration
and candidate =m_i= it evaluates:

#+begin_example
B == (x + m_i*y)*A
#+end_example

The correct candidate matches. With ~A != O~ and ~y != 0~, two distinct
scalars cannot both match. Per candidate this needs a G1 multiplication and
comparison; =x + m_i*y= may be precomputed. No wallet key, original =h=,
rerandomization scalar =t= or ciphertext decryption is needed.

The public-key variant, available to anyone knowing a candidate record, is:

#+begin_example
pairing(A, X + m_i*Y) == pairing(B, G2)
#+end_example

This is ordinary signature verification, not a failure of the PS signature's
unforgeability. Longer identity hashes and repeated rerandomization do not
remove the predicate.

** Precisely what rerandomization hides

For a fixed =m=, multiplying both signature points by uniform nonzero =t=
produces the distribution of a fresh signature on that same =m=. It hides
which original signing randomness produced this presentation. It does not
make signatures on different known messages indistinguishable.

[[file:../../alberta-buck-proofs.org][Theorem 2]] is valid with that fixed-message qualification. The broader
issuer-blindness wording in [[file:../../alberta-buck-paper.org][the paper]] is not supported by this theorem.
An unbounded observer should not be included in claims about hidden identity
messages: discrete-log and other computational assumptions remain necessary.

** Consequence and limit

The issuer can map a registration address to its known identity and repeat
that process for fresh accounts. Later public sender/recipient/event linkage
extends that label to activity involving those addresses. This is not, on its
own, a recovery of every hidden Note opening or mint-to-spend edge, nor proof
that the real person intended each operation attributed to the account.

* R2: the registration proof supplies an independent identity test
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: r2-proof
:END:

*Status:* reproduced using the proof fields decoded from the same accepted
Solidity transactions. This was missing from the initial follow-up review.

The current prover publishes:

#+begin_example
A_ps = m_tilde*A
s_m = m_tilde + e*m
#+end_example

An observer therefore tests candidate =m_i= using only public =A, A_ps, s_m, e=:

#+begin_example
s_m*A == A_ps + (e*m_i)*A
#+end_example

For ~e != 0~ it can equivalently recover the point
~m*A = e^(-1)*(s_m*A - A_ps)~. Recovering the scalar =m= from that point is
not necessary to compare known candidates. Neither issuer key nor =B= is
needed. This is especially relevant when evaluating proposed repairs.

Removing or blinding only the second signature point leaves this predicate
intact. The transcript must be redesigned as a complete hiding presentation
joined to account encryption; individually exposed linear proof components
cannot simply be assumed safe because they are called Schnorr commitments.

This is a concrete leakage result, not a claim that an arbitrary Schnorr
proof is insecure. The published statement and all proof fields determine
what is hidden. Calling a component zero knowledge cannot make a separately
revealed, candidate-testable function of its witness private.

* R3: identity disclosure enables credential harvesting
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: r3-harvesting
:END:

*Status:* current Solidity accepted a fresh synthetic attacker registration.

An attacker who receives Alice's canonical record knows =m=. It takes =(A,B)=
from her public registration, rerandomizes it, chooses its own account secret
and encryption randomness, and creates:

#+begin_example
pk_att = sk_att*G
E_att = (r_att*G, m*G + r_att*pk_att)
sigma_att = rerandomize(A, B)
proof_att = prove(sigma_att, m, r_att, sk_att, attacker_address, context)
#+end_example

It knows every witness the verifier requires. No victim account key, original
private credential or issuer signing secret is required. This is a fresh
proof for the attacker address, not replay of Alice's address-bound proof.
New nonces or additional context fields would not remove this capability.

The result is unauthorized creation of an account carrying the certified
identity. It is not by itself a demonstration of theft from an existing
account or redemption of a Note: those require the relevant authorization,
opening and spend witnesses. The issuer's independent ability to issue a
duplicate credential is a separate enrollment-trust problem.

Mitigation must leave some unforgeable registration capability unavailable to
a receipt holder. A hidden, nonextractable credential presentation is one
route; a certified holder secret is another design ingredient. Merely proving
control of a newly chosen account key is insufficient.

* R4: the existing blinding prototype is not a repair
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: r4-prototype
:END:

*Status:* the review-only [[file:../../scripts/review/issuer_linking_demo.py][issuer-linking demonstration]], Part B, is a
counterexample to a proposed partial repair, not a production implementation.
The reproduction below checks both identity matching and credential recovery.

The prototype changes the public pair to:

#+begin_example
A = t*sigma_1
B_blind = (x + m*y + b)*A
#+end_example

The pair alone can conceal =m= with appropriate independent blinding. However,
the prototype exposes both of these separate commitments and responses
(its code names the blinding scalar =t= and its commitment =A_t=):

#+begin_example
A_ps = m_tilde*A       s_m = m_tilde + e*m
A_b  = b_tilde*A       s_b = b_tilde + e*b
#+end_example

** Identity matching survives

R2 applies unchanged. Testing only the old signature predicates against
=B_blind= and observing zero matches does not test the full transcript.

** Public proof fields undo the blinding

For ~e != 0~, any observer computes:

#+begin_example
bA = e^(-1)*(s_b*A - A_b)
B_plain = B_blind - bA = (x + m*y)*A
#+end_example

The pair =(A,B_plain)= is a reusable ordinary signature. The observer does
not need =b=, =m=, an issuer key or the holder's original signature to recover
it. Once the observer knows =m=, R3 applies again. It can also make a new
showing accepted by the prototype verifier using this recovered signature.

The original demonstration's naive harvester re-blinded =B_blind= directly
and failed. That establishes only failure of that particular strategy; it
does not consider recovery of =bA= from the accompanying proof.

** Conclusions withdrawn from the earlier draft

- The prototype does not remove findings 1 and 2.
- The complete transcript is not shown to hide =m= merely because its pair
  of signature points does.
- The quoted +96 calldata bytes and three-pairing check describe this flawed
  construction, not the cost of a secure mitigation. No repaired cost is
  established here; pairing-term merging alone is not a privacy repair.
- Counting fields in an approval object establishes no general absence of
  other identity predicates.

The source prototype is retained as review evidence. Its optimistic printed
messages must be read with these counterexamples, not as security assertions.

* R5: identity-addressed Note keys permit additional tests
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: r5-note-keys
:END:

*Status:* the following equations were reproduced with the wallet's ElGamal
implementation, and the [[file:../../src/Notes.sol][Notes ABI]] was inspected. This review did not execute a
complete A1/A2 spend with membership and Note-binding circuits. Claims here
apply when the stated ciphertext is public; they are not a claim that all
current Note proof paths were exercised.

This issue survives a repair to registration privacy. The Identity Issuer and
record recipients know =m_rec=, so encryption to ~M_rec = m_rec*G~ is not
encryption under a secret held exclusively by the recipient.

** A1: direct recipient test

[[file:../../alberta_buck/wallet/unilateral_a1.py][A1 minting]] creates:

#+begin_example
eRec = (R, C)
R = r*G
C = M_rec + r*M_rec = m_rec*(G + R)
#+end_example

=spendCoupledA1= takes =eRec= in public calldata. An observer with candidate
records tests ~C == m_i*(G + R)~. This needs no signature, proof or issuer
key. For honestly generated ~R != -G~, distinct scalar candidates cannot
both match. The degenerate ~R = -G, C = O~ case does not give a unique match;
it is negligible with honest randomness and must be considered separately
in any future ciphertext-validation specification.

Thus the public spend can identify the recipient even if registration is
later made issuer-unlinkable. This alone does not establish its mint origin.

** A2: decryption and candidate-pair testing

[[file:../../alberta_buck/wallet/unilateral_a2.py][A2 minting]] creates:

#+begin_example
eIss = (R, C) = (r*G, M_noteIssuer + r*M_rec)
C - m_rec*R = M_noteIssuer
#+end_example

For known candidate sender/recipient lists, test:

#+begin_example
C == m_sender*G + m_recipient*R
#+end_example

A matching pair identifies those candidate roles; arbitrary candidate-pair
uniqueness is not guaranteed as a mathematical identity, although the tested
synthetic example had one match. If only the recipient's =m= is known, the
observer can decrypt an issuer point but may not know which real-world person
it denotes. Knowing only an unknown recipient's public identity point does
not supply its scalar. A KYC issuer covering only one participant must not be
credited with records it does not possess.

=spendCoupledA2= exposes =eIss= in calldata. Where a mint path also publishes
the same ciphertext, byte equality is additional public linkage and must be
audited for that path. This review does not assume every mint API does so.

** B1 and ordinary approvals are different

B1's depositor evidence is encrypted under the Note issuer's registered
account key, not automatically its identity scalar. Knowing the KYC record
does not imply knowing that independent account secret. R1/R2 can still
label visible registered accounts. The current B1 interface also deliberately
exposes an issuance-commitment handle to bind the spend to its public issuer;
that is distinct from recovering hidden A1/A2 links.

** Mitigation direction and API impact

For privacy from parties knowing identity data, use independently generated
receiving keys and prove their association with the certified recipient.
The proof must prevent key substitution and false evidence; simply accepting
any claimed recipient key is not enough.

Options include authenticated interactive key exchange, privately delivered
prekeys, or a reviewed credential-bound key discovery mechanism. A static
public per-person key or directory lookup can introduce its own linkage.

This changes the convenient unilateral API where a payer needs only the
recipient's identity point. Specify key availability, offline recipients,
rotation/recovery and delivery failures explicitly. Addressing, ciphertext
binding, spend proofs and receipt verification may all change; new circuit
artifacts/keys are needed if their statements change. Registration-only PS
work does not automatically require larger A1/A2/B1 circuits, but neither
does it resolve these Note-specific problems.

* R6: metadata, enrollment and accumulator boundaries
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: r6-metadata
:END:

** Issuance logs: source-established operational link

[[file:../../alberta_buck/wallet/issuer.py][The reference issuer]] logs =applicant_addr= alongside the canonical record
and =m= and uses that address for simulated revocation. If that address is a
later public account, the issuer already has a direct link. This observation
does not prove a production enrollment service operates the same way, or that
it identifies every fresh account. Define which enrollment identifiers may
be associated with subsequent accounts before making an address-blind claim.

** Issuer/policy metadata: conditional anonymity-set reduction

Common issuer keys and broad eligibility/epoch groups are necessary if the
issuer/key/policy are public. Per-person keys, serials or unique expiry values
can label a credential independently of signature hiding. Timing, networking,
funding and small anonymity sets need explicit assumptions. These are
additional risks, not prerequisites for the already-reproduced algebraic tests.

** Accumulator admission: separate certification from linkage

Current registration rejects nonzero unchecked =identityLeaf= values. That
fail-closed behavior must be preserved until admission proves the leaf's
relationship to the certified identity.

A public deterministic leaf =Poseidon(M)= permits a known-identity test. If
that leaf is published beside a registration or transaction, it can identify
that association. If only a population of leaves is published, it may reveal
membership without identifying which anonymous member made a transaction.
A root alone is not generally an individual-leaf lookup predicate. The
earlier blanket treatment of all roots/leaves as equivalent should not be used.

Randomized certified commitments and hidden membership/opening proofs are
possible alternatives. Admission authority, binding/hiding assumptions,
witness updates, accepted historical roots and revocation require an explicit
specification. This does not settle the separate membership-join/circuit
defects in finding 5 of the original review.

** Counterexample to the existing blinded-show prototype

The existing demo creates four identities, eight ordinary registrations and
four blinded prototype presentations. It reports that raw signature scans
miss the blinded pairs. Run it with these additional checks: the first scan
uses =A_ps=; the second recovers the plain signature from =A_t=; the final
check creates a fresh accepted prototype presentation from that credential.

#+name: reproduce-prototype-counterexample
#+begin_src sh
nix develop -c env PYTHONPATH=.:core/python BUCK_IDENTITY_BACKEND=py python -u - <<'PY'
import runpy
d = runpy.run_path('scripts/review/issuer_linking_demo.py')
from alberta_buck.wallet.bn254 import G1, ORDER, add, mul, neg, eq
from alberta_buck.wallet.ps import PSSignature, ps_verify
from alberta_buck.wallet.elgamal import elgamal_encrypt

count = 0
for registration in d['bregs']:
    signature, proof = registration['sigma'], registration['proof']
    A, e = signature.sigma_1, proof['e']
    s_m, _, _, s_t = proof['s']
    assert e != 0
    matches = [i for i, m in enumerate(d['kyc'])
               if eq(mul(A, s_m), add(proof['A_ps'], mul(A, (e*m) % ORDER)))]
    assert matches == [registration['truth']]
    # No private t, original credential or issuer key enters this recovery.
    tA = mul(add(mul(A, s_t), neg(proof['A_t'])), pow(e, -1, ORDER))
    recovered = PSSignature(A, add(signature.sigma_2, neg(tA)))
    m = d['kyc'][matches[0]]
    assert ps_verify(d['issuer'].pk_X, d['issuer'].pk_Y, recovered, m)
    count += 1

print('Proof-only identifications and recovered credentials:', count, '/', len(d['bregs']))
sk, r, t = d['R'](), d['R'](), d['R']()
pk = mul(G1, sk)
E = elgamal_encrypt(mul(G1, m), pk, r)
sig, pf = d['blinded_show'](recovered, m, r, pk, E, 0xBAD, sk, t, d['R'])
assert d['blinded_verify'](sig, E, pk, d['issuer'].pk_X, d['issuer'].pk_Y, pf, 0xBAD)
print('Recovered credential creates an accepted fresh prototype showing')

* Mitigation pathways, not a selected implementation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: mitigation-pathways
:END:

** A: complete PS anonymous presentation, joined to account encryption

Pointcheval--Sanders distinguish rerandomizing a signature from anonymously
proving possession. Their Section 6.2 uses two fresh masks; in additive notation:

#+begin_example
A = a*sigma_1
B = a*(sigma_2 + b*sigma_1) = (x + m*y + b)*A
#+end_example

It accompanies this pair with a zero-knowledge proof of the hidden message
and blinding value in the pairing relation. The published pair's distribution
alone is not the proof's security argument. See
[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/525][Pointcheval and Sanders, Short Randomizable Signatures, Section 6.2]].

For BUCK, the same hidden =m= must also appear in the ElGamal/account-key
relations. A reviewed joined proof must replace R2/R4's separately exposed
message/blinding multiples; copying their commitments is not the standard
presentation. Correct binding and hiding must be argued for the entire
transcript, including Fiat--Shamir derivation and auxiliary inputs.

This pathway can retain the one-message PS credential concept. R1--R3 do not
by themselves require changing issuance to multiple attributes, introducing
=u=, adding an accumulator or using a general-purpose SNARK. They do require
fresh authority for credentials whose reusable presentations are already
public; see migration below.

Evaluate how a secure presentation maps to available Ethereum precompiles.
Do not infer a deployable G1-only transcript or gas cost from the abstract
pairing equation. Measure encoding, commitments, transcript hashing, subgroup
and infinity handling, verifier work and cross-backend consistency.

** B: hidden membership in certified randomized commitments

An issuer-authorized accumulator can admit randomized credential commitments.
A joined proof demonstrates membership, knowledge of the opening and
consistency with the account ciphertext/key, without exposing a reusable
signature or deterministic person tag.

This may avoid a credential-signature verification inside each registration
circuit. It adds certified admission, root governance, witness availability,
revocation/history rules and potentially new proving/setup infrastructure.
Randomization must genuinely hide the identity, commitment binding must hold,
and independent proofs must not use mismatched witnesses. It is an alternative
architecture, not an established minimum repair.

** C: optional holder-bound credentials

A wallet-generated secret can be certified as an additional hidden attribute:

#+begin_example
credential = Sign_issuer(m, u, policy)
#+end_example

It may support a clearer holder-authorization/recovery design, provided a
registration proves knowledge of the certified =u= and does not publish a
credential reusable with a substituted =u=. Blind issuance or certified
commitments may keep =u= unknown to the issuer and counterparties.

An undisclosed credential with a sound nonextractable showing can already
block the specific R3 harvester under an honest issuer; a new =u= is therefore
optional for that threat. Conversely, =u= does not automatically stop an
issuer signing a second credential for the same identity with a different
secret. Protection against malicious duplicate enrollment needs additional
trust, policy or independently enforced enrollment constraints.

Define loss, recovery, delegation, rotation and intentional sharing separately.
The issuer knows identity data by design; a receipt must not include reusable
credential material or holder secrets.

** D: independent Note evidence keys and scoped receipts

R5 requires its own key-distribution and proof design. Keep evidence encrypted
to the actual payment parties under authenticated independent keys. A pool or
infrastructure operator should receive only the eligibility/payment proofs
its role requires, unless identity disclosure to that operator is intentional.

Bind receipts to payment, amount, roles, chain/contract context and authenticated
issuance/redemption evidence. Retain encrypted archives and the openings needed
to verify a designated transaction, separately from reusable authorization
secrets. Salt public receipt commitments when predictable identity data would
otherwise permit testing. Availability, historical context and permanent
retention are operational guarantees, not consequences of proof soundness.

A recipient can always publish identity information it legitimately learns.
The target is that this disclosure opens the designated transaction without
providing an account-creation capability or a predicate for unrelated accounts.

** Non-solutions and costs

More rerandomization, longer hashes, new account addresses, new nonces, extra
domain fields, or omitting signatures from storage while keeping calldata do
not remove R1/R2. A blinding value known to the observer, an exposed =bA=, or
a proof exposing =mA= does not repair the full presentation. Changing the
registry implementation or proxy pattern does not erase historical calldata.

No secure gas/proving-size delta is established. Compare candidate protocols
only after their statements, encodings and threat models are fixed. Measure
issuance, wallet/WASM proving, registration gas/calldata/storage, Note circuit
changes where needed, witness maintenance, revocation and recovery costs.

* Security requirements and acceptance plan

** Define the experiments first

For two holders with identical disclosed eligibility/policy information, give
the adversary the Identity Issuer's keys, issuance records and permitted
issuance transcripts, plus the complete public registration/transaction view.
Its ability to identify a challenge presentation should be no better than the
specified negligible advantage, subject to explicit metadata exclusions.

Distinguish honest-but-curious issuance, actively tagging issuance, malicious
duplicate certification, prior counterparties, infrastructure operators and
compromised wallets. Do not make an issuer-secret adversary's authenticity
game trivially impossible by also requiring it to be unable to issue credentials.
Privacy and enrollment authorization are different security goals.

Receipts intentionally opening the challenge transaction cannot be included
while still requiring that transaction to remain anonymous. Define selective
opening: which receipts may be disclosed, what they reveal, and which other
accounts must remain unlinkable. Timing/eligibility exclusions must be stated,
not silently attributed to cryptography.

** Minimum joined registration statement

#+begin_example
exists (m, credential, sk_a, r_a, presentation_randomness, optional_policy_witness):
    credential is valid under an accepted issuer/policy
    holder possesses the credential (and certified u, if that design uses u)
    pk_a = sk_a*G
    E_a = (r_a*G, m*G + r_a*pk_a)
    context authorizes this registrant, chain, registry and protocol version
#+end_example

The public transcript must also hide the relevant witnesses against the
defined observer. Sound equations alone do not supply this property.

- [ ] Reproduce R1--R5 on the legacy paths before changing them.
- [ ] Test the *whole* new transcript, not only the modified signature pair:
  raw-signature scans, R2-style response/commitment scans, and extraction of
  blinding multiples or reusable credentials.
- [ ] Give the harvester all public data and disclosed records; a fresh

- [ ] Test multiple accounts per identity, multiple credentials on one =m=,
  fresh encryption keys, noncanonical scalars/points and degenerate inputs.
- [ ] Reject proofs spliced from different credential, ciphertext, account-key,
  receiving-key and membership witnesses.
- [ ] Test domain/context replay, issuer/policy substitution and version handling.
- [ ] Cover A1/A2 candidate ciphertext tests after changing receiving-key design;
  separately preserve B1's intended public-issuer semantics.
- [ ] Test certified accumulator admission, historical roots, witness recovery
  and public metadata for the selected architecture.
- [ ] Check receipt disclosure does not release unrelated decryption or
  registration authority; test authenticated anchoring and offline verification.
- [ ] Run honest issuance, repeated registration, counterparty confirmation,
  recovery/renewal and applicable full Note lifecycle controls.
- [ ] Establish Python, Rust, JavaScript/WASM and Solidity accept/reject parity;
  rebuild matched circuit/verifier artifacts when circuit statements change.
- [ ] Obtain a security argument and independent cryptographic review. Failed
  dictionary attacks and passing examples are not proofs of unlinkability.

** Suggested sequence

1. Correct claims and label the current prototype as insufficient. Preserve
   existing soundness/authorization repairs and adversarial evidence.
2. Specify the smallest complete hidden-presentation design; separately decide
   whether holder secrets, accumulator changes and malicious-issuer resistance
   are requirements. Do not infer these choices from R1 alone.
3. Specify independent Note receiving keys and their delivery/proof lifecycle
   before promising issuer privacy for A1/A2.
4. Implement a synthetic executable specification and hostile full-transcript
   tests, then have the construction reviewed.
5. Port the reviewed relation across backends, benchmark it, and specify
   migration before changing production acceptance rules.

* Migration, compatibility and unresolved choices

Historical public signatures and proof fields remain testable. A Diamond
upgrade, new verifier or new ciphertext randomness cannot make that history
private. Already exposed reusable credentials remain authorization material
unless the repaired acceptance policy excludes them.

Issuing another signature on the same =m= under the same accepted key is not
enough to distinguish a legitimate holder from an attacker holding a harvested
signature. Consider a new accepted key/credential epoch, additional certified
holder binding, or another reviewed re-enrollment/conversion rule. The rule
must authenticate the holder independently of already-public capabilities.

Avoid publishing a direct old-account/new-account migration link when new
account privacy is required. Individually timed conversion can still link
accounts operationally; batching and broad eligible sets may be needed.
Document coexistence and retirement of legacy accounts honestly.

Credential/proof version changes must be distinguished from ordinary
API-compatible registry upgrades. Nothing in these attacks establishes a need
for existing Notes to fail merely because an IdentityRegistry implementation
is updated behind a stable interface. Specify historical verification and
legacy Note redemption behavior explicitly, alongside any intentionally new
credential policy or proof domain.

Open decisions include the threat model for duplicate issuance/tagging,
credential loss and recovery, unilateral/offline receiving-key discovery,
revocation without stable person tags, witness-service privacy, acceptable
metadata leakage and which old Notes/accounts can retain which guarantees.
These are not settled by the prototype or by this review.
