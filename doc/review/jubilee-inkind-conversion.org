#+TITLE: Jubilee keeper sales and in-kind BUCK conversion
#+DATE: 2026-10-30
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* Settlement and reserve sales

The Jubilee fund holds one adjustable basket receipt. A credit reduction or
renewal does not redeem that receipt. Instead, it calculates the selected
share of net fund cash plus the receipt's marked value, subject to the
facility's principal, the owner's minimum relief and a 10% net-cash limit.
The [[file:jubilee-marked-relief.org][marked-relief note]] describes that calculation and its valuation risks.

A separate =redeemScheduled= transaction lets governance or its keeper
replenish a reserve that is below target. The keeper specifies the exact
maximum shares, an overall minimum net BUCK result and per-constituent price
limits/minima. Since a future sale price cannot be known exactly, a bounded
sale may overshoot the reserve target; the existing investment schedule can
subsequently invest the excess. A basket exit that is under water does not
bypass its existing guard. A nonzero overall minimum rejects a zero-return sale.

* Atomic TOKEN conversion

A basket exit may return TOKEN rather than BUCK. The keeper provides a route
through the relevant constituent pool for every newly received TOKEN. The fund
snapshots each TOKEN balance before the sale so that unrelated existing
inventory cannot be spent. It measures the fund's net BUCK balance before and
after the sale and conversion; a Carrying pool's nominal transfer is not
mistaken for full-value proceeds. Missing routes, inadequate output or partial
conversion revert the whole keeper transaction, leaving receipt shares and
TOKEN balances unchanged.

A V3 callback accepts payment only from the exact active pool, for the
corresponding TOKEN and no more than the newly received amount. It must consume
all of that amount or roll the sale back. The outer reentrancy lock remains
active, and the self-call used to isolate the sale can only be entered by the
fund during that locked operation. Previously accumulated in-kind inventory is
not implicitly attributed to a new sale and requires an explicit disposition
in any migration.

Tests exercise a real V3 in-kind exit, zero and partial proceeds, bad routes,
price limits, Carrying pool fees, callback forgery, multi-asset atomicity and
fee-on-transfer rollback. The marked-value repayment path is tested separately
and does not perform these trades. Market impact, live oracle manipulation,
illiquid exits, historical in-kind inventory and differing market execution
still require broader simulation and audit; a mark is not a guaranteed
liquidation value.
