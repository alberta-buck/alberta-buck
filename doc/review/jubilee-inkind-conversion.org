#+TITLE: Jubilee in-kind redemption and BUCK conversion
#+DATE: 2026-10-29
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* The issue

The equity basket can return some TOKEN rather than BUCK when a receipt is
redeemed. The current experimental fund counts only net BUCK as relief and
retains the TOKEN. If that TOKEN is later converted or otherwise appreciated,
a subsequent claimant can receive its value even though it was paid out of the
earlier claimant's proportional redemption. The earlier claimant receives no
BUCK for it. This ordering effect must be addressed before relying on the fund
with a real multi-asset basket.

* Implemented prototype

Use the actual basket constituent pool to convert only TOKEN received by this
particular redemption. The facility owner supplies a per-constituent V3 price
limit and a minimum *net* BUCK receipt, and retains the existing minimum total
relief bound. The fund snapshots each constituent TOKEN balance before the
sale, so pre-existing inventory cannot be spent for this claimant. It measures
the fund's net BUCK before and after all transfers and conversions; pool
nominal transfers are not mistaken for full-value receipts. The actual net
proceeds, plus the selected share of pre-sale net cash, determine relief.

The sale and conversions run in an external self-call, so failure to find
a route for any newly received TOKEN can roll back just that sale and preserve
all its shares. The ordinary reduction and renewal revert when a TOKEN needs a
route; the owner may instead explicitly call =reduceCashOnly= or
=renewCashOnly= and accept their share of available pre-sale cash. This leaves
the unredeemed shares in the fund for remaining claims, so the owner choosing
cash-only also gives up any later recovery of those shares. A separate
deferred entitlement would be needed to preserve that future upside. An explicit price or
minimum-output failure should revert the owner's entire transaction rather
than silently weakening their bound. No low-K basket check is bypassed.

A V3 callback must accept payment only from the exact basket pool currently
being used, for the selected constituent TOKEN, no more than the newly received
amount. Consume all of that amount or roll back the sale: a partial swap must
not strand residual TOKEN and allocate its later value to another facility.
Keep the fund's outer reentrancy lock, and accept self-call entry only from the
fund itself during that locked action. Any old in-kind inventory from the
prototype is not retroactively attributed to a new claimant; production
migration needs an explicit disposition for such assets.

The prototype tests a real V3 in-kind exit, partial and zero proceeds, bad
routes and price limits, Carrying pool fees, callback forgery, multi-asset
atomicity, rollback and renewal. A simulated fee-on-transfer asset demonstrates
rollback and cash-only deferral; equal claims are tested in both orders under
identical execution. A no-route sale deferred without losing shares is
distinct from a successful TOKEN-to-BUCK sale that delivers zero BUCK.
Market impact, real fee-on-transfer pools and different execution across claim
orders need broader simulation and audit. Per-owner price bounds protect the
caller's trade but do not provide a protocol-wide market-impact policy.
