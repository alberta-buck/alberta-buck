#+TITLE: Migrating the household economy to net demurrage
#+DATE: 2026-10-29
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* Status and purpose

The supported five-year economy and both interactive worlds currently deploy
the *legacy signed-balance* BUCK token. They do not exercise =BuckNet=,
=JubileeFund=, insurer-specific pools or the new facility settlement model.
Passing their tests or acceptance criteria does not validate those contracts.
The new cash-only equity-basket integration proves useful plumbing but does
not reproduce the leveraged basket machine used by the mature economy. Keep
the old economy runnable and clearly identified until its replacement has
equivalent coverage; do not remove its distinct shock or exit tests yet.

Python and JavaScript can now link the two =BuckNet= libraries from built
Foundry creation-bytecode metadata. =make nix-venv-net-demurrage-smoke=
uses those linkers in isolated Anvil and Tevm fixtures, issues credit, ages it
and reduces it; the Python path compares against the demurrage reference.
The cash-only fixtures include a neutral-reference dynamic K, separate insurer
pools and nonzero premiums. An opt-in JavaScript diagnostic world uses the real
identity registry and verifies its own saved wiring and captured code hashes on
restore. Python/Anvil and JavaScript/Tevm can seed real BUCK/USDC V3 markets and report an aged pool's
actual net BUCK output from the token's =NetValueTransfer= event. An
experimental one-pool guard enforces minimum-net and exact-net buys atomically;
it does not provide a multi-hop route or establish a fair market price.
Both market fixtures verify that a purchase does not retire principal until
the owner authorizes and settles a particular NFT.
There is not yet a migrated household economy or leveraged basket.
The actual economy, interactive sandbox, production linking and full sandbox
snapshot paths remain unmigrated.

The authoritative implementation checklist is
[[file:demurrage-implementation-plan.org][the demurrage plan]]. The unresolved leveraged-basket valuation and liquidity
choice is described in [[file:basket-jubilee-crossholding.org][the cross-holding analysis]]. No five-year acceptance
campaign should be presented as evidence for the new contracts until the
deployment, agents, telemetry and basket have all been migrated and the
acceptance vectors below pass. [[file:net-demurrage-invariants.org][The transition invariants]] specify the contract
boundaries; the [[file:net-demurrage-interface-migration.org][core, Notes and proof migration plan]] covers the wider client,
proof, artifact and receipt work that must accompany this migration.

The shared refinancing comparison accepts explicit BuckNet forecasts for an
annual effective relief rate, the premium refund's expected initial net value
as a fraction of its nominal amount, the refund claim's annual net growth,
and the expected annual return of the wallet money's alternative use.
It counts relief only on newly issued principal and the premium, not on money
already in the ordinary wallet. An estimated loss of refund value is a
one-time cost. The marked refund's opportunity cost and expected growth, plus
the return foregone or demurrage avoided by spending wallet money, enter
annual carry. A net-model state must supply all four forecasts, an explicit
estimate of activated, unissued credit capacity and its shared issuance
limit; it may not silently inherit the legacy 2% assumptions or assume that
this capacity is zero. Legacy scenarios retain their prior defaults and the
same policy comparisons.

These are *decision forecasts*, not guarantees or settlement quotes. A future
net household adapter must explain how it derives and updates them from the
fund's marked assets and capped claims, the insurer's mixed Carrying pool and
any disclosed strategy. The shared operations can sample a single block's
authoritative =previewRelief= and selected claim seconds, annualizing their
ratio without implementing another basket valuation. A fresh claim has no
observable rate; a quote capped at principal yields only a lower bound; and
the current 10% liquid-cash refusal is reported separately. A closed,
no-return 100-BUCK example observes about 1.9801% after one year, not the 2%
linear target. These observations are not future returns.

For a proposed premium, the shared operations also estimate the new refund
fraction from the insurer pool's current gross/net BUCK account and the
full-value proposed deposit. This includes dilution from the deposit, but it
does not mark investments held elsewhere or predict what an insurer may
redeem before a refund; callers must label it as a cash-only observation. An
unknown or invalid insurer pool is not a zero-value refund. Funding reserve,
maturity, per-NFT issuance,
mark unavailability and the 10% liquid-relief limit still require separate
checks at execution. The simple annualized comparison does not yet forecast
claim saturation, future liquidity or nonlinear returns over the holding
horizon; run sensitivity comparisons rather than treating one estimate as
the actual settlement rate.

The new =BuckNet.issue= requires a selected NFT, activated coverage and desired
new spendable amount; insurance funding and K checks must use the actual
contract rules. The optional [[file:../../src/BuckNetQuoter.sol][BuckNetQuoter]] reads one NFT's current activated
coverage, outstanding principal, owner balance, K and funding factor. Its
=quoteIssue= returns total present coverage *after* the proposed activation,
the new full-value insurer premium, the pre-issuance BUCK reserve and the
maximum new spendable BUCK within current numerical limits. The reserve is
held, not spent. It is an estimate: a positive capacity does not imply that
the owner has the reserve or that a valid insurer pool, current claim book,
configured fund and unchanged K will be available at execution. Users must
also check =maturitiesPending= and handle transaction refusal. The cash-only
diagnostic world deploys this quoter, but no five-year household economy or
interactive sandbox has been migrated to it yet.

For the next household issuance integration, check each NFT's already
activated capacity with =quoteIssue(id, 0)= before activating more coverage.
The decision kernel now accepts activated, unissued facility capacity as a
separate input: it is new issuance eligible for future relief, not wallet BUCK
already issued, and it does not require a second premium deposit. Real Anvil
and Tevm tests exercise a ~coverage=0~ issue and verify that the premium and
activated coverage do not change. The isolated household state now obtains
that input from per-NFT quotes at a single block, applying shared account and
total-supply numerical limits once. It sets new issuance capacity to zero
while a maturity backlog blocks the claim book, while retaining spendable
wallet funds. Quotes are advisory: K is recomputed on issue, so re-quote the
selected facility before execution. The complete household world is still not
migrated. The current comparison uses
wallet BUCK first; evaluating whether to retain it and issue instead when its
alternative return is better is a separate policy choice to test.

The activated-capacity quote requires a verified non-Carrying owner and
distinct NFTs owned by that account. It reads the owner, each zero-coverage
quote, facility age and present asset mark, scaled account balances, total
issuance and the maturity gate at one block. Its potential capacity is at most
both the sum of individual quotes and the account/supply limit; its usable
capacity is zero if maturities are pending. It also reports the current value
of coverage not yet activated, excluding facilities whose issuance has reached
the 50-year limit. The policy bounds new spendable issuance *and the newly
issued premium principal* by the same shared limit. This uses the current
account room conservatively; a prior wallet sale or premium transfer may
change it. None of these properties promises that a later issue will succeed.
Quote additional coverage on the selected NFT, budget the full premium and a
reserve buffer that can survive a block of demurrage, and compare the total
cost of the reserve purchase with the projected benefit. A successful issue
increases principal by spendable issuance *plus* premium; the latter goes to
the insurer pool. The reserve remains the household's spendable BUCK. Quote
again immediately before submission and preserve the transaction receipt:
coverage, K, balances and the fund's maturity state can change between calls.
An interrupted issue or sale must stop and reconcile just like repayment.

Do not assume that quote capacity is strictly increasing with added coverage.
The premium rate can approach the current K, and separate integer rounding of
the K limit and premium can produce small local decreases. A coverage search
must either prove its bounds for the actual rates and rounding or conservatively
report that it cannot size the proposed issue. Test existing capacity, low K,
depreciation, rounding boundaries, partial funding and multiple NFTs against
the real quoter before replacing the legacy household activation path.

To retire, the owner authorizes a specific NFT/principal and minimum net
relief, then invokes =JubileeFund.reduce=. Relief uses the facility's share of
net liquid cash plus the marked receipt, and reverts if it exceeds 10% of
pre-settlement net cash. The borrower does not supply a swap route and no
Jubilee position is sold in that transaction. A separate keeper sale accepts
bounded conversion routes as described in [[file:jubilee-inkind-conversion.org][the in-kind note]]. Failures,
unavailable marks, insurer liquidity and unsettled maturities must remain
visible; they cannot be silently turned into an imagined payment. At 50 years
of pooled average age, =closeMatured= releases the NFT without spending the
owner's BUCK and transfers unburned issuance to Jubilee's deferred obligation.

The shared Python =NetCreditOps= and JavaScript =netCreditOps= now provide a
household facility summary and a one-NFT reduction plan. The summary reads the
ordinary wallet once and sums only the supplied, owner-verified NFTs; duplicate
and foreign IDs are errors. The plan caps principal against the latest actual
fund liquidity and the owner's spendable balance less a caller-supplied raw
BUCK buffer. It budgets against a minimum acceptable marked relief, not an
ideal claim, and does not assume the nominal premium refund will be full net
value. These are advisory reads; timestamps, callbacks and prices may change
before execution. The plan refuses to overwrite an existing authorization.
Settlement returns principal, actual scaled relief and nominal premium from
the authoritative event; the recorded owner debit excludes the refund. If the
second transaction fails or its outcome is uncertain, inspect the on-chain
authorization before retrying or canceling. No agent should count a purchase,
an authorization or a failed settlement as paid-down principal. These
primitives also provide ordered household retirement across several facilities:
each NFT is planned afresh and successful transaction receipts are kept if a
later settlement becomes pending. A later planning or preflight read failure
also returns the earlier completed receipts and a visible failure status. The
household retains its buyback reserve if a follow-up balance read fails and
waits for a later turn to reread state before spending again. Custom transaction
dispatchers must preserve submitted transaction hashes and signal uncertain
outcomes as required by the operations; an unknown submitted transaction must
not be represented as an ordinary preflight failure. A partial batch is not an exit. These
primitives still need to be wired into both existing household agents and all
exit paths; they are not an economic simulation by themselves.

One part of that wiring is now exercised in isolation: the existing adaptive
household's discount buyback can buy through the net-output guard and settle
the actual selected NFT in Python and JavaScript. The guard enforces a net
average purchase below basket parity, the repayment preserves BUCK held before
the purchase, and counters increase by the principal actually settled. If a
limited settlement leaves acquired BUCK, the next discount decision tries
that balance before buying more; the pre-purchase reserve is held at a
conservative fixed floor. A pending authorization stops further purchases and
is visible for reconciliation or cancellation. The household also retains the
selected NFT, principal, minimum relief and deadline when a settlement's outcome is uncertain:
an absent authorization can mean the transaction succeeded, so it cannot be
treated as a failure or canceled blindly. Cancellation after settlement is permitted
only while the same authorization is still present and its settlement is known
to have reverted; a matching authorization alone does not prove that a
submitted settlement will not mine. A guarded purchase with an uncertain
outcome also stops subsequent purchases until its receipt is reconciled. The JavaScript
=netHouseholdRepaymentOps= provides just these operations for a registered
holder with the market handshake and settlement allowance already in place;
proxy simulations must supply their own dispatch. Automatic retry and
persistence of this reserve across snapshots and its
handling when prices move to a premium are still required. Pending NFT and
principal, completed events and any transaction identifier must also survive
snapshots for reliable recovery. Submitted hashes and known-revert status now
reach the household. A caller can explicitly reconcile a saved hash against
the mined receipt and the matching guarded-purchase or selected-facility event;
successful repayments are recorded once, and confirmed buybacks become funds
available to retire debt. If the response to the *authorization* was lost,
the batch records that distinct phase and any earlier successful settlements.
Reconciliation requires the exact authorization event and still-matching
on-chain terms; it records no repayment. A known reverted authorization can
be cleared only after verifying no authorization is currently present. If
the authorization did succeed, it can be canceled after that
receipt and state are verified, subject to the single-dispatcher assumption
below. A missing receipt or transaction hash leaves the stop in place. The
household's daily turn now tries to reconcile a known purchase, authorization
or settlement receipt before performing any other action. A successful
reconciliation consumes that turn, and failed lookups remain stopped. While
stopped, income and external-debt payments are delayed; the existing monthly
accounting catches up when the household resumes. A long unresolved outage
therefore needs a separate treatment before it can model payment defaults. In
the single-dispatcher simulation, a confirmed authorization or reverted
settlement is canceled on a later turn after its exact terms are rechecked.
That cancellation has its own pending hash and must be reconciled against its
matching event and current on-chain state before any retry. A mined revert can
be retried; a missing hash remains stopped. An expired authorization with an
unknown earlier outcome is not automatically assumed safe to cancel.
The isolated tests assume a single dispatcher and immediate finality. Real
finality/reorganization handling, hash provenance across restores and
persistence have not yet been implemented.

The current Tevm snapshot saves chain state and time, *not transaction history
or logs*. A transaction hash alone therefore cannot recover a receipt after a
restore. Before enabling net-household saves, either retain and authenticate the
necessary receipts and chain provenance or refuse a snapshot while an outcome
is unresolved. Never restore an uncertain action as though it had failed.

The diagnostic assumes a single transaction dispatcher for each household.
With concurrent clients, another settlement may be submitted under the same
authorization after an earlier attempt reverted. Exact field matching and the
earlier revert alone do not rule that out: serialize the owner's transactions
and account for pending nonces and finality before allowing cancellation or
automatic retry in a real interface.

This does *not* enable the existing household world for =BuckNet=: setup,
issuance, the savings basket, full financial marks, asset exits, populations
and snapshot/restore have not been ported. The unsupported setup, issuance,
marks and exit paths fail explicitly in net mode. Do not remove these guards or
label the partial action as a complete agent until every lifecycle path and
its failure handling have been tested on the new contracts.

* Acceptance before retiring the legacy economy

- [ ] A deployed and bootstrapped household world names =BuckNet= and its
  separate, verified Carrying fund in the vector and demonstrates the intended
  leveraged basket under the chosen valuation policy. The five-year report
  exposes that provenance and actual relevant contract addresses.
- [ ] Python and JS consume common deterministic decision and operation
  vectors covering refinance, invest, discount buyback and explicit per-NFT
  retirement. Compare real balances, principal, premium, claim and cash flows,
  rather than only comparing decisions made from fabricated inputs.
- [ ] An idle million-BUCK facility has zero ordinary held BUCK, spendable
  issued-but-unspent BUCK, and accruing account demurrage and facility relief.
  A transfer and a market purchase do not retire it; an authorized reduction
  does. Two NFTs belonging to one owner retain independent ages.
- [ ] Real insurer pools receive their own full-value deposits, refund nominal
  amounts carrying their average demurrage, and restore their own liquidity or
  fail atomically. In-place renewal needs both owner and insurer consent and
  cannot worsen a pre-existing K shortfall.
- [ ] A Jubilee investment uses one expandable receipt. Marked relief, the
  10% cash limit, Carrying gross-up, independent keeper sales, real
  zero/partial proceeds, bounded in-kind conversion and maturity backlog are
  exercised and reported. Deferred matured issuance and insurer refunds,
  cash shortages and their effects on later claimants are visible.
- [ ] Market shocks include a discounted-BUCK buyback that explicitly retires
  actual obligations, reduces issuance and provides the expected demand/K
  response. Low-K exits remain guarded; households retry or report a genuine
  shortfall instead of being marked exited or receiving invented proceeds.
- [ ] A short working-tree smoke run and cross-language tests pass before
  fresh, frozen five-year baseline, policy and stress campaigns. Every
  completed run saves JSON and a readable adjacent PDF; the campaign index
  links both, records the source and distinguishes incomplete runs.
- [ ] Compare the retained stress and control-lab detections against their
  new counterparts. Remove an old actor, contract or scenario only after its
  unique checks have a demonstrated replacement, and record that evidence.

The modeling and computational costs of an agent that submits many individual
NFT transactions, calls real routing and processes claim maturities should be
measured during the smoke run. Arrival rates and heterogeneous thresholds must
remain explicit; approximations must not bypass owner authorization, actual
funding or observable contract failures.

* Ordered implementation and shared agent invariants

- [ ] Implement the selected nominal Carrying-service/refund semantics and
  verify actual net value at ordinary recipients; account for the intended
  neutral-service allowlist. Resolve the leveraged basket's cross-holding and
  exit valuation. A cash-only diagnostic may be run in parallel, but it cannot
  be labeled as a replacement for the leveraged five-year economy. Keep the
  existing low-K guard intact.
- [ ] Construct one explicit net-model deployment per runtime: actual linked
  token, credit, registry, dynamic K, verified external fund, one net-mode
  basket receipt, per-insurer pools and any quoter/keeper dependencies. Bind,
  authorize and verify each address in the same order as the contracts require.
  Version this graph in Python/JS snapshots and report provenance; fail closed
  if a restore names the wrong token, pool, library, chain or accounting model.
- [ ] Adapt the *existing shared* household policies instead of creating a
  second competing economic hypothesis. Preserve adaptive default and explicit
  always-invest, always-retire and other approved comparisons, randomized
  individual thresholds and arrival rates. Read actual per-NFT principal and
  age, full-value spendable balances and premium/coverage separately.
- [ ] Exercise refinancing as actual issuance followed by real BUCK-to-USDC
  routing and mortgage payment. Fund any required reserve before issuing;
  distinguish full-value premium paid and nominal refund. For surplus, invest
  in the basket or explicitly purchase BUCK and retire selected liabilities
  according to the policy and net trading costs. A wallet purchase alone must
  never reduce an NFT principal or be reported as an exit.
- [ ] Make the fund's observable keeper actions explicit: bounded maturity
  batches, cash checkpoints, scheduled investment, guarded independent sales,
  deferred burns and insurer refund retries. Report mark failures, stale-price
  guards, 10% cash-cap refusals, low-K exit failures, principal/deferred dust,
  actual proceeds and recovery. Retry or report a genuine shortfall; never
  invent a sale, cash or relief to keep a run moving.
- [ ] Drive the Python and JS paths through common deterministic operation
  vectors and actual EVM calls: issue, market sale, price shock, discounted
  buyback, per-NFT retirement, savings deposit/withdrawal, renew, maturity,
  refusal and retry. Compare real account/fund net and nominal balances,
  outstanding principal, insured premium, claim seconds and money flows.
  Supplement policy parity with these contract-state comparisons.
- [ ] In both sandboxes, show each investor's actual remaining basis, marked
  position and ROI, plus relevant facility liability and fund availability.
  Version and restore saved account/receipt state, authorization, clock, agent
  random streams and active keeper state. Notes and proof migration must meet
  the independent checklist, not be inferred from an economy run.
- [ ] Run a small working-tree smoke with errors and configuration visible;
  then run frozen source five-year baseline, adaptive and explicit policy
  comparisons plus agreed shocks. Every completed five-year run must preserve
  its JSON even if rendering fails and produce an adjacent PDF on success;
  the campaign index names model, contracts, seeds, configuration and both
  files, and flags incomplete/render-failure cases for repair.
- [ ] Compare the distinct existing shock, routing, low-K and diagnostic
  detections with the new suite. Remove legacy agents, scenarios or contracts
  only when replacement coverage is demonstrated and recorded.
