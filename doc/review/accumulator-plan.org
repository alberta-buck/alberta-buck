* Purpose and status
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: purpose
:END:

Branch =feature/identity-accumulator=, cut from the merge of
=feature/a-prime= into =feature/paper-editing=. The A' registration
presentation is done and merged; its plan and security argument are
[[file:a-prime-plan.org][the A' plan]] and Part I of
=alberta-buck-proofs.org=. This document is the plan of record for the
program that follows: how a certified identity enters the accumulator that
Notes membership proofs and credit issuance are checked against, without
handing anyone a test for an identity scalar.

The design was decided on 2026-09-20 and is recorded in the A' plan's
accumulator section; it is restated here with the decisions taken since,
and extended with the blast radius the change carries.

Deferred behind this program, deliberately: R5, the identity-derived A1 and
A2 receiving keys. Its architecture is now settled and prototyped in
[[file:notes-receiving-key.org][the receiving-key architecture]] -- notes
addressed to an independent receiving key, bound to the identity inside
this program's salted leaf so the binding is never public -- and it is the
implementation that waits, because the leaf it binds into is built here.
The circuit work of finding 5 is not deferred; it shares a trusted setup
with the leaf change and is sequenced with it, and the note-binding
substitution R5 needs belongs in that same rebuild.

* Design of record
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: design
:END:

Four decisions, whose reasoning is in the A' plan and is not repeated:

1. *Salted leaves where membership is private.* The leaf becomes
   =Poseidon(M.x, M.y, salt)=, one salt per identity per tree, exchanged in
   the attestation the authority already performs. Admission needs no proof
   and no gas. No authority can scan another's tree, and a party holding
   identity scalars can scan none.
2. *Admission stays off the registration path, permanently.* =register= and
   =bindContract= keep refusing a nonzero caller-supplied leaf. The leaf is
   the authority's attestation and the authority is the certifier.
3. *Feature checks are proofs the holder produces, not lookups*, captured
   in the receipt at the time of the payment where a counterparty needs
   durable evidence.
4. *One hiding generator everywhere*, with no known logarithm, retiring
   =H_SCALAR=.

Two decisions taken since, each of which changes the work materially.

** Salt only where membership is private

The privacy constraint applies to persons, not to institutions whose
membership is a fact they advertise. An insurer's standing, its permitted
asset classes and its limit band are public claims it wants counterparties
to verify. A deterministic leaf in a regulator's subtree is therefore
correct rather than a leak: the only thing it lets an observer test is a
fact the insurer publishes.

The rule: *salt where membership is private, do not where it is
advertised.* Consequences:

- Person-facing subtrees (identity registries, age, residence) carry salted
  leaves and are proven in zero knowledge.
- Institutional subtrees (insurance regulators, and any future public-role
  authority) carry deterministic leaves and can be proven with a plain
  Poseidon Merkle proof, which the registry can already verify on chain
  through =identityPoseidon=. No circuit, no trusted setup, no salt
  plumbing on that path.

** Lifetime-stable identity without regenerating every vector

The naive reading of "the identity scalar must be stable for life" is to
remove =epoch= from the certified record. That would change every
identity scalar in the repository, hence every identity point, leaf,
ciphertext, proof and fixture: ten vector files pin a scalar directly, and
the Notes end-to-end and mint fixtures are pinned to wallet-generated notes
derived from them.

It is unnecessary. Nothing validates =epoch=: =canonical_identity_data=
canonicalizes whatever mapping it is given and imposes no schema, and the
only code that mentions epochs is issuer documentation describing renewal
as a policy. So the field stays where it is and its meaning changes:

| Field | Meaning today | Meaning under this design |
|-------+---------------+---------------------------|
| =epoch= in the certified record | the credential's validity window, bumped at renewal, so the scalar changes | the epoch of first certification, never bumped, so the scalar is stable for life |
| validity, liveness, revocation | implied by the record's epoch | membership in the current-epoch registry subtree |

Renewal stops being a re-issuance and becomes a subtree update. Revocation
is removal from the current-epoch subtree, which
=registry/feature_authority.py= already implements by clearing the leaf and
pushing a fresh subtree root. Vector churn: none. Work: the renewal and
revocation ceremonies, and the passages in =alberta-buck-identity.org= that
describe epoch expiry.

Every other mutable fact follows the same pattern. Addresses, contact
points and anything else the holder can change live in external records
under the holder's control, and the registry's response to a change is
*re-association, not re-issuance*: the same identity is inserted into the
subtree for the new value and cleared from the old. One consequence to
record in the specification: the authority that attests a mutable fact
accumulates a change history for every identity it attests, which the
identity issuer never sees. A fresh salt on each re-association unlinks the
old and new leaves for anyone who later obtains tree contents but not that
authority's own records.

* The insurance regulator as a periodic attestor
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: regulator
:END:

Decided 2026-09-20: *a periodic attestor with coarse bands*, not live
infrastructure in the issuance path.

Insurer oversight is already a periodic review of underwriting quality,
portfolio risk, reserves attestation and reinsurance levels, and it already
concludes with an estimate of the size and risk of the new contracts the
insurer may write. None of that is novel to this system. What the
accumulator adds is making the conclusion available to contracts: an
insurer in current good standing may issue credits consistent with the
constraints of its current identity, and =BuckCredit= validates that
against the regulator's subtree before instantiating.

The authority envelope an attestation carries:

- standing: the insurer is licensed and currently in good standing;
- asset classes it may underwrite;
- a per-contract face band, coarse by decision, so the on-chain check is a
  bound rather than a balance, on a ladder of powers of ten from ten BUCK
  to a hundred million, cumulative, so that a neighbour underwriting a
  bicycle and an insurer underwriting a building sit on one scale.

Out of scope for this design, by decision: the assumption of existing asset
pools, contracts and company assets on the failure of an insurer by its
upstream reinsurers. The design assumes instead that an insurer's soundness
includes pools that will safely pay the expected rate of underwritten
payouts with a buffer, and that reinsurance triggered by an insurer's
failure, together with its removal from the regulator's subtree as an
issuer, includes topping those pools up where necessary to maintain future
payouts.

** Why this makes the credit gate cheap

=createCredit= has 89 call sites across 38 files, including twenty Foundry
suites, four simulation modules, five JavaScript files and seven design
documents. Adding a proof argument to its signature would touch all of
them.

The periodic-attestor decision removes the need. The insurer proves its
envelope once per review period through a separate entry point, which
verifies a Poseidon Merkle proof against the regulator's subtree root and
caches the envelope with an expiry. =createCredit= then performs a storage
read against the credit's own asset class, face and premium, with an
unchanged signature. Per-credit cost is a handful of SLOADs rather than ten
Poseidon calls or a pairing, the 89 call sites are untouched, and the
fixtures gain one authorization line in =setUp=, exactly as they already
gain the client's =setCreditIssuer= opt-in.

* Blast radius
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: blast-radius
:END:

Seven interfaces, surveyed on 2026-09-20 at the merge commit. The summary:
the leaf function is the expensive one, the record schema is the one that
looked expensive and is not, registration does not move at all, and credit
issuance is cheap only because of the periodic-attestor decision.

** Interface 1: the leaf function

The widest surface, because it is implemented four times and pinned by
every vector that records a tree.

| Layer | Location |
|-------+----------|
| Python | =alberta_buck/registry/tree.py=, =identity_leaf=; =wallet/unilateral_a2.py= subclasses the tree, so there is one definition, not two |
| Rust | =core/rust/buck-identity/src/notes.rs=, re-exported by =core/rust/buck-registry/src/tree.rs= |
| Bindings | =bindings/py-identity=, =bindings/js-identity=, surfaced as =identityLeaf= in =core/js/src/identity-core.js= |
| Circuits | the leaf hasher inlined in =circuits/identity_membership.circom= and =identity_membership_g1tie.circom= |

Consumers: the registry package throughout (=registry.py=,
=feature_authority.py=, =client.py=, =merkle_service.py=, =vectors.py=,
=kernel_vectors.py=), =wallet/unilateral_a2.py=, =wallet/kernel_vectors.py=,
=sim/identity.py=, the review suite, four SNARK input generators under
=scripts/snark/=, and two review scripts.

Vectors that pin a leaf or a tree: =core/vectors/identity-kernel-vectors.json=,
=registry-kernel-vectors.json=, =wallet-kernel-vectors.json=,
=test/vectors/registry/registry_vectors.json=, =identity_root.json=, and the
three Notes end-to-end fixtures.

Change: one salt argument, Poseidon-2 becomes Poseidon-3, four
implementations, every listed vector regenerated, and the four-backend
parity re-established.

** Interface 2: the root

| Direction | Consumers |
|-----------+-----------|
| On chain, readers | =src/Notes.sol= membership helper, =IdentityMembershipG1TieVerifierAdapter.sol=, =IIdentityMembershipVerifier.sol=, =test/IdentityRegistryV2.t.sol=, =test/NotesE2E.t.sol= |
| On chain, writer | =IdentityRegistry.setIdentityRoot=, governance only |
| Off chain | =registry/merkle_service.py= (the aggregator), =core/rust/buck-registry/src/aggregator.rs=, =sim/identity.py=, the g1tie and end-to-end input generators, =core/js/test/registry.vectors.test.js= |

Change: a documented admission authority and update policy, and a root
history if proofs in flight must survive an update. Notes currently reads
only the current root and rejects zero, so a history is an additive
mapping plus one changed check. The code change is small; the policy is the
work.

** Interface 3: the membership proof

Two circuits, one deployed verifier and adapter, the Notes helper, and the
wallet-side producers. The leaf salt is a new private input, and finding
5's three defects (the free tie point, the known generator logarithm, the
unchecked limbs and incomplete addition) are repaired in the same pass so
that one trusted setup covers both. The two circuits should collapse into
one, with the B1 path given a sound circuit of its own before the g1-tie
circuit is retired.

Matched set per rebuild: circuit, proving key, verification key, Solidity
verifier, fixtures.

** Interface 4: registration and binding, which does not move

=register=, =bindContract=, their credential overloads, the A' presentation
and proof, =trustIssuer= and the issuer key format are all unchanged. The
caller-supplied-leaf refusal becomes permanent and is documented as a
decision rather than a stopgap. This is the most useful line in the survey:
the identity layer A' just landed is not disturbed.

** Interface 5: credit issuance

=BuckCredit.createCredit=, 89 call sites in 38 files. Unchanged signature
under the periodic-attestor decision. New surface: a regulator attestation
entry point, the cached envelope and its expiry, and the per-credit check
against asset class, face band and premium. =BuckCredit= gains its first
dependency on the registry, which today it has none of.

** Interface 6: receipts

Capturing an attribute proof at payment time touches
=wallet/envelope.py=, =build_receipt.py=, =verify_receipt.py=, their
JavaScript counterparts, the receipt vectors, and
=alberta-buck-receipt.org=. Additive: a receipt without an attribute proof
stays valid.

** Interface 7: the certified record

Would have been the largest surface and is now documentation only, per
[[#design]]: the =epoch= field stays and is reinterpreted, so no identity
scalar changes and no vector is regenerated on this account. The passages
to revise are the epoch-expiry and renewal sections of
=alberta-buck-identity.org= and the issuer docstrings that describe renewal
as re-issuance.

** Summary

| Interface | Blast radius | Driver |
|-----------+--------------+--------|
| Leaf function | four implementations, eight vector files, four-backend parity | the salt |
| Membership proof | two circuits, one verifier, fresh trusted setup | the salt plus finding 5 |
| Root | five on-chain readers, five off-chain, one writer | authority and policy |
| Credit issuance | one contract, one new entry point, fixtures only | periodic attestor |
| Receipts | wallet and vectors, additive | attribute capture |
| Certified record | documentation only | epoch reinterpreted |
| Registration and binding | none | deliberate |

* What writing the specification settled
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: spec-findings
:END:

Five things the survey had left open, each of which reduces the work.

1. *Two leaf functions, not one changed function.* Keeping
   =identity_leaf= exactly as it is for public trees and adding
   =identity_leaf_salted= alongside it means every committed vector that
   records the unsalted leaf stays valid. Interface 1's blast radius
   shrinks from "every vector that records a tree" to the vectors for
   private trees only.
2. *No new cryptographic assumption.* Leaf hiding rests on Poseidon as a
   random oracle, which the Notes nullifier already rests on. The
   accumulator therefore adds nothing to the assumption set, though it
   does make the leaf's hiding computational where A's credential lock is
   information-theoretic. That asymmetry is open question 3.
3. *No witness service.* Because the leaves are hiding commitments, a
   private subtree can be published in full. Holders compute their own
   paths, and the witness-service privacy question the earlier review
   listed as open does not arise.
4. *The salt must not derive from the identity.* Deriving it from =m=
   would let any authority, which knows =m=, recompute every salt and
   scan. It derives from a wallet secret instead, which also makes it
   recoverable from seed material. The wallet has no key-derivation
   helper today, so that is new work in phase 2.
5. *A root ring is needed, not merely nice.* With periodic attestation,
   root updates are routine, and a single current root invalidates every
   proof in flight at each update. The ring size doubles as the
   protocol's statement of how long a revoked holder can still prove
   membership, which is open question 1.

* Phases and acceptance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: phases
:END:

1. *Specification.* Done: [[file:accumulator-spec.org][the accumulator
   specification]], v0.2, unreviewed. It covers the two leaf functions,
   the private and public tree classes, the aggregator and the root ring,
   admission, salt derivation and recovery, revocation, renewal and
   re-association, publication and witness updates, the attribute
   vocabulary, both proof statements, the insurer envelope and the credit
   gate, six security requirements with their assumption accounting, nine
   conformance tests. Five findings from writing it are recorded below.
   v0.2 resolves the five questions v0.1 left open: root records carry a
   timestamp and each consumer declares a maximum age, in place of a
   posting count; the epoch dimension is removed in favour of one live-set
   subtree per registry; leaf hiding stays on Poseidon, since no commitment
   gives an information-theoretically hidden Merkle leaf, with publication
   chosen over the withheld mode and its retroactive exposure recorded; the
   attesting authority takes a retention obligation rather than an
   acknowledgement; and insurer enumeration is accepted with the
   underwriter risk estimate dropped from the on-chain envelope. Five
   parameters remain to be set before phase 2.
   v0.3 sets them from the expected posting cadences, and two things
   followed rather than being parameters: the aggregator depth rises from
   10 to 20, because community authorities are a population rather than a
   roster, and delegated authority becomes part of the specification,
   because a regulator delegates to community sub-regulators exactly as
   attribute authority is delegated to clubs and congregations.
   v0.4 adds deployment and governance: one root authority per
   deployment, which for Alberta Buck is the provincial identity
   authority and which is as decentralized as whoever holds its key;
   on-chain enrollment of authorities; an aggregator that is accountable
   rather than trustless, with omission and forgery made provable by
   signed subtree roots and a published leaf list; the distinction
   between delegation rules that bind because they are scope subtrees
   and rules that are contractual; deployment isolation and recognition;
   and the statement that the protocol enforces the consistency of the
   attestation chain but not its accuracy.
2. *Python.* Done. The salted leaf, the private-tree flag and the
   clearance primitive in =registry/tree.py=; holder-derived salts in
   =wallet/salt.py=, recoverable from seed material and not derivable
   from the identity; the salt carried through attestation, revocation
   and lookup in =registry/feature_authority.py=; posted root records
   with timestamps and a per-consumer maximum age in
   =registry/merkle_service.py=; holder-produced attribute proofs in
   =wallet/attributes.py=; and the insurance regulator, its envelope and
   the issuance gate in =registry/regulator.py=, which is the executable
   specification the Solidity port of phase 4 is checked against.
   =alberta_buck/test/test_accumulator.py= carries the conformance
   tests, including the scan: a party holding every certified scalar and
   the whole published subtree decides no membership. 165 passed across
   the accumulator, registry, unilateral-A2, wallet, vectors,
   kernel-backend, issuer and review suites.

   Extended on 2026-09-20 with the receiving-key architecture that
   settled finding R5 ([[file:notes-receiving-key.org][its own document]]):
   =receiving_leaf= and =insert_receiving= in =registry/tree.py=, the
   third leaf function of the specification, and =mailbox_leaf= /
   =insert_mailbox=, the fourth -- the same association over the two POINTS,
   because the spend's prover holds the scalars and a payer holds no secret
   at all; =wallet/recvkey.py=, the receiving key derived from seed material
   rather than from the identity, with holder-produced binding evidence that
   discloses nothing; and =wallet/deposit_fold.py=, the folded deposit gate
   whose four relations over one witness refuse a payload thief that a split
   gate admits.  =alberta_buck/test/test_receiving_key.py= carries thirty-one
   tests, including the theft as a test rather than a warning.
3. *Circuits.* Done, with 32-level membership paths: the identity registry's
   subtree (12) and then the aggregator (20), the specification's two-level
   statement folded as one path -- the first build folded 20 levels only, and
   phase 4 found and corrected it.  The addressed spends prove
   membership inside one folded gate per flavour (=deposit_fold_a1=,
   =deposit_fold_a2=), which removed finding 5's three defects by
   construction rather than by repair; B1 proves it in
   =identity_membership_b1=, whose blind is proven on the hash-to-curve
   generator =H_PEDERSEN=.  Each has a dev-entropy setup, a generated
   verifier and real proofs spending on chain
   ([[file:notes-receiving-key.org::#fold-built][the gates as built]]).
   Since 2026-09-22 every leaf function leads with its own v2 tag, in all
   four backends and every circuit that hashes a leaf
   ([[file:notes-receiving-key.org::#reading-plan][step C]]).
4. *Solidity.* Done 2026-09-22.  The root authority and policy, the
   permanent caller-leaf refusal documented as such, and =BuckCredit='s
   attestation entry point and per-credit check.  The specification leaves six
   things to the port, decided here:
   - *Roles.*  =IdentityRegistry= gains a =rootAuthority=, appointed by
     governance, which enrolls and evicts subtrees and appoints the
     =aggregator=; the aggregator alone posts roots.  Governance keeps what
     it had, and =setIdentityRoot= is gone.
   - *Enrollment is per subtree.*  An authority with several predicates, as
     the insurance regulator has, enrolls each of its subtrees under the
     same posting key.  The record holds the subtree's aggregator slot, and
     a public membership proof takes its aggregator index bits from that
     slot, never from the caller: the slot is what makes a path a claim about
     a named subtree.
   - *Roots.*  A ring of 256 records, a root mapped to its posting time, and
     a maximum age per consumer, named by a v2 tag; setting one longer than
     the full ring covers is refused.  Spends name the identity root they
     proved against, and =Notes= accepts any retained root within its age.
   - *Binding the insurer to its leaf.*  No identity point is stored in the
     clear, public or not, so the attesting account proves that its
     registered credential opens to the =M= whose leaf it claims -- a
     Chaum-Pedersen proof over the stored key and credential, under its own
     Fiat-Shamir tag.  Without it any account could claim any insurer's
     envelope.
   - *What the envelope's rates are.*  The executable specification caches
     a maximum depreciation rate and premium rate that no subtree attests.
     They become subtrees like the face band, =insurer:depRate:<bp>= and
     =insurer:premium:<bp>=, and the general scope becomes
     =insurer:general=, since a hash of a name is never zero.  Subtree
     identifiers are =keccak= of the namespaced name, as scopes already are.
   - *The gate is configured once.*  Governance sets the registry, the
     regulator's namespace and the attestation period, and cannot unset
     them.  A =BuckCredit= never configured issues ungated: the simulations
     and the economic suites deploy that way, and a deployment profile
     (specification 17.5) =MUST= configure it.  The gate's own suite
     exercises every refusal against the Python reference.
   Two more things the port settled.  A reappraisal (=updateCredit=) is held
   to the parameter envelope too, or an insurer writes small and updates
   large; the scope is emitted at creation and not stored, so a reappraisal
   cannot re-check it.  And the gates' membership paths were one level short
   of the specification: a single 20-level path from the leaf cannot pass
   through a subtree, so every gate now folds 32 levels, subtree (12) then
   aggregator (20), and was set up again
   ([[file:notes-receiving-key.org::#reading-plan][step D]]).

   As built: =IdentityRegistry= is 23,166 of the 24,576 bytes a contract may
   deploy, so the insurer's identity opening lives in =BuckCredit=, its only
   consumer, reading the key and credential from the registry.  The
   specification asked for public membership to be measured both ways: a path
   through the aggregator (subtree 10 + aggregator 20) costs 913,642 gas on
   chain, and a path against a posted subtree root alone would cost 301,179.
   An attestation of eight predicates -- standing, band, two depreciation
   models, both rates, the general scope and one named scope -- costs about 8M
   gas, once per review period; storing public subtree roots (the
   specification's =MAY=) would cut it to about a third and is left for when
   a deployment's cadence asks for it.  Suites: =IdentityAccumulator.t.sol=
   (roles, enrollment, the ring, consumer ages, public paths),
   =InsurerGate.t.sol= (the gate end to end), and =InsurerGateVectors.t.sol=,
   which replays =scripts/gen_insurer_gate_vectors.py= -- the Python
   reference's subtrees, leaf, paths, opening and eight issuance decisions --
   byte for byte.
5. *Rust, bindings and JavaScript*, with regenerated vectors, as every
   identity change requires.
6. *Documents.* The epoch reinterpretation, the accumulator sections of the
   identity and Notes papers, and the insurer path in the credit
   documentation.

Acceptance: a party holding every certified identity finds no private tree
it can scan; a holder proves each of its own memberships and a revoked
holder cannot; an insurer outside the regulator's subtree cannot create a
credit, one inside it can within its envelope and not outside it; the Notes
membership path still verifies on the repaired circuit with the salted
leaf; four-backend parity holds; and no identity scalar in any vector has
changed.
