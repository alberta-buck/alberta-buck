The first sections read the completed =1a5afec= campaign in =build/sim/mended/=, before the
debtor-policy changes measured below. All 56 runs completed. Their PDFs and a linked index use
the saved vectors: [[file:../../build/sim/mended/index.html][open the campaign]]. No simulation was repeated to make these reports.

* What the casts contain

The common cast has one =MarketMakerWhale= to keep TOKEN/USDC markets on the historical feeds,
three =AnonymousArbAgent= and five =TokenAccumulatorAgent= to trade profitable cycles, 24
=BootstrapDMAgent= to seed the basket pools, five =FatCreditBorrowerAgent=, four =SaverAgent=,
one =PidKeeperAgent= and one =DirectorKeeperAgent=. The arbitrageurs connect prices; they do not
create a continuing flow of household credit. The basket implementations supply their own
upkeep machinery.

| Mature variant | Mortgage borrowers | Other additions to the common cast |
|----------------+--------------------+------------------------------------|
| debtors-mature | 24, day-0 arrivals | none |
| basketeers | 24, day-0 arrivals | 120 TOKEN depositors, 30 BUCK depositors |
| originate | 30, staggered arrivals | above, plus 60 arriving depositors |
| endog | 30, price-responsive arrival clock | same depositor counts |
| endog-excursion | same 30 | above, plus 5 cash arbs and 3 credit arbs |
| endog-raid | same 30 | above, plus one whale raid |
| endog-excursion-raid | same 30 | both excursion arbs and raid |

The mortgage class in these saved runs is =BuckCreditDebtorAgent=, with =refi_mode = "atomic"=.
It is not the newer =ExternalDebtRetireeAgent=, which compares carrying costs and permits partial
refinancing. The latter appears in the savings worlds. The older class asks monthly whether it
can convert its whole remaining mortgage, after fees and slippage, within its credit capacity
and its =theta x apr= tolerance. Otherwise it waits. A high LTV can therefore postpone the whole
refinance even when a partial one would pay. It buys the real funding reserve, activates insured
credit, sells BUCKs through BUCK/USDC and pays the external lender. It does not search the
BUCK/TOKEN/USDC alternatives itself.

The five large borrowers are aggregate stand-ins, each with eight zero-premium credit NFTs and
two to five million of face. They have no external mortgage ledger. Their target is a utilization
fraction of activated credit, with adoption, regime changes and a response to a dear BUCK.
They sell 30--60% of a draw into BUCK/TOKEN and keep the TOKEN; the rest goes into BUCK/USDC.
They do not finish that first leg with a TOKEN/USDC sale to a bank. Their reserve models the
insured gate's scale, since their own zero-premium credits bypass it.

The four savers buy a basket-relative discount and sell a premium from finite inventories.
The excursion credit arbs activate unused face when the BUCK is dear and buy back when cheap;
unlike the large borrowers, they can reactivate credit. There are only three, only in the
excursion variants. Their execution hurdle also refers to dollar par. The raid buys before
mid-run, dumps, then buys back; it can leave a lasting shortage when the other issuers cannot
replace the BUCKs it takes out.

* What actually happens

Selling newly issued BUCKs for USDC has the direction Perry describes: BUCK reserve rises,
USDC reserve falls, USDC per BUCK falls, and arbitrage transmits the discount into a basket
cost above one BUCK. The runs do have this flow. Three other model choices obscure or exhaust it.

First, the opening placement is deliberately outside the market. The mature large borrowers
and eligible day-0 mortgage borrowers exchange with the pool-seeding helper at the opening
price. Their new liens replace part of its lien, leaving the float and pool price unchanged.
That implements the earlier request to begin with a mature balance sheet rather than dump it
all in the first tick. Later refinancing uses the market.

Second, these are finite cohorts, not continuing origination. Every mortgage borrower in every
mature run eventually refinances. On the older upkeep:

| Variant | Borrowers | Day the last borrower has refinanced |
|---------+-----------+-------------------------------------|
| debtors-mature | 24 | 240 |
| basketeers | 24 | 240 |
| originate | 30 | 745 |
| endog | 30 | 1216 |
| endog-excursion | 30 | 1346 |
| endog-raid | 30 | 1045 |
| endog-excursion-raid | 30 | 1342 |

The opening =debtors-mature= placement refinances 17 of 24. Others follow as capacity permits.
In =originate= all 30 have arrived by day 715: the neighbor-attraction clock accelerates the
nominal five-year stagger. Across all baskets, every mature cohort finishes by day 1422 at the
latest. There is no next cohort. The whole-mortgage rule delays issuance; it is not a permanent
failure to issue in these completed runs.

Third, the large borrowers buy back BUCKs and then =burn= coverage, but never activate it again.
Buying back already reduces their signed liability; the burn additionally removes credit
capacity. In =originate= on the older upkeep, their combined limit falls from 13.413 million
BUCKs on day 0 to 3.773 million on day 365 and 18,011 BUCKs at the end. They placed 9.503 million,
sold another 7.317 million through the market, and bought back 16.649 million. K at its ceiling
cannot restore coverage the agents have deactivated. The underlying property face has not gone.

After refinancing, the saved mortgage policy also buys BUCKs to match a hypothetical mortgage's
amortization, regardless of discount. It deposits surplus only once, then accumulates cash.
Its separate optional buyback rule requires a USDC price below $0.98, rather than a discount
against the basket. In =originate= on the older upkeep, year-five household cash is $18.294
million, while recorded basket principal is only 1.168 million BUCKs. That policy does not
represent recurring investment of freed cash flow or a basket-relative bargain hunter.

These runs therefore do not test a continuing supply of cost-sensitive external refinancing
against discount-driven retirement. They do not establish that such refinancing would fail to
create inflationary pressure. The persistent shortage is entangled with the finite cohort and
the synthetic borrowers' activation ratchet.

* Rendering and viewing

The old matrix and frozen campaign scripts wrote JSON and metrics only. The optional Makefile
plot targets produced PNGs separately. The shared loop now renders a PDF after every completed,
saved run of at least 1825 days, including programmatic savings runs. It checks the plotting
dependency before starting. A PDF failure fails the command but preserves the completed JSON.

#+begin_src sh
python -m alberta_buck.sim.report path/to/run.json
python -m alberta_buck.sim.report build/sim/mended/runs/*.json \
    build/sim/mended/savings-s1/*.json build/sim/mended/savings-s2/*.json \
    --jobs 4 --index build/sim/mended/index.html
#+end_src

The index opens as a local file, without a server. Each PDF has an issuance overview, the
equilibrium plots, the market/basket plots, and a household page when relevant. The overview
shows activated limits, actual drawings, issuance and buybacks, remaining mortgages and the
number of borrowers who have arrived or refinanced. The source JSON hash is recorded in the PDF.
The original interactive viewer is also available via =make nix-venv-viewer VIEWER_PORT=8787=;
it is separate from report generation. No viewer server was started for this audit.

* The corrected debtor policy

Perry requested a carrying-cost decision that accepts unfavorable execution when interest
savings justify it. He selected an even split after refinancing: half retire their BUCK
liabilities only at a basket-relative discount after costs; half carry them and invest recurring
surplus in the basket. This is now implemented. The 56 reports above preserve the earlier policy.

All nine matrix casts with mortgage borrowers now select =refi_mode = "carry"=. The existing
=BuckCreditDebtorAgent= keeps its arrival clock and random draws, but calls the same refinance
decision and execution method as =ExternalDebtRetireeAgent=. It can refinance part of a mortgage
and return for more when credit permits. The decision compares interest and premium savings
and Jubilee relief with execution costs, the deposit's opportunity and carried age, and any
configured switching cost, penalty and risk charge. =theta= is the time allowed for savings to
repay those costs. The basic matrix household has no switching, penalty or risk charge; the organic
retiree retains its existing renewal, penalty and risk settings.

BUCK-denominated costs are valued at the basket's current dollar reference, calculated from
=basketValueInBuck x USDC-per-BUCK=. They are no longer implicitly valued at a fixed dollar.
The actual BUCK/USDC sale quote still includes its fee and price impact. The funding gate and
the credit-capacity bound remain the contracts' own checks.

=surplus_policy = "mixed"= assigns alternating members of each cohort to the two policies,
without a new random draw. A 24-member cohort has 12 of each; a 30-member cohort has 15.

- =discount=: after the external mortgage is retired, buy back only when the execution still
  offers the configured basket-relative discount, 2% by default. The size leaves even the last
  BUCK bought below that threshold after the fee and price impact, respects cash and the
  agent's impact budget, and never exceeds its current liability. Receipt of the BUCKs repays
  the lien; no coverage is burned. There is no scheduled amortization.
- =basket=: carry the BUCK liability and invest cash above the household's cash buffer every
  month. A separate creditless savings account receives the purchased BUCKs and holds the
  basket receipts. The investment therefore neither repays the household's lien nor depends
  on being allowed to redraw it. Deposits repeat; unspent or temporarily rejected deposits
  remain in that account and are counted in the household's books.

The policies are shared by both debtor classes. Explicit =surplus_policy = "legacy"= retains
the old amortization, single deposit and dollar-discount routine for comparison. The original
=atomic= and =paced= refinancing modes also remain selectable.

Telemetry now records the policy, recurring deposit count and principal, pending savings BUCKs,
discounted retirements, and the equity basket shares' current marked value. The household
comparison's original ledger still carries basket principal at par; the new PDF policy page
shows marked share value separately, before exit costs. Pro-rata receipts retain their
principal measure; they do not claim an equity-share mark.

The investing cohorts' =unwound= counters are zero. In =originate=, their 7.865 million of
principal is marked at 8.297 million BUCKs at the end; their household cash is at the $300,000
combined buffer. Another 8,136 BUCKs remain in the savings accounts waiting to deposit. Three
worlds record a last =Slippage()= on an attempted investment. These are the basket's price
guards, not missing funds; pending BUCKs remain visible and later months retry.

The retirement cohorts mostly keep cash, because the BUCK is mostly at parity or dear, not at
their required discount. In =originate= they end with $9.147 million cash and 4.047 million
BUCKs drawn. They no longer buy BUCKs merely to match the hypothetical mortgage's amortization.
Every mature household has retired its external mortgage by the end; portcast has one mortgage
still partly external, which the partial-refinance policy permits.

This correction does not settle the mature worlds. The simplest mature cast is materially
worse: its final basket cost of 0.8414 means the BUCK is about 18.9% dear. Its synthetic borrowers'
activated limit is 2.547 million BUCKs, with 2.458 million drawn, and K is at 0.95. The finite
cohort and those borrowers' failure to reactivate credit remain. Recurring investment also
creates continuing demand that these casts must supply. The near-parity final values in the
other mature worlds do not excuse their earlier deviations or their high K.

* Validation and remaining comparisons

The full Python suite passed: 1025 tests, five skipped. The final focused run passed 43 tests,
including two added checks that savings retirees retain range-valued cost settings.
Real-contract tests on both the pro-rata
and equity baskets cover partial refinancing, the even split, no retirement without a discount,
retirement without deactivating coverage, and repeated investments that preserve the liability.
Kernel tests include an unfavorable refinance whose interest savings justify it and a
basket-discount purchase above $1. All 76 existing agent draws and all five organic-retiree
draws retain their expressions and order. No contract or lab-fingerprint file changed.

The frozen follow-up campaign is =build/sim/debtor-policy/=. Its =source-manifest.json= records
the exact matrix source hashes and =source.patch= the changes over =efdb0c2=. It reuses Perry's nine
affected machine runs and runs the remaining eighteen older/pro-rata cells, four lean
comparisons and eight savings cells. Thirty new runs, eight at a time, with logs and PDFs
beside their vectors under =runs/=. =run.log= and =status.json= record progress. These comparisons
were still running when this account was written; no result from them is asserted above.

Review before the savings cells started found that the shared setup tried to parse their
range-valued switching and risk settings as scalars. The basic household now initializes those
costs to zero and leaves the organic retiree's existing draws to set its own. Matrix settings
and behavior are unchanged. The savings cells use a separate =savings-snapshot/= with that fix;
=savings-source-manifest.json= and =savings-source.patch= record it. No running snapshot was
edited. Both savings seeds and both upkeeps receive a short sequential preflight before the
long savings runs, warming their identity cache.
