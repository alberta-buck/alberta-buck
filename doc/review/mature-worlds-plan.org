#+TITLE: Mending the mature worlds: a plan
#+DATE: 2026-10-13
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2 num:t

This is a plan for four fixes to the simulator's cast, written so that someone who was not there
can carry them out. Perry agreed to all four on 2026-10-13. None of them touches a contract. Line
numbers are as of commit =87d5a6f= on =doc/basket-machine=; find things by name if they have moved.

* Why

In the five matrix worlds with mature debtors K is at its limit within weeks, and on the pro-rata
basket and the older upkeep the BUCK then stays 8% to 33% dear for a year. We read every account
through the first 60 days of one of them. The table and the account are in
[[file:../BASKET-MACHINE-RESULTS.org::*Why the mature worlds cannot issue enough][the results]]. Four things in the model make it:

1. The big borrowers set aside a BUCK for every BUCK they draw, and hold it while the credit is
   out. The protocol's gate asks a few percent, for the moment of the draw.
2. Everyone issues on day 0, into basket pools of 2.3 million BUCKs.
3. The shared swap helper keeps BUCKs it cannot forward. It lost 6.3 million in four days.
4. No borrower uses its unused limit because the BUCK is dear.

Savers also buy 15 million BUCKs into the day-0 crash. That is not a fault: it is what a saver
does, and with no crash it does not happen. Leave the savers alone.

* Fix 3: the swap helper keeps nothing

Do this one first. It is plumbing, and the other fixes are measured through it.

** What is wrong

=d.simlp= does two jobs. It is the account that seeded the BUCK/dollar pool, so it carries a lien
of about 50 million BUCKs against a credit whose limit moves with K (=deploy.py=, lines 588 to
640). It is also the transit account for every agent's swap: =_ProxyAgent._swap_via_simlp=
(=equilibrium_agents.py=, line 330) has the paying proxy transfer its tokens to =d.simlp= and then
calls =d.simlp.swap=, which pays the pool from =d.simlp='s own balance.

When K falls far enough that the helper's limit is under its lien, the helper has nothing to
spend. BUCKs that arrive repay its lien and cannot go on. The code then caps the swap to what the
helper can spend, which is nothing, and returns. The agent has lost its BUCKs and nobody has
recorded it. On day 0 of the mature worlds K falls to 0.38, the limit to 38 million, and in four
days 6.3 million BUCKs vanish this way.

** What to do

1. /Proxies swap for themselves./ Every proxy is a =SimLP= (=_new_proxy=, line 321), so it has
   =swap= and the callbacks. In =_swap_via_simlp= drop the transfer to =d.simlp= and call =swap= on
   the paying proxy:
   #+begin_src python
   payer                           = from_proxy or self.proxy
   # (keep the cap to the payer's own spendable BUCK, as now)
   d.chain.send(payer.functions.swap(pool_addr, recipient, zero_for_one, int(amount), sqrt_limit, t0, t1))
   #+end_src
   The pool then takes the tokens from the proxy in the callback. Delete the second cap, the one
   against =balanceOf(d.simlp)=. Keep the method's name: many callers use it.
2. /Depositors get a transit account with no credit./ =_DMBase._sell_proceeds= (=direct_mint.py=,
   line 125) is an EOA and cannot take a callback, so it needs a helper. Give it one that holds no
   credit and no lien, made on first use and kept on the deployment:
   #+begin_src python
   def _transit(d):
       """A SimLP with no credit of its own, for an EOA's swaps: what it is sent it forwards or returns."""
       t                           = getattr(d, "_transit", None)
       if t is None:
           t                       = d.chain.deploy("SimLP", sol_file="SimLP")
           idmod.bind_as_operator(d.chain, d.reg, t.address, True, False)
           d._transit              = t
       return t
   #+end_src
   The depositor's identity approval (the =_simlp_approved= block) must name the transit's address,
   not =d.simlp='s. After the swap, send anything still in the transit back to the depositor with
   the transit's =exec=. Do not add a field to =Deployment= for it: =deploy.py= is in the lab's
   fingerprint.
3. /Leave the other uses of =d.simlp= alone./ The whale's snaps (=agents.py=, line 291) and
   =_buy_token_from_usdc= (=direct_mint.py=, line 201) move TOKEN and dollars, of which the helper
   holds an endless stock and no lien. =_simlp_endow_buck= (line 588) and the pusher agents use
   =exec= to issue from the helper on purpose.
4. /Make it visible./ Add the helper's lien to each frame in =snapshot.py= as =simlp_drawn=
   (=-signedBalanceOf(d.simlp)=, or 0 when positive). It should be flat through any run.

** How to know it worked

- New test in =alberta_buck/test/test_rebalancing_sim_invariants.py= (it already takes a finished
  run's frames): =simlp_drawn= is the same in the last frame as in the first frame after day 0.
- =who-holds.py= on the world below: the row "pool-seeding helper" no longer falls from 49.6 to

* Fix 1: the big borrowers' reserve at the gate's own scale

** What is wrong

=FatCreditBorrowerAgent= (=equilibrium_agents.py=, line 1627) uses credit that pays no premium, so
the real gate never applies to it, and it models the gate itself. Its model is older than the
gate's correction. In =act= (the lever-up branch, lines 1950 to 2034) a draw of =delta= needs
=delta x ff= BUCKs in a separate escrow proxy, with =ff= about one at parity. The requirement
rolls: =reserve_req= (line 1817) is the sum over open tranches of =outstanding x ff=, so the
escrow holds about one BUCK for every BUCK still drawn. In the base world after five years the
big borrowers have drawn 11.0 million and hold 10.7 million in escrow.

The protocol asks something else (=Buck.mint=, and "The Funding-Factor Reserve" in
=alberta-buck-ethereum.org=). The minter must hold, at the moment of the mint, BUCKs worth the
insurance deposit that mint pays, times =fundingFactor=. The deposit is ten years' premium, 5% to
15% of the coverage. The factor is a half at parity. Nothing is locked afterwards.

** What to do

Add two settings in =setup=, after the existing draws and with =_spec=:

#+begin_src python
# How the simulated funding reserve is kept (settings; no draw):
#   "gate"     what Buck.mint's gate asks of an insured draw: fundingFactor x the deposit, for the draw only
#   "rolling"  the earlier model: ff x the whole draw, held while the credit is out
#   "none"     no reserve at all
self.reserve_mode               = str(_spec(scenario, cls, "reserve_mode", "gate"))
self.gate_deposit               = float(_spec(scenario, cls, "gate_deposit", 0.10))     # the deposit, of coverage: ten years at 100 bp
#+end_src

In the lever-up branch, when =reserve_mode == "gate"=:

#+begin_src python
k                               = d.kctrl.functions.buckK().call() / 1e18
factor                          = d.kctrl.functions.fundingFactor().call() / 1e18       # the contract's own, linear
e                               = self.gate_deposit
deposit                         = delta * e / (k - e) if k > e else 0                   # on the coverage that delivers delta
need_add                        = int(deposit * factor)
req                             = 0                                                     # nothing is owed for earlier draws
ff                              = need_add / delta if delta else 0.                     # what the throttle below divides by
#+end_src

The rest of the branch then works as written: buy the shortfall into the escrow, throttle the
draw to what the escrow funds, sell. Two more changes in that mode:

- Do not append to =self._tranches=. With no tranches =reserve_req= is zero and =_pop_tranches=
  releases nothing, which is right.
- After the sale, free the whole escrow: =self.pending_release = self._reserve_balance(d)=.
  =_release_pending= (line 1844) already sells pending reserve back, a share a day, and only while
  the BUCK is not cheap. That last condition is worth keeping: it holds BUCKs off a market that
  already has too many.

"rolling" keeps today's code path exactly, so an old run can be reproduced. "none" skips the
purchase and the throttle. The draws of =ff_cyc=, =ff_amp= and =ff_pow= stay in =setup= though
"gate" does not use them.

Update the class docstring's section (B), the note at line 431 of the same file, and
=alberta_buck/sim/EQUILIBRIUM.md= (its "Rolling reserve + two-sided toll" section records why
the rolling form was chosen over a cumulative one; add what replaced it and why).

** What to watch

The escrow was a large part of what held these worlds' books level: a borrower that buys back
most of what it sells adds little supply. With "gate" the big borrowers become real issuers of
about 10 million BUCKs a world. Expect K to rest lower than it did. If the BUCK turns cheap and K
runs to its floor of 0.20 in the base world, the borrowers' =util_target= is too high for a
world whose demand was tuned around the escrow. Say so and stop; do not tune it away quietly.

* Fix 2: an opening that is not a shock

** What is wrong

The mature worlds set =mid_frac = -0.5= for the big borrowers, so their adoption curve
(=_cap_frac=, line 1801) is complete on day 0 and their target is their whole share of their
limit. On the first tick they sell it: about 10 million BUCKs, part of it into basket pools that
hold 2.3 million. The debtors in those worlds arrive on day 0 as well (=arrive_frac = 0.0=) and
sixteen of them sell a whole mortgage's worth each. "Mature" was meant to say that this credit
is already out when the window opens. The model has it all issued in the window's first minute.

Even the quiet worlds open badly for the same reason, at a smaller size: in the base world the
basket costs 1.45 BUCKs on day 0.

** What to do

Give both agents a =bootstrap=, which =loop.run= already calls for every agent before the first
tick (=loop.py=, line 117). In it, credit that is to be out on day 0 is drawn and handed to the
pool-seeding helper at the pool's price. The helper's lien is the 50 million BUCKs that seeded
the BUCK/dollar pool. BUCKs handed to it repay that much of its lien. So the float is the same
size, no pool moves, and that much of the float is now the borrower's issue and not the
helper's. That is what "already out" means.

Two small methods on =_ProxyAgent=, used only in the opening:

#+begin_src python
def _otc_sell(self, d, buck_amt: int) -> int:
    """Hand `buck_amt` to the pool-seeding helper at the BUCK/dollar pool's price, for dollars from its stock.
    The helper's lien falls by as much: the float and every pool are untouched.  Returns dollars received."""

def _otc_buy(self, d, buck_amt: int) -> int:
    """The reverse: dollars to the helper for BUCKs it issues.  Returns dollars paid."""
#+end_src

=_otc_sell= is a BUCK transfer from the proxy to =d.simlp= (through =_proxy_exec=) and a dollar
transfer back (=d.simlp.functions.exec= of =usdc.transfer=). Price both ways with =buck_usd6= from
=gauge.py=. Cap a sale at the helper's lien.

*** The big borrowers

#+begin_src python
def bootstrap(self, d, scenario, ctr) -> None:
    """Open with the credit this borrower already has out: its day-0 target, placed with the helper at par."""
    if self.proxy is None or self.open_mode != "placed":
        return
    limit                       = d.buck.functions.creditLimit(self.proxy.address).call()
    target                      = int(self.util_target * limit * self._cap_frac(0, getattr(scenario, "days", 1)))
    got                         = self._otc_sell(d, min(target, limit))
    ctr["fatPlaced"]            = ctr.get("fatPlaced", 0) + ...
#+end_src

with =self.open_mode = str(_spec(scenario, cls, "open", "placed"))= in =setup=. ="market"= keeps
today's opening. On the first tick =drawn= is then at =target= and the lever-up branch has
nothing to do. No reserve is bought for a placed opening: it is the state the world starts in.

*** The debtors

A debtor whose =arrive_day= is 0 and which is not on the endogenous clock does its first month's
work in =bootstrap=, settled with the helper and not through the pools. The smallest change that
does this:

- Add =self._otc = False= and an =open= setting like the borrowers'.
- In =bootstrap=, for those debtors, set =self._otc = True=, call =self.act(d, scenario, 0, 0, ctr)=
  and set it back. The day-0 call from the loop then returns early, because =_last_month_day= is
  already 0.
- Where =act= sells the draw (=_sell_capped=, both the atomic and the paced branch) and where
  =_buy_for_gate= buys BUCKs (through =_buy_track=), go by =_otc_sell= and =_otc_buy= while
  =self._otc= is set.
- In =_atomic_plan= (line 2674), while =self._otc= is set there is no slippage and no depth to
  check: =need_b= is the mortgage at the pool's price and =d_eff= is zero. Keep the capacity check.

The debtor still mints through the real gate, still pays its deposit, and still keeps both of its
ledgers. Only the two market legs change.

*** A guard for later

The big borrowers can still sell several times a pool's depth in one step, at a shock
(=shock_mag=) or after a jump in K. Cap each leg of a sale by price impact, as the debtors do:

#+begin_src python
self.max_impact_bp              = int(_spec(scenario, cls, "max_impact_bp", 100))       # 0: no cap
#+end_src

and in the lever-up branch cap =internal= by =_impact_cap(BUCK reserve of pool_buck[i], bp)= and
=external= by the same for the BUCK/dollar pool (=_impact_cap=, line 477; reserves from
=active_reserves=). What is not sold this step is sold on the next: the target has not moved.
This changes how the scripted uptake shocks play out, which is the point, but check the shock
catalogue's worlds before taking its old numbers as still true.

** How to know it worked

On the world below, the day-1 column of =who-holds.py= shows the basket's cost in BUCK within 2%
of one, K within 0.02 of 0.75, the BUCK/dollar pool still holding about 50 million BUCKs, and the
helper's lien down by exactly what was placed. Add to =test_debtor_ledger.py= a case for a debtor
that opens placed: after =bootstrap= its mortgage is down, its books balance, and the BUCK/dollar
pool's =slot0= is what it was.

* Fix 4: borrowers who answer a dear BUCK

** What is wrong

A big borrower's target is =util_target x limit x cap_frac= (line 1946). The BUCK's price is not
in it. In the mature worlds the big borrowers sat with 5 million of unused limit for a year while
the BUCK was 8% to 33% dear. A dear BUCK is an offer to anyone with something to pledge, and a
borrower with room takes it. Perry's rule for debtors applies: they are smart.

The borrower already answers the other side. When the BUCK is cheap it retires faster
(=disc_gain=, line 2050).

** What to do

#+begin_src python
self.dear_full                  = float(_spec(scenario, cls, "dear_full", 0.05))        # uses its whole limit when the BUCK is this dear; 0: never
#+end_src

and where the target is set:

#+begin_src python
use                             = self.util_target * cap
if self.dear_full > 0 and use < 1:
    use                        += (1 - use) * min(1., max(0., 1 - bvib) / self.dear_full)
target                          = int(use * limit)
#+end_src

So at parity nothing changes, at 2.5% dear it uses half of what it had spare, and at 5% dear all
of it. When the BUCK comes back the target falls, the borrower is over it, and the existing
retire branch buys the BUCKs back. That is an arbitrage with its own credit, and it is what the
three credit arbitrageurs do in the two worlds that held. The impact cap from fix 2 paces it.

** How to know it worked

Run the world below with =agents.FatCreditBorrowerAgent.open=market= so the old shock still
happens. The BUCK should be back within 5% of its basket in weeks, not a year, and
=fat_drawn / fat_limit= should rise toward one while it is dear and fall again after.
