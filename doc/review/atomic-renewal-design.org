#+TITLE: Atomic renewal of one BuckCredit facility
#+DATE: 2026-10-29
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil toc:2

* Scope

The isolated =BuckNet=, =BuckCredit= and =JubileeFund= prototypes implement
renewal of one existing facility with its current insurer. The insurer
proposes an exact replacement, and the NFT owner independently authorizes the
same proposal and requests execution. An insurer change would require an
additional ownership/authorization design and remains separate. The
BuckCredit NFT and its coverage stay in place throughout the transaction.

The replacement specifies the face, depreciation terms, premium rate, desired
activated face, new issuance, maximum new premium, minimum acceptable relief,
deadline, current credit-terms hash, and expected outstanding principal. The
digest binds the token contract, chain, owner, NFT, all replacement terms and
limits. Each BuckNet constructs its own immutable =BuckRenewalConsent= contract
to hold the approvals, keeping the token under the EVM contract-size limit.
The consent contract cannot change credit or settle BUCK, and only Buck can
consume approvals. The insurer can replace/cancel its proposal; the owner can replace or
cancel its own authorization. A stale proposal fails if the credit terms or
principal changed. Credit terms include existing coverage, not merely a
reappraised face. No signature scheme or new on-chain identity service is
necessary for this first implementation.

* Transaction and accounting

1. Validate owner and insurer approvals and the deadline. A facility whose
   average issuance age has reached 50 years must be closed instead of
   renewed. Read its capped claim seconds and the aggregate benchmark, then
   checkpoint the reserve controller.
2. Compute its share of net liquid cash plus the basket receipt's marked
   value, net of the receipt's gain fee, capped by outstanding principal.
   Do not sell the position. Revert if the actual net relief exceeds 10% of
   pre-settlement net cash or the owner's minimum is not met. The keeper may
   independently replenish the cash reserve before a later attempt.
3. NFT principal and total issued supply are whole raw BUCK units. Round the
   computed fractional relief *down* to whole raw units before paying it and
   leave the fractional remainder in the fund. The owner explicitly accepts the
   minimum; this can be zero. This avoids creating fractional NFT principal or
   charging the owner for the fund's rounding. The selected facility's claim
   age is then reset; an unpaid target is not a guaranteed future entitlement.
4. If the original insurer deposit is invested, request its liquidity before
   taking Buck's lock. Inside the atomic settlement, return the entire original
   *nominal* deposit from the originally recorded Carrying pool, with that
   pool's average carried demurrage. Apply the full-value Jubilee amount to
   extinguish the same number of BUCK units directly from the fund, and record
   the reduced principal and reset claim age before changing terms.
5. Apply the insurer-approved replacement terms in place and activate any
   additional coverage. Do not remove or lower the old activated coverage.
   Determine the replacement premium from the resulting current coverage and
   new rate, bound it by the approved maximum, and pay full-value BUCK to the
   insurer's currently registered pool. The owner may fund it from held BUCK,
   the nominal pool refund, and the explicitly authorized additional issuance.
   Apply the funding-factor check before the new issuance supplies any reserve.
6. Synchronize the capped aggregate and ensure the final K shortfall is no
   worse than before, using K at settlement:
   =max(0, new principal - K * new current value) <=
    max(0, old principal - K * old current value)=. A failed check reverts the
   refund, issuance, approvals and every other change. Additional
   issuance is recorded only after the final limit check. BuckCredit applies
   the replacement terms and added coverage within one call.

An integration obtains the consent contract from =buck.renewalConsent()=,
reads =currentCreditTermsHash(id)= and the NFT's current issued principal,
constructs =BuckRenewal.Terms=, and asks the consent contract for
=renewalDigest(owner,id,terms)=. The insurer calls =propose=, the owner calls
=authorize=, and the owner calls =JubileeFund.renew(id,terms)=. Either party
can cancel its approval by replacing it with zero. A changed credit snapshot,
changed principal, expired deadline or mismatched terms rejects the renewal.

The replacement premium's rounding, any fixed-point floor on fund relief and
its actual effects on value must be tested independently. Other NFTs belonging
to the owner are not implicitly settled. Existing issued principal is never
called back merely because K falls.

* Contract boundaries and validation

- BuckCredit validates the appraisal and regulator envelope using the recorded
  insurer even when called by Buck, and permits rate changes on outstanding
  net-model issuance only through the authorized renewal path. Legacy issuance
  remains unaffected until migration.
- BuckNet revalidates both digests, current terms, owner, actual principal,
  premium limit, funding factor and K. Only the configured fund may execute
  the settlement. Neither the insurer's proposal alone nor a fund call without
  the owner's authorization suffices.
- The fund does not sell during renewal. It prepares the original insurer
  pool outside Buck's lock; reentrancy guards and rollback cover that callback.
- Tested cases include zero-age relief, rejection at 50-year maturity, multiple facilities and insurer pools, K cuts,
  repricing, fresh issuance, fractional rounding, expired/cancelled/stale
  proposals, failed pool liquidity, insufficient funding, cap violations,
  malicious callbacks and the identity/attestation gate. Both BuckNet and
  BuckCredit remain close to the EVM size limit and need a fresh size check
  after future changes. Independent audit, production pool implementations,
  migration and leveraged-basket interaction remain outstanding.
