#+title: Minimal per-block TOKEN-pair observer and execution cycle
#+date: 2026-10-05
#+options: toc:2 num:nil

* Scope and decision

Shelve commodity-cycle identification and spectral response fitting. Retain
the [[file:PAIR-OBSERVER.org][Python experiments]] as evidence, especially their uncertainty failures.
This specification proposes a smaller EVM sidecar beside the existing EMAs:
a two-state relative-price observer, empirical error measures, raw route
discrepancies and one coherent publication per serviced block.

The observation phase is now implemented in
[[file:../src/basket/BlockPairSignals.sol][BlockPairSignals.sol]], with
[[file:../test/basket/BlockPairSignals.t.sol][deterministic and real-V3 tests]]. It uses the existing wheel's SampleKind
interface and has no trading authority. The
[[file:control-lab/block-pairs/index.html][control-lab observation campaign]] and
[[file:control-lab/block-pairs/results.org][measured results]] compare it with the unchanged consumers. The paired basket
phase is now an isolated experiment; standalone cash legs, the combined
coordinator and arb/desk changes remain gated work.

The first 120-day campaign stopped at that gate. Clean-cycle rate RMSE improves
from 159.89 to 80.23 log bp/day versus the existing shortest EMA, but the
heuristic allowance covers only 57.45% of the smooth reference rates. A
deterministic acceleration test reproduces undercoverage without simulated
noise. Noisy-flat rate RMSE is also worse than the EMA comparator, although
the allowance correctly withholds directional declarations in this sample.
The review identified a missing lag timescale: a rate change over one sample
does not describe error accumulated over the filter's response time. Using
the alpha-beta tracker's derived =2*T + dt/2= lag allowance raises clean-cycle
reference coverage to 99.17%, and 20 bp noisy-cycle coverage to 99.09%, without
changing the rate estimates. The original datasets remain in
=baseline-v1-manifest.json=. This supports a separate bounded basket
experiment, not a general claim about profitable trading or arbitrary shocks.

The [[file:../src/basket/PairRebalancePolicy.sol][graded policy]] and
[[file:../src/basket/PairBasketEquityResponse.sol][paired response facet]] now implement that basket experiment. The
[[file:control-lab/pair-basket/index.html][eight matched 120-day runs]] and
[[file:control-lab/pair-basket/results.org][comparison report]] expose a further limit: clean-cycle transfers occur
mostly in the first month, while noisy-flat allocation error worsens. The
basket gate remains open for revision; do not infer repeatable reversion ROI
from the higher terminal equity marks or proceed to combine new consumers.

The first basket run also found two mechanical errors, now fixed and tested:
mark-only work consumed the elapsed trade budget, and waiting for a pair
blocked independent LP placement. The fixed facet uses a separate execution
clock and permits LP placement when no pair trade is ready. Both versions'
JSON remains available through their manifests. The corrected result still
needs policy work, so these fixes do not justify promoting the strategy.

The [[file:control-lab/pair-opportunities/results.org][pre-response opportunity audit]] now traces all 481 responses in each
of the clean and noisy-flat runs, preserving exact economic outcomes. After
day 30, every one of 756 large-separation slowing/closing candidate
pair-samples produces positive intent. Only one fills: 632 are blocked by
requiring both legs beyond their bands, 115 by minimum size, and eight
follow the legacy path. Restricting candidates to trades that improve BOTH
weights and have at least one material band excursion still gives 671
samples: 555 blocked by the second band, 115 too small, one filled.

There is also a directional interpretation error in the consumer. Ten of
45 clean-run fills sell the relatively cheaper TOKEN after the normalized
ratio has crossed its known historical reference. A negative A/B rate is
not always closure: for signed relative displacement =z=, closure requires
=z * dz/dt < 0=. The observer supplies motion; the consumer currently has
no separation/reference term. Defining that reference belongs to the
reversion policy, and must not turn the first observed price into an anchor.
The audit proposes corrections without changing the trading policy.

The smallest useful implementation does not need a fair-price forecast,
commodity period, acceleration state, DFT, dense covariance matrix, or
probability of a profitable reversal. It does need the distinction between
estimated motion, disagreement among venues, and error in its own predictions.
Its consumers retain separate books and objectives.

* Best-effort service and publication

Scheduling is best effort: callers choose fees and attempt sufficient ticks;
neither completion nor ordering relative to other transactions is promised.
Observation currently fits in one bounded wheel work item. Later execution
items may land separately and must validate the observation epoch, age and
current executable prices before using it. A missing later tick cannot spend
or reserve a budget indefinitely.

Assume a keeper or user transaction calls =runBlock()= with enough gas in
each target block, in the proposed combined coordinator. It reads every pool, completes
all pair calculations and publishes one generation before attempting a
bounded set of actions. No other transaction can interleave its execution.
Each action still rechecks the pools it will touch, because earlier actions
inside this transaction change them.

An ordinary contract has no autonomous end-of-block callback. It cannot
guarantee inclusion or know that another transaction will not follow it.
Thus the guarantee is /complete analysis when the service transaction runs/,
not knowledge of the block's eventual final state. A block-builder/sequencer
arrangement would be needed for a stronger ordering promise. See
[[https://docs.soliditylang.org/en/latest/introduction-to-smart-contracts.html#transactions][Solidity's transaction model]].

Use the deployment's native block identifier and elapsed timestamps
separately. Several L2 blocks can share a timestamp: complete their spot and
execution checks, but do not invent elapsed time or assimilate an identical
time-integrated observation again. Calls later in the same block may recheck
execution opportunities, but cannot repeat the observer update or replenish
that block's spending allowance.

The existing =WorkWheel.tick(maxWork,maxScan)= is a bounded round-robin
service, not a dependency-ordered full-block transaction. Its cursor may
start anywhere and its budget may stop before Sample. Keep it for general
maintenance; add a coordinator for this stronger service contract. Calling
the present wheel with larger limits alone does not establish the required
ordering and publication semantics.

The coordinator can be a wheel work item as well as directly callable. Other
transactions can fund its reward, but they cannot extend the gas limit of
the transaction that completes this cycle. Splitting a snapshot over several
transactions introduces changing spot states and a completion dependency on
later callers. For the initial six pools, use one bounded analysis call and
measure its cost before introducing that additional machinery.

* Observable inputs: two channels from the same pools

For N TOKENs and Q admitted quote currencies, read N*Q TOKEN/quote pools.
With three TOKENs and BUCK/USDC this is six pools. Also read BUCK/USDC for
triangle execution and common-mode checks. Pool registration fixes token
orientation, decimals and fee tier; amounts for execution use native units.

| Input | Use |
|-------+-----|
| =observe([0])= tick cumulative | Motion over elapsed, non-overlapping intervals |
| =slot0= sqrt price and tick | Current executable discrepancy and direction checks |
| Current active liquidity | Availability and execution sizing; not proof of price accuracy |
| Known pre/post-trade states | Our immediate intervention and its uncertainty |
| Existing basket quantities/books and raw bvib | Consumer decisions; never observer truth |

Store the last cumulative and its timestamp. For a common interval [t0,t1],
compute an oriented fractional mean tick:

#+begin_example
y[i,q] = orientation[i,q] * (C[i,q](t1) - C[i,q](t0)) / (t1-t0)
#+end_example

Preserve fractional ticks in fixed point rather than flooring each mean to
an int24. Handle the accumulator's signed wrap as the V3 implementation does.
These means are observations at the interval midpoint for the local linear
model. If interval lengths change, propagate from the previous midpoint to
the new midpoint, not simply by the newest interval's width. Twice the
midpoint timestamp avoids half-second rounding.

The natural arithmetic unit is a fractional tick: log price divided by
log(1.0001). Rates are fractional ticks per chosen time unit, consistently
used in gains, error allowances and response sizing. Multiplication by
log(1.0001) converts them to log-return rates; no on-chain logarithm is
needed. Decimal normalization is a registered constant, not a repeated
floating-point calculation.

V3 accumulates tick times elapsed seconds; swaps within a timestamp do not
add an elapsed-time price observation. The cumulative endpoint therefore
separates completed time from the current spot. The pool's
[[https://github.com/Uniswap/v3-core/blob/main/contracts/libraries/Oracle.sol][oracle implementation]] provides the exact behavior. Repeated overlapping
long TWAPs must not be counted as independent measurements each block.
A retained cumulative endpoint also avoids requiring the pool's ring buffer
to retain our previous endpoint after it has been cached.

Read spot separately. Do not feed each same-block spot change into the
time-series estimator as though a new time interval elapsed. Spot departures
remain immediately visible to arb and to execution checks. An interval mean
is delayed information; publish its effective time and age explicitly.

V1 admits a quote venue only when its full TOKEN vector shares both interval
endpoints. If one read fails, withhold that venue's directional update. On
recovery, prime a complete set of endpoints, then collect aligned intervals
and re-establish readiness; do not mix a two-block average from one pool
with one-block averages from the others. An excessive service gap similarly
resets directional readiness. The other complete venue can keep operating,
and all readable spots remain available to execution checks. This deliberately
sacrifices partial-venue coverage to keep the first implementation simple.

* Relative coordinates and no equilibrium anchor

Within each quote currency subtract the mean across TOKEN log prices:

#+begin_example
r[i,q] = y[i,q] - average_over_tokens(y[*,q])
pair[i,j,q] = r[i,q] - r[j,q]
#+end_example

The common quote-currency movement cancels before filtering and before
computing error statistics. A shared USDC fall must not inflate every
TOKEN's relative trend or apparent noise. Normalize constant decimal offsets
when reporting whole-TOKEN log ratios; those offsets do not affect rates.
Different timestamps or windows do not provide exact cancellation.

There are N(N-1)/2 requested pair outputs and N-1 independent relative
coordinates per quote. Filter the TOKEN coordinates with the same gains and
timestamps, then obtain pair estimates by subtraction. This preserves graph
identities such as A/C = A/B + B/C in log coordinates without a matrix solve.
Use one common set of venue weights for the whole vector when publishing a
consensus point estimate. Independently choosing a preferred venue for every
pair can destroy that identity.

No historical mean or pair price of 1 is needed to decide trade direction.
If A is overweight and B underweight, positive d(log(PA/PB))/dt means the
relative run is still outward; slowing or negative motion releases the
desire to transfer A into B. Allocation error belongs to the actual basket
book. A high nominal unit price alone never makes A overweight.

At initialization an observed level may seed /current state/, with zero
assumed velocity and an unready status. It is not an equilibrium anchor.
Use available on-chain history to warm the observer, or accumulate genuine
elapsed observations before enabling directional advice. Do not substitute
the first observation for a long-term fair price or treat zero initial rate
as evidence of a turn.

* Minimal observer: level and rate, with auditable errors

Use a fixed-gain alpha-beta observer for each centered TOKEN/quote series.
It is a two-state tracking filter. The purpose is to retain a rate estimate
and prediction residuals, not to rename a filtered price as a richer signal.
For elapsed midpoint time =dt= and one configured response time =T=:

#+begin_example
k     = dt / (T + dt)
alpha = 2*k - k*k
beta  = k*k
predicted = level + rate*dt
residual  = observation - predicted
level     = predicted + alpha*residual
rate      = rate + beta*residual/dt
#+end_example

For a constant sampling interval the unforced error recurrence has a repeated
pole at =1-k=: these gains give a damped local linear tracker. No fixed gain
is optimal for arbitrary prices. T controls responsiveness, not a presumed
commodity period. Choose and validate T against noise and decision latency;
updating every block does not require forgetting history every block.

Use the same T and gains across TOKENs and venues in the first implementation.
Different commodity dynamics and adaptive gains are deliberately out of scope.
This is smaller and more inspectable than carrying the Python prototype's
acceleration covariance and an unvalidated white-jerk model into Solidity.

For each pair and each venue retain two additional empirical quantities:

#+begin_example
pairResidual = residual[i,q] - residual[j,q]
Eprice = EWMA(abs(pairResidual))
Achange = EWMA(abs(pairRate_now - pairRate_previous) / dt)
#+end_example

The EWMA coefficients use elapsed time and a configured error-memory time.
For example, =g = dt / (Terror + dt)= and =E += g*(sample - E)= need only
fixed-point arithmetic; =T= and =Terror= must be positive.
Compute differences /before/ absolute values so the numeraire cancels.
Eprice measures failed relative-price predictions, including real news and
model lag; it is not a separately identified variance of measurement noise.
Achange measures absolute change in rate per unit time. It is a diagnostic,
not an additional acceleration state used for prediction. The first
implementation instead kept =Vchange = EWMA(abs(delta rate))= and added it
directly to the rate allowance. That covers only a sample's change and failed
the deterministic lag test. The corrected rate allowance is:

#+begin_example
Bv[q] = rateFloor + Eprice[q]/T + (2*T + dt/2)*Achange[q]
rateLo = min_over_valid_venues(pairRate[q] - Bv[q])
rateHi = max_over_valid_venues(pairRate[q] + Bv[q])
#+end_example

This interval covers the route estimates and their heuristic allowances.
For constant acceleration and cadence, =2*T + dt/2= is the exact steady
rate-error timescale of the stated alpha-beta recurrence. Variable dynamics
and shocks still require empirical checks; the coefficient is not fitted
against future simulator truth.
It is NOT a proved bound on latent truth or a calibrated 95% interval.
Similarly publish the range of route levels, current spot discrepancies,
and the per-route prediction errors separately. Do not divide an error by
sqrt(number of pools): two arbitraged venues need not be independent.
Two venues cannot, by disagreement alone, identify which price is wrong.

The value of this minimum is testable: price change /large compared with/
prediction error, agreement or conflict across routes, reliable outward
motion, and its cessation. A calibrated Kalman covariance is a later optional
upgrade to the same interface if measured coverage justifies it.

Tick granularity needs an explicit floor. Do not let quiet integer ticks
drive error estimates to zero. Fixed-point storage can pack level/rate and
the two error measures; arithmetic intermediates should remain 256-bit,
with checked range assumptions and configured limits on elapsed gaps.

The mean tick represents the last interval, not a measurement of this
instant's rate. Small block intervals make that delay small. After a longer
gap, merely extrapolating the level to now cannot restore missing information.
Age/readiness limits must reduce or disable directional advice independently
of whether the filter arithmetic can still produce a number.

* Pair publication and sizing semantics

A publication should expose at least:

#+begin_example
epoch, blockId, effectiveTime, availableVenueMask, readiness
pairLevel, pairRate, rateLo, rateHi
venueLevelSpread, venueRateSpread, predictionError
changeInOutwardRate, ownImpactUncertainty, qualityFlags
#+end_example

The minimal consensus is an equal-weight mean across eligible complete
venue vectors. Error envelopes preserve disagreements instead of hiding
them in that mean. A quote-route change invalidates continuity evidence
until re-established; it must not manufacture a turn. With one surviving
route, mark the loss of corroboration and retain an uncertainty allowance.

For A overweight and B underweight:

- Clearly positive A/B rate, sustained or increasing: quench discretionary
  transfer. Hard exposure or credit limits remain separate overrides.
- Positive but declining outward rate: gradually release quenching; size
  initial requests conservatively when the error envelope is wide.
- Envelope spanning zero: cessation is plausible, not confirmed closure.
  Allow only the configured small exploratory fraction of the allocation gap.
- Negative rate with an envelope below zero: a stronger closure signal;
  increase participation with closure speed and the remaining gap.

No rule promises a future reversal or a profitable holding period. All
requests must also clear fees and execution limits. Declining point estimates
with conflicting routes warrant less size than the same decline with route
agreement and small prediction residuals.

First size a seller's total release from its remaining excess allocation.
Split that release among eligible underweight recipients in proportion to
their deficits. Their pair rates affect eligibility and total urgency; they
must not independently authorize selling the same excess several times.
Net/check all donor and recipient capacities before executing. Recompute
capacity after each fill, even when the directional publication is reused.

Use elapsed-time turnover budgets, not a new full percentage of the gap for
every invocation. A cheap participation fraction is =u/(1+u)=, where
=u = responseRate * elapsedTime=. At small u this approaches
=responseRate * elapsedTime= and at large u stays below one. This avoids an
exponential in Solidity. The response rate increases as departure slows and
then with closure strength; calibrating that policy is separate from measuring
motion. Bound catch-up after missed blocks and share the allowance across
all attempts in the same block. A faster chain or an extra caller must not
multiply the intended daily turnover.

If no counter-TOKEN qualifies, an overweight TOKEN may be sold into the
basket's permitted cash reserve or used to repay debt, when standalone
divestment is justified. With no counterpair, compare its relative rate with
the TOKEN basket, using the existing pair rates and fixed snapshot weights.
An underweight TOKEN may be accumulated from available cash when its
standalone signal qualifies. Underweight status is not a reason to sell it.
Holding cash is allowed; compulsory immediate reinvestment would undo the
purpose of waiting for favorable relative motion.

* Three consumers, three objectives

| Consumer | Signal | Execution condition |
|----------+--------+---------------------|
| Basket | Relative rates/errors + actual allocation gaps | Bounded reversion-oriented transfer or cash leg, within inventory, credit, exposure, fees and depth limits |
| Basket arb | Current raw cross-venue routes | Atomic completed cycle with realized profit above the configured minimum after pool fees |
| Desk | Raw basket-value-in-BUCK and live execution prices | BUCK beyond outer entry band; spend only toward release boundary, respecting programme and capital limits |

Arb does not wait for a turn, good temporal model fit, or fresh historical
valuation when an atomic cycle can prove positive receipts. A large pool
dislocation is potentially its opportunity. The current =ArbKind= already
provides bounded triangle execution and a realized-profit check. A gas-profit
claim additionally requires a gas-cost valuation or the caller's own economic
decision; a positive TOKEN receipt alone is not net profit after gas.

For the desk, =bvib < 1= means BUCK is dear: sell/supply BUCK within the
outer-band policy. =bvib > 1= means BUCK is cheap: buy/absorb BUCK, including
repayment where applicable. Keep the existing hysteresis, inventory/credit
caps, finite turnover budget, capital floor and persistence requirement for
issuance. A post-trade check must prevent crossing the release boundary.
The residual interior error remains visible to K's existing P/I controller.
Relative TOKEN ratios cannot replace that common-mode monetary signal.

* Ordered coordinator, bounded actions

An initial implementation should do all analysis but limit how many trades
are attempted. Full observation coverage is different from executing every
possible trade in a block.

#+begin_example
runBlock():
    acquire reentrancy guard; reserve gas for finalization
    if this block is not analyzed:
        snapshot all registered pools at common endpoints
        update elapsed-time observer once
        compute all pair diagnostics; publish complete epoch/status
    attempt a bounded initial set of profitable atomic arb cycles
    compute K if due, using its existing validated raw reference
    refresh desk advice; attempt its bounded operation if eligible
    re-mark basket; compute and net bounded allocation requests
    execute bounded basket transfers/cash legs, rechecking each leg
    optionally attempt bounded arb on pools changed by these actions
    record final pool states and actual execution/account deltas
#+end_example

This is a default ordering to test, not a claim of optimal transaction
ordering. Arb first can reduce venue conflict before inventory-taking
operations. It must not consume the gas/work budget needed by other classes.
Each consumer owns its accounting and authorization; the coordinator cannot
spend desk capital as a basket budget or count arb profit twice.

Re-read executable quotes after preceding actions. Do not re-assimilate
same-timestamp cumulative data or iterate observer/trade/observer until it
manufactures consensus. A planned TOKEN-to-TOKEN transfer should execute
atomically in an isolated call with total-input/min-output limits. A failed
second leg must not silently become an unintended cash divestment. Explicit
cash legs have their own authorization and accounting.

Expected execution refusals should be caught at bounded call boundaries so
they do not discard a completed analysis. A gas-exhausted top-level transaction
cannot preserve partial work. Admission limits on N, Q and action counts,
bounded per-pool reads, and gas benchmarks are prerequisites for the
run-to-completion promise. A completed epoch can report unavailable pairs;
it cannot manufacture valid observations for a broken or empty pool.

* Our transactions and the next observation

At the exact timestamp of our swap, changing spot contributes no additional
elapsed time to the current cumulative observation. That removes the most
immediate self-confirmation loop. But the post-trade price persists into the
next interval and can affect it. The next state is not automatically free
of our impact.

Instrument execution contracts or their coordinator to read the pools
immediately before and after their own calls. EVM contracts cannot query old
transaction receipt logs as an on-chain database. Record signed tick changes,
timestamps and the affected pools, including changed intermediate pools.
These give known immediate impact, not a known counterfactual future path.

V1 should publish the intervention flags and a conservative decaying
uncertainty allowance. Do not infer an external reversal solely from an
interval dominated by our own recent trades. When a specific impact-decay
model has been validated, integrate its predicted offset over the SAME
interval as the cumulative observation and subtract that average once.
Subtracting the full immediate jump forever, or subtracting a spot offset
from an interval average, is incorrect. Compare corrected and uncorrected
estimates before relying on such correction for execution.

* Size, integration and acceptance

For N=3, Q=2 the core directional state is 12 level/rate scalars plus 12
pair/venue error scalars: six pool series and three pairs per venue.
There are also six cached cumulative endpoints, timestamps, readiness,
intervention state and configured pool metadata. Values can be packed, and
pair consensus outputs can be computed in memory or on demand. These are
state counts, not measured gas costs.

The update requires O(N*Q) pool observation/filter work and
O(N*(N-1)*Q/2) pair diagnostics. For the initial configuration there are only
six pair-route evaluations. There is no cubic matrix factorization, FFT,
normal-distribution function or unbounded loop in the proposed minimum.
Repeated pool reads and storage writes are likely to matter more than the
few arithmetic operations; that expectation must be benchmarked.

Implementation boundaries:

- New =BlockPairSignals= sidecar and small fixed-point tracking library;
  do not change the existing EMA service's meaning or silently substitute
  its output into K.
- New dependency-ordered coordinator; reuse existing consumers' accounting,
  arb settlement and desk guards. Integrate the paired basket plan through
  a dedicated response adapter, with explicit standalone cash actions.
- Retain the old EMA and Python estimators as comparison instruments.
  Archive the spectral arm; do not continue tuning commodity-cycle parameters.

Acceptance before execution authority:

1. Common-currency moves cancel in rates AND error statistics, including
   mixed token decimals/orientations. Pair graph identities remain coherent.
2. Constant levels, ramps, slowing departure, reversals, noisy flats,
   genuine jumps and correlated venue disturbances give inspectable outputs.
   Measure empirical error-band coverage instead of assuming it.
3. Multiple calls, swaps and callback reentry within a block cannot manufacture
   time, repeat assimilation, or duplicate a spending allowance. Test equal
   timestamps across different block identifiers and irregular service gaps.
4. Real V3 cumulative/spot behavior is tested across own swaps, failed swaps,
   missing history, zero liquidity and accumulator wrap. An outlier valuation
   must not disable independently profitable atomic arb.
5. Multi-counter allocations obey joint donor/recipient capacities. Paired
   trades roll back together; intentional cash trades stay separately visible.
6. Desk trades stay outside the release band; K still receives raw residual
   error. No consumer can consume another's capital or counted profit.
7. Benchmark complete cold/warm analysis and worst-case bounded execution
   with real pools, including failure paths, against the deployment's actual
   transaction/block gas limits and funded caller incentives.

* Staged implementation and experiment gates

1. Observation: install the passive sidecar, preserve the old EMA and all
   consumers, and show relative spot prices, levels, rates, prediction errors,
   route disagreement, readiness and receipt gas. Require exact economic
   equality to observer-off controls and inspect both clean and noisy-flat
   signals. Native-block cadence is tested separately from long-horizon
   six-hour market experiments.
2. Basket: only after the observation findings support the proposed direction
   gate, add graded release and joint donor/recipient budgets. Compare actual
   fills, allocation error, equity, leverage and monetary outcomes against
   the same initial world. Signal RMSE alone cannot establish reversion ROI.
3. Arb: isolate execution/sizing changes and injected single-pool disturbances.
   Require increased net receipts or reduced execution cost under matched
   opportunity and capital budgets. Do not give temporal-filter readiness a
   veto over an independently profitable atomic route.
4. Desk: isolate outer-band response changes. Require preserved capital and
   programme constraints, measurable premium/discount response, and continued
   P error for K. Keep the basket and arb choices fixed during this comparison.

Stop at the first invalidated assumption and record it, rather than enabling
later consumers behind a misleading signal. In particular, a rate allowance
that often excludes the known reference cannot be treated as calibrated
error evidence merely because the plotted rate looks smoother. Intervention
flags/correction and a consumer's memory of signed rate changes are required
before its own trades can contribute to directional advice; the passive
phase deliberately grants no such execution authority.
