#+BEGIN_ABSTRACT
The Alberta Buck /Wormhole/ ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-wormhole][Wormhole]]) and /Cash/
([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-cash][Cash]]) designs both need
a privacy-preserving bridge from an on-chain balance to a later on-chain mint.  In
the current series both designs supply their own mechanics: the Wormhole uses
per-certificate burn addresses and =_mint_noincrease=; Cash uses a Zerocash-style
commitment tree with Mode A/B/C spend predicates (bearer, stealth-targeted,
programmable).  The overlap is nearly complete -- both reduce to "burn into a
pool, mint out by SNARK" -- and maintaining two architectures doubles the
verifier surface, the circuit count, and the audit scope without buying
additional functionality.

This document collapses the two into one primitive.  A single Merkle tree of
note commitments and a single nullifier set replace both the burn-address
family and the Cash note pool.  Three /Identity bindings/ parameterise the
commitment:

- *Type 1 -- Encrypted Issuer Identity.*  Spend predicate requires the depositor's
  registered ElGamal credential to encrypt the same identity point the issuer's
  credential encrypts.  Subsumes the v1 Wormhole: the issuer and the eventual
  depositor must share an Identity scalar, but may use unlinkable key pairs.
- *Type 2 -- Public Issuer Identity.*  Spend predicate requires the depositor
  to be any registered identity; the issuer's public identity is bound to the
  note in plaintext.  A cheque whose maker is known, whose bearer is not.
- *Type 3 -- Stealth-Targeted to Recipient.*  Spend predicate requires the
  depositor to hold the secret of a stealth public key derived from the
  recipient's registered identity.  A cheque made out to a specific recipient,
  retrievable only by them.

Demurrage needs no new machinery.  The Note Pool /is/ simply the BUCK
ERC-20 contract's own balance -- a regular BUCK account accumulating
BUCK-age like any other account.  The contract tracks just one additional
scalar beyond the Merkle tree and nullifier set: \( F \), the sum of
face values of live (unspent) notes.  When a note of face \( v \) is
redeemed, the contract delivers
\( v_{\text{delivered}} = v \cdot (B - \kappa A)/F \) BUCK to the recipient,
where \( B \) and \( A \) are the contract account's balance and accumulated
BUCK-age in the existing ledger -- quantities already maintained by the
ERC-20 demurrage machinery for every account.  Donations sent to the
contract address (deliberate, philanthropic, or accidental) raise \( B \)
without raising \( F \), so subsequent redemptions deliver more than the
equilibrium pool-rate -- possibly more than face value.  Conservation is
automatic: demurrage is never actually transferred; the Jubilee balance
grows autonomously per block at rate \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \),
and every account (pool included) reduces its spendable balance by its
own \( \kappa A \) on outgoing transfers.  No Little's Law, no continuous
Jubilee stream, no separate pool obligation.

The consequence most relevant to users: only notes where the issuer cannot
later race the holder to spend are safe for long-term holding.  This is
*Type 1* (issuer = depositor, same Identity, no third party knows the note
secret) and *Type 3* (the issuer's knowledge of the note secret does not
suffice to spend -- only the stealth-derived secret can).  Type 2's public-issuer
attribution comes at the cost of retained issuer-spend capability, restricting
it to short-lived use cases (cheques presented within hours or days, not
stored for years).  This is an intrinsic cryptographic property, not a policy
choice. ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/images/alberta-buck-notes.pdf][PDF]],
[[https://perry.kundert.ca/images/alberta-buck-notes.txt][Text]])

* The Redundancy Between Wormhole and Cash

  The v1 Wormhole ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-wormhole][Wormhole]]) and
  the BUCK Cash proposal ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-cash][Cash]])
  both solve the same core problem by different mechanical routes.

** The Problem Both Solve

   Each requires a cryptographic pipeline that:
   1. Accepts a burn of an existing on-chain balance
   2. Creates a durable, privacy-preserving token (certificate or note)
      referencing the burn
   3. Permits a later on-chain mint that can be proved (in zero
      knowledge) to correspond to some prior burn
   4. Prevents double-spending via a global nullifier
   5. Preserves BUCK's demurrage economics (the minted amount must
      reflect accumulated demurrage across the burn-to-mint interval)
   6. Composes with the existing Identity system (so that the mint
      lands in a properly registered, KYC-verified account)

** The Two Current Mechanisms

   *Wormhole v1 (burn-address model).*  Each certificate is anchored
   by a distinct EVM address that receives a fixed-denomination
   transfer.  The burn is an ordinary =transfer= call; the ZK proof
   binds the destination mint to the source burn through the
   =_mint_noincrease= primitive that transfers balance and BUCK-age
   into the destination without adjusting =totalSupply=.  Each burn
   address costs an identity registration, an =approveFor=
   handshake, and a state-trie slot.

   *BUCK Cash (note-pool model).*  A contract-managed Merkle tree of
   note commitments replaces the per-burn-address state anchor.  A
   single burn transaction appends \( N \) commitments to the tree
   and emits the summed value into the contract reserve.  Each spend
   is a SNARK proving inclusion of some commitment and emission of a
   unique nullifier.  The spend /predicate/ inside the commitment
   distinguishes bearer (Mode A), stealth-targeted (Mode B), and
   programmable (Mode C) notes.

** The Overlap

   Functionally these are nearly identical.  Both provide
   commit-nullify privacy; both bind the burn to a later mint through
   a ZK relation; both produce one nullifier per unit redeemed; both
   depend on the Identity system for the minting-account side.  The
   divergence is cosmetic:

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Wormhole v1 anchor   | Cash note-pool anchor       |
   |----------------------+-----------------------------|
   | =addr_burn= (state trie leaf) | =cm_i= (Merkle tree leaf) |
   | =_mint_noincrease= to destination | =mint= directly (supply unchanged by note pool) |
   | Per-burn =approveFor= + =approve=  | No per-note approval (spend predicate) |
   | Fixed-denomination cohort anonymity set | Global pool anonymity set |
   | Spend relation: CP re-encryption (Theorems 3-6) | Spend relation: predicate-specific (Theorems 4, 7) |
   #+LATEX: }

   What neither design fully expresses is that the /spend relation/
   is orthogonal to the pool structure: Chaum-Pedersen re-encryption,
   stealth-address Schnorr, and arbitrary predicates can /all/ coexist
   as different spend circuits on the same note pool.  If we take
   this seriously, burn addresses and =_mint_noincrease= become
   redundant: the note pool absorbs the Wormhole as one spend-predicate
   family among many.

** Maintenance Cost of Duality

   Keeping both mechanisms in production doubles:
   - *Verifier code.*  Two Merkle-style lookups, two nullifier-set
     discipline rules, two edge-case sets for the auditor.
   - *SNARK circuits.*  The Wormhole CP-re-encryption circuit and
     the Cash bearer/targeted/programmable circuits do not share
     skeleton code; the proof systems compile separately.
   - *Demurrage bookkeeping.*  The Wormhole's per-certificate
     BUCK-age is computed against EVM storage; the Cash note's
     per-commitment \( (t_{\text{print}}, age_0) \) is computed in
     the SNARK.  Two distinct conservation invariants.
   - *Identity integration.*  Wormhole requires =approveFor= against
     the burn-address pseudo-account; Cash predicates compose
     differently with Schnorr authorisations.
   - *Documentation.*  Two designs to teach, two attack tables to
     maintain, two sets of open questions.

   A unified architecture reduces all of this to one audit, one
   circuit family, one invariant.

* The Single Primitive: Notes in One Global Pool

  The unified design replaces both mechanisms with a single primitive.

** The Note Contract State

   The BUCK contract gains exactly three new pieces of state:

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | State           | Type                       | Purpose                                         |
   |-----------------+----------------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
   | =noteTree=      | Merkle tree of commitments | Cryptographic anchor for every outstanding note |
   | =nullifiers=    | Set of used nullifiers     | Double-spend prevention                         |
   | =noteFaceSum=   | \( F \) in \( \mathbb{Z} \) | Sum of face values of all outstanding notes    |
   #+LATEX: }

   That is all.  The pool's nominal BUCK balance \( B \) and accumulated
   BUCK-age \( A \) are not tracked as separate state: /the pool is just
   a BUCK account/, specifically the ERC-20 contract's own address, and
   \( B, A \) are exactly the balance and BUCK-age the ERC-20 ledger
   already maintains for it.  No lazy integration, no shadow aggregates,
   no separate =poolT= timestamp.  BUCK transferred to the contract
   address (via mint, donation, or accident) joins \( B \) through the
   normal ERC-20 path; BUCK transferred out (via redemption) leaves
   through the normal ERC-20 path with the normal demurrage accounting.

** The Note Commitment

   A note commitment is a Poseidon hash of the fields the SNARK will
   later open:

   \[
   cm \;=\; H_{\text{cm}}\!\left(\text{type},\; v,\; \rho,\; \text{id}_{\text{payload}},\; \text{predicate}\right)
   \]

   with:

   - *type* \( \in \{1, 2, 3\} \): the Identity-binding mode
   - *v* \( \in \mathbb{Z}_q \): face value
   - *\( \rho \)*: per-note randomness (source of the nullifier)
   - *\( \text{id}_{\text{payload}} \)*: Identity-binding data (see next section)
   - *predicate*: optional additional spend predicate (for future Mode C
     programmability -- not elaborated in this document; a single bit
     =predicate == None= keeps the SNARK to the three base types)

   No per-note timestamp or baked-in BUCK-age: demurrage is computed at
   redemption time from the /pool's/ account state
   \( (B, A) \), not from the individual note's life history, so no
   per-note age fields need to ride in the commitment.

   The commitment is inserted as a leaf of =noteTree= at mint time.

** The Note Nullifier

   The nullifier is a pseudo-random function of the note's secret
   witness material:

   \[
   nf \;=\; \text{PRF}_{\text{key}}(\rho)
   \]

   where =key= is the spend-side secret specific to the note type
   (the issuer's \( sk \) in Type 1, any registered spender's \( sk \)
   in Type 2, the stealth \( sk^\ast \) in Type 3).  The PRF is
   instantiated via Poseidon in the SNARK-friendly setting.

   The nullifier is emitted on deposit; double-spend is rejected by
   set-membership check.

** Lifecycle

   #+begin_example
   mint (burn):
     # Standard ERC-20 transfer from caller to contract address.
     # Caller pays their own outgoing-transfer demurrage through the
     # normal BUCK accounting path.  Contract balance B gains v; its
     # accumulated BUCK-age A starts earning on the new v from here.
     transfer v BUCK from caller to contract
     for each of {cm_1, ..., cm_N}:
        append cm_i to noteTree
     noteFaceSum += sum(v_i)

   deposit (mint-out):
     verify SNARK:
       - Merkle inclusion of some cm in noteTree
       - Knowledge of (v, rho, id_payload, predicate)
       - Predicate satisfied by witness (type-specific)
       - nullifier correctness
       - v is a public output of the SNARK
     require nf not in nullifiers
     nullifiers += nf
     # Contract reads its own balance and BUCK-age from the ERC-20 ledger:
     B = balanceOf(contract)
     A = buckAgeOf(contract)
     delivered = v * (B - kappa * A) / noteFaceSum
     # Standard ERC-20 transfer from contract to recipient.  Contract's
     # demurrage accounting on (B, A) is updated by the normal BUCK path;
     # no special-case bookkeeping.
     transfer delivered BUCK from contract to recipient
     noteFaceSum -= v
   #+end_example

   The deposit amount is /less than face/ by the pool's current
   demurrage-per-face-unit \( \kappa A / F \), but may equal or exceed
   \( v \) if donations have pushed \( B > F \) (see "Donations and
   Negative Effective Demurrage" below).  No Jubilee payment is emitted
   from inside the deposit: the Jubilee grows autonomously on every
   block at rate \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \), and the
   contract's share of that growth is already reflected in \( A \).

* Three Identity Bindings

  Every note commits to one of three /Identity-binding/ payloads.  The
  binding determines who can spend the note and what observers learn
  about the issuer.

** Type 1: Encrypted Issuer Identity ("Wormhole")

   *Identity payload.*
   \[
   \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)} \;=\; E_{\text{issuer}} \;=\; (R_i, C_i) \;=\; (r_i G,\; M_{\text{issuer}} + r_i \cdot pk_{\text{issuer}})
   \]

   where \( pk_{\text{issuer}} \) is the issuer's own identity public key
   at mint time, \( M_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \), and
   \( r_i \) is fresh randomness.  This is a standard ElGamal encryption
   of the issuer's Identity point under their own key -- the same
   structure registered credentials use.

   *Spend predicate.*  A depositor with registered credential
   \( E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d) \) under their own \( pk_{\text{dep}} \)
   submits a SNARK proving the Chaum-Pedersen relation
   \( C_i - sk_{\text{issuer}} R_i = C_d - sk_{\text{dep}} R_d \).  That
   is, the two credentials decrypt to the same Identity point.

   This is Theorem 6 of the proofs document ([[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Proofs]]) lifted verbatim
   into the SNARK; a SNARK-verified instance of the CP relation implies
   \( m_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{dep}} \), i.e., /same underlying
   Identity/.

   *Consequence.*  Only accounts whose underlying Identity scalar
   matches the issuer's can spend a Type 1 note.  This is the Wormhole
   semantics: Alice mints a Type 1 note from address \( A_1 \) and later
   deposits at address \( A_2 \), where both addresses carry Alice's
   Identity (different registered credentials, same \( M \)).  Observers
   cannot link the two addresses beyond the Identity-constant relation
   they already have.

** Type 2: Public Issuer Identity ("Signed Cheque")

   *Identity payload.*
   \[
   \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(2)} \;=\; (m_{\text{issuer}},\; \sigma_{\text{issuer}})
   \]

   where \( m_{\text{issuer}} \in \mathbb{Z}_q \) is the issuer's
   Identity scalar /in plaintext/ and \( \sigma_{\text{issuer}} \) is a
   Schnorr signature on \( cm \) under the issuer's registered
   \( pk_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \) (or a signed
   commitment to the note; details equivalent).

   Anyone reading the commitment openings learns who issued the note:
   the Identity point \( M_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \)
   is in the clear, and since this is a public-identity account, its
   real-world identity is world-readable from the IdentityRegistry.

   *Spend predicate.*  The SNARK proves:

   1. Knowledge of \( \rho \) such that \( nf = \text{PRF}_\rho(\cdot) \)
   2. \( \sigma_{\text{issuer}} \) verifies against the registered
      \( pk_{\text{issuer}} \) in IdentityRegistry (SNARK-inside
      signature verification)
   3. The depositor is /some/ registered identity: Schnorr proof of
      knowledge of \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for a \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) that
      IdentityRegistry attests to (anonymous-valid) or whose public
      identity is published (public)

   No CP-equality is required -- the issuer's identity is already known,
   the depositor's identity is anyone properly registered.

   *Consequence.*  Any registered identity can deposit.  The issuer
   retains the right to spend their own note (by virtue of knowing
   \( \rho \)), so holders of Type 2 notes bear /printer-race risk/: if
   the issuer kept \( \rho \), they can race the holder to deposit.
   Type 2 is appropriate for transactions that close within hours or
   days, not long-term holding.  See section "Long-Term Holding Safety".

** Type 3: Stealth-Targeted to Recipient ("Addressed Cheque")

   *Identity payload.*
   \[
   \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(3)} \;=\; (R_e,\; pk^\ast)
   \]

   where \( R_e = r_e G \) is an ephemeral published with the note and

   \[
   pk^\ast \;=\; H_{\text{st}}(r_e \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}}) \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}}.
   \]

   \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \) is /not/ in the payload -- it would reveal
   the target to observers.  Only the recipient, scanning notes with
   their own \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \), can recognise a note as
   addressed to them: for each candidate note they compute
   \( s = sk_{\text{recipient}} \cdot R_e \) and check whether
   \( H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \) equals the note's
   \( pk^\ast \) (see Corollary 3 of Theorem 7 in [[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Proofs]]).

   *Spend predicate.*  The SNARK proves knowledge of a scalar \( \alpha \)
   with \( \alpha \cdot G = pk^\ast \).  By Theorem 7 this implies the
   prover knows the stealth secret
   \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot sk_{\text{recipient}} \),
   which reduces to knowing \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \) (the issuer
   cannot compute \( sk^\ast \) from \( r_e, pk_{\text{recipient}} \)
   alone without breaking DLog).

   The depositor also produces a Schnorr PoK of knowledge of
   \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for their own registered \( pk_{\text{dep}} \),
   binding the deposit to a compliant mint-out identity.  Optionally,
   the recipient's credential \( E_{\text{recipient}} \) can be used
   directly: the SNARK proves CP-equality between the decrypted
   \( M_{\text{recipient}} \) and the depositor's registered
   \( E_{\text{dep}} \), making the deposit identity-binding.  The
   minimal-privacy variant omits this and lets any registered depositor
   submit the stealth-spend (suitable when the recipient wants
   pseudonymous redemption to a fresh account).

   *Consequence.*  Only the recipient -- the holder of
   \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \) -- can spend the note.  The issuer
   /cannot/ spend what she printed (Theorem 7, Corollary 1 -- "printer
   non-fungibility").  Third parties who see the published note learn
   nothing about \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \).  Type 3 is safe for
   long-term holding because the issuer's knowledge of \( r_e \) does
   not translate into spending power.

** Comparison of the Three Bindings

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Property                     | Type 1 Encrypted Issuer               | Type 2 Public Issuer                   | Type 3 Stealth-Targeted       |
   |------------------------------+---------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+-------------------------------|
   | Issuer known to observers    | No (ElGamal-encrypted)                | Yes (plaintext)                        | No (not in payload)           |
   | Recipient known to observers | No (anyone with same \(M\) can spend) | No (any registered identity can spend) | No (stealth addr)             |
   | Observer links burn-mint     | Only via timing; not by identity      | Public flow with anonymous recipient   | Not at all                    |
   | Issuer can spend own note    | Yes (=Wormhole= self-case)            | Yes (knows \(\rho\))                   | No (Theorem 7 Corollary 1)    |
   | Long-term holding safe       | Yes (issuer = intended spender)       | No (issuer race)                       | Yes (printer non-fungibility) |
   | Replaces                     | Wormhole v1 certificates              | Bank cheques, attribution payments     | Cash Mode B targeted notes    |
   | Spend-circuit soundness      | Theorem 6 (CP-equality)               | Schnorr + signature-in-SNARK           | Theorem 7 (stealth Schnorr)   |
   #+LATEX: }

   The three together span every existing use case in the
   wormhole/cash series.  The Cash /Mode A/ (pure bearer, no identity
   hook at all) is intentionally absent from this scheme; see "What
   Gets Dropped" below.

* The Interaction Matrix: Note Binding x Account Mode

  The current Identity system ([[https://perry.kundert.ca/range/finance/alberta-buck-identity][Identity]]) distinguishes two
  registered-account visibility modes:

  - *Anonymous-valid.*  The account is provably KYC-verified but the
    underlying Identity is encrypted.  Counterparties need a bilateral
    =approve= handshake to learn who they are dealing with.
  - *Public.*  The account publishes its Identity claim in the clear.
    Counterparties see the real-world Identity without an =approve= step.

  Permissionless accounts are excluded: they have no registered
  Identity and therefore cannot be either the minting side of a note
  nor the depositing side.  Every cell below requires at least one
  endpoint to be a registered identity.

  Crossing the three note bindings with the two account modes at
  deposit time produces six cells.  Each row corresponds to a note
  /type/; each column corresponds to the /depositor account/ mode.

  #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
  | Note type           | Anonymous-valid depositor                   | Public depositor                                  |

  |                     | Alice (issuer) and Alice' (depositor)       | Alice (issuer, private) deposits to her public    |

  |                     | Issuer (any) targets a specific             | Issuer targets a public identity.  The public     |
  |                     | anonymous-valid recipient.  Only recipient  | identity is still not in the payload (stealth     |

  Several observations:

  1. *The Wormhole's full functionality (Type 1, Cell 1 and 2)*
     collapses to a single note-pool spend circuit.  No burn
     addresses, no =_mint_noincrease=, no approval machinery for
     pseudo-accounts.

  2. *Public-issuer cheques (Type 2)* supply the long-missing
     "cashier's cheque" primitive: a public issuer commits to a
     transfer that any registered holder can realise.  This is the
     natural form for payroll issuance, charitable disbursements, and
     attribution-preserving payments that current BUCK flow lacks.

  3. *Stealth-targeted cheques (Type 3)* preserve the "personal
     cheque" semantics from BUCK Cash's Mode B while gaining the
     property that the depositor can be a distinct /account/ from
     their registered identity if desired (the SNARK binds the
     stealth secret, not the specific deposit address).

  4. *Public-to-public transfers via Type 2* (Cell 2.2) are a
     degenerate case: the same information flow could happen via a
     direct public =transfer=.  The value of Cell 2.2 is the
     /timing/: the cheque can be printed, mailed, or held by the
     issuer before the recipient decides which of their public
     accounts to deposit to.

  5. *Account modes are independent of issuance*: the issuer's
     account visibility at mint time and the depositor's account
     visibility at deposit time are chosen independently.  The matrix
     above shows the /depositor/ dimension; a full 3x2x2 enumeration
     would duplicate most cells without adding structural insight.

* Demurrage at the Pool Level

  The central simplification of this design: *the Note Pool is just a
  regular BUCK account.*  Specifically, it is the BUCK ERC-20 contract's
  own balance.  BUCKs transferred to the contract's address join the
  pool; BUCKs transferred out (as note redemptions) leave the pool.  The
  contract account accumulates BUCK-age like every other account and
  participates in the ordinary demurrage accounting with no special-case
  code.  The only piece of additional state is a single scalar \( F \),
  the sum of face values of live notes.

** Reminder: How BUCK Demurrage Actually Works

   Demurrage is /not transferred/ on any per-account basis.  It is an
   accounting entry maintained on each account's state:

   - Every block, the Jubilee account's balance grows by
     \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \cdot \Delta t \).  This is a
     single global operation; the sum of all other accounts' nominal
     balances is held constant.
   - Each account \( X \) has a running BUCK-age \( A_X \) that grows
     at rate \( B_X \) (its nominal balance) per unit time.
   - When account \( X \) transfers \( v \) BUCK out, its spendable
     fraction is \( (B_X - \kappa A_X)/B_X \); the outgoing transfer
     costs the account \( v \cdot B_X / (B_X - \kappa A_X) \) of its
     nominal balance, and the corresponding \( \kappa A_X \cdot v /
     (B_X - \kappa A_X) \) demurrage "owed" is simply extinguished
     from \( (B_X, A_X) \) against the Jubilee's autonomous growth.

   System-wide invariant (preserved by every transfer):

   \[
   \sum_{\text{all accounts } X} B_X \;+\; B_{\text{Jubilee}}
   \;=\; \text{constant}.
   \]

   The Jubilee balance reflects the aggregate accrual
   \( \kappa \int_0^t \text{totalSupply}(s)\,ds \); individual accounts'
   \( \kappa A_X \) quantities track /their share/ of that total.

** The Pool Account's Demurrage

   Treat the BUCK contract address as an ordinary account.  Let:

   - \( B \) = the contract's ERC-20 balance (= sum of all BUCK
     transferred in via mints and donations, minus all BUCK
     transferred out via redemptions, modulo the standard demurrage
     adjustment on outgoing transfers).
   - \( A \) = the contract's accumulated BUCK-age, as maintained by
     the ERC-20 ledger.

   \( B \) and \( A \) are already on chain; the Notes design queries
   them, it does not compute them.  The pool's /effective spendable
   value/ at any moment is

   \[
   E \;=\; B - \kappa \cdot A.
   \]

   This is the amount the contract would be able to transfer out if it
   emptied itself in a single call -- exactly the same definition that
   applies to any other account.

** The Deposit Formula

   When a note of face \( v \) is redeemed, the contract delivers

   \[
   v_{\text{delivered}} \;=\; v \cdot \frac{E}{F} \;=\; v \cdot \frac{B - \kappa A}{F}.
   \]

   The interpretation is exact: each live-note face-unit is backed by
   the pool's effective value divided equally across all outstanding
   face.  A deposit claims its proportional share.

   Equivalently, the effective per-note demurrage rate is
   \( 1 - E/F = 1 - (B - \kappa A)/F \).  In the "ideal" case where
   \( B = F \) (every BUCK in the pool corresponds to a live note's
   face value, no donations or losses), this reduces to
   \( \kappa A / B \) -- exactly the pool account's own demurrage rate,
   which is the BUCK-wide \( \kappa \) scaled by the pool's average
   BUCK-age-per-balance.

** Donations and Negative Effective Demurrage

   Any BUCK transferred to the contract address /without/ minting a
   corresponding note increases \( B \) but not \( F \).  This happens
   naturally in several ways:

   1. *Accidental transfers.*  A user fat-fingers the contract address
      as a recipient.
   2. *Philanthropic donations.*  A user deliberately sends BUCK to
      the contract to subsidise the pool.
   3. *Recovered collateral.*  Mechanism-level refunds (e.g., an
      issuer's collateral unlock flowing into the pool) sent to the
      contract address.
   4. *Rounding / dust.*  Accumulated fractional-unit residuals from
      integer arithmetic in redemption divisions.

   Each such transfer raises \( E/F \).  If donations are large enough
   that \( E/F > 1 \), the next redemption delivers /more/ than its
   face value: "negative effective demurrage".  This is not a bug --
   it is the mathematically clean outcome of the pool being
   over-backed relative to its note obligations, and the excess is
   distributed pro-rata across live notes as they are redeemed.

   The property is desirable:

   - /Donations land./  Philanthropists can cheaply subsidise all
     outstanding noteholders.  There is no accidental burn sink: a
     donation is consumed by the next round of redemptions.
   - /Accidents self-heal./  A misdirected transfer to the contract
     doesn't vanish; it becomes a pro-rata benefit to live
     noteholders (who may include the sender).
   - /Over-collateralisation is cheap to express./  An issuer wishing
     to guarantee "notes redeem at \( \geq 1.01 \times \) face" can
     simply fund the pool ahead of the corresponding mints.

** Conservation is Automatic

   No additional Jubilee stream is emitted from the pool.  The pool
   account participates in the BUCK system's ordinary demurrage
   accounting:

   - The Jubilee account grows at rate
     \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \) per block.  The pool's
     balance \( B \) is part of \( \text{totalSupply} \), so the
     pool's share of Jubilee growth is exactly
     \( \kappa \cdot B \) -- the same rate a regular holder with
     balance \( B \) would contribute.
   - When the contract transfers \( v_{\text{delivered}} \) out, the
     standard ERC-20 demurrage accounting reduces \( (B, A) \) as
     for any other outgoing transfer.
   - When users transfer BUCK to the contract, the sender pays the
     standard demurrage on /their/ outgoing transfer; the contract
     receives a clean \( v \) that begins accruing age immediately.

   Conservation of the system total
   \( \sum_X B_X + B_{\text{Jubilee}} = \text{constant} \) is
   preserved transaction-by-transaction, with no pool-specific
   invariant to maintain.

** Numeric Example

   Suppose the pool holds \( B = 10\,000\,000 \) BUCK, has accumulated
   \( A = 5\,000\,000 \) BUCK-years (average BUCK-age of 0.5 yr), and
   \( F = 10\,000\,000 \) BUCK of live notes outstanding.  Then
   \( E = B - \kappa A = 10{,}000{,}000 - 0.02 \cdot 5{,}000{,}000 =
   9{,}900{,}000 \), and \( E/F = 0.99 \): every note redeems at 99%
   of face, independent of how long the note itself was held.

   Now suppose an anonymous donor sends 500,000 BUCK to the contract
   address.  \( B \) rises to \( 10{,}500{,}000 \); the donor's
   BUCK-age transfers along with the principal per standard ERC-20
   semantics (add to \( A \)), but the donor's demurrage on the
   /outgoing/ transfer is paid out of their own account, not the
   pool's.  The new ratio is roughly \( E'/F \approx 1.04 \): the
   next redemption delivers ~104% of face.  As redemptions drain
   \( F \) faster than \( B \), the premium compresses back toward
   1.0 and eventually below.

   No Jubilee payment was emitted by the pool during any of this; no
   aggregate-level invariant was adjusted.  The contract's \( (B, A) \)
   move through ordinary ERC-20 transitions; \( F \) moves through
   increments on mint and decrements on deposit.

** Who Bears the Demurrage, Really

   The simpler model has a sharper answer: *the current noteholder
   bears the demurrage that the pool accrued while holding their
   BUCK-equivalent.*  When you mint a note of face \( v \), you
   transfer \( v \) BUCK to the pool.  While the pool holds that
   \( v \), it accrues BUCK-age at rate \( v \).  When /any/ note is
   redeemed, it pays its share of the pool's total BUCK-age
   (\( \kappa A \)) weighted by its fraction of face (\( v/F \)).

   This is not exactly "you pay for your own note's age": if the pool
   has high turnover, \( A \) stays small relative to \( B \), and
   your per-note demurrage is correspondingly small even if you
   personally held for a long time.  Conversely, in a stagnant pool,
   even a short-held note pays a larger share.  The pool is a
   /mutualised/ demurrage system among noteholders -- and in the
   limit where you are the only noteholder, it reduces to "you pay
   for your own age" exactly.

* Long-Term Holding Safety: The Printer-Trust Distinction

  Pool-rate demurrage makes every note type equally cheap to /hold/.
  What distinguishes them for long-term storage is not demurrage but
  the /printer-trust/ model: who, besides the intended holder, retains
  information that could let them spend the note?

** The Printer-Trust Model

   At mint time, the issuer knows:
   - The note's commitment preimage
     \( (v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}, \text{predicate}) \)
   - Whatever additional secret is packed into \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}} \)
     (the issuer's \( sk_{\text{issuer}} \) for Type 1, the ephemeral
     \( r_e \) for Type 3, etc.)

   The question is whether this knowledge enables the issuer -- or
   anyone else with the same knowledge -- to spend the note later.

** Type 1 (Encrypted Issuer Identity): Safe

   The spend predicate requires a Chaum-Pedersen proof of equality
   with the depositor's /registered/ credential.  The issuer is the
   intended eventual depositor (possibly via a different registered
   account sharing the same \( M \)).  No third party can spend: even
   with full knowledge of \( \rho \), a non-issuer's registered
   credential does not encrypt \( M_{\text{issuer}} \), so the CP
   relation fails.

   *Long-term holding is safe.*  The only person who can spend is the
   issuer, and the issuer is the intended holder.

** Type 2 (Public Issuer Identity): Unsafe for Long-Term Storage

   The spend predicate requires only knowledge of \( \rho \) plus any
   registered identity.  The issuer minted the note, so the issuer
   knows \( \rho \).  If the issuer retains any copy of \( \rho \)
   after printing the physical note -- in a database row, a printed
   backup, a backup transaction -- the issuer can race the holder to
   deposit.

   This is not a cryptographic failure; it is a policy failure.  A
   trustworthy issuer /could/ destroy \( \rho \) after printing.  But
   the /holder has no way to verify that destruction/.  The note is
   only as safe as the issuer's operational discipline.

   *Long-term holding is unsafe.*  Type 2 notes should be cashed
   promptly (hours to days).  They are a cheque, not a savings
   instrument.  In the failure mode where the issuer does retain
   \( \rho \), legal recourse is via the issuer's registered
   Identity -- which is the point of making the issuer /public/: they
   cannot deny having printed the note, and the registered Identity
   is subpoena-able.

   For use cases that need long-term safety with public attribution
   (e.g., an inheritance instrument where the issuer's identity must
   be known), the natural solution is to use Type 3 (stealth-targeted)
   and separately publish the issuer's identity in a signed
   accompanying statement rather than bake it into the commitment.

** Type 3 (Stealth-Targeted to Recipient): Safe

   The spend predicate is a Schnorr PoK of the stealth secret
   \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot sk_{\text{recipient}} \).
   Computing \( sk^\ast \) requires \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \), which
   only the recipient possesses.  The issuer knows \( r_e \) and can
   compute \( s = r_e \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \) and
   \( H_{\text{st}}(s) \), but /cannot/ compute \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s)
   \cdot sk_{\text{recipient}} \) without \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \)
   (Theorem 7, Corollary 1 -- "printer non-fungibility").

   *Long-term holding is safe.*  The issuer's knowledge of every
   mint-time secret does not enable them to spend.  Only the intended
   recipient, through their own \( sk \), can redeem.

** Summary: Which Notes Can Be Saved?

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Note type | Issuer can spend later? | Long-term holding safe? | Comparable instrument              |
   |-----------+-------------------------+-------------------------+------------------------------------|
   | Type 1    | Only to themselves      | Yes                     | Savings account (self-owned)       |
   | Type 2    | Yes (knows \( \rho \))  | No                      | Uncashed cheque (needs to clear)   |
   | Type 3    | No (Theorem 7)          | Yes                     | Registered bond (recipient-locked) |
   #+LATEX: }

   The consequence: /only Type 1 and Type 3 notes are suitable for
   long-term holding./  A user printing a Type 2 cheque and placing it
   in a safe deposit box for a decade is, cryptographically, trusting
   the issuer's operational discipline for a decade; a user doing the
   same with Type 1 or Type 3 is trusting only the cryptography.

   This constrains how the note types map to use cases:

   - *Long-term savings, cold storage, estate, self-wormhole:*
     Type 1 (if you are your own beneficiary) or Type 3 (if a named
     heir is the beneficiary).
   - *Cheques, payroll, invoices:* Type 2, cashed promptly.
   - *Targeted gifts, remittances, inheritance:* Type 3.
   - *Wormhole-style cross-address transfers:* Type 1 exclusively.

* Required Identity Cryptography

  The unified design re-uses the existing Identity machinery
  ([[file:alberta-buck-identity.org][Identity]]) wherever possible, adding one new note-side
  primitive and one re-use of the Theorem 7 stealth-address
  construction.

** Components Already Present

   The following are unchanged from Identity v1 and are used directly:

   1. *Pointcheval-Sanders issuer signature* \( \sigma \) on the
      Identity scalar \( m \).  Re-randomisation continues to be used
      for unlinkable credential generation (Identity Fountain).  No
      change.
   2. *ElGamal registered credential*
      \( E = (R, C) = (r G, M + r \cdot pk) \) where
      \( M = m \cdot G \).  Continues to be the on-chain identity
      witness.  No change.
   3. *Chaum-Pedersen equality of discrete logs*.  Used by Type 1
      spend predicate for the same-\( M \) proof; identical to the
      =approve= flow's CP proof, just wrapped in a SNARK.  Theorem 6
      applies unchanged.
   4. *Schnorr PoK of \( sk \) for registered \( pk \)*.  Used by Type
      2 and Type 3 spend predicates to bind the depositing account to
      a registered identity.  Single-statement case of CP, Theorems
      3-5 apply verbatim.
   5. *IdentityRegistry* mapping between on-chain addresses and
      registered credentials (PS signatures, ElGamal ciphertexts,
      public-claim flags).

** New Primitive: The Note Commitment Hash

   A SNARK-friendly commitment hash \( H_{\text{cm}} \) (Poseidon) is
   needed to compute the commitment inside the spend circuit.  The
   hash is deterministic, collision-resistant over the note's field,
   and efficient in R1CS; Poseidon over the BN254 scalar field is the
   natural choice and is already used by the Aztec and Zcash Sapling
   circuits.

   No novel cryptographic construction; only a circuit-level primitive
   that is standard in modern SNARK systems.

** New Primitive: The Nullifier PRF

   The nullifier PRF \( \text{PRF}_{\text{key}}(\rho) \) is also
   Poseidon-based, keyed differently per type:

   - *Type 1:*  key = \( sk_{\text{issuer}} \) (which, per Theorem 6,
     equals the depositor's \( sk \) since same \( M \)).  Nullifier
     is therefore computable by the depositor and uniquely determined
     by \( \rho \) and the Identity scalar shared between issuer and
     depositor.
   - *Type 2:*  key = \( \rho \) itself; nullifier =
     \( H_{\text{nf}}(\rho) \), publicly computable by anyone who
     knows \( \rho \).  This is the reason Type 2 is vulnerable to
     issuer-race: whoever knows \( \rho \) can compute the nullifier
     and race a deposit.
   - *Type 3:*  key = \( sk^\ast \) (the stealth secret, known only
     to the recipient).  The nullifier is uniquely bound to
     \( (sk^\ast, \rho) \); only the intended recipient can compute
     it.

   Cross-type nullifier-space separation is enforced by a
   type-domain-separator byte in the PRF input, so collisions across
   types are avoided.

** Re-Used Primitive: Theorem 7 (Stealth Address)

   The stealth-address construction \( pk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s)
   \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \) is proved sound in the proofs
   document (Theorem 7) under DLog on \( G_1 \) alone.  The same
   construction is used here verbatim for Type 3 notes.  No
   modification, no new assumption.

** The Re-Encryption Operation at Mint

   Type 1 and Type 3 require the issuer to perform a re-encryption
   operation at mint time:

   *Type 1 mint-time re-encryption.*  The issuer takes their own
   registered \( M_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} \cdot G \) and
   produces \( E_{\text{issuer}}^{(\text{note})} = (r_i G, M_{\text{issuer}} +
   r_i \cdot pk_{\text{issuer}}) \) with fresh \( r_i \).  This is
   just a fresh ElGamal encryption under the issuer's existing pk; the
   same operation Alice performs at =approve= time.

   *Type 3 mint-time stealth derivation.*  The issuer picks
   \( r_e \leftarrow \mathbb{Z}_q^\ast \), computes \( R_e = r_e G \),
   computes the shared point \( s = r_e \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \)
   (the issuer can do this because she knows \( r_e \) and
   \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \)), and computes \( pk^\ast =
   H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot pk_{\text{recipient}} \).  She packs
   \( (R_e, pk^\ast) \) into the commitment.  She does /not/ compute
   \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(s) \cdot sk_{\text{recipient}} \): she
   can't, because she lacks \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \).

   Both operations are single-curve-multiplication and single-hash
   operations on BN254 G_1.  No pairing, no new hardness assumption.

** The Re-Encryption Operation at Spend (Type 1 Only)

   A Type 1 depositor must produce a fresh ElGamal encryption
   \( E_{\text{dep}} = (R_d, C_d) = (r_d G, M_{\text{issuer}} + r_d
   \cdot pk_{\text{dep}}) \) and a SNARK-internal CP proof that
   \( E_{\text{issuer}}^{(\text{note})} \) and \( E_{\text{dep}} \)
   decrypt to the same \( M \).  This is Theorem 6 (end-to-end
   re-encryption correctness) lifted into the SNARK circuit: the
   statement proved is identical to the on-chain CP proof from
   =approve=, just encoded as a SNARK witness rather than a
   Fiat-Shamir NIZK.

   The extractable witness is \( (sk_{\text{issuer}}, r_d) \), and
   since \( sk_{\text{issuer}} \) is bound (by registration) to
   \( M_{\text{issuer}} \), the proof establishes that the depositor
   owns the registered credential that encrypts the same \( M \) the
   issuer encrypted.

** The Schnorr PoK Operation at Spend (Type 2 and Type 3)

   Both Type 2 and Type 3 spenders include a Schnorr PoK of
   \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for their registered \( pk_{\text{dep}} \),
   Fiat-Shamir bound to the spend transaction.  For Type 3, an
   /additional/ Schnorr PoK of \( sk^\ast \) for \( pk^\ast \) is
   required, as per Theorem 7.

   Both Schnorr proofs are standard; no novelty.

** Summary: New Cryptographic Components

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Component                          | Status                                     | Assumption |
   |------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------+------------|
   | Poseidon commitment hash           | New circuit primitive (standard in SNARKs) | ROM-like   |
   | Poseidon nullifier PRF             | New circuit primitive                      | ROM-like   |
   | PS signature (issuer)              | Re-used from Identity                      | q-SDH      |
   | ElGamal credential + re-encryption | Re-used from Identity                      | DLog       |
   | Chaum-Pedersen equality proof      | Re-used (lifted into SNARK) from Identity  | DLog + ROM |
   | Stealth address derivation         | Re-used from Proofs Theorem 7              | DLog + ROM |
   | Schnorr PoK of \( sk \)            | Re-used from Identity                      | DLog + ROM |
   | SNARK proof system (Groth16/PLONK) | Standard on BN254                          | system-dep |
   #+LATEX: }

   Only the Poseidon hash family is new, and it is already standard
   in the SNARK ecosystem.  No novel hardness assumption is
   introduced.

* The SNARK Circuits

  Three spend circuits (one per note type) and one mint circuit are
  required.  Alternatively, a single circuit conditioned on a
  public =type= input subsumes all three spends; this is the more
  efficient deployment choice but costs a larger universal verifier.

** Mint Circuit

   *Public inputs.*  =totalFace= (the total face value \( \sum v_i \)
   of the notes being minted), \( \{cm_1, \ldots, cm_N\} \) (commitments
   to be appended), and =issuerRegistryEntry= (a Merkle root or index
   into IdentityRegistry attesting to the issuer's registered
   credential).

   *Witnesses.*  For each note \( i \):
   \( (type_i, v_i, \rho_i, \text{id}_{\text{payload},i},
   \text{predicate}_i) \), plus the issuer's private material for
   whatever \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}} \) the note requires.

   *Constraints.*
   1. \( \sum v_i = \texttt{totalFace} \) (amount conservation).
   2. Each \( cm_i = H_{\text{cm}}(\text{type}_i, v_i, \rho_i,
      \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}, \text{predicate}_i) \) (opening
      binding).
   3. For each Type 1 note: \( \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}^{(1)} =
      (R_{i,i}, M_{\text{issuer}} + r_{i,i} \cdot pk_{\text{issuer}}) \)
      with \( R_{i,i} = r_{i,i} G \), and \( M_{\text{issuer}} \)
      matches the issuer's registered ElGamal decryption.
   4. For each Type 2 note: \( \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}^{(2)} =
      (m_{\text{issuer}}, \sigma_i) \) with \( \sigma_i \) a valid
      Schnorr signature on \( cm_i \) under
      \( pk_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} G \).
   5. For each Type 3 note: \( \text{id}_{\text{payload},i}^{(3)} =
      (R_{e,i}, pk^\ast_i) \) with \( R_{e,i} = r_{e,i} G \) and
      \( pk^\ast_i = H_{\text{st}}(r_{e,i} \cdot
      pk_{\text{recipient},i}) \cdot pk_{\text{recipient},i} \), for
      each note's target recipient.

   *Contract side.*  The contract verifies the proof, pulls =totalFace=
   BUCK from the caller (ordinary ERC-20 transfer, caller pays their
   own outgoing-transfer demurrage), appends each \( cm_i \) to
   =noteTree=, and increments =noteFaceSum= by =totalFace=.  No other
   state is touched -- the contract's \( (B, A) \) update through the
   normal transfer path.

** Spend Circuit (Type 1 -- Encrypted Issuer)

   *Public inputs.*  =noteRoot= (current Merkle root of =noteTree=),
   =nullifier=, =faceValue= (the note's face \( v \), revealed),
   =depositorRegistryEntry= (pointer into IdentityRegistry for the
   depositor's credential).

   *Witnesses.*  \( (v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)},
   \text{path}) \), \( sk_{\text{issuer/dep}} \) (shared, by Theorem 6),
   the depositor's fresh encryption randomness \( r_d \), and the
   depositor's registered credential
   \( E_{\text{dep}}^{(\text{registry})} \).

   *Constraints.*
   1. \( cm = H_{\text{cm}}(1, v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)},
      \text{pred}) \) opens to a leaf of =noteTree= under =path=
      (Merkle inclusion).
   2. =nullifier= = \( H_{\text{nf}}(1 \| sk_{\text{dep}} \| \rho) \).
   3. \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(1)} = (R_i, C_i) \); the
      registered credential \( E_{\text{dep}}^{(\text{registry})} =
      (R_d^{\text{reg}}, C_d^{\text{reg}}) \) exists in
      IdentityRegistry; CP-equality \( C_i - sk_{\text{dep}} R_i =
      C_d^{\text{reg}} - sk_{\text{dep}} R_d^{\text{reg}} \) holds
      (Theorem 6).
   4. =faceValue= \( = v \) (witness \( v \) revealed as public input).

   The contract checks the nullifier is unused, reads its own \( (B, A) \)
   and \( F = \texttt{noteFaceSum} \), computes
   \( \texttt{delivered} = v \cdot (B - \kappa A) / F \), transfers
   =delivered= BUCK to the recipient via standard ERC-20, and
   decrements =noteFaceSum= by \( v \).  The demurrage arithmetic
   lives entirely in the contract, not the SNARK.

** Spend Circuit (Type 2 -- Public Issuer)

   *Public inputs.*  =noteRoot=, =nullifier=, =faceValue=,
   =issuerPkClaim= (the issuer's public-identity claim for
   human-readable verification, redundant with the circuit's own
   check but useful for wallets), =depositorRegistryEntry=.

   *Witnesses.*  \( (v, \rho, \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(2)},
   \text{path}) \), \( sk_{\text{dep}} \), the depositor's Schnorr
   nonce \( k_{\text{dep}} \).

   *Constraints.*
   1. Merkle inclusion of \( cm \) under =path= (as Type 1).
   2. =nullifier= = \( H_{\text{nf}}(2 \| \rho) \) (publicly derivable
      from \( \rho \) -- this is the first-come-first-served property
      of Type 2).
   3. \( \text{id}_{\text{payload}}^{(2)} = (m_{\text{issuer}},
      \sigma_{\text{issuer}}) \) with \( \sigma_{\text{issuer}} \)
      verifying against \( pk_{\text{issuer}} = m_{\text{issuer}} G \)
      in IdentityRegistry.
   4. Schnorr PoK of \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for
      \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) in IdentityRegistry, Fiat-Shamir bound
      to =msg.sender=, =chainid=, =nullifier=.
   5. =faceValue= \( = v \).

   The contract's delivery computation is identical to Type 1:
   \( \texttt{delivered} = v \cdot (B - \kappa A) / F \), computed
   outside the SNARK.

** Spend Circuit (Type 3 -- Stealth-Targeted)

   *Public inputs.*  =noteRoot=, =nullifier=, =faceValue=,
   =depositorRegistryEntry=.  \( pk^\ast \) is /not/ a public input:
   revealing it would let an observer look up
   \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \) in IdentityRegistry and deanonymise the
   target.  Instead, \( pk^\ast \) is a private witness, and the
   Merkle inclusion proof binds it to the commitment.

   *Witnesses.*  \( (v, \rho, R_e, pk^\ast, \text{path}) \),
   \( sk_{\text{recipient}} \), the stealth secret
   \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(sk_{\text{recipient}} \cdot R_e) \cdot
   sk_{\text{recipient}} \), \( sk_{\text{dep}} \), Schnorr nonces.

   *Constraints.*
   1. Merkle inclusion of \( cm = H_{\text{cm}}(3, v, \rho, (R_e,
      pk^\ast), \text{pred}) \).
   2. =nullifier= = \( H_{\text{nf}}(3 \| sk^\ast \| \rho) \).
   3. \( sk^\ast \cdot G = pk^\ast \) (Theorem 7 completeness).
   4. \( sk^\ast = H_{\text{st}}(sk_{\text{recipient}} \cdot R_e) \cdot
      sk_{\text{recipient}} \) (Theorem 7 setup; also binds
      \( pk_{\text{recipient}} \) indirectly via this relation).
   5. Schnorr PoK of \( sk_{\text{dep}} \) for
      \( pk_{\text{dep}} \) in IdentityRegistry.
   6. =faceValue= \( = v \).

   As with Types 1 and 2, the contract computes
   \( \texttt{delivered} = v \cdot (B - \kappa A) / F \) outside the
   SNARK.

   /Optional identity-binding./  If the recipient wishes to bind the
   deposit to their registered identity (for tax/audit purposes),
   the circuit additionally proves CP-equality between
   \( E_{\text{dep}}^{(\text{registry})} \) and a re-encryption of
   the Identity point \( M \) decrypted from
   \( E_{\text{recipient}}^{(\text{registry})} \), matching the
   recipient's own credential.  This variant is opt-in; the
   minimal-privacy variant simply requires /any/ registered depositor.

** Circuit Engineering Budget

   Ballpark constraint counts (orders of magnitude, BN254 Groth16):

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Circuit element                      | Approx. constraints |
   |--------------------------------------+---------------------|
   | Poseidon commitment hash (1 call)    | ~250                |
   | Poseidon nullifier PRF (1 call)      | ~250                |
   | Merkle path verification (20 levels) | ~5000               |
   | ElGamal / Chaum-Pedersen (Type 1)    | ~2000               |
   | Schnorr PoK (Types 2/3)              | ~500                |
   | Stealth-address relation (Type 3)    | ~1500               |
   | Range check on \( v \)               | ~100                |
   #+LATEX: }

   Total spend-circuit constraint count is in the range 6,000-10,000,
   comparable to Zcash Sapling's Spend circuit (~25,000 including
   note-commitment randomness features we don't need) and
   Aztec's basic note-spend circuit.  On-chip proving time on a
   modern smartphone: a few seconds.

** Common Circuit Gadgets

   A base-level library of gadgets is shared across all spend types:

   - Merkle inclusion (Poseidon path)
   - Poseidon hash wrapper
   - Schnorr PoK
   - Range proofs on field elements (for \( v \))

   Type-specific gadgets layer on top.  This is standard SNARK
   library architecture (Aztec Noir, Circom, Halo2 all support this
   layering).

#+LATEX: \clearpage
* Migration from v1 Wormhole and BUCK Cash

  The transition is orderly and preserves all existing cryptographic
  anchors.

** Wormhole v1 to Notes

   A v1 burn-address certificate \( (addr, v, age) \) maps to a
   single Type 1 note:

   #+begin_example
   migrate_v1_to_notes(addr, v, age):
     sk_addr    = recovered Schnorr key bound to addr
     pk_addr    = sk_addr * G
     M_addr     = registered M for addr (from IdentityRegistry)
     r_i        = fresh random
     R_i, C_i   = ElGamal(M_addr, r_i, pk_addr)
     rho        = fresh random
     cm         = Poseidon(1, v_effective, rho, (R_i, C_i), None)
     # v_effective = v - kappa * age folds the certificate's
     # accrued BUCK-age into its face value at migration time.
     submit mint proof: burn (addr, v, age), append cm to noteTree
   #+end_example

   The v1 certificate's accumulated demurrage is discharged at
   migration (by computing the certificate's current effective value
   \( v_{\text{eff}} = v - \kappa \cdot \text{age} \) and using that
   as the note's face).  After migration the note participates in the
   pool as a normal Type 1 note; its subsequent demurrage is the
   pool-rate, not the per-certificate rate.  The v1 burn address is
   emptied; its BUCK (net of demurrage) flows into the contract
   balance \( B \) exactly as any other mint.

** BUCK Cash to Notes

   - *Cash Mode B (stealth-targeted)* maps one-to-one to Type 3; the
     cryptographic structure is identical.
   - *Cash Mode C (programmable)* can be expressed as a Type 1, 2, or
     3 note with a non-trivial =predicate= field in the commitment,
     evaluated by the spend circuit.  The three base types subsume
     the most common programmable variants (time locks are a
     predicate on \( t_{\text{mint}} \); oracle conditionals are a
     predicate on oracle state; multi-sig is a conjunction of
     multiple Schnorr PoKs).
   - *Cash Mode A (pure bearer)* does not map to any of the three
     types.  See "What Gets Dropped" below.

** v1 Coexistence During Transition

   The unified Notes architecture can be deployed alongside the v1
   Wormhole during a transition window.  The v1 burn-address pool
   remains a valid anonymity set; new users mint into the Notes pool;
   v1 holders migrate at their convenience.  The Notes contract's
   =noteFaceSum= reflects only Notes-pool activity; v1 certificates
   continue to accrue per-certificate demurrage as before, via their
   individual burn-address accounts.

   The final /old/ v1 certificate need not be migrated -- it can
   continue to be redeemed against its burn-address anchor
   indefinitely, at the cost of the contract maintaining two
   verifier paths.  A practical deadline (e.g., 5 years from Notes
   launch) is reasonable.

* What Gets Dropped: Pure Bearer

  The Cash document's /Mode A -- bearer note with QR code sealed under
  a hologram, anyone who possesses the QR can deposit/ does not map
  cleanly onto the unified scheme.  The reason is the same as why
  Mode A in the Cash design required a physical hologram seal: a
  purely secret-based predicate (=know(rho)= with no identity hook)
  has no cryptographic mechanism preventing a malicious issuer from
  retaining \( \rho \) and racing the holder.

** Why Mode A Is Absent

   Three routes considered:

   1. /Blind minting/ -- the issuer does not know \( \rho \) at mint
      time.  Requires the holder to participate in minting (giving a
      blinded commitment).  Incompatible with the "print a stack of
      bearer notes and hand them out" use case.

   2. /Trusted printer/ -- hardware security module or audited print
      service that provably erases \( \rho \).  Works in principle;
      undermines the "trustless bearer instrument" pitch.

   3. /Short expiry/ -- Mode A notes expire after a bounded period,
      limiting the issuer-race window.  Equivalent to Type 2 with a
      short-term contract; the use case is then cheque-like, not
      cash-like.

   None of these route options preserve the "$20 bill-equivalent"
   property that Mode A was designed for.  The unified scheme
   therefore /does not attempt/ to provide a pure-bearer cryptographic
   note, and defers that use case to a separate design dimension:

** The Physical-Token Escape Hatch

   A pure-bearer instrument remains available via the /physical/
   trust model that all physical bearer instruments already rely on.
   Specifically: an issuer operating a print service with a
   hardware-attested erasure of \( \rho \) can issue Type 2 notes
   with a public warranty that the service did in fact erase
   \( \rho \).  The warranty is an off-chain social/legal claim, not
   a cryptographic one, but it is identical in kind to the trust
   extended to the Bank of Canada when it issues physical polymer
   banknotes (whose security rests on BoC printing discipline, not
   on cryptography).

   This keeps the pure-bearer door ajar without requiring the
   unified scheme to pretend it solves the cryptographic problem of
   making a truly trustless bearer note.  BUCK Cash's original Mode A
   was arguably already conceding this point via the tamper-evident
   seal ("the seal makes it safe to display, but any unsealed QR is
   spendable by anyone"): the seal is a physical-world primitive, not
   a cryptographic one.

** Use-Case Coverage Without Mode A

   The canonical Mode A use cases re-route cleanly:

   #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
   | Use case                   | Cash Mode A          | Unified scheme                             |
   |----------------------------+----------------------+--------------------------------------------|
   | Tip jar                    | Printed Mode A sheet | Public-issuer Type 2 cheques, held briefly |
   | Walking-around money       | Printed Mode A notes | Self-issued Type 1 in a hot wallet         |
   | Gifts to unknown recipient | Printed Mode A       | Type 2 signed cheque, short expiry         |
   | Disaster preparedness cold | Printed Mode A hoard | Self-issued Type 1, long-term safe         |
   | Underbanked payment rail   | Physical Mode A      | Type 2 cheque from issuer's office         |
   #+LATEX: }

   Every use case that truly requires "no identity link at all" is
   handled by the physical-token escape hatch above.  Every use case
   that tolerates an /encrypted/ identity link (which is most of
   them) gains stronger cryptographic guarantees via Types 1 and 3.

* Security Assumptions and Theorems Used

  The unified Notes design rests on the same cryptographic foundations
  as the existing Identity system, plus one already-proved additional
  theorem:

  #+LATEX: {\scriptsize
  | Primitive / Theorem              | Usage in Notes                 | Assumption           |
  |----------------------------------+--------------------------------+----------------------|
  | PS signature (issuer credential) | Mint-circuit issuer-binding    | q-SDH                |
  | ElGamal on \( G_1 \)             | Type 1 identity payload        | DLog on \( G_1 \)    |
  | Theorem 6 (CP re-encryption)     | Type 1 spend predicate         | DLog + ROM           |
  | Theorem 7 (stealth-targeted)     | Type 3 spend predicate         | DLog + ROM           |
  | Schnorr PoK (sigma protocols)    | Type 2, Type 3 depositor check | DLog + ROM           |
  | Poseidon hash/PRF (SNARK-side)   | Commitments, nullifiers        | ROM-like (circuit)   |
  | SNARK proof system (e.g., PLONK) | Wrapping all of the above      | system-specific      |
  | Merkle tree (noteTree)           | Commitment anchor              | collision-resistance |
  #+LATEX: }

  No /novel/ cryptographic assumption is introduced.  The only new
  primitive not already in BUCK is the Poseidon hash family, which is
  standard in SNARK deployments (Aztec, Zcash Sapling/Orchard,
  Starknet, Mina) and well-studied.

** Demurrage Conservation as a Contract Invariant

   Because the pool is a regular BUCK account, no Notes-specific
   demurrage invariant is needed: the system-wide BUCK invariant

   \[
   \sum_{\text{all accounts}} B_X \;+\; B_{\text{Jubilee}}
   \;=\; \text{constant}
   \]

   covers it automatically.  The Notes contract only needs to
   maintain two auxiliary local invariants:

   1. \( F \geq 0 \) (no negative note face sum).
   2. Every \( v \) appearing in a deposit equals the \( v \) that
      appeared in the corresponding mint (enforced by the SNARK via
      the Merkle inclusion of \( cm \) binding \( v \)).

   Both are trivial to prove from the contract's state machine; no
   queueing-theoretic assumption is required.  The arithmetic
   \( \texttt{delivered} = v \cdot (B - \kappa A)/F \) is a simple
   contract-side computation against on-chain ERC-20 state.

* Open Questions

  The unified design resolves most of the architectural redundancy
  but leaves a handful of economic and cryptographic questions worth
  sharpening before commitment:

** Donation-Driven Premia and Their Incidence

   Because \( E/F \) can exceed 1 whenever donations accumulate
   faster than redemptions drain them, the redemption rate seen by
   users is subject to external "weather".  A large donation
   immediately before a redemption benefits that single depositor;
   a large donation spread across many redemptions benefits all of
   them proportionally.  This is equitable /in expectation/ but
   bursty in any given window.

   Whether to smooth the bursts -- e.g., by rate-limiting the
   fraction of \( E/F > 1 \) a single redemption can claim, or by
   time-averaging \( B \) -- is a design question.  The simplest
   answer is to do nothing: the pool is transparent, the premium
   math is public, and the on-chain sequencing is first-come.

** Stranded Value on Lost Notes

   A lost note contributes to \( F \) indefinitely.  Its share of
   \( B \) is never claimed by the intended redeemer; instead, the
   pool's BUCK-age continues to accrue against that unclaimed face
   value, and future redeemers pay demurrage partially on behalf of
   the lost note.  As \( A/F \) creeps upward with unreclaimed
   value, \( E/F \) drifts downward; holders of live notes see a
   slowly growing demurrage tax until the lost face value washes
   out.

   A reclaim path is possible: after a very long period (say 99
   years), the contract could treat \( F \) as including only notes
   whose commitments are demonstrably recent, via a rolling
   window.  Lost value then re-surfaces as donation-equivalent at
   the window boundary.  This requires careful circuit and
   economic-policy design and is deferred.

** Integer-Arithmetic Rounding

   The deposit formula \( v \cdot (B - \kappa A)/F \) involves
   integer division.  Residuals left behind by truncated division
   remain in \( B \) (they are never transferred out), raising
   \( E/F \) for the /next/ depositor -- the rounding is
   self-healing rather than drift-accumulating.  Choice of rounding
   direction (floor vs. banker's) is a minor contract-level
   decision with no cryptographic impact.

** Predicate Programmability vs. Circuit Simplicity

   Keeping three fixed types simplifies the circuit count.  Adding
   programmable predicates (time locks, oracle conditions,
   multi-sigs) re-introduces the complexity of Cash Mode C.  A
   compromise: support a /bounded/ predicate language in the circuit
   (time-lock + oracle-condition + up-to-3-sig) rather than a fully
   general one.  The bounded language covers 90%+ of the use cases
   Mode C was designed for while keeping the verifier compact.

** Universal Spend Circuit vs. Three

   Deploying three distinct spend verifiers costs gas per transaction
   (one verifier per call).  A universal verifier that takes =type=
   as a public input and branches internally costs a larger but
   single verifier.  Gas trade-off depends on production transaction
   mix; if Type 1 dominates (Wormhole-style), three verifiers are
   cheaper.  Empirical question, resolvable at deployment time.

** Regulatory Interaction with Redemption Premia

   Jurisdictional regulators (FINTRAC, OSFI) have rules around
   bearer instruments and tax treatment of negative-interest
   accounts.  BUCK's on-chain demurrage was argued through
   separately, and the Notes contract inherits that treatment with
   no pool-specific novelty -- the contract is just another BUCK
   account.  The one new wrinkle is that donations can push
   \( E/F > 1 \), producing redemption premia that look economically
   like small interest payments.  Whether these count as ordinary
   income, capital gain, or bearer-instrument appreciation is a
   tax-counsel question rather than a protocol one.  Not blocking
   but worth flagging.

* Summary

  The Wormhole and BUCK Cash designs converge naturally on a single
  note-pool primitive.  Three Identity-binding types --
  Encrypted-Issuer (Type 1, Wormhole-style), Public-Issuer (Type 2,
  signed cheque), Stealth-Targeted (Type 3, addressed cheque) --
  together span every use case in the existing series while removing
  the burn-address / =_mint_noincrease= machinery, the per-certificate
  Identity registration, and the separate Cash Mode A/B/C taxonomy.

  Demurrage needs no new machinery: the Note Pool is literally the
  BUCK ERC-20 contract's own balance -- a regular BUCK account with
  its own \( B \) and \( A \) in the ERC-20 ledger.  The only
  Note-side state beyond the Merkle tree and nullifier set is one
  scalar \( F \), the total face value of live notes.  Redemption of
  face \( v \) delivers \( v \cdot (B - \kappa A)/F \): the note's
  proportional share of the pool's effective spendable value.
  Conservation falls out of the existing BUCK accounting -- the
  Jubilee grows autonomously at \( \kappa \cdot \text{totalSupply} \)
  per block, with no Notes-specific obligation stream.  Donations
  (deliberate, philanthropic, or accidental) raise \( B \) without
  raising \( F \), reducing or inverting the effective demurrage
  subsequent redeemers pay -- a useful property, not a bug.

  The printer-trust distinction produces a sharp partition of which
  note types are safe for long-term holding: only /Type 1/ (issuer =
  beneficiary) and /Type 3/ (stealth-targeted to beneficiary) can be
  held for arbitrary durations.  /Type 2/ is cryptographically a
  short-term instrument regardless of how long a user might wish to
  keep it, because the issuer retains the spending secret.  This is
  an intrinsic property that cannot be remedied by additional
  cryptography; it follows directly from the Type 2 identity payload
  carrying the issuer's public identity and no recipient-binding.

  Pure-bearer notes (Cash Mode A) are not replaced by any
  cryptographic Type; they remain the domain of physical trust
  models (tamper-evident seals, trusted printers).  This is a
  philosophical refinement of the Cash proposal rather than a
  retreat: Cash Mode A already relied on a physical hologram for
  its security, and the unified design makes the physical/crypto
  boundary explicit.

  No novel cryptographic assumption is introduced.  Theorems 6 and
  7 of the Proofs document ([[file:alberta-buck-proofs.org][Proofs]]) apply verbatim to the Type 1
  and Type 3 spend predicates, respectively; Type 2 reduces to
  Schnorr.  The only new circuit-level primitive is Poseidon, which
  is already standard across the SNARK ecosystem.  Implementation
  scope is 6-9 months for a focused team.

  The unification is, in the small, a refactor that removes
  redundancy.  In the large, it crystallises the Alberta Buck design
  around a single cryptographic primitive (the note pool) whose
  Identity-binding dimension produces the full range of financial
  instruments the series aspires to, and whose economic
  dimension (pool-rate demurrage) elegantly solves the lost-note
  problem that motivated the original BUCK Cash demurrage discussion.
